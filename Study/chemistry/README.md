# Chemistry Path / 化学学习路径

The product specification is [Plan.md](Plan.md). This folder contains the first independent Study app.

Current status: **Content-rich learning prototype; the full V0.1 progress/mastery/review loop remains incomplete.** The bilingual home and learning path now lead into 118 available lessons (538 quiz questions and 96 separate quick checks), covering Scientific Thinking & Lab Basics, Matter & Particles, Elements/Compounds/Mixtures, atomic structure through ions, the periodic table, Ions & Chemical Bonding, Chemical Formulae & Naming, Chemical Reactions & Equations, Acids, Bases & Salts, Metals & Redox, the mole concept, Solutions & Concentration, Gases, Energetics, Reaction Rates, Chemical Equilibrium, Electrochemistry, Organic Chemistry, Intro Analytical Chemistry, and Integrated Problem Solving. Lessons have real-life hooks, interactive models, misconception repair, practical observations or virtual challenges, bilingual vocabulary and immediate-feedback quizzes. The current quiz stores its latest completed score locally; it does not yet establish independent mastery or schedule reviews. Availability does not establish scientific review or learning gains from child trials.

Lessons 117–118 add a **corrosion evidence bridge** with two on-screen activities, 12 quiz questions and six quick checks. The home sequence starts with “Why does iron rust?”, then compares six authored records across matched days. The grades describe visible rust. In the same A/B comparison, day one shows no visible difference and day five supports a difference. After the existing displacement and galvanising lessons, learners test eight Zn/Cu–Fe connectivity states and predict the electron donor. This model describes sustained coupling and its direction. It leaves local iron corrosion, actual rates and protection lifetime open. The sequence continues to “Why chemists use moles”.

**家庭使用。** 两节新课各用一次学习时间，约 18–19 分钟。第一课做完后，请孩子合上选项，说清 A/B 哪个条件不同以及第 1、5 天各支持什么。第二课请孩子画出两条通路，先解释锌连接时的电子方向，再换铜重新判断。隔天可以重问其中一题，记录孩子能独立说明的依据。

Lessons 115–116 add an **introductory pH-evidence bridge** after “Acids, bases and pH” and continue to “Neutralisation”. Nine paired methyl-orange/BTB records practise compatibility, overlapping ranges and the limits of exact-pH claims. Six complete relative aqueous inventories, each counted over 1–3 volume portions, separate H₃O⁺/OH⁻ classification, whole charge and sampling volume across 18 states. Acidic, neutral and basic examples can all have zero net charge; the records are scaled mean-composition models, not literal cup-sized ion counts. Two lazily loaded activities add 12 quiz questions and six quick checks. The older pH introduction now scopes the 0–14 display, while the salt lesson and its question explain dispersed hydrated ions rather than new salt molecules in solution. See [the pH evidence family guide](docs/ph-evidence-bridge.md).

Lessons 113–114 bridge **reaction evidence and atom-versus-molecule counts** after “Balancing equations” and continue to “Acids, bases and pH”. Eight authored record cards separate appearance from supplied identity evidence, including an unresolved endpoint after repeated cooling. Five specified molecular reaction ledgers with coefficients 1–4 provide 320 candidate inventories. Equal molecule totals can accompany either a balanced or an unbalanced atom ledger; peroxide decomposition also requires oxygen contributions from both products. Two lazily loaded activities add 12 quiz questions and six quick checks with bilingual goals, worked examples and own-words tasks. Neither record cards nor atom accounting claim live experiments, reaction conditions or completion. See [the reaction evidence family guide](docs/reaction-evidence-bridge.md).

Lessons 111–112 bridge **formula units, batches and polyatomic-group counts** after “Naming molecular compounds” and continue to “What is a chemical reaction?”. Five specified monatomic-ion pairs provide 180 candidate inventories for independent charge and simplest-ratio judgments. Six supplied formulas with coefficients 1–4 provide 24 counting maps, separating formula units, whole ions and internal atom compositions. NH₄NO₃ tests nitrogen contributions from two ions; Li₂O₂ preserves peroxide identity rather than reducing every atom subscript. Two lazily loaded activities add 12 quiz questions and six quick checks. The older ionic-formula lesson now scopes its reduction rule to specified monatomic-ion counts. See [the formula bridge family guide](docs/formula-bridge.md).

Lessons 109–110 bridge **shared-electron inventories and Lewis accounting** after “Structure shapes properties” and continue to “Reading chemical formulae”. Five supplied neutral molecules compare dot and line notation, whole valence inventories, overlapping local counts and omitted core electrons. A second model audits 144 restricted H₂/O₂/N₂ candidate drawings against both global budgets and local duet/octet targets, distinguishing a correct diagnosis from a valid draft. Two lazily loaded activities add 12 quiz questions and six quick checks with bilingual goals, worked examples and own-words tasks. The models do not claim actual geometry, orbital or spin descriptions, reaction predictions or a complete explanation of oxygen magnetism. See [the Lewis bridge family guide](docs/lewis-bridge.md).

Lessons 107–108 bridge **periodic positions and electron-transfer budgets** after “Group 18” and continue to “Why atoms make bonds”. Ten specified particle records distinguish ion arrangements from neutral ground-state element positions, retaining the He exception and Ca²⁺ counterexample. Five specified ion pairs with 1–4 atoms on each side provide 80 candidate inventories, testing electron supply, demand and charge without claiming reaction or completion. Two lazily loaded models add 12 quiz questions and six quick checks, with the same goals, worked examples and own-words scaffolding as preceding bridges. The original period lesson now states its neutral ground-state condition. See [the periodic bridge family guide](docs/periodic-bridge.md).

Lessons 105–106 bridge **atomic identity and isotope-weighted means** after “What is an ion?” and continue to “The periodic table”. Five given record pairs separate nuclear inventories from signed charge, including particles that differ isotopically and are both ions, plus equal-electron counterexamples. A twenty-card composition model compares two isotope pairs, two supplied mass-data modes and 21 proportions: 84 calculations, with predictions, numeric attempts, endpoint checks and sample/standard distinctions. Two lazily loaded models add 12 quiz questions and six quick checks. The older isotope lesson now avoids presenting isotope identity and ion status as mutually exclusive. See [the atomic bridge family guide](docs/atomic-bridge.md) for pacing, NIST data rounding and model limits.

Lessons 103–104 bridge **atoms, molecules and molecular formulas** after “What is an atom?” and return to “Protons” next. Learners classify six specified samples, enter atom/molecule counts, then multiply every atom type in five molecular formulas by 1–4 whole copies. Monatomic He, molecular O₂, O₂/O₃ mixtures and equal atom inventories with different grouping challenge common shortcuts. Two lazily loaded models add 12 quiz questions and six quick checks with bilingual goals, worked examples and own-words tasks. Existing introductory wording now distinguishes element types from elemental substances and scopes molecular-diagram rules away from ionic crystals. See [the molecule bridge family guide](docs/molecule-bridge.md).

Lessons 101–102 add a **beginner particle-evidence bridge**, after the original five Matter & particles lessons: audit phase diagrams without growing or losing molecules, then distinguish dissolving from melting and follow sugar through ordinary filtration and ideal water removal. Both include explicit prerequisites, three goals, four explanations, a delayed-reveal worked example, three quick checks, six concept-tagged quiz questions and an own-words task. Two lazily loaded models add 12 quiz questions and six quick checks; the second recommends “Pure substances and mixtures” next. Model counts, labels and coordinates are schematic, with closed-boundary and recovery assumptions visible. See [the particle bridge family guide](docs/particle-bridge.md).

Lessons 91–93 extend those ledgers into **Acid–base particles**: proton donor/acceptor roles → hydroxide equivalents and neutralisation → carbonate versus hydrogen carbonate acid budgets and gas boundaries. Each lesson has prerequisites, objectives, four explanations, a delayed-reveal worked example, three quick checks, six concept-tagged quiz questions and an own-words task. Lessons 94–96 build a guided **Electron ledgers** sequence: oxidation number versus whole-species charge → electron-balanced half-reactions → limiting displacement reactants and complete solid/solution inventories. Explicit HCl/Cl⁻ and NH₃/NH₄⁺ comparisons connect to proton-transfer lessons, with O₂ and peroxide counterexamples to overgeneralised rules. Multipliers can be balanced without being simplest; ideal Zn/Mg–Cu²⁺ calculations retain nitrate spectators and separate leftover metal from new copper. Three lazily loaded models add 18 quiz questions and nine quick checks, with the same objectives, examples and own-words scaffolding as earlier guided courses. See [the family guide, evidence and model limits](docs/electron-ledgers.md).

Lessons 71–72 connect molar concentration to numerical titration through prediction-first dilution diagrams and a measured-volume → moles → concentration ledger. Modules keep their topic grouping; the exported lesson list is sorted by stable lesson number so later additions do not renumber saved progress.

Lessons 73–74 connect tenfold pH steps, dilution and acid strength. Their ideal 25 °C models include water ionisation for extremely dilute HCl and calculate (rather than fix) the ethanoic-acid ionisation fraction within 0.001–0.100 mol/L. Equations stay in optional expandable panels, with learner-facing links to [OpenStax pH](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/14-2-ph-and-poh) and [acid strength](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/14-3-relative-strengths-of-acids-and-bases). Pure calculation tests cover neutrality, dilution limits, weak-acid equilibrium and the strength/concentration reversal.

Lessons 75–76 practise fair controls and average rates with authored, explicitly labelled gas-volume records, then distinguish the instant of an equilibrium disturbance from subsequent reaction. The fictional A₂ ⇌ 2A model solves equal forward/reverse flows with a conserved atomic inventory, including compression, endothermic-forward heating, catalysts and added product. Numerical tests cover controls, interval differences, inventory conservation and the fact that an adjustment does not fully undo an addition. Optional learner links point to [RSC graph interpretation](https://edu.rsc.org/lesson-plans/interpreting-rate-of-reaction-graphs-14-16-years/95.article) and [OpenStax equilibrium shifts](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/13-3-shifting-equilibria-le-chateliers-principle).

Lessons 77–78 turn recipe ratios into limiting-reactant and percent-yield calculations. A prediction-first water recipe tracks leftover reactants and conserves mass and both atom inventories across every offered stock combination. Authored lime-collection records separate dry pure CaO from an inert impurity, explaining apparent yields above 100% without claiming the theoretical maximum can be exceeded. Learners compare feed → moles → target product and investigate shortfalls instead of guessing a cause. Both lessons include six instant-feedback questions and optional [OpenStax reaction-yield reading](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/4-4-reaction-yields).

Lessons 79–80 distinguish a cell's external electron route from its internal ion route, then compare galvanic electrode signs with externally powered copper plating. The discrete Zn/Cu inventory balances each solution and the salt-bridge reservoir after compensating ion transfers; missing paths stop sustainable model steps. The ideal Cu/Cu²⁺ plating prediction conserves electrode copper mass and connects current × time to charge and deposition in an optional extension. Both lessons include six instant-feedback questions and optional [OpenStax galvanic-cell](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/17-2-galvanic-cells) and [electrolysis](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/17-7-electrolysis) reading. Tests cover all inventory stages, path interruptions, electron stoichiometry and every offered plating setting.

Lessons 81–82 compare atom inventory with connectivity, then follow ethene into hydrogen addition and polyethene repeats. The structure detective distinguishes redrawings, branched isomers and different formulas, with near-atmospheric boiling-point context linked to [NIST butane](https://webbook.nist.gov/cgi/cbook.cgi?ID=C106978&Type=TBOIL) and [isobutane](https://webbook.nist.gov/cgi/cbook.cgi?ID=C75285&Type=TBOIL) records. Diagram labels retain their font size on small screens. Addition models check atom counts and carbon valence; polymer views explicitly show interior segments with continuing bonds, not complete capped molecules. Seven numerical/graph tests cover inventories, relabelling, bond order and repeat segments. Each lesson adds six instant-feedback questions; optional [OpenStax hydrocarbon reading](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/20-1-hydrocarbons) supports further exploration.

Lessons 83–84 connect soup-sized batches to calorimetry, then use equation receipts and endpoint maps to introduce Hess’s law. Authored neutralisation records distinguish temperature rise, total solution heat, per-mole estimates and uncounted outside heat transfer; assumptions remain visible in an expandable panel. The Hess puzzle checks signed species inventories, direction, multipliers and CO cancellation rather than only a heat total. Its rounded standard-state values are linked to [OpenStax enthalpy](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/5-3-enthalpy), with optional [calorimetry reading](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/5-2-calorimetry). Both lessons have six immediate-feedback questions. Eight calculation tests cover every offered equation pair, atom conservation, cycle closure, batch scaling and heat signs. All activities are virtual, with fixed-size readable labels and compact mobile reflow.

Lessons 85–86 turn grams into composition evidence rather than memorised subscripts. A shared-scale mass/mole bar model compares two CH₂O empirical ratios with different molecular molar masses and an iron-oxide 1:1.5 ratio that must be scaled, not arbitrarily rounded. Hydrate records subtract tare, wait for repeated cooled readings and reject known solid loss even after constant mass; a magnesium-sulfate transfer case gives seven rather than five waters. Model records are authored, with rounded atomic masses and stated residue assumptions; no home heating procedure is provided. Ten calculation tests cover formula scaling, rounding limits, independent molecular multipliers, all weighing stages, spillage rejection and salt transfer. Both lessons add six immediate-feedback questions and optional links to [OpenStax composition](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/3-2-determining-empirical-and-molecular-formulas), [hydrate notation](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/2-7-chemical-nomenclature) and [PubChem magnesium sulfate heptahydrate](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/24843).
