# Chemistry Path / 化学学习路径

The product specification is [Plan.md](Plan.md). This folder contains the first independent Study app.

Current status: **Phase 1 in progress**. The bilingual home and learning path now lead into eighty-six complete lessons, covering Matter & Particles, Elements/Compounds/Mixtures, atomic structure through ions, the periodic table, Ions & Chemical Bonding, Chemical Formulae & Naming, Chemical Reactions & Equations, the opening lessons in Acids, Bases & Salts, Metals & Redox, the mole concept, Solutions & Concentration, Gases, Energetics, Reaction Rates, Chemical Equilibrium, Electrochemistry, Organic Chemistry, Intro Analytical Chemistry, and Integrated Problem Solving. Each lesson includes a real-life hook, an interactive model, misconception repair, a practical observation or virtual challenge, a bilingual vocabulary panel, and instant-feedback mastery questions saved locally.

Lessons 71–72 connect molar concentration to numerical titration through prediction-first dilution diagrams and a measured-volume → moles → concentration ledger. Modules keep their topic grouping; the exported lesson list is sorted by stable lesson number so later additions do not renumber saved progress.

Lessons 73–74 connect tenfold pH steps, dilution and acid strength. Their ideal 25 °C models include water ionisation for extremely dilute HCl and calculate (rather than fix) the ethanoic-acid ionisation fraction within 0.001–0.100 mol/L. Equations stay in optional expandable panels, with learner-facing links to [OpenStax pH](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/14-2-ph-and-poh) and [acid strength](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/14-3-relative-strengths-of-acids-and-bases). Pure calculation tests cover neutrality, dilution limits, weak-acid equilibrium and the strength/concentration reversal.

Lessons 75–76 practise fair controls and average rates with authored, explicitly labelled gas-volume records, then distinguish the instant of an equilibrium disturbance from subsequent reaction. The fictional A₂ ⇌ 2A model solves equal forward/reverse flows with a conserved atomic inventory, including compression, endothermic-forward heating, catalysts and added product. Numerical tests cover controls, interval differences, inventory conservation and the fact that an adjustment does not fully undo an addition. Optional learner links point to [RSC graph interpretation](https://edu.rsc.org/lesson-plans/interpreting-rate-of-reaction-graphs-14-16-years/95.article) and [OpenStax equilibrium shifts](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/13-3-shifting-equilibria-le-chateliers-principle).

Lessons 77–78 turn recipe ratios into limiting-reactant and percent-yield calculations. A prediction-first water recipe tracks leftover reactants and conserves mass and both atom inventories across every offered stock combination. Authored lime-collection records separate dry pure CaO from an inert impurity, explaining apparent yields above 100% without claiming the theoretical maximum can be exceeded. Learners compare feed → moles → target product and investigate shortfalls instead of guessing a cause. Both lessons include six instant-feedback questions and optional [OpenStax reaction-yield reading](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/4-4-reaction-yields).

Lessons 79–80 distinguish a cell's external electron route from its internal ion route, then compare galvanic electrode signs with externally powered copper plating. The discrete Zn/Cu inventory balances each solution and the salt-bridge reservoir after compensating ion transfers; missing paths stop sustainable model steps. The ideal Cu/Cu²⁺ plating prediction conserves electrode copper mass and connects current × time to charge and deposition in an optional extension. Both lessons include six instant-feedback questions and optional [OpenStax galvanic-cell](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/17-2-galvanic-cells) and [electrolysis](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/17-7-electrolysis) reading. Tests cover all inventory stages, path interruptions, electron stoichiometry and every offered plating setting.

Lessons 81–82 compare atom inventory with connectivity, then follow ethene into hydrogen addition and polyethene repeats. The structure detective distinguishes redrawings, branched isomers and different formulas, with near-atmospheric boiling-point context linked to [NIST butane](https://webbook.nist.gov/cgi/cbook.cgi?ID=C106978&Type=TBOIL) and [isobutane](https://webbook.nist.gov/cgi/cbook.cgi?ID=C75285&Type=TBOIL) records. Diagram labels retain their font size on small screens. Addition models check atom counts and carbon valence; polymer views explicitly show interior segments with continuing bonds, not complete capped molecules. Seven numerical/graph tests cover inventories, relabelling, bond order and repeat segments. Each lesson adds six instant-feedback questions; optional [OpenStax hydrocarbon reading](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/20-1-hydrocarbons) supports further exploration.

Lessons 83–84 connect soup-sized batches to calorimetry, then use equation receipts and endpoint maps to introduce Hess’s law. Authored neutralisation records distinguish temperature rise, total solution heat, per-mole estimates and uncounted outside heat transfer; assumptions remain visible in an expandable panel. The Hess puzzle checks signed species inventories, direction, multipliers and CO cancellation rather than only a heat total. Its rounded standard-state values are linked to [OpenStax enthalpy](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/5-3-enthalpy), with optional [calorimetry reading](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/5-2-calorimetry). Both lessons have six immediate-feedback questions. Eight calculation tests cover every offered equation pair, atom conservation, cycle closure, batch scaling and heat signs. All activities are virtual, with fixed-size readable labels and compact mobile reflow.

Lessons 85–86 turn grams into composition evidence rather than memorised subscripts. A shared-scale mass/mole bar model compares two CH₂O empirical ratios with different molecular molar masses and an iron-oxide 1:1.5 ratio that must be scaled, not arbitrarily rounded. Hydrate records subtract tare, wait for repeated cooled readings and reject known solid loss even after constant mass; a magnesium-sulfate transfer case gives seven rather than five waters. Model records are authored, with rounded atomic masses and stated residue assumptions; no home heating procedure is provided. Ten calculation tests cover formula scaling, rounding limits, independent molecular multipliers, all weighing stages, spillage rejection and salt transfer. Both lessons add six immediate-feedback questions and optional links to [OpenStax composition](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/3-2-determining-empirical-and-molecular-formulas), [hydrate notation](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/2-7-chemical-nomenclature) and [PubChem magnesium sulfate heptahydrate](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/24843).
