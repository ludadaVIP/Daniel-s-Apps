## 相同小份重复加入

三壶水每壶都有 3/4 L。先不要背乘法规则，想象把每壶分成三个四分之一升的小份，再把三壶合并。一个完整参考框始终是 1 L；把图放在一起，不会把“一升”改成三升。数相同大小的小份，才知道总水量。

整数乘分数可以表示相同分数量的重复相加。这里整数说有几组，分数说每组多少。分母指明每小份的大小；重复加入组数不改变这个大小，只改变有多少小份。分子和分母同时乘同一个数是在改写等值分数，不是把量增加那么多倍。

### 例 1 · 三壶四分之三升

1. 每壶 **3/4 L**，共有 **3 壶**。先估计：总量应比两升多、比三升少。
2. 每壶三个 **1/4 L** 小份，三壶共有 **3×3=9** 个同样小份。
3. **3×3/4=9/4 L**，不是 9/12 L。
4. 四个四分之一是一升，九个四分之一是 **2 1/4 L=2.25 L**，符合先前估计。
5. 用相加核查：**3/4+3/4+3/4=9/4 L**。单位仍是升，不是壶数。

工作台每行是一组，粗框每个一升。分子超过分母时，一组会跨过一个完整框，但框大小和一升标记保持不变。先预测总量，再改组数或每组量，最后核对；修改后旧结果会清空。

### 例 2 · 每组本身超过一

1. 每组 **5/4 L**，已有一升又四分之一升；现在有 **2 组**。
2. 一组五个四分之一，两组 **2×5=10** 个四分之一。
3. **2×5/4=10/4=5/2 L=2.5 L**。
4. 不能把分母也乘二写成 10/8，因为那只是仍等于 5/4 的等值分数。
5. 对照两组一又四分之一相加，确实为两升半。乘积大于每组，但不因为图多画了几个框就重新定义整体。

同样的想法适用于长度：五段各 1/6 m 的彩带，总长 **5×1/6=5/6 m**。也适用于零组：没有任何一组加入，**0×7/8=0**。零组与每组容量都要读清，不能因为知道某容器能装水，就把未加入的容量算进实际总水量。

数量有两个角色。说“四组各三分之二”时，可把 4×2/3 看作 2/3 的四次重复；数值上交换因数相等，但题目中“组数”和“每组量”的含义不同，解释时仍应保留它们。不要把份数和升数加在一起。

**带走一个发现：** 先说每组量和组数，再数同样大小的单位分数。家长可以让孩子指着一升粗框解释：为什么分母不随重复次数改变？出口题换成四壶各 3/8 L，先独立合计，再用相加或估计核查。

## Repeat amounts made from identical parts

Three jugs each hold 3/4 L. Before using a rule, imagine each jug as three quarter-litre pieces and combine them. One complete reference frame always means 1 L. Putting drawings together does not change a litre into three litres. Count equal-sized parts to find the total.

A whole number times a fraction can represent repeated addition of identical fractional amounts. The whole number counts groups; the fraction describes each group’s amount. Repeating groups changes the number of parts, not the size named by their denominator. Multiplying numerator and denominator together rewrites an equivalent fraction instead of multiplying the quantity.

### Example 1 · Three three-quarter-litre jugs

1. Each jug contains **3/4 L**, with **3 jugs**. Predict a total between two and three litres.
2. Each contributes three **1/4 L** parts. Together there are **3×3=9** quarter-litres.
3. **3×3/4=9/4 L**, rather than 9/12 L.
4. Nine quarters make **2 1/4 L=2.25 L**, matching the estimate.
5. Check by addition: **3/4+3/4+3/4=9/4 L**. The answer measures litres, not the number of jugs.

Each workshop row is one group, with thick one-litre frames. A numerator above its denominator extends a group beyond one frame while retaining frame size. Predict, change the inputs and then check. Changing an input clears the previous result.

### Example 2 · Each group already exceeds one

1. Each group is **5/4 L**, or one and a quarter litres, with **2 groups**.
2. Two groups of five quarter-litres contain **2×5=10** such parts.
3. **2×5/4=10/4=5/2 L=2.5 L**.
4. Writing 10/8 would keep the original 5/4 value rather than doubling it.
5. Adding one and a quarter twice confirms two and a half. Extra drawn frames do not redefine the reference whole.

The same reasoning gives five 1/6 m ribbons a total length of **5/6 m**. Zero groups contribute nothing: **0×7/8=0**. A container’s possible capacity is not water actually added when there are no groups.

Retain the roles of group count and amount per group. Numerically exchanging factors preserves their product, but an explanation of a story should still identify which quantity counts groups and which measures litres. Do not add group counts to litre amounts.

**Takeaway:** identify group count and group amount, then count identical unit-fraction parts. A parent can ask why repeated addition leaves the denominator’s part size unchanged. Transfer to four jugs of 3/8 L in the exit ticket, using addition or an estimate to check.
