# 线性回归的直观理解

散点不像数列规则那样要求每点精确落在线上。回归给一个可检查的近似关系。本课用**同一x处竖直残差的平方和**理解直线拟合，并明确模型能说到哪里。

## 一条核心想法

每条记录保留成对的(x,y)，不可分别排序后重新配对。候选线ŷ=a+bx给同一x处的拟合值，残差e=y−ŷ。点在线上方残差正，下方负。普通y对x最小二乘回归选择a、b，使Σe²最小；不是到线的最短距离，不是穿过最多点，也不是让正负误差直接抵消。

含截距很重要，本课不强迫线过原点。最优平方和也可能大于0，拟合值不是未来真实观测的保证。所有练习为虚构观测，不由关联推断因果。

### 例1：比较两条直线的可核对误差

三对数据(1,3)、(2,3)、(3,6)。候选ŷ=1+x在相同x处给2、3、4，残差1、0、2，平方和1+0+4=5。它确实穿过中间一个点，但这不足以证明最优。

另一条ŷ=1+1.5x给2.5、4、5.5，残差0.5、−1、0.5，平方和0.25+1+0.25=1.5。没有穿过任何一个点，却比前条平方和小。带符号残差和为0，不代表每个误差为0。

最小二乘的核验可用中心化数据：x均值2、y均值4，x离差−1、0、1，y离差−1、−1、2。因此Sxx=1+0+1=2，Sxy=1+0+2=3，b=Sxy/Sxx=3/2，a=4−(3/2)×2=1。它过均值点(2,4)，不一定过任何原始点，也不一定过原点。公式用于核验，主要概念仍是比较平方误差。

### 例2：外推、弯曲与没有唯一斜率

同一拟合线在x=2给4，而实际观测y=3；残差−1显示模型与观测不同。在x=4给7，超出已观察的x范围[1,3]，属于外推。即使x在范围内也需检查模型，但外推尤其依赖趋势延续，不能把7当真实结果保证。

若所有x都为2，对应y为3、4、6，则Sxx=0。没有x变化来区分斜率：任何满足a+2b=13/3的直线都给相同最小平方误差。原x=2处最佳共同拟合值唯一为y均值13/3，但a和b不唯一，原x之外的预测也不唯一。不能把分母0时的斜率强设为0。

另一个案例(0,4)、(1,1)、(2,0)、(3,1)、(4,4)有明显弯曲关系，最小二乘直线却水平ŷ=2，斜率0，残差为2、−1、−2、−1、2，平方和14。零线性斜率不等于没有关系；结构性弯曲残差提示直线会遗漏规律。

## 残差工作台与家庭挑战

保留原始配对，切换候选a、b，先预测候选平方和，再揭示最优线与逐点表。紫色候选线画竖直残差；绿色最优线在观测x范围内为实线、范围外虚线。线段超出固定图窗会裁切，精确表仍给原值。改候选只清候选误差，不抹掉已经独立算出的最优斜率；改查询x只清相关预测和范围判断。

家庭用三对虚构数据重复例1，逐条画竖线、写平方和，再明确x=4属于外推。可试新增有效远点(6,0)：拟合会明显改变，应报告敏感性并核来源，而不是为了更漂亮的线随意删除。模型评估还需要量的意义、残差、来源和新数据，不靠一个最小平方和自动完成。

## 家长核对与迁移

出口必须保留三条残差、平方和、拟合值与观测的区别、外推和因果边界。另用常数x论证检查孩子是否理解“参数不唯一”与“原x处共同拟合值唯一”的区别。未来统计推断会研究模型不确定性，本课不提供置信区间、显著性或因果结论。

# Intuition of linear regression

Scatter does not require every observation to obey an exact rule. Regression proposes an approximation that can be checked. This lesson understands a line through **squared vertical residuals at the same x**, while stating the limits of the model.

## One central idea

Retain the identity of every (x,y) pair; independently sorting the two columns would create new, false pairs. A candidate ŷ=a+bx provides a fitted value at each observed x. Residual e=y−ŷ is positive above the line and negative below. Ordinary least squares for y on x minimizes Σe² over intercept and slope. It does not minimize shortest perpendicular distances, count the points hit or cancel signed errors directly.

The intercept matters: this lesson does not force a line through the origin. Even the smallest squared sum can be positive, and a fitted value does not guarantee a future observation. All examples are fictional observations and do not establish causation.

### Example 1: Two lines with auditable errors

The pairs are (1,3),(2,3),(3,6). Candidate ŷ=1+x gives fitted values 2,3,4 at those same x values. Residuals 1,0,2 have squared sum 1+0+4=5. The candidate hits one observation exactly, but that alone does not make it best.

The line ŷ=1+1.5x instead gives 2.5,4,5.5. Residuals 0.5,−1,0.5 have squared sum 0.25+1+0.25=1.5. It hits none of the points exactly yet improves the objective. Residuals summing to zero does not mean that individual errors vanish.

Audit the least-squares result with centred values. Mean x is two and mean y four. Their deviations are −1,0,1 and −1,−1,2, so Sxx=2 and Sxy=3. Hence b=Sxy/Sxx=3/2 and a=4−(3/2)×2=1. The fitted line passes through the mean point (2,4), not necessarily any individual point or the origin. The formula verifies the result; comparing squared errors remains the main idea.

### Example 2: Extrapolation, curvature and a nonunique slope

At x=2 the fitted line gives four while the observed y is three, producing residual −1. At x=4 it gives seven. This extrapolates beyond observed x range [1,3]. In-range predictions also require model checks, while extrapolation especially depends on the trend continuing. Seven is a model output, not a guaranteed actual result.

If every x equals two and y values are 3,4,6, then Sxx=0. No x variation distinguishes slopes: every line satisfying a+2b=13/3 has the same minimum squared error. The best common fitted value at the observed x is uniquely mean y=13/3, but slope and intercept are nonunique. Predictions away from two also differ across these equally fitting lines. Division by zero does not justify assigning slope zero.

The separate pairs (0,4),(1,1),(2,0),(3,1),(4,4) form a clear curve, yet their least-squares line is horizontal at ŷ=2. Residuals 2,−1,−2,−1,2 have squared sum fourteen. A zero linear slope does not mean no relationship; systematic residual curvature shows what the line misses.

## Residual workbench and family challenge

Keep the original pairs and change candidate intercept and slope. Predict its squared sum before revealing the optimum and exact table. Purple candidate residuals are vertical. The green fitted line is solid across observed x values and dashed outside them. Lines are clipped to the fixed display window; exact tables retain the values. Changing a candidate resets its error task, not an independently calculated optimal slope. Changing query x resets only the prediction and scope tasks.

On paper, repeat the three-pair example, draw each vertical residual, sum its square and identify x=4 as extrapolation. An additional valid distant point (6,0) changes the fitted line substantially. Check its source and report sensitivity instead of removing it simply to improve appearance. Assessment needs variable meaning, residuals, source information and new data; a minimum objective alone does not validate the model.

## Parent review and transfer

The exit must retain all three residuals, their squared sum, the fitted-versus-observed distinction, extrapolation and causal limits. The constant-x explanation separately checks why coefficients can be nonunique while the common fit at that observed x remains unique. Later statistical inference will study model uncertainty; this lesson supplies no confidence interval, significance claim or causal conclusion.
