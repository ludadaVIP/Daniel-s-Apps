## 一个方框，指向一个暂时未知的数量

方框 **□** 表示暂时不知道、但需要确定的数。它不是零，不是计算按钮，也不总是题目最后的总数。先把对象说完整：“方框是原有张卡数”“方框是被拿走的量”“方框是每袋支数”。同一个符号的位置改变，所求对象也可能改变。

同一道题里的相同方框按约定表示同一个未知数。□+□=14 不能一处填五、另一处填九，虽然它们的和是十四。新的一道独立题由新的关系决定未知数，不要求方框总代表七。这个约定让图、语言和符号保持一致。

### 例 1 · 未知加数

1. 已知 **□+7=19**。方框是一个未知加数，十九是和。
2. 从总量十九中去掉已知的七：**19−7=12**。
3. 候选 **□=12**。它是方框的值，不是把整条式子合计后的值。
4. 代回原关系：**12+7=19**，左右都是十九，候选满足关系。
5. 若试十，左边 **10+7=17**，右边仍十九，不满足；不能因为十“看起来差不多”就通过。

### 例 2 · 未知去掉量

1. 已知 **24−□=9**。二十四是原量，九是剩余，方框是去掉量。
2. 原量减剩余得到去掉量：**24−9=15**。
3. 候选 **□=15**，代回 **24−15=9**，关系成立。
4. 若误算 **24+9=33**，代回 **24−33** 不会等于九。先辨对象比看到减号就随手相减更可靠。
5. 也可核查 **9+15=24**，剩余加去掉量恢复原量。

### 相同组、平均分与零

4×□=28 表示四个相同未知量合成二十八，平均每个七；不是把四当作“加了四”。□÷5=6 表示未知原量平均分成五份，每份六，所以原量三十。把三十代回三十除五为六，核查所求确实是原量。

□+6=6 中方框为零，是有效解。方框起初不代表零，但由关系可以确定它恰好为零。零答案与未知标记的含义不能混为一谈。送出或收到的数量也要保留张卡、支数、长度等单位；把十五张卡写成十五袋，即使数字相同也改变了对象。

工作台先提供已知关系，试代输入由你填写，可以使用等值分数或小数。核对时分别显示左右值；它不会预先把方框答案放进滑块或替代说明。试对一次说明这个数满足原式，还应能解释它从哪个数量关系得到。

**带走一个发现：** 先命名未知对象，再根据位置找候选数。相同方框同题同值，新题由新关系决定；代回原式检验比只猜一个数可靠。

## A box points to a quantity not yet known

A box **□** represents a number not yet known. It is not zero, an operation button or always the final total. Name its object first: original card count, removed amount or pencils per bag. Changing its position may change what is being sought.

Identical boxes in one equation represent the same unknown by convention. □+□=14 cannot use five in one place and nine in the other, despite their sum being fourteen. A separate problem determines its unknown through a new relationship; the symbol need not always mean seven.

### Example 1 · An unknown addend

1. In **□+7=19**, the box is an unknown addend and nineteen is the sum.
2. Remove the known seven from the total: **19−7=12**.
3. Try **□=12**. This is the box’s value, not a total of the entire equation.
4. Substitute: **12+7=19**. Both sides have value nineteen.
5. Trying ten gives **10+7=17**, unequal to nineteen. A plausible-looking value is not enough.

### Example 2 · An unknown removed amount

1. In **24−□=9**, twenty-four is original, nine remaining and the box removed.
2. Original minus remaining gives removed: **24−9=15**.
3. Try **□=15** and check **24−15=9**.
4. Incorrectly adding 24+9 gives thirty-three, which does not satisfy the original. Identify the object before choosing an operation.
5. Also check **9+15=24**, restoring the original.

### Equal groups, sharing and zero

4×□=28 represents four equal unknown amounts totalling twenty-eight: seven each. It does not mean four has been added. □÷5=6 means an unknown original shared into five equal parts of six, giving thirty. Substitution checks that thirty divided by five is six.

In □+6=6, zero is a valid solution. An unknown box does not initially mean zero, but its relationship may determine zero. Keep measurement and count units attached to objects: fifteen cards and fifteen bags are not the same quantity.

The workshop provides only a known relationship. You enter a trial value, optionally using an equivalent fraction or decimal. Checking shows each side’s value without preloading the solution into a slider or image description. A successful trial satisfies the equation, but you should also explain the quantity relationship.

**Takeaway:** name the unknown before finding a candidate. Identical boxes within one problem have one value; a separate problem has its own relationship. Substitute into the original rather than only guess a number.
