## 百分数先回答“占哪个整体多少”

桌上有四十张同样的卡，其中十张印着圆点。问“有几张圆点卡”，答案是十张；问“圆点卡占全部卡片的百分之几”，需要把十与四十比较。百分数描述**部分相对于指定整体的比例**，不是在部分个数后随手添一个百分号。

“百分之二十五”写成 **25%**，意思是与 **25/100** 等值：把同一个参考整体看作一百等份，所说的量相当于其中二十五份。百分号本身表示“除以一百”。实际整体可以是四十张卡、八十张卡或十二升水，不必真的有一百件东西。

### 例 1 · 十张为什么是 25%

1. 指出整体：**全部四十张卡**，不是十张圆点卡。
2. 部分与整体的比为 **10/40**，约分为 **1/4**。
3. **1/4=25/100=25%**。它描述的是“四分之一”，不是多做了十五张卡。
4. 如果用百格图，涂二十五格只是把同样比例换到标准图上；原桌面仍只有十张圆点卡。
5. 核查：四十张的四分之一确为十张。把一张卡看成 1% 会错，因为这里一张卡占整体 **1/40=2.5%**。

工作台先画真实数量的卡片，再画等值的百格图。改变实际总数时，必须重新用部分除整体；只保持标记张数不变，比例也可能改变。改变输入后旧反馈会清空，先估计再核对。

### 例 2 · 一张也可能超过或不到 1%

1. 假想样本有 **八十张卡**，其中 **一张**有星号。
2. 比例为 **1/80**，要写成每百份，系数是 **100÷80=1.25**。
3. 所以 **1/80=1.25%**。百分数前面的数不必是整数。
4. 百格图应涂一整格，再涂下一格的四分之一；不能为了图方便把它取整为 1%。
5. 核回：**80×1.25/100=1** 张。实际数量一张与标准图上的 1.25 格分别对应自己的整体。

**零与完整整体：** 五十张中没有标记，比例为 0/50=0%；四十张全部有标记，40/40=100%。这里整体必须正数，空样本不能用 0/0 定义标记百分率。真实部分是整体的子集时不会超过 100%；“需求为参考量的 125%”则是后面另一种比较情境，需要明确参考量。

**换个整体再问：** 四十张中的十张与八十张中的二十张都是 25%，但标记张数不同。相反，同样十张在四十张中占 25%，在八十张中只占 12.5%。比较百分数之前要先说清整体，不能因为比例相同就说人数、张数或实际量一样。

**家长陪学：** 让孩子用一句话同时说出“哪一组是整体”“哪一部分被比较”“百分数描述什么”。允许用等值分数、同长色条或口头说明。不要把标准百格图上的格数当作原数据，也不要要求真实卡片必须能按整数张分成一百堆。

**带走一个发现：** 百分数把比例统一成每百份；它保留部分与整体的关系，不能替代实际数量。

## A percentage first asks “how much of which whole?”

There are forty equal cards on a table, ten with dots. “How many dotted cards?” asks for a count: ten. “What percentage is dotted?” asks for the relationship between the ten and all forty. A percentage describes a **proportion relative to a specified whole**, rather than a count followed by a percent sign.

**25%** means the same number as **25/100**. Imagine one reference whole in one hundred equal parts: the selected quantity corresponds to twenty-five such parts. The percent sign means divide by one hundred. The actual whole might contain forty cards, eighty cards or twelve litres; it need not contain one hundred objects.

### Example 1 · Why ten cards represent 25%

1. Identify the whole: **all forty cards**, not just the ten dotted cards.
2. Compare part with whole: **10/40=1/4**.
3. **1/4=25/100=25%**. This expresses one quarter; it does not create fifteen additional cards.
4. Twenty-five shaded cells on a hundred grid standardize the same proportion. The actual table still contains ten dotted cards.
5. Check: one quarter of forty is ten. One source card is **1/40=2.5%** of this collection, rather than 1%.

The workshop draws actual cards before showing an equivalent hundred grid. If the actual total changes, compare the part with its new whole. Keeping the marked count unchanged need not preserve its percentage. Changing an input clears old checked feedback; predict again before checking.

### Example 2 · A single card need not represent 1%

1. A fictional collection contains **eighty cards**, with **one** starred card.
2. The proportion is **1/80**. Its per-hundred coefficient is **100÷80=1.25**.
3. Thus **1/80=1.25%**. A percentage coefficient need not be a whole number.
4. Shade one complete hundred-grid cell and one quarter of the next. Do not round to 1% just to fit whole cells.
5. Check **80×1.25/100=1** card. One actual card and 1.25 standardized cells refer to different wholes.

No marked cards in fifty gives 0/50=0%. All forty cards marked gives 40/40=100%. The sample whole must be positive: an empty sample cannot define a marked percentage through 0/0. A subset of a real collection cannot exceed 100% of that collection. A later statement such as “a requirement is 125% of a reference amount” compares quantities in a different context and must identify its reference.

Ten of forty and twenty of eighty are both 25%, but their actual counts differ. Conversely, ten of forty is 25%, while the same ten of eighty is only 12.5%. Always name the whole before comparing percentages; identical percentages do not guarantee identical counts, people or measured quantities.

**For a parent:** listen for the whole, selected part and meaning of the percentage in one connected explanation. Equivalent fractions, equal-length bars and spoken reasoning are all useful. Do not mistake standardized grid cells for original observations or insist that actual cards be physically divided into one hundred integer piles.

**Takeaway:** percentages standardize proportions per hundred. They preserve a part-to-whole relationship, not an actual count.
