# 有理数乘除法

## 从重复相加扩展到数的映射

正整数倍可以看成重复相加，但分数倍、零倍和负数倍需要更广的关系。乘一个正因子，按其绝对值缩放到零的距离而不改变所在侧；乘负因子，先按大小缩放，再关于零反射；乘零把所有位置映到零。这里描述数的映射，不假装存在“负三份实物”可供直接重复相加。

这与分配律一致。例如乘负一应使数 a 与结果和为零，因为 `a×1+a×(−1)=a×[1+(−1)]=a×0=0`。所以负因子的方向规则有运算关系支持，不只是图案约定。无零因子时，同号相乘为正，异号为负；若有零因子，积为零，不把零硬分为正负。

### 例 1：两次方向与分数大小

计算 `(−3/2)×(−2/3)`。原位置在零左侧，因子绝对值为 `2/3`，先把到零距离从 `3/2` 缩为一，再因负因子反射到右侧，结果一。

精确运算也给 `(+6)/6=1`。对比 `(−5/4)×2/3=−10/12=−5/6`：正因子不反射，且绝对值小于一，使到零距离缩小。数值本身从负 1.25 变为约负 0.833…，反而变大，因此“乘法一定变大”与“绝对值缩小就数值缩小”都不准确。

三个负因子时可逐步检查：`(−2/3)×(−2/3)=4/9`，再乘负 `2/3` 为 `−8/27`。不要把所有有负号的乘积都记为正。下一课会把同一因子重复相乘写成乘方，先明确底数范围。

### 例 2：除法要能乘回去

计算 `(−3/4)÷(−1/2)`。设商为 q，必须满足 `q×(−1/2)=−3/4`。原除数非零，它的倒数为负二，因此 `q=(−3/4)×(−2)=3/2`。

反查 `(3/2)×(−1/2)=−3/4`，恢复被除数。倒的是除数，不是被除数；负号也不能丢。对比相反数：负半的相反数为正半，检验“和为零”；它的倒数负二检验“积为一”。两种逆关系再次分清。

## 除零为什么停下

`0÷(−2/3)=0` 有定义，因为除数非零，零是唯一满足乘回关系的商。

若非零数除以零，要求 `0×q` 等于非零数，不可能做到。若 `0÷0`，则任何 q 都能使 `0×q=0`，无法确定唯一商。两种情况都不在有理数除法的定义范围内，不能分别随意填零或一。零也没有倒数，工作台不会生成一个假的倒数或数轴终点。

除以一个绝对值小于一的非零数，会放大被除数的绝对值；除以绝对值大于一的数则缩小。符号再由反射关系决定。例 `(−5/6)÷(10/9)=(−5/6)×9/10=−3/4`，结果绝对值小于原量，但数值更靠右。始终说明自己在比较数值大小还是到零距离。

## 用逆运算核验

在工作台切换乘、除与第二数的正负，先预测符号和大小，再展开结果。第二数调零时比较乘零与除零，别把有定义的零积和未定义的商混淆。家长要求孩子指出原除数、它的倒数、精确商和乘回等式；能讲清边界，才说明规则有意义。

# Multiplying and dividing rational numbers

## Extend repeated addition to a numerical map

Positive integer multiplication can represent repeated addition. Fractional, zero and negative factors need a broader relationship. A positive factor scales distance to zero without changing sides; a negative factor scales by its magnitude and reflects across zero. A zero factor maps every position to zero. This is a numerical mapping, not physical repeated addition of a negative number of copies.

The rule agrees with distributivity: `a×1+a×(−1)=a×[1+(−1)]=a×0=0`, so multiplication by negative one produces a’s opposite. With nonzero factors, equal signs give a positive product and different signs a negative product. A zero factor gives zero, which is neither positive nor negative.

### Example 1: combine direction and magnitude

For `(−3/2)×(−2/3)`, the original position is left of zero. Scale its magnitude `3/2` by `2/3` to obtain one, then reflect for the negative factor. The result is positive one, matching the exact product `6/6=1`.

Compare `(−5/4)×2/3=−10/12=−5/6`. A positive factor preserves the side and a magnitude below one shrinks the distance. The numerical value nevertheless increases from −1.25 toward zero. Neither “multiplication always makes bigger” nor “smaller magnitude means smaller value” is generally correct.

With three negative factors, proceed in stages: `(−2/3)×(−2/3)=4/9`, then another `−2/3` gives `−8/27`. A product containing negative signs is not automatically positive. The next lesson introduces powers as repeated multiplication with a clearly specified base.

### Example 2: a quotient must multiply back

For `(−3/4)÷(−1/2)`, quotient q must satisfy `q×(−1/2)=−3/4`. The divisor is nonzero, with reciprocal negative two, giving `q=(−3/4)×(−2)=3/2`.

Check `(3/2)×(−1/2)=−3/4`. Invert the divisor, not the dividend, and retain its sign. The opposite of negative one half is positive one half, checked by sum zero; its reciprocal is negative two, checked by product one. These inverse relationships are different.

## Why division by zero stops

The expression `0÷(−2/3)=0` is defined: the divisor is nonzero and zero is the unique quotient satisfying the multiplication check. For a nonzero dividend divided by zero, `0×q` cannot recover the dividend. For `0÷0`, every q satisfies `0×q=0`, so the quotient is not uniquely determined. Both zero-divisor cases are undefined. The workshop generates neither a false reciprocal nor a false endpoint.

Dividing by a nonzero magnitude below one enlarges the dividend’s magnitude; dividing by one above one shrinks it. Signs determine reflection separately. For `(−5/6)÷(10/9)`, multiply by `9/10` to obtain `−3/4`. Magnitude decreases while numerical value moves right. State which comparison you mean.

Predict sign and magnitude before revealing a workshop result. Set the second number to zero and compare multiplication with division. Identify original divisor, reciprocal, exact quotient and multiplication check; explaining the boundary shows why the rule works.
