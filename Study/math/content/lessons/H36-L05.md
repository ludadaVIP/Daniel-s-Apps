# 圆锥曲线综合建模

现实模型除了方程，还要交代坐标、单位、允许范围和所求量。本课用一个假想椭圆屋顶截面，连接曲线交点、约束区间与完整宽度。所有长度采用米，不把一个几何计算直接当作结构承载或施工验证。

## 先定义截面与净高

以对称中心的地面点为原点，x水平、y竖直，屋顶截面为x²/25+y²/9=1且y≥0。水平半轴5米、竖直半轴3米，所以地面跨度10米、最高点3米。方程包含的下半椭圆不属于屋顶，必须保留y≥0。

净高H表示从水平地面向上至少有H米空间。若希望以中心对称区间开一条矩形通道，所求是所有屋顶高度≥H的横坐标，以及该区间的完整长度。先求水平线y=H与上半截面的边界交点，再检查区间内是否全部满足高度条件。

### 例1：从交点得到完整可用宽度

取H=9/5米。代入H²/9=9/25，得x²/25=16/25，所以x=±4。边界点为(−4,9/5)、(4,9/5)。上半椭圆高度随|x|增大而降低，因此区间[−4,4]内全部屋顶高度至少H，完整可用宽度为4−(−4)=8米。

不能只报4米，因为那是半宽。矩形通道若取该最大对称宽度，两上角最接近屋顶，内部横坐标的屋顶更高；这个理由补足了仅找到两个边界点的不足。这里计算的是理想截面下的几何可用空间。

若净高升为H=12/5米，H²/9=16/25，得x²=9，横坐标[−3,3]，完整宽6米。更高的要求减少可用宽度，而不是改变屋顶半轴。两种高度的精确分数均保留单位，图上近似位置只用来检查。

### 例2：顶点单点与超过顶高不同

H=3米时，方程给x²/25+1=1，因此只有x=0。可行横坐标集合为{0}或[0,0]，水平宽0。它仍有一个合法顶点(0,3)，但没有正宽度通道。

H=4米超过最高点，代入要求x²/25=1−16/9<0，没有实数横坐标，集合为空。此时不应也写成“一个宽度0的开口”，因为连一个可行点都不存在。H=0时则含两地面端点，完整宽10米。

把整个截面等比例放大2倍，半轴变10米和6米。若净高也按相同比例变18/5米，则边界横坐标±8米，宽16米。必须同时说明比较时净高是否跟着放大，不能只凭相似图形默认所有现实要求不变。

## 图与资格检查

紫线只把上半椭圆作为屋顶，灰虚线的下半部分仅为排除范围参照；橙虚线是净高。顶点标一个不同交点，超顶高不生成假交点。坐标两轴单位长度一致，横纵轴都标米，避免把比例示意误认成精确形状。

工作台同时询问可行x集合、完整宽度和全部边界交点，核验表另列半宽，帮助发现把半宽当全宽的错误。有限图窗不定义屋顶范围，阴影或画出的矩形也不代替代入与区间检查。

## 让结论与模型条件一起输出

报告原点、方向、单位、半轴、上半截面条件、地面和净高定义，再写交点、区间、宽度及边界情形。声明模型假定零厚度精确椭圆、水平地面与居中矩形通道；它没有处理材料、屋顶厚度、荷载、安装余量或结构稳定。

家长可追问下半圆点是否可用、4米为什么不是完整宽，以及宽0与空集有什么不同。出口报告由人工复核，目的在于让学生把数字结论与可行范围、假设和限制连在一起。

# Conic modelling problems

A real-world model needs coordinates, units, an allowed region and a clearly defined target in addition to an equation. Use a fictional elliptical roof section to connect intersections, feasible intervals and full width. Lengths are in metres; geometric clearance alone does not verify structural or construction conditions.

## Define the section and clearance

Place the origin at the ground-level symmetry centre, with horizontal x and vertical y. The roof is x²/25+y²/9=1 with y≥0. Its horizontal semiaxis is five metres, vertical semiaxis three, ground span ten and maximum height three. The lower half included by the equation is not part of the roof.

Clearance H means at least H metres above the level floor. For a centred rectangular passage, find every horizontal position where roof height is at least H, then the full length of that interval. Intersections with y=H locate its boundaries, but the interval between them still needs verification.

### Example 1: From intersections to full available width

Take H=9/5 metres. Then H²/9=9/25, giving x²/25=16/25 and x=±4. Boundary points are (−4,9/5) and (4,9/5). The upper ellipse’s height decreases as |x| increases, so every x in [−4,4] has height at least H. The full available width is 4−(−4)=8 metres.

Four metres would be only the half-width. For a rectangular passage occupying the maximum symmetric interval, the upper corners are the limiting locations; roof height is greater at interior horizontal positions. This fills the logical gap between finding two boundary points and claiming the whole passage fits the ideal section.

At H=12/5 metres, H²/9=16/25, hence x²=9. The feasible interval is [−3,3] and full width six metres. Higher clearance reduces available width; it does not alter the roof’s semiaxes. Keep exact fractions and metre units rather than reading rounded pixels as exact boundaries.

### Example 2: An apex singleton differs from infeasibility

At H=3 metres, x²/25+1=1 gives only x=0. The feasible set is {0}, equivalently [0,0], with width zero. The apex (0,3) remains a legal point, but there is no positive-width passage.

At H=4 metres, substitution demands x²/25=1−16/9<0. No real horizontal coordinate is feasible. This is an empty set, not another zero-width opening: even one feasible point is absent. At H=0, both ground endpoints qualify and the full span is ten metres.

Scale the whole section by two to semiaxes ten and six metres. If clearance also doubles to 18/5 metres, the boundary positions become ±8 and the width sixteen. State whether clearance is scaled too; similar-looking pictures do not mean every physical requirement stayed unchanged.

## Audit the picture against the model

Purple is the upper roof, grey dashed is the excluded lower-half reference, and orange dashed is clearance. The apex gives one distinct intersection; above it no intersection is invented. Both coordinate axes use the same unit length and are labelled in metres.

The workbench asks separately for the feasible x set, full width and all roof intersections. Its audit also lists half-width to expose confusion between half and full span. A finite window does not define the physical roof, and a drawn rectangle cannot replace substitution and interval reasoning.

Report origin, axes, units, semiaxes, upper-section restriction, floor and clearance definition before the final interval and width. State the assumptions of an exact zero-thickness ellipse, level ground and a centred rectangular passage. Thickness, materials, loads, margins and stability remain outside this geometric calculation.

Ask whether lower-half points are allowed, why four metres is not the full width in the first example, and why width zero differs from an empty feasible set. A human reviews the report linking numerical conclusions to restrictions and assumptions; automated answers verify the stated geometric quantities.
