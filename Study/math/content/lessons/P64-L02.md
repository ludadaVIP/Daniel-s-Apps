## 把零的两边放在同一条数轴上

整数包含正整数、负整数与零。在本课数轴上，零是参照，向右数值增大，向左数值减小，每个相邻整数之间的间隔相同。不能把左半边另画一套“向左越大”的规则。

从左到右的一小段顺序是 **−4、−3、−2、−1、0、1、2、3、4**。负号与数字一起表示位置。零是整数，但既不是正数，也不是负数；它不是左右两类中的某一个。

### 例 1 · 从零向左五步

1. 站在 **0**，还没有走任何一步。
2. 第一次向左一个单位到 **−1**，不是到零。
3. 继续向左依次到 **−2、−3、−4、−5**。
4. 总共走五个间隔，终点 P 为 **−5**。
5. 核查：P 在 −6 与 −4 中间，且到零有五个单位间隔。把零算成第一步会少走一格。

数轴刻度是间隔边界，移动跨过的是两个边界之间的单位长度。六条线之间可以只有五个间隔；不要把刻度线条数直接当路程。

### 例 2 · 标签可以稀疏，位置不能变

1. 仍以每格一单位，只给偶数刻度写标签。
2. 在 **−4 与 −2** 之间的整数是 **−3**；没有印出数字并不表示该位置不存在。
3. 从零向右三个间隔到 **+3**，与三格向左的 −3 位于不同侧。
4. 改成每四格标一个数字，小格仍一单位，+3 与 −3 的位置都不变。
5. 核查邻居：−1 的左邻是 −2，右邻是零；靠近负号的数码不能代替整个位置。

所有正整数都在零右边，所有负整数都在零左边。表示同一位置的写法可以不同，例如 +3 与 3；−0 与 +0 都是零，不创造两个起点。数轴只画有限窗口，不表示整数在边界处结束。

工作台给出“从零向左五格”等目标，你用左右按钮每次走一格。先在纸上预测对应整数，再移动点 P，最后核对目标。改变目标会把点送回零并清空反馈；只改标签间隔则保留点的位置。抵达显示边界后，相应按钮会停用，边界只是这次练习的可操作范围。

**解释检查：** “−5 因为有五”不够。需要说“零左边”“每格一单位”“五个间隔”。如果孩子能在反向问题中指出 −5 右边一格是 −4，就比只记住一串数字更能说明理解。

**家长陪学：** 可以在纸条上用相同长度画间隔，先标零与向右箭头，再让孩子填邻居。不要用不同大小格子来暗示不同数值。允许口述、手指移动或画图，不用计时判断能力。**带走一个发现：** 零点、方向和同一单位间隔一起确定整数的位置，稀疏标签不改变这套关系。

## Put both sides of zero on one number line

Integers include positive integers, negative integers and zero. Here zero is the reference, values increase rightward and decrease leftward, and neighboring integers have equal spacing. The negative side does not use a separate rule that larger values lie farther left.

A short left-to-right sequence is **−4, −3, −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, 3, 4**. Read the sign and digits together. Zero is an integer but neither positive nor negative, rather than a member of one side’s category.

### Example 1 · Five moves left from zero

1. Start at **0**, with no move completed yet.
2. One unit left reaches **−1**, not zero.
3. Continue left to **−2, −3, −4 and −5**.
4. Five intervals have been crossed, so endpoint P is **−5**.
5. Check that P lies between −6 and −4, five unit intervals from zero. Counting zero as the first step would stop one interval early.

Ticks mark interval boundaries. Six boundary lines can enclose five intervals; a tick count is not automatically the distance travelled.

### Example 2 · Labels can be sparse without moving values

1. Keep one unit per small interval but label only even integers.
2. The integer between **−4 and −2** is **−3**, even without its own printed label.
3. Three intervals right from zero reach **+3**, on the opposite side from three intervals left at −3.
4. Labeling every fourth interval still preserves unit size and both positions.
5. Check neighbors: −1 has −2 immediately left and zero immediately right. Unsigned digits alone do not determine these positions.

Every positive integer lies right of zero and every negative integer left. Different notation can represent one position: +3 and 3 agree, while −0 and +0 both represent zero rather than two starting points. A finite window does not mean that the integers end at its boundaries.

The workshop gives a target such as five steps left from zero. Use left and right buttons to move P one unit at a time, predict its integer, then check the target. Changing the target returns P to zero and clears feedback. Changing only label frequency preserves the point. Buttons stop at the displayed boundary, which limits this interaction rather than the integer system.

An explanation such as “−5 because there are five” is incomplete. Name the left side of zero, one unit per interval and five moves. Recognizing that one step right of −5 reaches −4 provides stronger evidence than reciting a list alone.

**For a parent:** draw equally spaced marks on paper, establish zero and a rightward arrow, then ask for neighboring integers. Keep intervals equal and accept spoken, pointed or drawn reasoning without timing. **Takeaway:** zero, direction and equal unit spacing establish integer positions; sparse labels preserve those relationships.
