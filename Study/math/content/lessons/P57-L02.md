## 平均数是一种公平分配

桌上四堆卡片数量不同。想象把它们合在一起，再公平分成四堆；每堆应该多少？这就是本课理解平均数的入口。移动卡片不会改变总张数，分成几堆也没有改变。因此平均数要把完整总量除以观察值个数。

平均数是一个概括数据的数，单位仍与观察值相同。平均卡数用张，平均时间用分钟。它描述假想公平分配的结果，并不保证现实中每个人真的拥有相同数量，也不保证原数据里有人恰好等于这个数。

### 例 1 · 不增加卡片，分成相同份额

1. 原始四堆是 **1、3、6、6 张**。先预测公平分配会是多少。
2. 总量 **1+3+6+6=16 张**，有四个观察值。
3. 从最多的一堆向最少的一堆移卡，保持总量十六、堆数四不变。
4. 最后可以成为 **4、4、4、4**，平均数 **16÷4=4 张**。
5. 原数据没有任何一堆四张，但平均数仍是四；它不是在选一堆“中间看着合适”的数据。

工作台一次只移一张，每次先说总数是否会变。重新选择数据组会恢复原始记录，不把上一组的核对结果带过来。也可以不移动，直接用总量与份数解释；操作是为了看清理由，不是必须完成的游戏步骤。

### 例 2 · 分数平均数仍是精确值

1. 三堆卡是 **1、2、2 张**，总量 **5 张**。
2. 有三个观察值，平均 **5÷3=5/3 张**，也可说一又三分之二张。
3. 若卡必须保持整张，不能真的把三堆分得完全一样；这不妨碍平均数作为公平份额的度量存在。
4. 把平均写成 1 会丢掉总量，把它写成 2 又会多出总量。
5. 检查 **3×5/3=5**，准确恢复原总量；1.67 只是近似，不等于精确 5/3。

零也必须计入观察值个数。三天收集卡片 **0、4、8 张**，总十二，平均 **12÷3=4 张**。若漏掉零那天而除二，会得六张，回答的是另一个问题：“在有卡片的两天中平均多少？”同样，缺失一天记录不是零张；若不知道那天收集量，就不能冒充三天资料齐全。

平均数也能逆向使用：四人的平均卡数六张，总量应为 **4×6=24 张**。这里仍不知道各人实际几张。数据 **2、2、2、18** 的平均也是六，但三个人都只有两张。十八这个较大的值把平均数拉高，所以描述“多数人的情况”时，还要看原始分布。不能为了得到喜欢的结论擅自删除真实的大值；应该说明它的影响。

**带走一个发现：** 总量和份数共同决定平均数，零要数、精确分数要保留。出口题先独立求总量，再说除数为什么是三。家长可问“把平均份额乘回人数能恢复总量吗”，并听孩子说明平均与实际的区别。

## The mean is a fair-share amount

Imagine combining unequal card piles and sharing the total equally among the same number of piles. How much should each receive? Moving cards preserves both total cards and pile count. This explains why the mean uses the complete total divided by the number of observations.

The mean summarises data in the original unit: cards for card counts, minutes for times. It describes a hypothetical fair share. Actual people need not hold identical amounts, and no original observation has to equal the mean.

### Example 1 · Equal shares without adding cards

1. Start with **1, 3, 6 and 6 cards**. Predict an equal share.
2. Total **1+3+6+6=16 cards**, with four observations.
3. Move cards from a largest pile to a smallest, preserving sixteen cards and four piles.
4. The piles can become **4, 4, 4, 4**. Mean is **16÷4=4 cards**.
5. None of the original piles contained four, yet the mean is four. It is not a selected pile that merely looks central.

The workshop moves one card at a time. Predict whether the total changes before moving. Choosing a new data set restores its original observations and clears the previous checked result. You may also explain the total and shares without physically moving every card: the action supports the reasoning.

### Example 2 · An exact fractional mean

1. Piles **1, 2 and 2** total **5 cards**.
2. Three observations give mean **5÷3=5/3 cards**, or one and two thirds.
3. Whole cards cannot be physically divided into three equal whole-card piles. The mean still exists as a fair-share measure.
4. A mean of one would lose total cards, while two would create extra cards.
5. Check **3×5/3=5**, restoring the exact total. Decimal 1.67 is only an approximation, not exactly 5/3.

Zero counts as an observation. Three days with **0, 4 and 8 cards** total twelve and have mean **12÷3=4**. Omitting the zero and dividing by two answers a different question: the average among the two days with cards. A missing day is different again: an unknown count cannot be silently replaced with zero and described as complete data.

Work backwards too: a mean of six cards among four people implies **4×6=24 cards** in total, without revealing individual counts. Data **2, 2, 2, 18** also have mean six, although three people hold two. The large eighteen raises the mean, so inspect the distribution when describing most people. Do not delete a genuine large value to obtain a preferred conclusion; explain its influence.

**Takeaway:** total and observation count jointly determine the mean. Include zero and retain exact fractions. For the exit ticket, find the total independently and justify dividing by three. A parent can ask whether multiplying the fair share back restores the total, and listen for the distinction between average and actual observations.
