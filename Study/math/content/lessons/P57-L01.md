## 从原始回应到能核对的表

想知道今天小组主要怎样来到活动室，先把问题说清楚：“你今天主要怎样来？”每人只选择一种主要方式，同一人只记录一次。这与“你最喜欢怎样来？”不是同一个问题：今天坐公交的人也可能最喜欢骑车。先有可回答的问题，再收集数据，最后才画图。

原始记录是一条条实际收到的回答；**频数**是一类回答出现多少次。分类要明确：步行、骑车、公交、其他。“其他”指确实回答了另一种方式，不是把看不懂、没录完和没回答都放进去。有人没有回答，要保留缺失标记，不能替他猜一种交通方式。

### 例 1 · 七条邀请记录

1. 七人被邀请，原始记录依次为：**步行、公交、步行、骑车、公交、步行、无回应**。
2. 按次序逐条核对，一条只进入一类：步行记三次，骑车一次，公交两次，其他零次。
3. 频数和为 **3+1+2+0=6 人**，表示六人提供了交通方式。
4. 另有无回应一人，**6+1=7**，与邀请人数相符。
5. 零个“其他”表示没有回答者选择其他方式；无回应一人表示不知道那个人的方式，两者含义不同。

在工作台先读当前原始卡片，选择分类后核对录入。选错时回看原始回应，不增加表中数量。还没录入的卡片也不是“无回应”：它已经有原始资料，只是整理尚未完成。先检查所有邀请都有去向，再读表下结论。

### 例 2 · 从总数找遗漏的类别

1. 另一组邀请 **12 人**，有 **2 人无回应**，所以提供方式的有 **12−2=10 人**。
2. 回答中步行四人、公交三人、其他一人，其余骑车。
3. 已知方式共 **4+3+1=8 人**，骑车为 **10−8=2 人**。
4. 完整核对：**4+2+3+1+2=12 人**，最后一个二是无回应。
5. 若直接用十二减八，会把两个无回应者也算进骑车，得到错误的四人。

数据要有标题、对象、时间、问题和单位。表里数的是人，不能把公交次数与人数相加。同一人多选时必须另订“按人计还是按选择计”的规则；本课明确每人一次单选，因此类别互不重复。真实调查若发生重复、含糊回答或录入错误，要回看资料并说明修正，不能为了让总数好看而改答案。

小组是观察到的**样本**，不自动代表全校，更不能代表全新西兰。在门口找七位自愿者，可能漏掉走另一道门或不愿作答的人。我们可以说“这个小组有三名回答者今天主要步行”，不能因此说“全校最喜欢步行”。若要更大范围的结论，需要合适的问题和收集方法。

**带走一个发现：** 分类频数之和加缺失，应该能核对全部邀请。解释题请指出自己在描述谁、回答了什么问题、哪些资料还不知道。家长核查分类与结论范围，不以是否说出某句固定术语判理解。

## From raw responses to a checkable table

Begin with a clear question: “What was your main way to the activity room today?” Each learner chooses one main method and is recorded once. This differs from asking for a favourite method: someone who travelled by bus today may prefer cycling. Define the question before collecting data or drawing a chart.

A raw record is one received response. **Frequency** counts how often a category appears. Define categories such as Walk, Bike, Bus and Other. Other means a different method was actually stated; it does not mean unreadable, unfinished or unanswered. Keep a missing marker rather than guessing a method for someone who did not answer.

### Example 1 · Seven invitation records

1. Seven people are invited. Their records are **walk, bus, walk, bike, bus, walk, no response**.
2. Classify each once: Walk three, Bike one, Bus two and Other zero.
3. Category frequencies sum to **3+1+2+0=6 people**, the number who supplied a method.
4. Add one missing response: **6+1=7**, matching invitations.
5. Zero Other responses means no respondent chose that category. One missing response means a method is unknown. These are different statements.

In the workshop, read the current raw card, select a category and check before recording. A wrong classification does not add a count. An unentered card is also different from no response: the raw information exists, but has not yet been organised. Reconcile every invitation before interpreting the completed table.

### Example 2 · Find an omitted category

1. Another group invites **12 people**, with **2 missing responses**. Thus **12−2=10** provide a method.
2. Of those respondents, four walk, three use a bus and one gives another method; the rest bike.
3. Listed categories total **4+3+1=8**, leaving **10−8=2 cyclists**.
4. Check **4+2+3+1+2=12**, with the final two representing missing responses.
5. Subtracting eight directly from twelve would incorrectly classify the missing people as cyclists.

A useful table identifies its title, group, time, question and unit. Counts of people cannot be added to bus-trip counts. A multiple-response question would require different counting rules; this lesson explicitly allows one main-method response per person, so categories do not overlap. Revisit raw records when a duplicate, ambiguous response or entry error appears, and explain corrections rather than changing data to make a total look tidy.

The observed group is a **sample**, not automatically the whole school or New Zealand. Gate volunteers may exclude people using another entrance or declining to answer. “Three respondents in this group mainly walked today” is supported; “The whole school prefers walking” is not. A broader conclusion needs a suitable question and collection method.

**Takeaway:** category frequencies plus missing records should reconcile all invitations. Explain whom the conclusion describes, what question was answered and what remains unknown. A parent checks classification and scope rather than exact terminology.
