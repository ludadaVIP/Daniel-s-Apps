## 倒数为什么能完成除法

现在总量和每份量都是分数：3/4 L 中有多少个 2/3 L？先用同样小份比较，再理解“乘除数倒数”，顺序不能倒过来只背规则。倒数的特点是与原来的非零数相乘恰好得一，不是变负号，也不是随便翻转题里任意分数。

商乘回每份量，应恢复已有总量。这条逆运算关系能解释算法，也能检查是否翻错了对象。除数为正时，总量零合法；零作为除数则无法定义一般商，也没有乘法倒数。

### 例 1 · 九个小份除每份八个

1. 已有 **3/4 L**，每份 **2/3 L**。
2. 共同小单位为 **1/12 L**，已有 **9/12 L**，一份需要 **8/12 L**。
3. 小份单位相同，比较九个与每份八个：**9÷8=9/8 份**。
4. 等价乘法 **3/4×3/2=9/8**，因为 **2/3×3/2=1**，3/2 是原除数的倒数。
5. 核回 **9/8×2/3 L=3/4 L**。完整一份后剩 **1/12 L**，它占一份的 **1/8**，这两个分数参照不同。

一般地，a/b 与 c/d 可化成同样的 1/(bd) 小份。已有 ad 个，每份 bc 个，商为 **ad/(bc)**，恰等于 **a/b×d/c**。这里 c 不能为零；所以翻转的是除数 c/d，而不是原总量 a/b。共同分母可以不是最小公倍数，只要小份单位相同便可比较。

### 例 2 · 结果不足一份

1. 已有 **5/8 L**，标准每份为 **3/4 L**。
2. 把每份改为 **6/8 L**，已有五个八分之一，一份需六个。
3. 精确商 **5÷6=5/6 份**，说明无法装满一个完整标准份。
4. 用倒数计算：**5/8×4/3=20/24=5/6**，与同小份比较一致。
5. 核回 **5/6×3/4 L=5/8 L**。若翻被除数得到 8/5，通常不能恢复原量。

倒数不等于相反数。3/5 的倒数为 5/3，二者乘积一；−3/5 是相反数，用于相加得零，是不同关系。任何数乘零都为零，不会得到一，因此零没有倒数。不能给 2/5÷0 编造一个结果；而 **0÷2/5=0** 可乘回核查，完全合法。

工作台先选已有升数和每份量，图中升标尺不变。两次核对依次展示共同小份、精确商与余量；然后选择除数的倒数并说明为什么。选错时回看“相乘得一”，不把翻转变成无条件操作。改题会清空旧商和旧选择。

**家庭挑战：** 纸上用 2 L 假想果汁，对比每份半升与四分之三升时的精确份数、完整份数和余量。再用乘回关系解释 9/8 份标准量。无需真的倒水或采购。家长核查对象、单位与逆运算理由，不要求固定句子。**带走一个发现：** 先解释同小份，再用倒数简算，最后乘回原除数核查。

## Why reciprocals complete division

How many 2/3 L groups are represented by 3/4 L? Compare equal small pieces first, then understand multiplication by the divisor’s reciprocal. A reciprocal multiplies a nonzero number to make one. It does not merely change its sign or invert whichever fraction is convenient.

The quotient multiplied by group size must restore the available amount. This inverse relationship explains and checks the algorithm. A zero dividend with a positive divisor is valid; zero as divisor cannot define general division and has no multiplicative reciprocal.

### Example 1 · Nine pieces divided by eight per group

1. Available water is **3/4 L** and each target group is **2/3 L**.
2. Use **1/12 L** pieces: the total is **9/12 L**, with **8/12 L** per group.
3. Equal piece units allow **9÷8=9/8 groups**.
4. Equivalently **3/4×3/2=9/8**, because **2/3×3/2=1** and 3/2 is the divisor’s reciprocal.
5. Check **9/8×2/3 L=3/4 L**. After one full group, **1/12 L** remains, equal to **1/8 of a group**, not the same reference unit.

In general, a/b and c/d can use common pieces of size 1/(bd). The available amount has ad pieces and a group needs bc, giving **ad/(bc)=a/b×d/c**. Here c must be nonzero. Invert divisor c/d, retaining dividend a/b. A common denominator need not be the least one provided both amounts use identical pieces.

### Example 2 · Less than one group

1. Available water is **5/8 L**, with **3/4 L** per group.
2. Rewrite the group as **6/8 L**: five eighths available, six required.
3. Exact quotient is **5÷6=5/6 groups**, so no complete group can be filled.
4. The reciprocal calculation **5/8×4/3=20/24=5/6** agrees.
5. Multiplying back **5/6×3/4 L=5/8 L** restores the total. Inverting the dividend instead would generally fail that check.

Reciprocals and additive opposites differ. The reciprocal of 3/5 is 5/3 because their product is one; −3/5 is its additive opposite because their sum is zero. Any number multiplied by zero remains zero, never one, so zero has no reciprocal. Do not invent a value for 2/5÷0. In contrast **0÷2/5=0** multiplies back correctly.

The workshop first reveals a common partition and then exact groups, complete groups and measured remainder. Only then choose the divisor’s reciprocal and justify it through a product of one. Changing the problem clears old checked results and choices.

**Family challenge:** on paper, compare 2 L of fictional juice using half-litre and three-quarter-litre groups. Report exact groups, complete groups and remaining litres, then explain the nine-eighths example through multiplication back. No pouring or purchase is required. A parent checks relationships, units and inverse reasoning. **Takeaway:** explain common pieces, simplify with the reciprocal, then multiply back to verify.
