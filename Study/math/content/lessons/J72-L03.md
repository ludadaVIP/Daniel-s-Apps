# 乘法分配律与去括号

## 外部因子要连接到每一项

分配律 `k(a+b)=ka+kb` 解释为什么括号外的因子要乘里面的每一项。这适用于正因子、负因子、分数因子和零；符号与系数按 J71 的有理数乘法处理。括号里的项是完整的带符号数量，`3x−4` 可看成 `3x+(−4)`，不能把负四的符号丢掉。

单独的外部负号表示乘负一。例如 `−(x²−2x+3)` 是负一依次乘 x²、负 2x 和正三，所以得到 `−x²+2x−3`。这不是随意“改几个符号”的口诀，而是同一条分配关系。

### 例 1：负因子的两条连接

展开 `−2(3x−4)`。

1. 外部负二乘 3x，得到 `−6x`。
2. 外部负二乘负四，得到正八。
3. 结果 `−6x+8`，两个原项都已处理。

`−6x−4` 漏乘了常数；`−6x−8` 把负乘负的符号算错。代 x=0，原式为八，这两种错式立即暴露常数问题。但证明对所有 x 成立的理由是逐项分配，不是只在零处吻合。

工作台显示一个因子到两项的连接；调整任一量后旧展开会隐藏、旧答案清空。先预测，再打开图检查每条连接是否都存在。图里展示的是代数关系，不用箭头的长度充当系数大小。

### 例 2：先展开，再合并

计算 `3(x+2)−2(x−1)`。

第一组展开为 `3x+6`。第二组包含外部减号，应整体看成负二乘括号，得到 `−2x+2`，不是 `−2x−2`。完整式子成为 `3x+6−2x+2`，合并为 `x+8`。

若保留 `3x−2x+8`，数量已经等值，但仍有重复一次项，尚未完成“展开并合并”的要求。若只给原来的 `3(x+2)−2(x−1)`，关系虽没有变，也未执行要求的整理步骤。

## 分数与零也遵循同一条关系

`(1/2)(4x²−6x)=2x²−3x`，两个系数都乘半。不能只把平方项折半而保留负 6x。两项的字母部分不同，之后不再合并。

`0(x²−2x+5)=0`，零乘每项都零，不能保留常数五。零项省去后应明确写结果零。外部因子为一时原项保持，为负一时各取相反数；这些边界可以帮助检查程序化套规则有没有遗漏。

更长的式子也只是同一过程：`−3(2x−1)+2(x²−x)` 先得到 `−6x+3+2x²−2x`，再按平方、一次、常数归类，得到 `2x²−8x+3`。每一步保留其余完整项，不把展开某组后的中间数误当整体结果。

## 给家长看的说明

孩子应能圈外部因子，连到每个带符号项，读出每个乘积，再决定哪些同类项可以合并。请他们特意检查常数和括号中原本为负的项，因为漏乘或符号错误常发生在这里。一次代入可以发现错误，通用正确性仍来自分配律及精确系数关系。

# Distributive law and expanding brackets

## Connect the outside factor to every term

The distributive relationship `k(a+b)=ka+kb` explains why an outside factor reaches every inside term. It works for positive, negative, fractional and zero factors using rational multiplication. Treat `3x−4` as `3x+(−4)` so the complete signed constant is retained.

A lone outside minus is multiplication by negative one. Thus `−(x²−2x+3)` gives `−x²+2x−3`, with negative one multiplying all three terms. Sign changes follow one relationship rather than an arbitrary pattern.

### Example 1: two products from a negative factor

Expand `−2(3x−4)`.

1. Negative two times 3x is `−6x`.
2. Negative two times negative four is positive eight.
3. The result is `−6x+8`, accounting for both original terms.

The form `−6x−4` misses the constant’s factor; `−6x−8` has the wrong sign. At x=0, the original gives eight, exposing both mistakes. The general justification remains term-by-term distribution, not one matching assignment.

Workshop connectors show the same factor reaching both terms. Changing a control hides the old expansion and clears its answer. Predict first, then inspect every connection. Connector length does not measure a coefficient.

### Example 2: expand before collecting

For `3(x+2)−2(x−1)`, the first group becomes `3x+6`. The second outside factor is negative two, giving `−2x+2`, not `−2x−2`. The complete expression becomes `3x+6−2x+2`, then `x+8`.

The equivalent intermediate `3x−2x+8` still contains repeated linear types. Repeating the original factored form also leaves the requested expansion unfinished. Complete the specified transformation rather than merely preserve value.

## Fractions and zero use the same rule

For `(1/2)(4x²−6x)`, both coefficients are halved, giving `2x²−3x`. The square and linear terms remain unlike. Multiplying only the square term by one half would miss a contribution.

The expression `0(x²−2x+5)` is zero because every contribution is multiplied by zero. No constant survives. Factor one preserves all terms; factor negative one takes every opposite. These boundaries test whether the rule has been applied consistently.

A longer example, `−3(2x−1)+2(x²−x)`, first becomes `−6x+3+2x²−2x`, then `2x²−8x+3`. Retain the rest of the expression while expanding each group. Identify the factor, each signed term, each product and the final collection. Trial values reveal errors; distribution and exact coefficient relationships justify the general result.
