# 变量变化的现实解释

## 一条式子还需要数量、单位和范围

`y=kx+b` 只是数学形式，应用时还要说明 x、y 各代表什么、用什么单位、哪些输入允许，以及均匀变化假设在哪里有效。图线过零以后能继续算出负数，不保证情境允许那个值；整数份数也不能因为画了连续坐标轴就变成任意实数。

本课所有规则都是明确给定的虚构模型，用来练习解释，不声称是真实测量或实际收费。观察数据可帮助检验模型，但有限记录不保证范围外也成立。模型假设、代数结果与有效预测应分开报告。

### 例 1：排水到零就停止

虚构水箱 `V=12−2t`，t 是分钟，V 是升，有效范围 `0≤t≤6`。初始十二升，每分钟减少二升。在 `t=5/2` 时，`V=12−5=7 L`，时间在范围内，输出有效。

令 V=0，得 t=6 分钟，这是空箱边界。继续机械代 t=7，会得到负二，但这不是负水量的有效预测，而是把式子延长到了模型未允许的范围。它也不同于“方程无解”：求空箱时方程有解六，越界输入则没有被本模型定义为可用情境预测。

另一个模型 `V=9−(3/2)t`，在零至六分钟有效，t=4 得三升。变化率为每分钟负 1.5 升，不是只报“负一点五”而不说明所比较的两个量。连续时间允许半分钟等输入，在给定假设下可以连线。

### 例 2：完整件数与负温度

虚构卡片费 `C=3n+5` NZD，n 为零至六的整数张数。四张费用十七元，每张三元的重复量与一次固定五元分开。n=0 时仍五元，是本题明确给定的规则，不推广到真实商家。n=2.5 不是完整张数，因此只画允许的整数点，不用连线默许半张。

假想摄氏模型 `T=−4+(3/2)t`，t 用小时，范围零至四小时。初始负四度，每小时升 1.5 度，两小时后负一度。负读数在这里有效，与不能接受负水量不同。温度线只画给定时段，不能保证接下来整天都按此速度升温。

## 读图的陡峭与实际变化

排水横轴分钟、升温横轴小时，纵轴分别升与摄氏度，不能把两条线的视觉陡峭或两个率的数字直接拿来比较“谁变化更快”。即使同一模型，仅改轴刻度也能改变屏幕倾角。先明确比较的同一种数量和单位，才计算变化率。

工作台允许把输入推到范围外，报告机械代入结果与无有效预测的原因；数值作答只在允许输入时出现。这种反馈帮助孩子区别算术错误、整数限制与模型边界。切换模型会更换轴标签、单位、允许范围和图的连续/离散形式，并清除旧数值任务。

家长追问“零输入代表哪一时刻？”“固定量重复几次？”“最晚允许到哪里？”再让孩子举一个无效输入并说明原因。能把图、表、式和条件互相翻译，比只算出一个正确输出更接近建模理解。

# Interpreting variables in context

## A formula needs quantities, units and a domain

The form y=kx+b needs meanings for x and y, units, permitted inputs and an interval over which uniform change is assumed. Algebra can produce values outside a context. A continuous coordinate system also does not turn integer counts into arbitrary inputs.

All rules here are explicitly supplied fictional models for interpretation, not real observations or actual pricing. Data can help test a model, but finitely many records do not guarantee behaviour elsewhere. Keep model premises, algebraic results and valid predictions separate.

### Example 1: stop draining at empty

A fictional tank follows V=12−2t litres, with t in minutes and 0≤t≤6. It starts at twelve litres and loses two litres per minute. At t=5/2, V=7 L, a valid output within the domain.

Setting V=0 gives t=6 minutes, the empty boundary. Mechanical substitution of t=7 gives −2, but this is an algebraic extension, not a valid negative-volume prediction. It differs from an equation having no solution: the empty-time equation has solution six, while the later input is outside this model's permitted prediction domain.

A second supplied model V=9−(3/2)t on zero to six minutes gives three litres at t=4. Its rate is negative 1.5 litres per minute, with both quantities and units specified. Continuous time permits fractional-minute inputs and a connected line under the stated assumption.

### Example 2: whole items and negative temperature

Fictional cost C=3n+5 NZD permits integer card counts zero through six. Four cards cost seventeen; the per-card term repeats and the fixed fee occurs once. Charging five at zero is part of this fictional rule rather than an assertion about merchants. Input 2.5 is not a whole-card count, so plot only permitted integer points without silently adding a continuous domain.

A fictional Celsius model T=−4+(3/2)t uses hours on zero to four. It begins at −4°C and rises 1.5°C per hour, giving −1°C after two hours. Negative temperature is allowed here, unlike negative tank volume. The model makes no promise of the same warming rate all day.

## Visual steepness and numerical change differ

Draining uses minutes and litres; warming uses hours and degrees. Neither screen angles nor bare rate numbers establish which changes faster in a meaningful common quantity. Even one model changes apparent steepness when scales change. Define comparable quantities and units first.

The workshop allows out-of-domain test inputs and separately reports the algebraic extension and why no valid prediction is asserted. Numerical tasks appear only for allowed inputs. Switching models changes axis labels, units, domain and continuous/discrete representation, clearing the previous task.

A parent asks what zero input means, how often the fixed amount repeats and where the model stops. Give one invalid input with a reason. Translating among graph, table, expression and conditions demonstrates more modelling understanding than producing one output alone.
