# 有理数加法

## 有符号的份数仍要同样大小

正负分数相加，也是在合并用同一单位表示的数量。负号给方向，分母说明每份多大；不能因出现负号就省略通分。`−3/4` 是负三个四分之一，`1/2` 是正两个四分之一，合并后剩负一个四分之一。分母不是另一组需要相加的对象。

可以借相消理解规则：同号量沿相同方向合并；异号量抵消同样大小的部分，剩余方向由绝对值较大的一方决定。完全相反、大小相等时和为零。这个说明依赖同一个原单位，若一块披萨大小与另一块不同，不能直接用“正半块”和“负半块”就说物理量相消。

### 例 1：向右移动，结果仍可负

计算 `−3/4+1/2`。

1. 用四分之一作共同小单位，`1/2=2/4`。
2. 合并分子 `−3+2=−1`，得到 `−1/4`。
3. 数轴从 `−3/4` 出发向右走半单位，即两个四分之一格，终点 `−1/4`，尚未到零。
4. 反查：`−1/4−1/2=−3/4`，回到起点。

变化为正只说明向右，不说明终点为正。终点符号由相对零的位置决定。工作台可以先看起点，预测结果，再展开有向线段；线段长度半单位不是答案的绝对值四分之一。图中每一个原单位始终同长。

### 例 2：同号与异号的两种合并

`−2/3+(−1/6)`：先写成 `−4/6+(−1/6)=−5/6`，两个负量都向左，大小相加而方向不改。

对比 `1/3+(−5/6)`：写成 `2/6+(−5/6)=−3/6=−1/2`。正的两个六分之一抵消负量中的两个六分之一，还剩负三个。若只看到“一正一负”就写零，会忽略两项大小不同；只有互为相反数，例如 `−7/5+7/5` 才完全抵消。

小数也可沿用相同理由。`−0.6+1.1=−6/10+11/10=5/10=1/2`，不是把 0.6 与 1.1 的数码随意加减再猜符号。

## 通分与运算性质一起使用

`−5/4+2/3` 用十二分之一为共同单位，得到 `−15/12+8/12=−7/12`。不能算成 `−3/7`，那样把分母也加了，改变了每一份大小。结果是精确分数；近似小数不能在要求精确时替代它。

加法交换次序仍有相同的和；多个加数也可在合法结合后先配出相反数，例如 `(−3/4+1/2)+3/4=1/2`。但这不说明减法、除法也能随意交换或结合。每一种变形都要知道所用性质，不把“计算方便”当等值理由。

## 向家长解释你的模型

不要只报“异号相减”四个字。指出两项各有多少个共同小单位、相消掉多少、剩余在哪一侧。再用数轴或逆运算核验。改变第二项使终点恰为零，或者仍在负侧，观察变化方向与位置符号如何分别描述。能用同一套关系解释零、异号、同号、分数和有限小数，才说明运算不只是记忆符号。

# Adding rational numbers

## Signed parts still need equal sizes

Adding signed fractions combines quantities in a common unit. The sign gives direction and the denominator gives the part size. A negative sign does not remove the need for common denominators. Negative three quarters and positive one half become negative three quarters and positive two quarters, leaving negative one quarter.

Same-sign amounts combine in one direction. Opposite-sign amounts cancel equal magnitudes, leaving the direction of the larger magnitude. Only equal opposite amounts cancel completely. This depends on a common original unit: halves of physically different-sized wholes cannot simply be cancelled as equal amounts.

### Example 1: moving right can still finish negative

Calculate `−3/4+1/2`.

1. Use quarters: `1/2=2/4`.
2. Add signed numerators, `−3+2=−1`, for `−1/4`.
3. Start at `−3/4` and move half a unit right, two quarter-intervals. The endpoint `−1/4` is still left of zero.
4. Check by subtracting the change: `−1/4−1/2=−3/4`.

A positive change means rightward movement, not a positive endpoint. Position relative to zero determines the result’s sign. The workshop first displays the start; predict before revealing the directed segment. Its half-unit length is not the endpoint’s quarter-unit magnitude. The original unit retains its length throughout.

### Example 2: same-sign and opposite-sign combinations

For `−2/3+(−1/6)`, write `−4/6+(−1/6)=−5/6`. Both amounts point left, so magnitudes combine with a negative sign.

Compare `1/3+(−5/6)=2/6+(−5/6)=−3/6=−1/2`. Two positive sixths cancel two of the negative sixths, leaving three negative sixths. One positive and one negative do not automatically sum to zero; equal magnitude is also required, as in `−7/5+7/5=0`.

The same reasoning works for decimals: `−0.6+1.1=−6/10+11/10=5/10=1/2`. Do not operate on unsigned digits and guess a sign afterwards.

## Use common units and justified properties

For `−5/4+2/3`, use twelfths: `−15/12+8/12=−7/12`. Adding denominators to get `−3/7` would change the sizes of the parts. Keep the exact fraction when exactness is requested.

Addition can be commuted and associated, allowing convenient cancellation such as `(−3/4+1/2)+3/4=1/2`. Those properties do not license arbitrary reordering of subtraction or division. Name the property that preserves value rather than claiming a rearrangement is valid merely because it is convenient.

Explain each term in common-sized parts, the amount cancelled and the remaining direction. Verify using the line or inverse operation. Change the second term until the endpoint is zero or remains negative, and distinguish movement direction from position sign. One coherent relationship should handle zero, same signs, opposite signs, fractions and terminating decimals.
