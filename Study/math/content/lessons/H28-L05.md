# 解释假设与结果

一个结果必须和产生它的条件一起报告。“费用49”少了方案、人数、用量和单位，无法指导决策。本课把基准方案变成一份有范围、有未知、有重算条件的报告。

## 区分规则、假设与结果

虚构工作坊题设规则：库存4张、正活动备用5张、每包8张且整包采购；A收7加每包6筹码，B收19加每包4筹码；容量20人；n=0取消，需求费用均0。这些是模型的给定规则。

本次压力分析假设人数n∈{18,19,20}，每人用量q∈{3,4}，没有额外损耗或收费，预算70筹码。六个(n,q)组合就是这次声明的全部情景，不带发生概率。基准取下界人数18、每人3张。正活动需求D=nq+5，缺口D−4，包数向上取整，再分别计费。

### 例1：基准够用不代表全部情景够用

基准18人q=3，需求59、缺55、7包，A费用49，B费用47。两个都在70内，但基准只是一行。

枚举六情景：18、19、20人且q=3时，包数7、8、8；q=4时，包数10、10、11。A费用依次49、55、55与67、67、73；B费用47、51、51与59、59、63。人数20、q4时需求85、缺81，需要11包；10包加库存只有84张，少1张，故不能用10包把费用压低。

需求与最少包数随正人数和用量不减，费用也不减，所以该范围最高费用在人数20、q4处。枚举表与单调性推理相互核验。A最高73超过70，不能保证六情景都可行；B最高63≤70，且六情景人数均≤20，故在这些明确假设内全部可行。

### 例2：把预算加大仍无法消除容量限制

把人数范围扩为18至22整数，q仍3或4，预算改200。尽管两计价方案的费用都可承担，21和22人仍超容量20，因此两方案都不能在全部新情景下实施。改变预算不改变房间。

反过来，把预算保持70但用量扩至每人5张，也超出上一份六情景报告。20人q5需求105、缺101、13包，B费用71，原63筹码的最坏值不再有效。这个新例不推翻上一份带条件的结论，它说明假设改变后必须重新计算。

## 报告写给能采取行动的人

可写：“在库存4、备用5、整包8张、所列费用与容量20的规则下，假设18–20人且每人3或4张，没有其他损耗收费。A基准49、最高73筹码；B基准47、最高63。预算70时B在全部六情景通过，A有失败情景。结论仅覆盖这些假设。需确认实际人数与材料用量；若损耗、价格、额外收费或人数范围变化，重新计算。”

这比较了两种明确方案，不证明B优于所有可能供应方式。没有给情景概率，所以“最坏费用63”不是95%置信上限，也不是活动有某种成功概率。现实供应、质量、价格是否符合题设仍未知，数学模型不能凭空保证。

工作台更改人数上界时，若下界与方案不变，基准预测可保持；最高费用与“全部可行”必须针对当前范围重核。核验表列出全部组合，预算与容量分列，便于追踪失败原因。正式出口和家庭报告由家长复核完整理由，不能仅靠输入“假设、风险、范围”等关键词自动完成。

# Communicating assumptions and results

Report a result alongside the conditions that produced it. “Cost 49” omits plan, attendance, usage and units. This lesson turns a baseline calculation into an actionable report with scope, unknowns and recalculation triggers.

## Separate rules, assumptions and derived results

The fictional workshop rules specify stock four, five spares for a positive event, whole packets of eight, A prices seven plus six per packet, B prices nineteen plus four per packet and capacity twenty. Attendance zero cancels with zero demand and cost. These are given model rules.

This stress analysis assumes n∈{18,19,20}, cards each q∈{3,4}, no additional losses or charges and budget seventy toy credits. The six pairs are the entire stated scenario set, with no assigned probabilities. The baseline uses lowest attendance eighteen and usage three. For positive events demand is nq+5; subtract four, round packets upward and apply each price rule.

### Example 1: An affordable baseline does not cover every case

At eighteen people using three each, demand is 59, shortage 55 and packets seven. A costs 49 and B47, both within seventy. That is just one row.

For attendance eighteen, nineteen and twenty with q=3, packet counts are seven, eight and eight. With q=4 they are ten, ten and eleven. A costs 49,55,55 and 67,67,73. B costs 47,51,51 and 59,59,63. Twenty using four each demand 85, shortage 81 and eleven packets. Ten packets plus stock supply only 84, so using ten to lower the reported cost leaves one card missing.

Demand, minimum packet count and cost are nondecreasing in positive attendance and usage. The maximum over this range therefore occurs at twenty people using four each. Enumeration and monotonic reasoning check each other. A reaches 73>70 and fails to cover all six. B reaches 63≤70 and every attendance fits twenty, establishing feasibility throughout these explicit assumptions.

### Example 2: More money cannot remove a capacity limit

Extend attendance to every integer eighteen through twenty-two, keep q three or four, and raise budget to 200. Both price plans are affordable, but twenty-one and twenty-two exceed capacity twenty. Neither plan is feasible throughout the new scenario set. Changing money does not enlarge the room.

Alternatively keep budget seventy and allow five cards per person. This is outside the earlier six-case assumption. Twenty using five demand 105, shortage 101 and thirteen packets; B costs 71. The earlier maximum 63 no longer applies. This does not refute the earlier conditional report; it demonstrates why changed assumptions require recalculation.

## Write for someone making a decision

A report could state: “Under stock four, five spares, whole packets of eight, the stated prices and capacity twenty, assume eighteen through twenty people, three or four cards each and no other losses or charges. A has baseline 49 and maximum 73 credits; B has baseline 47 and maximum 63. At budget seventy, B passes all six scenarios and A fails some. This conclusion covers only those assumptions. Confirm actual attendance and usage; recalculate if losses, prices, charges or the attendance range change.”

This compares two explicit plans, not every possible supplier. Without a probability model, maximum cost 63 is neither a 95% confidence bound nor a success probability. Real supply, quality and agreement with the fictional prices remain unknown. The calculation cannot supply missing assurances.

Changing the attendance upper bound can preserve a baseline prediction when the lower bound and plan remain unchanged. Maximum cost and feasibility throughout must nevertheless be checked for the current range. The audit enumerates every pair and separates budget from capacity to expose failure causes. A parent reviews the full exit and family report; entering words such as assumptions, risk and scope does not automatically complete the reasoning.
