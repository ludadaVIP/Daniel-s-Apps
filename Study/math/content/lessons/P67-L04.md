## 有利对象数除以全部等可能对象数

袋中有同样大小、充分混匀的圆片，我们**假设每一枚等可能被抽到**。在这个非空有限模型中，事件概率为 **P=有利对象数÷全部对象数**。有利指符合所问事件，不是好坏评价。不同颜色或字母只是分类，同字母的不同圆片仍是不同对象。

分母是全部对象数，不是类别名称数。概率是零到一之间的无单位比例；有利次数或有利对象数本身不是概率。如果对象大小、抽取位置或机制让各对象不等可能，就不能不加条件地直接套这个计数比。

### 例 1 · 三个 A 不是三分之一

1. 袋中 **A 三枚、B 两枚、C 一枚**，总数 **6**。
2. 抽到 A 的有利对象是三枚。
3. **P(A)=3/6=1/2**，也可写 0.5 或 50%。
4. 不能因为有三种字母而给 1/3，也不能只填有利数三。
5. 事件改为 A 或 B，有利三加二共五枚，分母仍是整个袋六枚，所以概率 5/6。

这两个类别不重叠，一枚圆片不可能同时标 A 和 B，因而可以相加。若事件有重叠条件，则需避免把同一对象重复算；不要把“或”机械理解为永远直接相加。

### 例 2 · 概率零不同于空袋

1. 非空袋只有五枚 A、三枚 B，总数八。
2. 没有 D，所以 **P(D)=0/8=0**，是这个模型中的不可能事件。
3. 抽到 A 或 B 覆盖全部八枚，概率 **8/8=1**。
4. 若袋里根本没有对象，就没有这次抽取的结果，不能写 0/0=0。
5. 空袋模型未定义与非空模型里的不可能事件，是两个不同情形。

若只有一枚 A、两枚 B，则 P(A)=1/3。要求精确值时，0.33 不是 1/3；可用 2/6 等值分数，或精确的带分数百分数写法，但不能只因看起来接近就当相同。比例为零不证明此前没记录到的某结果永远不可能；要看理想模型中是否真的没有该对象。

工作台可改 A/B/C 个数，圈出目标事件；圈选不改变各圆片的实际抽取机会。每次抽后放回，才保持重复时还是同一组对象。若不放回，后续袋内组成会改变，需要重新建模，不能把前次比例原封不动搬到下一次。

家长让孩子先逐枚数总数，再圈事件对象，最后才写分数。把所有计数设零时，工作台会停用核对并说明空袋未定义，而不是画出一条假的零概率。带走一个发现：**先确认等可能与非空，分子数事件、分母数全部，保留精确比例与重复抽取条件。**

## Count favourable objects over all equally likely objects

The fictional bag contains same-sized mixed tokens, with **each individual token assumed equally likely** to be drawn. For this nonempty finite model, **P=favourable objects/all objects**. Favourable means matching the event, not good or bad. Repeated letters still mark distinct physical objects.

The denominator counts all objects, not category names. Probability is a unitless proportion between zero and one, rather than a favourable count. If size, position or the drawing mechanism makes objects unequally likely, the simple count ratio needs a different justification.

### Example 1 · Three A tokens do not imply one third

1. **Three A, two B and one C** give **six** tokens overall.
2. Three objects qualify for A.
3. **P(A)=3/6=1/2**, also 0.5 or 50%.
4. Three category names do not justify 1/3, and the count three alone is not a probability.
5. For A or B, five objects qualify while the denominator remains six: probability 5/6.

A and B are disjoint labels here, so their favourable counts add without overlap. For overlapping event descriptions, avoid counting the same object twice; “or” does not universally justify adding probabilities without examining the sets.

### Example 2 · Probability zero differs from an empty bag

1. A nonempty bag contains five A and three B, total eight.
2. D is absent: **P(D)=0/8=0**, an impossible event in this model.
3. A or B contains every token: **8/8=1**.
4. An entirely empty bag supplies no drawing outcome; 0/0 is not zero.
5. An undefined empty-bag model differs from an impossible event in a valid nonempty model.

With one A and two B, P(A)=1/3. The decimal 0.33 is not its exact replacement. Equivalent fractions such as 2/6 are accepted, but numerical closeness alone does not establish equality. Failure to observe something in a record also does not establish probability zero; inspect whether the model actually lacks the outcome.

The workshop changes counts and highlights event tokens without changing their drawing chances. Replacement after a draw retains the same model for repetition. Without replacement, the composition may change and later probabilities must be reconsidered. Count the whole bag, identify the favourable objects and only then write the fraction. With all counts zero the workshop explains the undefined model and disables checking rather than assigning a fake zero. The takeaway requires equal likelihood and a nonempty total, an event numerator and whole-model denominator, exact proportions and explicit repetition conditions.
