# 序列、递推与通项

剧院第几排、练习的第几天、纸卡的第几轮，都有顺序。数列把每个允许的序号对应到一个项值；先区分“第几个”与“是多少”，再谈规律。

## 一条核心想法

**序号n、项值aₙ和生成规则是三种不同信息。** 本单元aₙ默认在正整数n上定义，因此a₁是第一项，a₄是第四项；n=4不表示项值也是4，也不自动允许a₀或a₁.₅。

通项把n直接对应到aₙ，递推则说明怎样由已知项得到下一项。递推还需足够的初始条件。数列也可以另行规定从0开始，例如后面的余额B₀；那是另一套明确约定，不能悄悄改变本题的域。

### 例1：三次转移，到第四项

给a₁=2，且对所有正整数n有aₙ₊₁=aₙ+2。逐项得到2、4、6、8，所以a₄=8。从第一项到第四项走了3步，不能再多加一次2而得到10。

同一完整递推可写aₙ=2+2(n−1)=2n。代n=1恢复首项2；代一般n时，2(n+1)−2n=2符合每步增量。这里得到通项的依据是完整规则和首项，而非只观察前三个数就断言所有后续。

若另给完整通项aₙ=n²+1，则a₅=5²+1=26。要代入的是序号5，而不是把前一项或项值当作输入。公式可以在小数上算数，不代表原数列因此多出了小数序号的项。

### 例2：相同前三项，不同第四项

只观测到a₁=2、a₂=4、a₃=6，没有后续规则。公式aₙ=2n符合观测，第四项为8；但bₙ=2n+(n−1)(n−2)(n−3)也符合。

当n=1、2、3时，附加乘积有一个因子为0，故两套公式的前三项完全相同。n=4时乘积3×2×1=6，因此b₄=8+6=14。由此可知8是可能的延伸，却不是原条件唯一强迫的答案。不能把“另一个公式更复杂”当成排除它的数学条件。

如果题目另外明确“这是等差数列”，就增加了约束，后续可以据此确定。必须区分原本给出的条件和自己添加的假设；下一课将研究这个额外结构。

## 规则与离散点工作台

选择完整加法递推、平方通项、倍增递推或仅前三项观测，再选n。n=0在本题外域；观测模式下n≤3的已知值仍可取，n>3需回答信息不足。

对照两种延伸只是展示可能性，不替观测数据选定一个规则。点只画在整数序号上；点间的空白没有定义新的项。扩大图窗不会增加数学定义域，超窗值保留在精确核验表。

## 家长核对与迁移

解释题用两个公式说明不足；出口则用a₁=1、增量2n+1核对平方通项。应听见首项与任意n的恒等关系，而不只列1、4、9、16。后面的等差和等比通项都会再次使用“从第一项走n−1步”，储蓄课再明确为什么B₀另有含义。

# Sequences, recurrence and nth term

Rows in a theatre, practice days and rounds of a paper activity all have an order. A sequence assigns a term value to each allowed index. Separate “which position?” from “what value?” before discussing patterns.

## One central idea

**The index n, term aₙ and generating rule are different information.** Here aₙ uses positive integer indices unless stated otherwise. Thus a₁ is the first term and a₄ the fourth; n=4 does not mean its value is four, and neither a₀ nor a₁.₅ is automatically allowed.

An nth-term formula maps an index directly to its value. A recurrence describes how to obtain a later term from known ones and needs sufficient initial conditions. A different model may explicitly start at zero, as with later balances B₀. That is a separate convention rather than a silent extension of the present domain.

### Example 1: Three transitions reach term four

Let a₁=2 and aₙ₊₁=aₙ+2 for every positive integer n. Iteration gives 2,4,6,8, so a₄=8. Reaching term four from term one takes three transitions, not four; an extra addition would incorrectly give ten.

The same complete recurrence yields aₙ=2+2(n−1)=2n. Substituting n=1 recovers the initial two, while 2(n+1)−2n=2 matches every increment. The justification comes from a complete rule and initial condition, rather than three observations alone.

For a separately supplied formula aₙ=n²+1, a₅=5²+1=26. Substitute the index, not a previous term or value. Although an algebraic expression can be evaluated at decimals, that does not add decimal-indexed terms to the stated sequence.

### Example 2: Same prefix, different fourth terms

Suppose only a₁=2,a₂=4,a₃=6 are observed. Formula aₙ=2n fits and gives a₄=8. But bₙ=2n+(n−1)(n−2)(n−3) also fits.

At n=1,2,3 the added product has a zero factor, leaving identical observations. At n=4 its value is 3×2×1=6, so b₄=14. Eight is a possible continuation, not the only one forced by the data. A preference for a simpler formula is not an extra mathematical condition supplied by the question.

If the question explicitly adds that the whole sequence is arithmetic, it supplies additional structure. Distinguish stated premises from assumptions you introduce. The next lesson investigates that structure.

## Rule and discrete-point workbench

Select a complete additive recurrence, quadratic formula, doubling recurrence or observation-only mode, then choose n. Zero lies outside this lesson’s index domain. In observation mode, known values at indices 1–3 remain available, while later indices require insufficient.

Comparing candidate extensions demonstrates possibilities without selecting a hidden rule for the observations. Points occur only at integer indices; gaps between them define no additional terms. Enlarging the viewing window does not enlarge the mathematical domain, and exact values remain available beyond it.

## Parent check and transfer

The reasoning task uses two formulas to explain insufficient evidence. The exit checks the square rule using initial value one and increment 2n+1. Listen for the initial condition and an identity for arbitrary n, rather than merely 1,4,9,16. Arithmetic and geometric formulas both retrieve n−1 transitions later; the savings lesson separately establishes the meaning of B₀.
