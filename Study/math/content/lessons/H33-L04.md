# 二项分布

把每次试验记成成功1、失败0，固定做n次，X记录成功总次数。若各次独立、成功率都为p，X服从二项分布，记作Bin(n,p)。二分类只是一项条件；固定次数、独立与同成功率也不可漏。

## 先核模型，再代公式

恰好k次成功的一条特定顺序，概率为pᵏ(1−p)ⁿ⁻ᵏ。哪些位置成功有C(n,k)种选择，各顺序互斥且在独立同p条件下概率相同，所以P(X=k)=C(n,k)pᵏ(1−p)ⁿ⁻ᵏ，k=0,…,n。

“至少k”合并k及更大次数，“至多k”合并不超过k的次数，“恰好k”只取一项。每题先用文字或集合写出事件，避免只看到数字就选某一柱。

### 例1：四枚公平硬币

四次独立公平抛币，X为正面数。恰两次正面有C(4,2)=6种顺序，每种概率(1/2)⁴=1/16，所以P(X=2)=6/16=3/8。只算HHTT会得到1/16，漏掉另外五条顺序。

至少一次的补事件是零次，因此P(X≥1)=1−(1/2)⁴=15/16。它不是P(X=1)=4/16，也不能把“至少”误读成“至多”。完整分布的分子为1、4、6、4、1，总和16，对应归一化核验。

把X写成四个0/1指示变量之和，每项期望p、方差p(1−p)。期望相加给E(X)=np=2；独立使方差相加，得Var(X)=np(1−p)=1。这里的期望加法与方差加法条件不同，不能都声称无需独立。

### 例2：改变p与检查失效情形

三次独立试验，每次p=1/3。恰一次成功有三个位置，每条顺序概率(1/3)(2/3)²=4/27，合计4/9。若错误地把成功次数0、1、2、3视作四个等可能值，就会得到完全不同的模型。

有限袋中不放回抽取时，抽中什么会改变下一次袋内比例，试验通常相依，不能只因每次都有红/蓝二分类就套二项式。若成功率每次不同，即使独立，计数也一般不是同p二项分布。一直做到首次成功则次数不固定，所定义的变量也不同。

p=0时X以概率1取0，p=1时以概率1取n，二者仍是合法退化分布，方差0。n=0可作为空重复的边界：唯一计数0、质量1。公式里的零次幂表示空乘积1，不能把这些端点随意除以p或1−p处理。

## 让边界留在图表里

工作台展示0到n的所有计数槽，包括零概率项；支持集则只列正质量取值。改事件时重新核哪些槽要相加，不用固定一条“尾概率”代替所有问题。选择不放回或遇成功停止时，工作台先指出条件缺失，不显示一个冒充正确答案的二项概率。

家长可让学生解释组合因子的来源，或反问六条顺序为何能相加。出口要逐项列条件并给一个不适用的情形。有限实验直方图看着接近二项形状，不足以证明背后的试验独立；模型依据来自设计与明确假设。

# Binomial distribution

Record each trial as success one or failure zero. With a fixed number n of independent trials sharing success probability p, the success count X follows Bin(n,p). Binary labels alone are insufficient: fixed count, independence and common p all matter.

## Check the model before using its formula

A particular ordered sequence containing k successes has probability pᵏ(1−p)ⁿ⁻ᵏ. There are C(n,k) choices of success positions. These sequences are disjoint and have equal mass under the stated assumptions, giving P(X=k)=C(n,k)pᵏ(1−p)ⁿ⁻ᵏ.

Exactly k uses one count. At least k includes all counts from k upward; at most k includes counts up to k. Translate words into an event before choosing bars or calculating a tail.

### Example 1: Four independent fair tosses

For four fair tosses, exactly two heads has six possible orders. Each has mass 1/16, so P(X=2)=6/16=3/8. Counting HHTT alone loses five other orders.

At least one head complements zero heads, giving1−1/16=15/16. This is not exactly one, whose probability is4/16. The full count distribution has numerators1,4,6,4,1 over sixteen, providing an independent total-mass check.

Write X as a sum of four success indicators. Each has mean p and variance p(1−p). Adding expectations gives np=2; independence permits adding variances to obtain np(1−p)=1. Linearity of expectation and variance addition do not have identical conditions.

### Example 2: A different success rate and invalid assumptions

For three independent trials with p=1/3, exactly one success has three positions. Each ordered sequence has mass(1/3)(2/3)²=4/27, so the probability is4/9. Treating the four count values as equally likely would describe a different distribution.

Without replacement from a finite bag, outcomes generally change the next conditional success probability and create dependence. Independent trials with varying success probabilities also generally fail the common-p binomial model. Continuing until first success does not give a fixed trial count and defines a different random variable.

At p=0, X is zero with probability one; at p=1 it is n with probability one. Both are valid degenerate cases with zero variance. The boundary n=0 represents no trials and a constant zero count. Zero powers in the formula represent empty products equal to one; do not divide by p or1−p at these endpoints.

## Retain boundaries in the representation

The workbench keeps every count slot from zero through n, including zero masses, while listing only positive-mass values in the support. Changing the event requires reconsidering which slots contribute. In an invalid experimental setting, the interface explains the missing assumptions instead of presenting an unjustified binomial probability.

Explain where the combination factor comes from and why ordered events can be added. A finite histogram resembling a binomial shape does not establish independence. The model needs experimental design or explicit assumptions, not appearance alone.
