# 储蓄增长与数列模型

每期增长后再存10，与先存10再增长，是不同的时间顺序。数列模型不仅要给倍率，还要说明初始时点、每期动作和怎样入账，才能得到可核验的余额。

## 一条核心想法

**先写每期操作顺序，再推余额；先声明舍入规则，再解释金额。** B₀定义为第一期所有操作之前的余额。即使采用期初存款，B₀也还没有包含第一笔定期存入；每完成一期恰好发生一次存款。

期末存入为Bₖ=qBₖ₋₁+c，期初存入为Bₖ=q(Bₖ₋₁+c)。本课均为虚构学习情境，以“期”计，不对应现实账户利率，不计税费。理想模型保留精确值；逐期入账模型在每次增长后的余额上按分四舍五入。显示到分不等于真的逐期舍入了账户。

### 例1：同样投入，不同存入时点

B₀=100 NZD、q=1.1、每期存10，不逐期舍入。期末存：B₁=100×1.1+10=120，B₂=120×1.1+10=142。期初存：B₁=(100+10)×1.1=121，B₂=(121+10)×1.1=144.1。

两期累计投入都为100+2×10=120，增长所得却分别22与24.1。不能把期初存款又在期末加一次；也不能把余额中的增长所得再次算作投入。

期末模型的余额不是简单等比：B₁/B₀=6/5，B₂/B₁=71/60并不相等。可以分解贡献：起存额为B₀qⁿ，n次期末存款贡献c(1+q+…+qⁿ⁻¹)；期初存款则让每笔多增长一期，贡献再乘q。q=1时两模型都成为B₀+nc，n=0时空和为0，余额仍B₀。

### 例2：逐期按分入账与仅显示近似

起存0.05 NZD、不加存，每期倍率1.1。理想模型两期后是0.05×1.1²=0.0605 NZD，显示到分可写≈NZ$0.06，但精确余额仍0.0605。

若规则改为每期增长后立即按分入账，第一期0.055记0.06，第二期0.066记0.07。结果0.07与只在最后把0.0605舍入成0.06不同。差异来自操作规则，不是两套计算“精确程度随便选”。

正余额恰在半分时向上入账，是本工作台的明确规则。原始起存与存款在入账模型中均为整分。若要模拟另一种金融产品或其他舍入方法，需要另给规则；这些虚构数字不是现实收益承诺。

## 时间线、流水与首次整期

工作台将期初存、增长、期末存逐格排列。预测余额、累计投入和0至6期内首次达标，再看精确流水。改变经过期数只重算该期余额与累计投入，不改变独立的首次达标问题；仅比较另一存入时点不替换当前模型。

标准期末模型B₄=192.82、B₅=222.102，因此首次至少200在第5期。不能在图中4、5之间插值得一个“首次整期”。若开始B₀已经达标，答案可为0。另一个不加存模型在6期内尚不足200，但第8期可以达到，窗内未达到不等于永远无法达到。

## 家庭挑战与家长核对

纸上比较同样100起存、每期存10、倍率1.1的两种理想安排。3期后期末存166.2、期初存169.51，累计投入均130，增长所得36.2和39.51。两种安排首次至少200却都在第5期，较高余额不必改变同一个整期目标答案。

家长核对完整时点与不逐期舍入假设，并让孩子指出第4期尚未达标。若切换真实入账模型，应重新逐期算，不能把无舍入闭式直接当入账结果。后续数据建模再讨论模型假设、范围与验证，而不是把一个课堂公式当作对现实的保证。

# Sequences in savings growth

Growing a balance before depositing ten differs from depositing ten before growth. A sequence model needs an initial time, ordered actions and a posting rule as well as a multiplier.

## One central idea

**Specify operation order before calculating balances, and rounding policy before interpreting money.** B₀ is defined before every first-period action. Even for start deposits it excludes the first recurring deposit. Exactly one recurring deposit occurs per completed period.

End deposits use Bₖ=qBₖ₋₁+c; start deposits use Bₖ=q(Bₖ₋₁+c). These fictional learning examples use periods rather than a real account rate and exclude taxes and fees. The ideal model retains exact values; the posting model rounds each grown balance to cents. Displaying cents does not itself mean the account was rounded each period.

### Example 1: Equal contributions, different deposit timing

Let B₀=100 NZD,q=1.1 and recurring deposit 10, without per-period rounding. End deposits give B₁=120 and B₂=142. Start deposits give B₁=(100+10)×1.1=121 and B₂=(121+10)×1.1=144.1.

Both two-period contribution totals are 100+2×10=120, while gains are 22 and 24.1. Do not add a start deposit again at the end, or count earned growth as a new contribution.

End-deposit balances are not simply geometric: B₁/B₀=6/5 differs from B₂/B₁=71/60. Instead separate contributions. The initial balance grows to B₀q^n and n end deposits contribute c(1+q+…+q^(n−1)). Start deposits each receive one additional growth period, multiplying that contribution by q. At q=1 both models become B₀+nc; at n=0 the empty deposit sum is zero and the balance remains B₀.

### Example 2: Per-period cent posting versus a rounded display

Start with 0.05 NZD, no deposits and multiplier 1.1. After two ideal periods the balance is 0.05×1.1²=0.0605 NZD. Its displayed approximation may be≈NZ$0.06 while the exact model retains 0.0605.

Under immediate per-period cent posting, period one grows to 0.055 and posts 0.06. Period two grows to 0.066 and posts 0.07. This differs from rounding only the final ideal 0.0605 to 0.06. The difference comes from operation rules rather than an arbitrary choice of numerical accuracy.

At a positive half-cent tie, this workbench rounds upward. Initial and recurring posted money must already be whole cents. Another product or rounding convention would need a separately stated rule; these classroom numbers are not real return promises.

## Timeline, ledger and first whole period

The workbench orders start deposit, growth and end deposit explicitly. Predict a balance, contributions and first target period within 0–6 before inspecting the ledger. Changing elapsed n resets the balance and contributions but does not change the separate first-target question. Comparing another timing does not replace the active model.

For standard end deposits B₄=192.82 and B₅=222.102, so the first balance at least 200 occurs at period five. Interpolating between graph points does not give a first whole period. A target already met by B₀ gives zero. A separate no-deposit model is below 200 through period six but reaches it at period eight: a bounded search without a hit does not establish impossibility forever.

## Family challenge and parent check

Compare ideal start/end arrangements with initial 100, deposits 10 and multiplier 1.1 on paper. After three periods balances are 166.2 and 169.51; contributions are 130 and gains 36.2 and 39.51. Both first reach 200 at period five, so a larger balance need not change the whole-period target result.

Check the timing and no-per-period-rounding assumptions, then request the predecessor balance still below target. Switching to cent posting requires a new periodwise calculation rather than direct substitution into the unrounded closed form. Later data modelling retrieves assumptions, scope and validation instead of treating a classroom formula as a real-world guarantee.
