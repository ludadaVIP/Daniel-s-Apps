## 不是挑一个符号，而是说清一个关系

应用题先找每个数的角色与未知量，再选择运算。把所有数字随意相加、看到“每”就一定乘，都会忽略问题真正要找的量。

- 两部分合成整体，用加法求总量。
- 整体减去已知部分，用减法找另一部分；两个可比数量的差距也可用减法。
- 已知组数与每组量，用乘法求总量。
- 已知总量与组数，用除法求每组量；已知总量与每组量，则用除法求组数。

这些关系不是机械关键词表。相同的“每盒四个”，可以用乘法求几盒总量，也可以用除法求给定总量可装几盒。

### 例 1 · 先说明单价与数量

假想本子每本 NZ$3，买四本。

1. 3 是每本的元数，4 是本数，未知是总元数。
2. 总价为 **3×4=NZ$12**。
3. 3+4 把每本价格与本数直接相加，不表达总价关系。
4. 检查四个三元合成十二元，单位回到问题中的元。

### 例 2 · 先求成本，再求余额

有假想预算 NZ$30，材料每套 NZ$6，买四套。

1. 每套价钱与套数形成等组，成本为 **6×4=NZ$24**。
2. 预算作为整体，由成本与余额组成。
3. 余额为 **30−24=NZ$6**。
4. 检查 24+6=30。不能直接从三十元减去四套；套数不是一个元数部分。

### 让单位帮忙检查关系

五袋每袋七个橙子，组数五与每袋量七相乘，得到三十五个橙子。二十八个烤饼每盒四个，则给出了总量和每盒量，求的是七盒；不能因为两个问题都有“每”就都乘。

三十米绳子用掉十二米，剩十八米，18+12=30 米。两段已走距离七千米与八千米合成十五千米；如果在问两段差距，才是一个千米。

遇到多一步的问题，可以先画一张关系图，把一个中间数量找出来，再把它放入下一条关系。每一步写清单位，不必一次把所有数字塞进长算式，也不用靠猜运算顺序。

工作台先显示已知量与未知量，让你选择运算。选错时解释数量角色，暂不暴露最后得数；选对后看相应图和反向检查。切换故事后重新判断，不能把上一题的符号当成下一题的规则。

**家庭挑战：** 用纸卡设计假想新西兰文具采购，预算 NZ$30。自己选择两种物品的整数单价与数量，画等组，计算成本、合计与余额；再把卡片装袋，说明是在求每袋量还是袋数，保留余量。不要真的购买，也不计分。

## Explain the relationship rather than guess a symbol

Identify each number's role and the unknown before choosing an operation. Adding every number arbitrarily or multiplying whenever “each” appears overlooks the quantity being sought.

- Two parts combine into a whole: addition finds the total.
- Whole minus known part finds another part; subtraction can also find a comparison difference.
- Known group count and amount per group use multiplication for the total.
- Known total and group count use division for amount per group; known total and amount per group use division for group count.

These are relationships, not a keyword table. The same phrase “four per box” may use multiplication to find the total in several boxes or division to find box count for a given total.

### Example 1 · Identify price and count

Imaginary notebooks cost NZ$3 each; buy four.

1. Three is dollars per book, four is book count, and total dollars is unknown.
2. Total price is **3×4=NZ$12**.
3. Adding 3+4 directly mixes price per book with book count and does not describe total cost.
4. Four equal three-dollar amounts make twelve dollars, returning to the unit requested.

### Example 2 · Find cost before balance

An imaginary NZ$30 budget buys four kits at NZ$6 each.

1. Unit price and kit count form equal groups: cost is **6×4=NZ$24**.
2. The budget is a whole comprising cost and balance.
3. Balance is **30−24=NZ$6**.
4. Check 24+6=30. Four kits cannot be subtracted directly from thirty dollars; kit count is not a dollar part.

### Use units to check relationships

Five bags with seven oranges each multiply group count by amount per group, giving thirty-five oranges. Twenty-eight scones packed four per box instead provide total and group size; the unknown is seven boxes. Both contain “each”, but they need different operations.

Using twelve metres from thirty leaves eighteen, checked by 18+12=30 metres. Walking seven kilometres and then eight gives fifteen altogether; asking for their difference would instead give one kilometre.

For a two-step problem, draw a relationship diagram and find an intermediate quantity before using it in the next relationship. Keep each step's unit. There is no need to put every number into one long expression or guess an order without understanding.

The workshop presents known and unknown quantities before operation selection. A mismatch explains roles without revealing the final result; a matching operation reveals a model and check. Reconsider each new story rather than reuse the preceding symbol automatically.

**Family challenge:** use cards for an imaginary New Zealand stationery purchase with an NZ$30 budget. Choose whole-dollar prices and counts for two items, draw groups and calculate each cost, combined cost and balance. Then pack cards into bags and distinguish amount per bag from bag count, retaining leftovers. No actual purchase or score is needed.
