# 运动中的速度与加速度

位置说在哪里，速度说位置怎样变，加速度说速度怎样变。本课把两阶导数与方向、速率、转向连起来，再用转向分段计算路程。

## 方向和大小分开

直线运动位置s(t)以米计，时间t以秒计，则v=s′单位米/秒，a=v′单位米/秒²。速度有正负方向，速率是|v|，永不为负。非零v处，速率导数为sign(v)a，所以v和a同号时加速、异号时减速。

v=0只表示该瞬间静止，是否转向须查左右符号。a=0只是一瞬间条件，不能推出一整段匀速。对区间端点，用所给运动区间内的单侧变化解释；本例多项式可延伸到附近，但报告仍限定实际给定时间。

### 例1：回程使路程大于位移大小

s(t)=t³−6t²+9t=t(t−3)²，0≤t≤4。v=3t²−12t+9=3(t−1)(t−3)，a=6t−12。

速度在(0,1)正、(1,3)负、(3,4)正；1和3秒前后都变号，是转向。位置s0=0、s1=4、s3=0、s4=4。位移s4−s0=4米，而路程|4−0|+|0−4|+|4−0|=12米。

平均速度为位移/时间=1米/秒，平均速率为路程/时间=3米/秒。只算|s4−s0|得到4，会漏掉往返。分段理由来自v符号，而不是随意将时间划成几个均匀小格。

### 例2：负速度与零加速度各说明什么

t=0时v9、a−12，速度与加速度异号，速率正从9减小。负加速度不表示“向负方向运动”；方向由速度决定。

t=2时v−3、a0，向负方向以速率3运动，速率在该瞬间导数0。在(1,2)速度负、加速度负，速率增大；在(2,3)速度负、加速度正，速率减小。因此a2=0并不表示整个1–3秒匀速。

将位置全部乘−1，速度与加速度都反号，但速率、路程和加速/减速分类不变。位置加10米，只改坐标原点，速度、加速度、位移和路程都不变；这可核查自己有没有把位置值误当变化率。

## 瞬时静止不一定转向

另一个位置函数s=t³在0有v=3t²=0，但左右速度都非负，所以通过原点时不转向。这是“v=0必转向”的反例。本例1与3秒能够判转向，是因为已检查两侧符号，不是只因为解出了零点。

工作台一次只画位置、速度或加速度中的一种，纵轴单位随之改变；不能把米、米/秒、米/秒²当同一物理量叠在一轴上随意比较大小。精确表保留选中时刻数据，路程表保留各段有向位移与绝对长度。切视图和平移位置不清除仍有效的预测；变所选时刻也不改变全段路程任务。

出口说明应含导数、单位、符号区间、转向判断、分段路程与位移，并区分瞬间结论和整段结论。家长核验这些联系，不能只以12这个总数或几个运动术语自动认定理解。

# Velocity and acceleration

Position says where an object is, velocity how position changes, and acceleration how velocity changes. Connect two derivatives to direction, speed and reversals, then use reversals to calculate distance.

## Separate direction from magnitude

If straight-line position s(t) is in metres and time in seconds, v=s′ has units m/s and a=v′ has units m/s². Velocity is signed; speed is|v| and never negative. Where v≠0, the derivative of speed is sign(v)a. Matching nonzero velocity and acceleration signs increase speed; opposite signs decrease it.

Zero velocity means rest at an instant, with reversal requiring a sign check on both sides. Zero acceleration at an instant does not establish constant velocity over an interval. At an interval endpoint interpret changes from the available side. This polynomial can extend beyond the interval, but the motion report remains limited to the stated times.

### Example 1: Return travel adds distance

Take s(t)=t³−6t²+9t=t(t−3)² on0≤t≤4. Then v=3(t−1)(t−3) and a=6t−12.

Velocity is positive on(0,1), negative on(1,3), positive on(3,4). Signs change at one and three, establishing reversals. Positions are s0=0,s1=4,s3=0,s4=4. Displacement is four metres; distance is|4−0|+|0−4|+|4−0|=12 metres.

Average velocity is displacement divided by four seconds, one m/s. Average speed is distance divided by four, three m/s. The expression|s4−s0| misses return travel. Splitting follows velocity signs, not arbitrary equal time steps.

### Example 2: Negative velocity and zero acceleration

At zero, v9 and a−12 have opposite signs, so speed decreases from nine. Negative acceleration does not say that motion is negative; velocity determines direction.

At two, velocity−3 and acceleration zero describe negative-direction motion at speed three with an instantaneous zero speed derivative. On(1,2), velocity and acceleration are both negative and speed increases. On(2,3), velocity is negative and acceleration positive, so speed decreases. Zero acceleration at two therefore does not make the whole1–3 interval constant-speed motion.

Multiplying every position by−1 reverses both velocity and acceleration, preserving speed, distance and the speeding/slowing classification. Adding ten metres shifts the origin and changes neither derivative nor displacement or distance. These transformations check whether position has been mistaken for rate.

## Instantaneous rest need not reverse direction

For s=t³, velocity3t² is zero at zero but nonnegative on both sides, so there is no reversal. This refutes “v=0 always means reversal”. The selected motion reverses at one and three because signs actually change, not merely because a zero was found.

The workbench plots one of position, velocity and acceleration at a time, changing vertical units accordingly. Metres, m/s and m/s² are different quantities and should not be compared as if they shared one physical axis. Exact tables preserve the selected values and each segment’s signed displacement and absolute distance. Switching plots or translating position retains unchanged predictions; changing the selected instant does not change the whole-interval distance task.

For the exit, connect derivatives, units, sign intervals, reversals, distance and displacement, distinguishing an instantaneous condition from an interval claim. A parent reviews those connections; the number twelve or a few motion terms alone do not establish understanding.
