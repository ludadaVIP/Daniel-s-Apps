# 总体、样本与抽样误差

推断之前先回答：想了解谁，实际观察了谁？总体是问题所指的全部单位，样本是本次观察到的部分单位。参数描述固定总体，统计量由样本计算，会随抽到的单位变化。同样的数字不等于同一个单位，编号身份必须保留。

## 用已知真值的演示理解未知参数

设六个虚构记录的等待时间为：A1、A2、A3各0分钟，B1、B2、B3各6分钟。目标始终是全部六个编号，均值μ=(0+0+0+6+6+6)/6=3分钟。我们公开真值只是为核查误差；真实抽样研究通常正因为不知道总体参数才需要推断。

一次样本均值记作x̄，带符号抽样误差为x̄−μ。它不是测量错误的同义词：即使每个数都测量准确，随机抽到不同人也会有变异。设计偏差则是指定抽样设计下E(X̄)−μ，是跨所有可能样本的概念。

### 例1：同一总体，两次不同误差

抽到A1、A2，样本均值0，误差0−3=−3分钟，表示低估。抽到A1、B1，样本均值(0+6)/2=3，误差0。两个样本都来自相同总体，但本次误差不同。

后一个样本碰巧准确，不足以证明任意抽样方法都好。比如专门挑选一位A、一位B，也会在这个特定数值模型中恰好均值3；这不自动给所有测量目标或别的总体一个无偏保证。评价设计要检查它如何赋予样本概率，而不能只看一次数字接近。

完整六编号简单随机抽二人，每个二人子集等可能，样本均值的期望为3，设计无偏。然而抽到两个A仍可能低估，抽到两个B仍可能高估。无偏不要求每一个样本都等于真值。

### 例2：框内普查不等于目标总体普查

若抽样框只收录A1、A2、A3，B组三人没有机会进入样本。无论抽一位、两位还是全部三位，样本均值都为0，所以此设计针对全部六人的均值偏差为−3。

把A框中的人数由1增至3可以消除框内的随机变异，却不能把遗漏的B组补回来。相反，如果观察全部六个固定记录，只有一个完整样本，抽样变异为0。但真实研究仍可能有测量、录入、非响应或目标定义问题，不能把“普查”当作一切错误都消失。

## 参数、统计量与设计各占一层

工作台把六张卡保持为六个不同编号。选择抽样框、每次人数和本次子集，先预测目标均值、本次均值与带符号误差，再打开设计期望、偏差与方差核验。即使数值重复，也不把两张卡合并为一个人。

随机抽样与随机分配也不同：前者决定谁被观察，后者决定已参加者接受什么处理。代表性、推广范围与因果性各有设计依据，不能由一个准确均值包办。

家长可以追问谁被遗漏、哪个量在重复抽样时变化。出口解释要区分一次误差与设计偏差，并指出演示中知道μ只是核验工具。真正报告应写清目标总体、抽样框、样本来源、统计量和剩余不确定性。

# Population, sample and sampling error

Inference begins by identifying the target population and the units actually observed. A parameter describes the fixed population. A statistic is calculated from a sample and can vary between samples. Equal numerical measurements do not turn distinct IDs into one person.

## Reveal truth only to audit the demonstration

Consider six fictional waiting-time records: A1, A2, A3 each have zero minutes, and B1, B2, B3 each have six. The target remains all six IDs, with population mean three minutes. We reveal this truth to check errors. In real research, the parameter is usually unknown and motivates the inference.

For a realised mean x̄, signed sampling error is x̄−μ. It can occur even when every measurement is accurate. Design bias is E(X̄)−μ under a specified sampling procedure, a property across possible samples rather than one draw.

### Example 1: Two samples, different errors

Sample A1, A2 has mean zero and error −3 minutes, an underestimate. Sample A1, B1 has mean three and zero error. A single accurate result does not certify every design.

Deliberately selecting one A and one B also gives mean three in this particular numerical example. That coincidence does not guarantee unbiasedness for other targets or populations. Evaluate how the design assigns probabilities to samples rather than judging only one close result.

Under full-frame simple random sampling of two IDs, all two-person subsets are equally likely and the expected mean is three. The design is unbiased, yet an all-A sample can underestimate and an all-B sample overestimate. Unbiasedness does not promise an exact answer every time.

### Example 2: A frame census can miss the target

If the frame includes only A1, A2, A3, every B member has zero selection chance. Sampling one, two or all three A members always gives mean zero, producing bias −3 against the six-person target.

Increasing sample size within that restricted frame removes neither the omitted group nor the bias. Observing all six fixed records instead leaves one complete sample with zero sampling variability. A real census can still suffer measurement, recording, nonresponse or definition problems; eliminating sampling variation is not eliminating every error source.

## Keep the inferential levels separate

The workbench retains all six cards as distinct units. Choose a frame, sample size and subset, then predict the population mean, sample mean and signed error. Its audit separately reports design expectation, bias and variance.

Random sampling differs from random assignment: one selects participants, the other allocates treatments among participants. Population generalisation and causal conclusions need their respective design support. One accurate-looking mean cannot establish both.

Explain who was excluded and what varies across repeated samples. State that known truth is a demonstration device, then report the actual target, frame, sample source, statistic and remaining uncertainty. Distinguishing one error from design bias is the central task.
