## 忘记一个事实，可以从已知事实出发

乘法事实值得熟悉，但一时想不起并不等于无法思考。可以把一个因数拆成两个容易计算的量，仍然数同一批物体。关键是另一个因数必须作用于两个部分。

在等行阵列里，行数不变，每行量被拆成左、右两部分。分界线移动不会增加或删去物体，两块的总量之和必须恢复整个阵列。这给出了分配策略的数量理由。

### 例 1 · 用五与三找回 6×8

1. 六行，每行八个。把每行的八拆成五与三。
2. 左块有六行，每行五个，**6×5=30**。
3. 右块仍有六行，每行三个，**6×3=18**，不是总共只有三个。
4. 合并：**6×8=6×(5+3)=30+18=48**。
5. 改成每行四与四，则 24+24 仍为 48。这是在重新分组同一总量。

### 例 2 · 从七个十补偿到七个九

1. 7×10=70 比较容易算。
2. 想要每行九个，就从每行十个里各少一个。
3. 七行共少七个，因此 **7×9=7×10−7×1=70−7=63**。
4. 不能只做 70−1，因为那只减少一个总物体，没有在七行各减少一个。
5. 也可以拆成七个五与七个四：35+28=63，作为另一个检查。

### 两部分都需要重复

6×(5+3)=6×5+3 是常见误写。右边三个是每行量，必须重复六次。只乘左块会漏掉五行的右块，33 不是同一个阵列的总量。

拆分不限定为五。4×13 可以看成四个十与四个三：40+12=52。如果问题只问“第二块 4×3 有多少”，应回答 12，而不是总量 52；读清楚未知量仍然重要。

也可把已知事实加倍。9×3=27，九行每行六个可以看成两个九行三列阵列，所以 9×6=27+27=54。加倍的是每行量，总量也加倍，行数没有同时再加倍。

零行或每行零个时，总量为零。工作台仍允许分界线在边上：一块可以为空，但两块相加仍必须等于原总量。改变每行量会改变总量；只改变分界线不会。

**小检查：** 用 8×10−8 求 8×9，并用另一种拆分核对。每个“8”在计什么，减去的八个物体来自哪里？

## Rebuild a forgotten fact from facts you know

Multiplication facts are useful, but forgetting one does not stop reasoning. Split a factor into two easier quantities while counting the same objects. The other factor must apply to both parts.

In an equal-row array, row count stays fixed while every row is split into a left and right part. Moving the cut creates or removes no objects. The two region totals must recover the original array, giving a quantity reason for distribution.

### Example 1 · Rebuild 6×8 from five and three

1. Use six rows of eight, splitting each eight into five and three.
2. The left region has six rows of five: **6×5=30**.
3. The right region still has six rows of three: **6×3=18**, not just three in total.
4. Combine: **6×8=6×(5+3)=30+18=48**.
5. Splitting each row into four and four instead gives 24+24=48. This is regrouping the same total.

### Example 2 · Compensate from seven tens to seven nines

1. The known fact 7×10=70 is easy to use.
2. To get nine per row, remove one from each row of ten.
3. Seven rows lose seven objects, so **7×9=7×10−7×1=70−7=63**.
4. Subtracting only 70−1 removes one object in total, rather than one from every row.
5. Another check splits into seven fives and seven fours: 35+28=63.

### Both partial amounts repeat

Writing 6×(5+3)=6×5+3 fails because three is an amount per row and must repeat six times. Multiplying only the left region omits five rows of the right region. Thirty-three is not the total of the original array.

Five is not the only useful split. For 4×13, use four tens and four threes: 40+12=52. If the task asks only for the second region 4×3, answer 12 rather than total 52. Identifying the unknown still matters.

Doubling a known fact is another strategy. Given 9×3=27, nine rows of six can be formed from two nine-by-three arrays. Thus 9×6=27+27=54. The amount per row doubles, and so does the total; the row count does not also double.

Zero rows or zero per row give zero total. The workshop permits a cut at the edge, creating an empty region whose zero still combines correctly with the other region. Changing objects per row changes total; moving only the cut does not.

**Quick check:** calculate 8×9 using 8×10−8, then check with another decomposition. Explain what the eights count and where the removed objects come from.
