# 指数定律与负指数

想象一张图每一步放大为原来的2倍。向前一步乘2，向后一步就应除以2。指数不只是“写了几次乘法”，还可以记录这种可逆的步数。

## 一条核心想法

**对非零底数，指数减少1，幂值除以一次底数。** 从2³=8开始，向左依次得到2²=4、2¹=2、2⁰=1、2⁻¹=1/2。负指数表示倒数，绝不直接表示负的结果。

同底相乘aᵐaⁿ=a^(m+n)，同底相除aᵐ/aⁿ=a^(m−n)，幂的乘方(aᵐ)ⁿ=a^(mn)。本课m、n为整数；涉及零次或负次幂时要求a≠0。乘积的幂(ab)ⁿ=aⁿbⁿ也要保证各式有定义。这些法则来自乘除结构，不能把乘法替换成加法。

### 例1：沿数表走到负指数

求3⁴÷3⁶。可以先约去四个共同的3，剩下分母3²，所以结果1/9；也可以减指数，3^(4−6)=3⁻²=1/9。两种表示互相核验。若认为结果是−9，乘回3⁶并不能得到3⁴。

再求(2⁻²)³。三个1/4相乘为1/64；乘指数得2⁻⁶也是1/64。指数相乘的规则与同底相乘时指数相加，是两个不同结构，先看括号外到底在做什么。

### 例2：负号的位置改变问题

(−2)⁻²=1/(−2)²=1/4，因为底数是整个−2。相反，−2⁻²=−(2⁻²)=−1/4，负号没有被平方。用括号圈出底数，比背诵“负负得正”更可靠。

5⁰+5⁻¹=1+1/5=6/5，不能合成5⁻¹。0⁻¹需要除以0，所以无定义；本课也不定义0⁰。不能把aⁿ/aⁿ=1用于a=0，原来的商已经没有意义。

## 先预测，再操作

在幂数表选择2、3或1/2，先预测一个负指数值，再显示表。用向右乘、向左除解释每一格。改变底数或指数会清空对应预测；显示和隐藏表只改变你看到的信息，保留作答。

## 迁移与家长核对

纸上缩放从16 cm开始，每步减半。倒退2步是16×(1/2)⁻²=64 cm，而非负长度。出口题后再口头说明“倒退”和“倒数”的联系。家长若发现错误，先让孩子圈底数，再问哪一步在乘、哪一步在除；把括号读错和不理解倒数分开记录。下次在有理指数和增长模型中再次调用此结构。

# Exponent laws and negative powers

Imagine a picture doubled at each step. One step forwards multiplies by two; one backwards must divide by two. Exponents can record these reversible steps as well as repeated multiplication.

## One central idea

**For a nonzero base, reducing the exponent by one divides the value by the base.** Starting at 2³=8 and walking left gives 2²=4, 2¹=2, 2⁰=1 and 2⁻¹=1/2. A negative exponent specifies a reciprocal, never an instruction to make the answer negative.

For integer m and n, same-base products add exponents, aᵐaⁿ=a^(m+n); quotients subtract them, aᵐ/aⁿ=a^(m−n); and powers of powers multiply them, (aᵐ)ⁿ=a^(mn). Require a≠0 whenever zero or negative powers are involved. The product rule (ab)ⁿ=aⁿbⁿ likewise requires every expression to be defined. These laws describe multiplication and division, not addition.

### Example 1: Walk backwards through the power table

Evaluate 3⁴÷3⁶. Cancelling four common factors of three leaves 1/3²=1/9. Subtracting exponents instead gives 3^(4−6)=3⁻²=1/9. The representations audit one another. Multiplying a proposed answer of −9 back by 3⁶ would fail to recover 3⁴.

Next evaluate (2⁻²)³. Three copies of 1/4 multiply to 1/64. Multiplying exponents gives 2⁻⁶, also 1/64. Multiplying exponents in a power of a power differs from adding exponents in a same-base product. Identify the operation outside the brackets before choosing a rule.

### Example 2: Locate the minus sign

(−2)⁻²=1/(−2)²=1/4 because the complete base is −2. In contrast, −2⁻²=−(2⁻²)=−1/4: the external minus sign is not squared. Circling the base is more reliable than applying a remembered sign slogan without reading the expression.

Also, 5⁰+5⁻¹=1+1/5=6/5. It cannot be combined into 5⁻¹. Zero to power −1 would require division by zero and is undefined; this lesson also leaves 0⁰ undefined. The argument aⁿ/aⁿ=1 cannot justify a zero-base case because the original quotient is already undefined.

## Predict, then manipulate

Choose base two, three or one half in the power table. Predict a negative power before revealing the table, then explain every step using multiplication rightwards and division leftwards. Changing the mathematical base or exponent resets its prediction. Showing or hiding the table preserves your work because it only changes the display.

## Transfer and parent check

A paper scaling model starts at 16 cm and halves per step. Two steps backwards gives 16×(1/2)⁻²=64 cm, not a negative length. After the exit question, explain the link between going backwards and taking a reciprocal. A parent can ask the learner to circle the base, then identify each multiplication or division. Distinguish a bracket-reading error from uncertainty about reciprocals. Retrieve the structure again in rational exponents and growth models.
