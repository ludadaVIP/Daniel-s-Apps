## 一层一层数空间

一个积木块长 4 cm、宽 3 cm、高 2 cm。只看正面，后面可能藏着小块；只数顶面，也会漏掉下面的层。先预测每层多少颗、共几层，再用逐层位置图检查。每颗小正方体的棱长都是 1 cm，占有 **1 cm³** 的空间。

面积测一层面片覆盖多少平方单位；体积测立体占据多少立方单位。一个 1 cm² 方格只给出面，增加 1 cm 的单位高度才对应一个 1 cm³ 小正方体。立体投影有遮挡，不能把屏幕上看到的多边形面积当实际体积。

### 例 1 · 从底层到整个长方体

1. 底层沿长方向每行 **4 颗**，沿宽方向 **3 行**。
2. 一层共有 **4×3=12 颗**单位立方体。
3. 高为 **2 cm**，所以有两个 1 cm 高的完整层。
4. 总体积 **12×2=24 cm³**，即 **V=长×宽×高**。
5. 逐层图两层各十二颗，合计二十四；不是底面十二平方厘米，也不是三条棱相加的九厘米。

在工作台切换“立体示意”与“逐层看”，再勾选全部层。每格标记该层一颗立方体的位置，不是说立方体变成没有厚度的面片。换层只改变观察位置，原来的积木没有增减。更改尺寸会清空旧核对结果；先独立说层数，再点击核对。

### 例 2 · 内部尺寸才能确定容量

1. 一个理想长方体容器的**内部**长 5 cm、宽 4 cm、高 3 cm。
2. 内部每层有 **5×4=20** 个单位立方体位置，共三层。
3. 可用内部空间为 **20×3=60 cm³**。
4. 已知 **1 cm³=1 mL**，容器装满至题给内部高度时容量为 **60 mL**。
5. 若 5、4、3 cm 只是外部尺寸，壁厚或内部尺寸未知，便不能断言容量就是六十毫升。

“容积”关注可容纳的内部空间；“体积”关注指定物体或几何体占据的空间。单位能够换算，不代表对象可以互换。真实容器还可能有弧角、壁厚或未装满；这里明确使用理想长方体和装满条件，避免把简化模型当测量事实。

正方体三条相邻棱相等。棱长 3 cm 时，每层九颗、共三层，体积 **3×3×3=27 cm³**。棱长加倍为 6 cm，三个方向都加倍，体积变成八倍；不能仅因边长二倍就说体积也是二倍。

已知体积 84 cm³、长 7 cm、宽 4 cm，一层贡献 28 个单位立方体，高为 **84÷28=3 cm**。代回 **7×4×3=84 cm³**。若尺寸用 dm，体积用 dm³；**1 dm³=1 L**。内部 2×3×4 dm 的容量是 24 L，不能写 24 mL。

**带走一个发现：** 先建立一层，再计层数，把被遮挡的内部也算进去。解释题请同时指出测的是外部还是内部；家长核查对象、单位和层计数依据。出口题换成分米与升，仍用同一个空间模型推理。

## Count space one layer at a time

A block is 4 cm long, 3 cm deep and 2 cm high. A front view can hide cubes behind others; counting only the top misses lower layers. Predict cubes per layer and layer count, then inspect position maps. Each small cube has 1 cm edges and occupies **1 cm³**.

Area measures covering in square units; volume measures occupied space in cubic units. A 1 cm² face gains a 1 cm unit height to form a 1 cm³ cube. An isometric drawing has occlusion: its projected screen area is not the block’s volume.

### Example 1 · From one layer to a whole prism

1. The bottom layer has **4 cubes per row** and **3 rows**.
2. One layer contains **4×3=12 unit cubes**.
3. Height **2 cm** gives two complete layers, each 1 cm high.
4. Total volume is **12×2=24 cm³**, or **V=length×depth×height**.
5. Two layer maps each show twelve cube positions. This is neither bottom-face area twelve square centimetres nor the sum of three edge lengths.

Switch between solid and layer views, then display all layers. Each tile marks a cube’s position in that layer; it does not turn a cube into a face without thickness. Selecting another layer changes observation rather than adding or removing cubes. Changing dimensions clears the old checked result. Explain the layer count before revealing the volume.

### Example 2 · Capacity needs internal measurements

1. An ideal rectangular container has **internal** dimensions 5 cm, 4 cm and 3 cm.
2. Each internal layer has **5×4=20** unit-cube positions, with three layers.
3. Available internal space is **20×3=60 cm³**.
4. Given **1 cm³=1 mL**, capacity when filled to the stated internal height is **60 mL**.
5. If those dimensions describe only the exterior, missing wall thickness or internal measurements prevents concluding capacity is sixty millilitres.

Capacity concerns available internal space; volume concerns the specified object or geometric solid. Convertible units do not make different measurement objects interchangeable. A real container may have rounded corners, thick walls or be partly filled. Here an ideal prism and a filled-to-height condition make the model explicit.

A cube has three equal adjacent edges. With 3 cm edges, nine cubes per layer and three layers give **3×3×3=27 cm³**. Doubling every edge multiplies volume by eight, because each of the three directions contributes a factor of two.

Given volume 84 cm³, length 7 cm and depth 4 cm, a unit-height layer contributes twenty-eight cubes. Height is **84÷28=3 cm**; verify **7×4×3=84 cm³**. Decimetre dimensions give cubic decimetres, and **1 dm³=1 L**. A container with internal dimensions 2×3×4 dm has capacity 24 L, rather than 24 mL.

**Takeaway:** build one layer, count all layers and include hidden interior positions. Explain whether measurements are internal or external. A parent checks the object, units and layer reasoning; the exit ticket transfers the same spatial model to decimetres and litres.
