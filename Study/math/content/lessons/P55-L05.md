## 把未知对象和等量关系一起写清

应用题里先**设未知量**，完整写它代表的对象与单位，再列等量关系。x 不是自动表示总数；它可以是原有张数、每袋支数、每段米数或原有钱数。每个数字也有对象，不能把袋数三当作三支铅笔加到每袋支数上。

等号两边应对应同一量。例如“原有+收到=现在总量”“原有−花去=剩余”“相等组数×每组量=总量”。关系确定以后求解，再把解代回原故事，不只检查抽象符号是否像公式。

### 例 1 · 未知原有量

1. 收到 **7 张卡** 后共有 **19 张**。设原有 **x 张**。
2. 关系：原有加收到为现在，列 **x+7=19**。
3. 两边减七，得 **x=12 张**。
4. 代回故事：原来十二张，再收到七张，正好十九张。
5. 写 **7+x=19** 同样有效；交换等号两边也可。写 **x−7=19** 改成了“送出七张”的情境，不能因为也能算出一个数就接受。

### 例 2 · 未知每段长度

1. **5 m 彩带** 平均分成 **4 段**。设每段长 **x m**。
2. 段数乘每段长度是总长，列 **4x=5**；两边数值都对应米的总量。
3. 两边除四，得 **x=5/4 m=1.25 m**。
4. 代回：四段每段 1.25 m，合计 **4×1.25 m=5 m**。
5. x 是每段米数，不是段数四，也不是总长五。1.25 m 的长度合法，不把非整数强行截成一米。

### 原量、每组量与单独额外量

纸上假想花去 NZ$1.25 后剩 NZ$2.75。设原有 x 新西兰元，x−1.25=2.75，原有四元；四减去花费恢复剩余。这里只是纸上情境，不需要采购。

三袋相同铅笔共二十四支，设每袋 x 支，3x=24，x=8 支。若三袋各 x 张卡，另有两张散卡，共二十张，则关系是 **3x+2=20**。写 **3(x+2)=20** 把两张散卡放进每个袋里，重复计成六张。这一题只要求说明与列关系；两步方程将在后续进一步研究，不必跳过对象解释去套复杂程序。

蓝彩带比长 3/4 m 的红彩带多 1/4 m。设蓝长 x m，可列 x−1/4=3/4，蓝长一米；也可用“红长+多出的量=蓝长”说明。不同有依据的关系可以有效，评价不要求孩子背出唯一的一句话。

工作台先选符合故事的关系，支持等号反向或加数交换的等价写法。关系核对后才试代、选逆运算和展开解。改变故事收起旧答案与单位，不把“每袋八支”误带进“每段几米”的新问题。独立作答、自己的解释和家长复核仍由课程练习记录。

**带走一个发现：** 先定义 x 与单位，再把每一项指回故事对象。关系成立、操作合法、解代回情境成立，三步一起才可靠。

## Define the unknown together with its equality relationship

First **define the unknown** with its object and unit. x may mean original cards, pencils per bag, metres per piece or original money; it does not automatically mean a total. Known numbers also have objects. Three bags cannot be added as three pencils to a per-bag count.

Both sides should refer to the same quantity: original+received=current total; original−spent=remaining; equal-group count×each amount=total. Form the relationship, solve, then check the story rather than only recognise a familiar formula.

### Example 1 · An unknown original

1. Receive **7 cards** and now have **19**. Let the original be **x cards**.
2. Original plus received gives current total: **x+7=19**.
3. Subtract seven from both sides: **x=12 cards**.
4. Twelve original cards plus seven received restores nineteen.
5. **7+x=19** also works, as does reversing equality. **x−7=19** instead models giving away seven and does not retain this story.

### Example 2 · An unknown length per piece

1. Divide **5 m of ribbon** into **4 equal pieces**. Let each length be **x m**.
2. Piece count times each length gives total: **4x=5**.
3. Divide both sides by four: **x=5/4 m=1.25 m**.
4. Four pieces of 1.25 m restore **5 m**.
5. x is a per-piece metre length, not the piece count or total length. A fractional length is valid; do not truncate it to one metre.

### Originals, equal groups and separate extras

Imagine spending NZ$1.25 with NZ$2.75 left. Original dollars x satisfy x−1.25=2.75, giving four dollars. Substitute to recover the remainder. This paper story requires no real purchase.

Three equal bags containing twenty-four pencils give 3x=24 and eight per bag. If three bags each hold x cards, plus two loose cards, twenty altogether, write **3x+2=20**. **3(x+2)=20** repeats the two extras in every bag, giving six extras. Here only form and justify the relationship; later units develop two-step equation solving.

For blue ribbon 1/4 m longer than red ribbon of 3/4 m, define blue length x m. Then x−1/4=3/4 gives one metre. A justified red+extra=blue relationship also works. Do not require one memorised sentence when another sound explanation retains the objects.

Choose the story relationship before trying values or revealing a solution. Equivalent reversed equalities and swapped addends are accepted. Changing story clears the old answer and unit, so eight pencils per bag does not leak into a metre-length task. Independent answers and parent-reviewed explanations are recorded through the lesson practice.

**Takeaway:** define x and its unit, connect every term to the story, use a valid operation and substitute into the original context.
