# 波动与离散程度

## 中心相同，分布仍能不同

“通常多大”与“分散多远”是两个问题。中位数给排序中心，不能说明每条都接近它。比较分布时先核变量含义与单位，再用共同刻度画图，说明有效条数和四分位数约定。本课沿用奇数排除整体中位数、最小–最大须的规则。

极差是最大减最小，反映整个观测跨度；IQR 是 Q3−Q1，反映排序中间部分的跨度。它们都带原变量的单位，不是人数，也不是“好坏”的通用分数。“更稳定”必须说清根据哪个指标、在哪一批记录中。某活动距离更大是否更好，也取决于实际问题，不能由图自动决定。

### 例 1：两个中位数五

虚构 A=4、4、5、5、5、5、6、6、6，B=0、1、2、4、5、6、8、9、10，均为九条同单位观察。两组中位数都是第五条五。

A 下半 4、4、5、5 的中位数是 9/2，上半 5、6、6、6 的中位数六，所以 IQR=3/2；全体极差 6−4=2。B 下半 0、1、2、4 给 Q1=3/2，上半 6、8、9、10 给 Q3=17/2，所以 IQR=7，极差十。

这批记录里，A 按极差与 IQR 都比 B 集中，但不能说“A 每条都等于五”，也不能从九条记录证明所有未来情况。数轴必须共同标明单位与范围；给 A 单独放大、给 B 单独缩小，会让两个箱看起来差不多宽而掩盖数值差异。

### 例 2：只改一个尾值，两个指标给不同结论

保留 A9 编号，将它的六改成三十，其他八条不变：4、4、5、5、5、5、6、6、30。中位数仍五，极差变成 30−4=26。下半仍 4、4、5、5，上半现在是 5、6、6、30；两个中间值仍六与六，所以 Q3 仍六，IQR 仍 3/2。

与 B 比，A 现在全体跨度更大，中央跨度却更小。准确表述是“按极差 A 更散，按 IQR A 中间部分更集中”，不要强求所有指标给同一排名。IQR 对本例极端尾值变化不敏感，不等于它永远不受异常或多条变化影响。五个值 4、5、5、5、30 的上半只有 5、30，Q3 就是 35/2；小样本的分半边界会改变影响方式。

## 异常值先查来源

数值大不自动意味着错误。应核原记录、单位、测量条件与转录过程。如果原卡确认三十，就保留并讨论它对结果的影响；若原卡明写三而录成三十，可有依据纠正，并记录旧值、新值、编号和原因。不能删到图形符合自己希望的结论，也不能悄悄改变样本条数。

把所有记录同时加四，只平移分布：最大与最小都加四，极差不变；Q1/Q3 同加四，IQR 也不变。若同单位正倍数缩放，比如全部乘二，两个跨度都乘二。比较单位不同的数据前，先作共同单位换算，不把 cm 的数字直接与 m 的数字比大小。

探索区可以保留同一条身份改变尾值、切换共同数轴并揭示统计量。改视窗不清答案，换原记录清旧结论。家长应听孩子同时说“中心、哪个跨度、依据哪批数据”，而不是只接受一句“这个更稳定”。

# Variability and spread

## Equal centres do not fix a distribution

Typical size and variability answer different questions. A median does not say every value lies close to it. Before comparing groups, check variable meaning, units, observed counts and the quartile convention. Use a common numerical scale. This lesson excludes an odd middle observation and uses min–max whiskers.

Range=max−min describes the full observed span; IQR=Q3−Q1 describes a central span. Both retain the original unit. Neither is a count or universal quality score. Define “more consistent” by a measure and by the records being described. Whether a larger distance is desirable depends on the question.

### Example 1: two medians of five

Fictional A=4,4,5,5,5,5,6,6,6 and B=0,1,2,4,5,6,8,9,10 each contain nine observations in the same units. Both medians are five.

A has lower-half median 9/2 and upper-half median six, so IQR=3/2; range=6−4=2. B has Q1=3/2 and Q3=17/2, so IQR=7; range=10. A is tighter by both measures in these records. This does not make every A value five or establish all future behaviour.

Use the same labelled axis for both boxes. Independently enlarging A and shrinking B can hide their numerical difference behind similar pixel widths.

### Example 2: one tail change, two comparisons

Retain record A9 but change its six to thirty: 4,4,5,5,5,5,6,6,30. The median remains five and range becomes 26. The lower half is unchanged; upper half 5,6,6,30 still has median six. IQR remains 3/2.

A now spans farther than B overall but has a smaller central span. Say “A has greater range but smaller IQR” rather than demanding a universal consistency ranking. Resistance to this particular tail change does not make IQR immune to unusual records. With only 4,5,5,5,30, the upper half 5,30 has median 35/2; small-sample boundaries change how extremes matter.

## Check sources before changing unusual records

Large does not automatically mean wrong. Check source, units, conditions and transcription. If a source verifies thirty, retain and discuss it. If it documents three but the entry says thirty, correct with a log of identity, old/new values and evidence. Do not delete until the preferred conclusion appears or silently change n.

Adding four to every observation translates the distribution. Extrema and quartiles all gain four, leaving both differences unchanged. Multiplying by a positive factor multiplies both spreads by that factor. Convert units consistently before comparing cm with m.

The workshop retains identities when a tail record changes. Window changes preserve writing; changed observations reset the old task. A parent listens for centre, named spread measure and record scope, not only “this is more stable”.
