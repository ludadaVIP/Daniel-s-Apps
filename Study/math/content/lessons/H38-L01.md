# 建立现实问题的数学模型

本课把一个现实问题的简化版本写成可核验的模型：雨水箱能否每天供应校园花园所需的水？全部数字均为虚构教学资料，不是实测或采购建议。主要目标是把变量、单位、先后顺序和限制写清楚，而不是一开始就寻找最优答案。

## 从文字到逐日规则

设屋顶面积A，单位m²；日雨量r，单位mm；收集率η，无量纲；水箱容量C和存水s，单位L。1mm深的水铺在1m²面积上是0.001m³，也就是1L，所以当天入箱前收集量I=Aηr。取A=20、η=3/4时，I=15r。这个公式假定收集率固定，且量的是入箱前收集量，尚未扣水箱溢流。

本题初始存水0，五天雨量[6,0,0,6,0]mm，每天需求30L，无蒸发和备用水，先收雨再用水。每天先把s+I限制到容量：b=min(C,s+I)，溢流为s+I−b；实际供水q=min(30,b)，日末存水b−q，未满足需求30−q。每个量都非负，不能把−10L存水解释成已经供出了不存在的水。

### 例1：容量上限为什么要先应用

取C=60L。首日6mm雨产生20×3/4×6=90L，初始0，最多存60，因此溢出30L。再供当天30L，日末30L。若先从90减掉需求再考虑容量，会误以为没有溢流；那相当于改成边用水边收雨或先用后收，已不是题设顺序。

次日无雨，从30供30，日末0；第三天仍无雨，只能供0，缺30。第四天又收90，溢30、供30、余30，第五天供30、余0。因此五天共收集180L、总需求150L，仍有第三天缺水。先后与容量不能被总量不等式替代。

### 例2：改变参数，保留规则

把C改90L，首日收90无需溢流，供30后余60；接着余30、0，第四天再余60，第五天余30，每天都满足需求。提高容量在固定其他条件下可以减少早期溢流，让水跨天储存。

若面积改10m²，收集率仍3/4，首日只收45L。即使容量90，首日供30后仅余15，第二天便缺15，第三天缺30。大水箱不能创造未收集到的水。收集率0时进水全0，容量0时也没有可供存水；画水位时应单独处理0容量，不能除以0。

## 模型不是资料本身

工作台可以改变面积、效率、容量与查看日期。先预测进水、溢流和日末存水，再打开完整五天账本。图上水位对应日末而非进水后的最高水位；图示长度只是辅助，精确水量用账本核验。

现实使用还需检查雨量、有效屋顶面积、管道损失、时间分辨率、蒸发、漏水、维护和需求变化。若收集与用水同时发生，要改用更细时间步或另立顺序；不能只把公式换个数字而忽略原假设。解释哪些量是给定、哪些是选择、哪些是算出，也是建模的一部分。

出口要求用变量、单位、规则、前三天账本解释总量与逐日可用量的不同，由人审核。家长可问“毫米乘平方米怎么变成升”和“先收后用在哪一步体现”；若仍混淆，先回到分段规则和任务依赖，逐步列量，不急着优化。

# Building a real-world mathematical model

Model a simplified practical question: can a rainwater tank meet a school garden’s daily demand? All numbers are fictional teaching data, not measurements or purchasing advice. The central task is to state variables, units, order and constraints before searching for an optimum.

## Translate the story into daily rules

Let roof area A be in m², rainfall r in mm, capture fraction η be dimensionless, and capacity C and storage s be in litres. One millimetre over one square metre is 0.001 m³, or one litre. Collected inflow before storage is therefore I=Aηr. With A=20 and η=3/4, this becomes I=15r. Fixed capture efficiency is an assumption; tank overflow has not yet been deducted.

Initial storage is zero. Five-day rainfall is [6,0,0,6,0] mm, daily demand is 30 L, and there is no evaporation or backup supply. Collect before use. First set b=min(C,s+I), spilling s+I−b. Then serve q=min(30,b), close with b−q and record unmet demand 30−q. These quantities stay nonnegative. Negative storage cannot represent water that was actually supplied.

### Example 1: Apply capacity before demand

At C=60 L, day one collects 20×3/4×6=90 L. Starting empty, the tank stores sixty and spills thirty. Serving demand then leaves thirty. Subtracting demand from the inflow before applying capacity would falsely remove that spill; it would describe another timing model, not the stated collection-before-use rule.

Day two has no rain and uses the remaining thirty, closing at zero. Day three also has no rain, serves zero and has deficit thirty. Day four collects ninety, spills thirty, serves thirty and retains thirty; day five uses that remainder. Total inflow is 180 L against total demand 150 L, yet day three is short. Capacity and timing cannot be replaced by a total-volume inequality.

### Example 2: Change parameters while retaining the rules

At C=90 L, day one stores all ninety and closes at sixty. Subsequent closing stores are thirty, zero, sixty and thirty. Every demand is met. Greater capacity can reduce early spill and carry water between days when the other inputs remain fixed.

Now halve the roof area to ten square metres, keeping capture fraction 3/4. The first inflow is only 45 L. Even a 90 L tank closes day one at fifteen, leaving a fifteen-litre deficit on day two and thirty on day three. A larger tank cannot create water that never arrived. Zero efficiency gives zero inflow; zero capacity stores nothing and must not be used as a denominator for a water-level proportion.

## A model differs from its input data

Change area, capture fraction, capacity and inspected day in the workbench. Predict inflow, overflow and closing storage before revealing the five-day ledger. The drawing shows end-of-day water, not the peak immediately after collection; verify exact amounts using the table.

Real use would require evidence about rainfall, effective roof area, pipe losses, time resolution, evaporation, leakage, maintenance and demand. Simultaneous collection and use may require smaller time steps or a different timing rule. Identify given quantities, decisions and calculated outputs. The exit explains units, rules and the first three days for human review. Ask how millimetres and square metres produce litres and where collection-before-use appears. Return to piecewise rules and task dependencies if the learner is skipping intermediate quantities.
