## 先说一整组，再写式子

代数式可以记录一个数量关系，而不急着为字母指定数值。本课 n 表示每袋圆点数时允许非负整数；若题目说“一个数 n”，则按纯数理解，不能把物品个数的限制强加到所有式子。读情境时先找**每组、组数、额外量和先后顺序**，再写运算。

括号表示需要作为整体处理的一组。**3(n+2)** 有三份“n 加二”；**3n+2** 则是三份 n 之后总共加一次二。两个式子外观很近，却表达不同构造。

### 例 1 · 每袋两个和袋外总共两个

1. 三袋，每袋 n 个圆点和两个方块。一袋总数是 **n+2**。
2. 三袋重复这个整体，得到 **3(n+2)**。
3. 展开为三份 n 加三份二，即 **3n+6**；这两种写法等值。
4. 换情境：三袋各 n 个，所有袋外另放两个，式子为 **3n+2**。
5. 用 n=0 检查固定量：前者六，后者二；空袋不代表额外方块也消失。

画图时圆点随 n 改变，带加号的方块表示固定量。n=0 时仍保留三个组框，帮助看清“每组两个”在三组出现了三次。写式子题不应只填当前图的总数十五，因为改变 n 后十五不再描述结构。

### 例 2 · 少四、减去与一半的顺序

1. “两个相同数，每个比 n 少四”，每个数是 **n−4**。
2. 两个相加为 **2(n−4)=2n−8**，不是只少一次四的 2n−4。
3. “从十减去 n”写 **10−n**；n−10 是从 n 减十，顺序不同。
4. “n 的一半再加三”写 **n/2+3**，也可写 **(n+6)/2**。
5. **(n+3)/2** 是先加三再折半，和前一个要求不同。用 n=2 检查，前者四，后者二点五。

一般等值必须在所有允许输入下成立。重复加法可说明 **3(n+2)=3n+6**，分配或合并同类项也要保持每项。只试一个输入不足以证明：2n 与 n+1 在 n=1 都为二，到 n=2 就变成四和三。一次不符能排除通用等值，一次符合不能确认。

答案框可写 `3(n+2)`、`3*n+6` 或 `n+n+n+6` 等值形式，支持括号、加减、乘常数和除非零常数。本课要求一个字母的一次式；写清指定字母，不加等号，不用某个数值代替通式。错误反馈后，先查每组固定量出现几次，再修改。

家长请孩子把同一组纸片改成“额外两个在袋内”和“额外两个在袋外”，每改一次都口述一个通用式，并用零输入检查固定量。带走一个发现：**式子由结构决定，括号保护整体，等值写法不只一种。**

## Describe one whole group before writing the expression

An expression records how quantities relate without immediately assigning a value to the letter. If n counts circles per bag, allow nonnegative whole numbers. If it simply denotes a number, follow that numerical domain instead. Identify each group, the number of groups, extras and operation order before translating the story.

Brackets protect a whole group. **3(n+2)** contains three copies of “n plus two.” **3n+2** contains three copies of n, followed by two added once overall. Similar-looking notation can encode different constructions.

### Example 1 · Two per bag versus two outside overall

1. Three bags each contain n circles and two squares. One bag holds **n+2**.
2. Repeat that whole group three times: **3(n+2)**.
3. Three copies of n and three copies of two give **3n+6**, an equivalent expression.
4. Change the story to three bags of n with two extras outside all bags: **3n+2**.
5. At n=0, their fixed totals are six and two. Empty circle counts do not remove the fixed squares.

Circles change with n while plus-marked squares show extras. At zero retain all three group boxes, making three occurrences of the fixed two visible. Entering fifteen, the current diagram’s total, cannot describe the construction after n changes. An expression-writing question requires a general expression.

### Example 2 · Four less, subtraction order and taking half

1. Two equal numbers are each four less than n, so each is **n−4**.
2. Their sum is **2(n−4)=2n−8**, not 2n−4, which removes four only once.
3. Subtracting n from ten gives **10−n**. Reversing the order to n−10 changes the instruction.
4. Half of n, then add three, gives **n/2+3**, also writable as **(n+6)/2**.
5. **(n+3)/2** instead adds three before taking half. At n=2 these give four and two-and-a-half.

General equivalence must hold for every allowed input. Repeated addition explains **3(n+2)=3n+6**; distribution must retain every term. One numerical trial is insufficient: 2n and n+1 both give two at n=1 but give four and three at n=2. A failing trial rejects a universal claim, whereas one successful trial does not establish it.

The answer box accepts equivalent forms such as `3(n+2)`, `3*n+6` and `n+n+n+6`. It supports brackets, sums, products by constants and division by nonzero constants. These lessons ask for a linear expression in the named letter; omit an equal sign and avoid replacing the rule by a current numerical total. After feedback, count how many times each fixed quantity occurs before revising. For a family activity, move paper extras inside each bag or outside all bags and describe the corresponding expression each time. Use zero to inspect the fixed total. Structure determines the expression, brackets preserve groups, and equivalent notation can take several valid forms.
