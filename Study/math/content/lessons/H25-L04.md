# 导数作为瞬时变化率

一个区间能有平均速度，一个时刻又怎样谈速度？答案不是把区间长度设为0后相除，而是研究非零区间越来越短时，平均变化率是否从两侧趋同一有限值。本课把这个极限叫作该点导数f′(a)。

## 值、平均率、瞬时率

f(a)是高度或状态，差商[f(a+h)−f(a)]/h是当前区间平均率，f′(a)是h→0时差商的共同有限极限。三个对象需要分别计算。某次数值碰巧相同，并不使定义相同。

位移s(t)的导数是有符号瞬时速度，单位为位移单位/时间单位。速度为负表示沿负方向；速率是速度的绝对值，不能在尚未说明求什么量时先丢符号。工作台的时间以参照时刻为0，负输入表示参照时刻之前，不表示负的时长。

本课讨论有限双侧导数。若左右差商趋向不同数，就没有这个导数；连续函数也可能如此。不能把左右斜率平均一下，或仅看画面哪边更像直线来决定。

### 例1：平方位移的有符号速度

s(t)=t²米，在t=2秒，非零h的差商为4+h，极限为4米/秒。若h=1，当前平均速度是5米/秒，不能把它说成该时刻的精确速度。

在相对时间t=−1，速度2t=−2米/秒，对应速率2米/秒。若给位移加4米，所有位置上移，但两个时刻相减时常数抵消，瞬时速度不变。原函数的高度与导数斜率是不同量，即使t=2时两个数恰好都为4。

### 例2：连续尖点与水平穿越

f(x)=|x|在0的点值是0，左右函数值均趋0，因此连续。差商却是|h|/h：h>0时为1，h<0时为−1。两侧不一致，所以f′(0)未定义。这里缺的是共同变化率，不是函数值。

g(x)=x³在0的差商h²从两侧趋0，导数0、切线水平。左侧函数值负，右侧正，曲线穿过水平线，并没有在此处达到局部最大或最小。单点导数0既不能推出函数恒定，也不能单独保证极值；后续函数分析会补足判据。

## 工作台与出口

选择平方、三次或绝对值，固定a后移动非零h。金线为当前割线，绿线为存在时的有限导数切线。改变h只要不改变导数答案，就保留该预测；在|x|的0处不绘制假切线，h=0也不绘制假割线。

核验表同时给左右差商。对平方和三次，使用展开式解释极限；对绝对值尖点，用分段差商明确反驳可导性。有限图像与表格只辅助理解，不能以“线很平滑”代替规则。

出口比较两种函数在0的值极限与差商极限。家长可分别问“这里连续吗？”“这里可导吗？”，要求用不同的量回答，而不是把两句话当作同义词。

# Derivative as instantaneous rate

An interval has an average velocity, but how can an instant have a velocity? Do not divide after setting the interval length to zero. Investigate whether averages over nonzero shrinking intervals approach the same finite number from both sides. That limit is the derivative f′(a).

## Value, average rate and instantaneous rate

The value f(a) is a height or state. The quotient [f(a+h)−f(a)]/h is a current interval average. The derivative is its common finite limit as h approaches zero. Calculate these separately; occasional numerical agreement does not make their definitions interchangeable.

For position s(t), the derivative is signed instantaneous velocity with position units per time unit. A negative velocity indicates direction; speed is its absolute magnitude. Do not discard the sign before identifying the requested quantity. Workbench time is relative to a reference instant, so a negative input means before that instant rather than a negative elapsed duration.

We study finite two-sided derivatives. Unequal limiting quotient values prevent such a derivative even for a continuous function. Averaging the slopes or judging which side looks straighter does not resolve disagreement.

### Example 1: Signed velocity for square position

For s(t)=t² metres at t=2 seconds, the quotient is 4+h and its limit is 4 m/s. At h=1, the interval average is 5 m/s, not the exact velocity at that instant.

At relative time t=−1 the velocity is −2 m/s and speed is 2 m/s. Adding four metres to the position rule translates all positions but cancels in differences, leaving velocity unchanged. Function height differs from derivative slope, even though both happen to equal four at t=2 for the unshifted square rule.

### Example 2: A continuous corner and a horizontal crossing

For |x| at zero, the point value and both limiting values are zero, so the function is continuous. Its quotient |h|/h is one for positive h and negative one for negative h. There is no common finite derivative. The missing object is the agreed rate, not the function value.

For x³ at zero, the quotient h² tends to zero from both sides, giving a horizontal tangent. Function values are negative on the left and positive on the right: the curve crosses the horizontal line rather than attaining a local maximum or minimum. A zero derivative at one point does not establish constancy or, by itself, an extremum. Later function analysis supplies further criteria.

## Explore and explain

Choose square, cubic or absolute value, fix a and move h. Gold is the current secant; green is the finite-slope tangent when it exists. Changing h preserves a derivative prediction whose answer stays fixed. The zero corner has no invented tangent and h=0 has no invented secant.

The exact table retains both sides. Expanded quotient identities explain square and cubic limits, while the piecewise absolute-value quotient disproves differentiability at the corner. Graphs and finite tables support interpretation rather than replacing the rule with a visual impression of smoothness.

For the exit, compare the value limits and quotient limits of the two functions at zero. A parent can ask separately about continuity and differentiability, requiring evidence about the appropriate quantities instead of treating the words as synonyms.
