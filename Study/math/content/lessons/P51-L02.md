## 顺序来自结构，不来自符号名字

括号把一段表达式作为整体。先处理括号内的运算，再按所在层级处理乘除、加减；括号内也遵守同样的层级规则。乘与除没有谁永远优先，加与减也没有谁永远先做，同级按原式从左往右。

这个约定让同一写法有明确含义，避免不同读者把它算成不同问题。我们可以用有依据的等值变形设计策略，但不能无说明地添括号或删括号，然后把新式当原式。

### 例 1 · 有括号与无括号的和

1. **24+36÷6** 中，36÷6 是加到二十四上的商。
2. 先求商六，再 **24+6=30**。
3. **(24+36)÷6** 中，整个和成为被除数。
4. 先求和六十，再 **60÷6=10**。
5. 两式的分组不同，结果三十与十不同；括号不是可随意忽略的装饰。

### 例 2 · 乘除同级从左到右

1. **24÷6×2** 没有括号，先做左侧除法。
2. **24÷6=4**，接着 **4×2=8**。
3. **24÷(6×2)** 则先算括号里的十二，再 **24÷12=2**。
4. 后式改变除数的分组，不是前式的等值写法。
5. “先乘后除”会错误地得到二；正确规则是乘除同级，按原式先后处理。

### 加减也有同级顺序

20−6+4=14+4=18，不能先把六与四合成十再做二十减十。那是在原式没有的地方加入括号，改成 20−(6+4)。加与减同级，不是加法总先于减法。

60−(8+4)×3 先求括号十二，再乘三得到三十六，最后六十减三十六得到二十四。每一段的作用范围都保留：三乘的是括号里的和，不是括号外整个差。

若括号在右边，例如 2×3+(4+5)，本工作台先处理右侧括号得到九，再算左侧乘积六，最后合为十五。左右独立部分在有依据的策略中可以分别计算，但逐行示范选择先括号，不能一边说先括号，一边先展开无关的外部乘法。

括号不一定在每个特殊数值下都改变最终结果，例如乘一可能碰巧让两种写法同值。那不是可以任意删括号的证明。先核查表达式关系，再决定是否有合法的等值变形，不用偶然相同的答案代替一般规则。

工作台让你先选这次结构对应的规则，再展开每一步。错误选择会解释应看括号、层级还是同级顺序，不把一个口诀当所有题的答案。可以把每行等值步骤读给家长听，说明哪一段刚被替换。

**带走一个发现：** 我识别括号与层级，保留同级先后；不同分组可以代表不同计算，不能擅自改变。

## Order follows structure rather than operation names

Brackets make a subexpression one whole. Handle operations inside brackets, then multiplication/division and addition/subtraction at the relevant level. Brackets themselves follow the same precedence rules. Neither multiplication over division nor addition over subtraction always comes first: equal-level operations go left to right.

This convention gives one written expression a clear meaning. Justified equivalent transformations may support a strategy, but unexplained additions or removals of brackets can create another expression.

### Example 1 · A sum with and without brackets

1. In **24+36÷6**, the quotient 36÷6 is added to twenty-four.
2. Find quotient six, then **24+6=30**.
3. In **(24+36)÷6**, the entire sum is the dividend.
4. Find sum sixty, then **60÷6=10**.
5. Different grouping gives thirty versus ten. Brackets are not decoration to ignore freely.

### Example 2 · Equal-level division and multiplication

1. **24÷6×2** has no brackets, so begin with leftmost division.
2. **24÷6=4**, then **4×2=8**.
3. **24÷(6×2)** first gives twelve inside brackets, then **24÷12=2**.
4. The latter changes divisor grouping rather than preserving the original expression.
5. “Multiply before divide” would wrongly give two. Division and multiplication share precedence and follow their original order.

### Addition and subtraction also share a level

20−6+4=14+4=18. Combining six and four first creates 20−(6+4), with brackets absent from the original. Addition does not always precede subtraction.

For 60−(8+4)×3, resolve twelve inside brackets, multiply by three for thirty-six, then subtract from sixty for twenty-four. Three multiplies the bracketed sum rather than the entire outer difference.

For right-side brackets in 2×3+(4+5), the workshop resolves nine inside brackets before calculating six in the left product and adding to fifteen. Independent parts can be calculated separately in justified strategies, but a demonstration claiming “brackets first” should visibly follow that choice.

Brackets need not change the value for every special set of numbers: multiplying by one may make distinct structures coincidentally agree. Such agreement is not permission to delete brackets generally. Check relationships rather than treat an accidental matching result as a rule.

Choose the applicable structure rule before revealing steps. Feedback identifies brackets, precedence or equal-level order, rather than rely on one slogan for every question. Explain to a parent which complete part each equivalent line replaces.

**Takeaway:** identify brackets and levels, preserve equal-level order and justify changes in grouping rather than alter them arbitrarily.
