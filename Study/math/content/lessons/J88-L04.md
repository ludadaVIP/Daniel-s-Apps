# 相关不等于因果

## 图回答“同时怎样变”，不自动回答“为什么”

正向关联说两个量在这些观察中往往同向变化。因果说改变一个因素会使另一个量发生变化，需要排除其他合理解释并说明适用范围。强相关、点很多、线很直，都不能单独完成这个推理。条件不足不是证明“绝对没有因果”，而是目前证据还不支持所声称的因果结论。

共同因素可能同时影响两个量；方向也可能与主张相反；不同群体的选择方式还可能制造模式。提出一个可能解释，是在检验原推断是否必然，不是已经证明自己的故事。要把候选解释与验证它的证据分开。

### 例 1：八天零食与租车的构造反例

工作台使用程序固定构造的虚构八天数据：凉天 C 的数对为 (1,1)、(1,3)、(3,1)、(3,3)，暖天 W 为 (7,7)、(7,9)、(9,7)、(9,9)。横轴零食销量（份），纵轴租车次数（次）；这些不是实际商家的经济记录。

合并后，点总体从左下往右上。但同天气内，较小 x 和较大 x 都对应相同的两种 y，形成四角方阵，没有合并图那样的线性上升。天气可能同时改变游客活动与购买行为，组间差别便能产生总体关联。因此“零食卖得多导致租车多”不是仅由这张图保证的结论。

分组是检查候选共同因素的办法，但不保证每个真实数据都呈这个形状，也不保证控制一个因素就控制了全部。该构造展示原推断不可靠，不声称天气已经被确认为实际唯一原因。

### 例 2：伞、湿路面与更强的检验

观察到湿路面与雨伞同时多，不能直接推“雨伞使路面湿”。下雨可能使路面湿并促使人们用伞；这提供共同原因和因果方向的候选。需要天气记录、时间先后等证据继续检查，不能仅因这个故事听起来合理就把它当作已经验证的结论。

若要研究纸片折法 A/B 对下滑距离的影响，可在可行条件下把相似纸片随机分配到折法，使用相同桌面、释放位置和测量方法，重复并记录全部观察与缺失。这种安排帮助把处理差异与其他差异分开；有限结果仍有随机波动，要说明范围，不能一次实验就保证所有材料都一样。

这里“随机分配”是决定哪张纸片接受哪个处理；“随机抽样”是决定从哪个总体选哪些纸片进入研究。前者有助因果辨别，后者关乎覆盖与推广范围。随机抽更多观察天没有随机改变零食变量，不能自动消除天气混杂。两种随机操作不要用一个词含糊替代。

## 设计、结果、结论分开

选择“更好的设计”按钮仅表示提出后续方案，并没有执行干预，也没有自动得到结果。还要真正收集、检查、分析，在合适比较下讨论差异与随机波动。本课不要求真实招募、购买或进行任何商家干预，全部可用纸上虚构情境说明。

自动题只核“已有证据是否充分”这类有限结论；共同因素、反向解释和实验方案由家长阅读具体理由，不靠出现几个术语就通过。孩子可以提出别的合理共同因素，只要明确它是待验证的候选，并用它指出原主张的逻辑缺口。

家长追问：“观察到了什么？主张改变了什么？谁被随机分配哪个处理？还可能有什么解释？结论能推广到哪里？”这五问帮助把图形描述连到证据，而非把相关背成一句禁止讨论因果的口号。

# Correlation is not causation

## A co-pattern does not explain itself

Positive association describes quantities tending to vary together in observations. A causal claim concerns what changing one factor would do to another. It needs evidence against other plausible explanations and a defined scope. More points, a straighter line or a strong association alone do not supply that evidence. Insufficient evidence is not proof that no causal effect exists.

A common factor might influence both variables; direction may be reversed; selection can also produce a pattern. Proposing a plausible alternative tests whether the original inference is necessary. It does not automatically verify the alternative story.

### Example 1: a constructed eight-day counterexample

The workshop uses fictional cool-day pairs (1,1),(1,3),(3,1),(3,3) and warm-day pairs (7,7),(7,9),(9,7),(9,9). x counts snack portions and y bike rentals. These are constructed records, not actual business evidence.

Pooled points rise from lower left to upper right. Within each weather group, both low and high x have the same two y values, forming a rectangle without that rising linear pattern. Weather could affect both activity and purchasing. Group differences can therefore produce the pooled association without proving snacks cause rentals.

Stratifying examines a candidate common factor; it does not promise this shape in every real data set or control every other factor. The counterexample exposes the inference gap without proving weather is the sole actual cause.

### Example 2: umbrellas, wet roads and stronger designs

Umbrellas and wet roads appearing together do not prove umbrellas wet roads. Rain might wet roads and prompt umbrella use. This offers common-cause and direction explanations requiring weather and time-order evidence, not merely a convincing story.

To investigate fold A/B effects on card-slide distance, where feasible randomly assign comparable cards to folds, standardise desk, release point and measurement, replicate and retain all results and missingness. This helps separate treatment differences from other differences. Finite outcomes still vary; define the scope rather than guaranteeing every material behaves identically.

Random assignment determines which unit receives which treatment. Random sampling determines which units enter from a population. Assignment helps causal discrimination; sampling concerns coverage and generalisation. Randomly selecting more observed days does not randomly manipulate snacks or automatically remove weather confounding.

## Separate design, results and conclusion

Selecting a stronger design proposes a next step. It neither runs an intervention nor obtains its result. Collection, checking, analysis and comparison against random variation remain necessary. This lesson needs no real recruitment, purchases or business intervention; fictional paper scenarios suffice.

Automatic tasks check limited claims such as evidence sufficiency. A parent reads explanations and designs rather than counting causal vocabulary. Alternative reasonable factors are welcome when presented as candidates that reveal a logical gap.

Ask what was observed, what intervention is claimed, who received a randomly assigned treatment, what alternatives remain, and where the conclusion applies. These questions connect a graph to evidence without turning the lesson into a slogan that forbids investigating causes.
