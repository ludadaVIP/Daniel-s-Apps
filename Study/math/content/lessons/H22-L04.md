# 抛物线的焦点与准线

二次函数图像曾让我们认识抛物线。本课换一种可检验的定义：**到一个固定点F与一条固定直线l距离相等的点集**。F不在l上，l叫准线；到直线的距离必须沿垂线量。

## 等距条件决定方程

先取顶点V(0,0)、水平轴。规定焦点F(p,0)，准线x=−p，其中p≠0。这里p是从顶点到焦点的有向位移；焦点到准线的距离是2|p|。本课采用y²=4px约定，有些资料把系数写成2p，参数意义随之不同，不能混用。

点P(x,y)到F的距离为sqrt((x−p)²+y²)，到准线距离为|x+p|。两边均非负，等距可平方为(x−p)²+y²=(x+p)²，整理得y²=4px。

反过来，若y²=4px，则上述两距离平方相等；因两者非负，它们相等。因此方程与定义完全对应，没有凭空加入额外点。p>0时x≥0，向右开口；p<0时x≤0，向左开口。顶点是焦点到准线垂线段的中点。

### 例1：焦点(2,0)，准线x=−2

V在两者中间，为(0,0)，p=2。方程y²=8x。P(2,4)满足16=8×2；PF=4，到准线的垂足D(−2,4)给PD=4。两段相等的距离不是到顶点的距离。

若把y²=8x错读为焦点(4,0)，就混淆4p与2p。另如x²=8y，交换的是轴向：焦点(0,2)，准线y=−2，向上开口；不能只见系数8就固定写水平焦点。

### 例2：平移与负参数一起处理

顶点V(1,−2)，竖直轴，p=−1/2。相对顶点坐标X=x−1、Y=y+2满足X²=4pY=−2Y，所以(x−1)²=−2(y+2)。焦点是(1,−5/2)，准线y=−3/2，开口向下。

P(0,−5/2)代入得1=−2(−1/2)=1。PF为水平长度1，准线垂足D(0,−3/2)，PD也为1。负p只改变方向；长度始终非负。不能把准线放在焦点同侧，也不能忘记中心位移。

## 工作台中的距离证据

工作台可以换顶点、轴向、正负半整数p，并沿曲线选择P。描点参数t不是横坐标：水平轴时相对坐标为(pt²,2pt)，竖直轴交换角色。该参数用于构造示例，完整定义仍是等距。

图中PF为实线、PD为虚线，D确在准线上且PD垂直；精确表核对两段非负长度。移动P后两距离会一起变化，不是把某个固定数当作全部点的距离。仅换图窗保留预测；方程改变须重新写方程。

p=0不再是非退化抛物线：焦点落在准线x=0上，sqrt(x²+y²)=|x|只给y=0。有限描点不能替代这个边界说明。出口要给出等距方程、平方整理与反向恢复条件，家长核对推理而非看到“焦点、准线”几个词就算通过。

# Parabola focus and directrix

Quadratic functions introduced the parabola as a graph. Here it is a **locus equidistant from a fixed point F and a fixed line l**, with F not on l. The line is the directrix. Distance to it must be perpendicular.

## Equal distances determine the equation

Start with vertex V(0,0) and horizontal axis. Let F(p,0) and directrix x=−p, where p≠0. Parameter p is signed vertex-to-focus displacement; focus-to-directrix distance is 2|p|. This lesson uses y²=4px. A source using 2p uses a different parameter convention.

For P(x,y), focus distance is sqrt((x−p)²+y²) and directrix distance is |x+p|. Both are nonnegative, so equality may be squared to (x−p)²+y²=(x+p)², giving y²=4px.

Conversely, this equation makes the squared nonnegative distances equal, so the distances themselves agree. Thus the equation and locus are equivalent. Positive p gives x≥0 and a rightward opening; negative p gives x≤0 and a leftward opening. The vertex lies halfway along the perpendicular from focus to directrix.

### Example 1: Focus (2,0), directrix x=−2

The vertex is (0,0), so p=2 and y²=8x. Point P(2,4) satisfies 16=8×2. Its focus distance is four; the directrix foot D(−2,4) also gives PD=4. Neither is a distance to the vertex.

Reading focus (4,0) from y²=8x confuses 4p with a 2p convention. For x²=8y, the axis changes: focus (0,2), directrix y=−2 and upward opening. A coefficient alone does not identify the axis.

### Example 2: Translate and reverse direction

Take vertex (1,−2), vertical axis and p=−1/2. Relative coordinates X=x−1,Y=y+2 satisfy X²=−2Y. Thus (x−1)²=−2(y+2), with focus (1,−5/2), directrix y=−3/2 and downward opening.

Point P(0,−5/2) satisfies 1=−2(−1/2). Its horizontal distance to F is one. Its directrix foot D(0,−3/2) also gives distance one. A negative parameter changes orientation, while lengths remain nonnegative. Retain the vertex translation and put the directrix opposite the focus.

## Distance evidence in the workbench

Change vertex, axis and signed half-integer p, then choose a trace point. The trace parameter t is not x: relative coordinates on a horizontal-axis parabola are (pt²,2pt), with roles swapped vertically. It constructs examples; the full definition remains equal distances.

Solid PF and dashed PD correspond to exact table entries. D lies on the directrix and PD is perpendicular. Moving P changes both distances together; they are not a single fixed length for the entire curve. Changing only the window preserves predictions.

At p=0 the focus lies on directrix x=0, and sqrt(x²+y²)=|x| gives only y=0, a line. The exit must state the distance equation, simplify its square and restore the original equality. Review the argument itself, not isolated focus/directrix keywords.
