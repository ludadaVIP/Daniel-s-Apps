# 平方根与算术平方根

## 一个符号与一组解，是两个问题

如果实数 x 的平方等于 a，x 就是 a 的一个平方根。“求 a 的平方根”要找所有这样的实数；√a 则约定表示 a 的非负平方根，叫算术平方根或主平方根。约定让根号成为单值符号，不能同时把正负两个值塞进 √a。

正数有一正一负两个平方根，大小相同、互为相反数；零只有一个平方根零；负数没有实数平方根。理由是实数平方永远非负。本课始终在实数范围讨论，“没有实数根”不是数值零，也不是说负数不存在。复数是后续另一数域，不能在一道实数题中不作说明就切换。

### 例 1：九的两根与 √9

因为 3²=9、(−3)²=9，方程 x²=9 的实数解是 −3 和 3，可写成 ±3。± 是同时列出相反两数的简写，不是一个新的单值数。只写三会漏解，只写负三也会漏解；顺序先负后正或先正后负都可以。

但 √9=3，取的是非负主根。−√9=−3，负号本来在根号外，对主根再取相反数，不会因为“主根非负”而消失。检查输入前先问：题目要一个符号的值，还是一个方程的全部根？

同样，x²=1/4 有两解 ±1/2，因为两者平方均为1/4；√(1/4)只等于1/2。分母也要平方，不能误写成 ±1/4。

### 例 2：零、负数与先平方后开方

x²=0 只有 x=0。写“−0、0”没有给出两个不同的解，零的相反数仍是零。x²=−4 没有实数解；±2 的平方都是正四，代回立即能检出错误。

再算 √((−3)²)。括号内先平方为九，主根是三。因此“平方再开方总是回到原数”少了条件：一般正确式是 √(a²)=|a|。a≥0 时 |a|=a；a<0 时 |a|=−a。它回到的是非负大小，而不是一定保留原来的方向。

对 a=−3，|a|=3；对 a=0，|a|=0；对 a=1/2，|a|=1/2。用这些边界例核规则，比只试正整数更能发现漏掉的绝对值。与此不同，(√a)²=a 先要求 a≥0，使 √a 在实数中有意义。

## 数轴、长度与代回

同一个正 a 的两根在实数轴上关于零对称；主根只在零的右侧或零点。若 a 表示正方形面积，几何边长取非负主根；负根虽然能满足代数平方方程，却不是负的几何长度。必须按所问对象解释，不能以“长度不负”删掉纯方程的负解。

探索先作答，再揭示根与原式检验。改变数轴窗口不改变解；负被开方数不画虚假的实数点。点的屏幕位置只作显示，根式或分数标签保留精确值。家长可请孩子分别读出 √9、−√9、x²=9，再说明每个负号的位置与每次平方的对象；自动核对根集合不代替这些解释。

# Square roots and principal roots

## One symbol and a solution set answer different questions

If a real x satisfies x²=a, it is a square root of a. Finding the square roots means finding all such real numbers. The symbol √a instead denotes the nonnegative root, called the principal square root. This convention makes the radical a single-valued symbol.

A positive number has two opposite roots, zero has just zero, and a negative number has no real square roots because real squares are nonnegative. “No real roots” is not a numerical zero and does not mean negative numbers do not exist. Complex numbers belong to a later domain, not an unannounced change within a real-number task.

### Example 1: two roots of nine, one value of √9

Since 3²=9 and (-3)²=9, x²=9 has solutions -3 and 3, abbreviated ±3. The ± notation lists two opposite numbers; it does not create a new single value. Either root alone is incomplete, and listing order is irrelevant.

However √9=3. An outside -√9=-3 takes the opposite of the principal root; the external minus does not vanish. First identify whether the question asks for a radical’s value or every equation root.

Similarly x²=1/4 has solutions ±1/2, while √(1/4)=1/2. The denominator is squared too; ±1/4 would be wrong.

### Example 2: zero, negatives and squaring first

x²=0 has only x=0. Writing -0 and 0 does not give distinct solutions. x²=-4 has no real solution; both ±2 square to positive four, so substitution rejects them.

For √((-3)²), square inside first to obtain nine, then take principal root three. Squaring and then taking a principal root does not always restore the original sign. The general identity is √(a²)=|a|: it equals a for a≥0 and -a for a<0.

Check a=-3, zero and 1/2 rather than testing positive integers only. In contrast, (√a)²=a first requires a≥0 so that the real radical is defined.

## Line, length and substitution

The two roots of a positive a lie symmetrically about zero. The principal root lies at or to the right of zero. A square’s side uses the nonnegative root of its area, but that geometric interpretation must not erase the negative solution of a pure equation.

Answer before revealing roots and substitution. A changed line window does not change solutions; negative radicands create no false real-line points. Pixels approximate placement while radical or fraction labels remain exact. A parent asks the learner to distinguish √9, -√9 and x²=9 and explain each sign. Automatic set checking does not replace that reasoning.
