# 配方法的面积解释

## 两条长条与一个缺角

先把二次项系数化为一。对 x²+2px，正长度示意可从边长 x 的正方形开始，把一次项拆成两条面积 px 的长条，分别放在右边和下边。每条宽 p、长 x，两条不重叠；它们围出的外框缺少 p×p 的角。补上面积 p² 后，完整大正方形边长 x+p，得到恒等式 x²+2px+p²=(x+p)²。

面积图解释的是展开结构。为方便作图用了 x>0、p>0，不表示原方程自动只研究正根；代数恒等式对所有实数仍成立。若 p 为负，用有符号代数配方，不能把负宽度画成正面积块。改变图例的 x 只改变示意尺寸，不会改题目方程，更不会替你选根。

### 例 1：x²+6x=7 的缺角是九

把6x拆成3x与3x，两条长条各宽3，缺角边长3、面积9。方程两边都加9：x²+6x+9=16，改写为 (x+3)²=16。由于实数平方等于16，x+3=4或−4，因此 x=1或−7。

核原式：1+6=7；49−42=7。负根−7必须保留，因为本题没有正长度情境限制。只画得出正长度不代表负根不成立。若只在左边补九而右边仍写七，会得到另一个方程；“补成平方”不能牺牲等式等价性。若只加三，则 x²+6x+3并不是 (x+3)²。

### 例 2：分数半系数与负一次项

x²+3x=1 的半系数为3/2，需要同加 (3/2)²=9/4。于是 (x+3/2)²=13/4，开方取两分支 x+3/2=±√13/2，再移去3/2，得到 x=(−3±√13)/2。分数是精确值，不应先把根式换成短小数再继续。

对 x²−4x=1，半系数是−2，平方仍为4。同加四得 (x−2)²=5，根为2±√5。这里沿用同一代数结构，完全不需要负宽度图。若 x²+4x=−5，则同加4后为 (x+2)²=−1：实数平方不可能为负，故没有实根，而不是 x=−2。

## 每一步都有依赖

配方顺序是：非零二次系数先归一；常数移到右边；同加半系数的平方；左边写成平方；按右边的符号取所有实数分支；最后解出 x 并代回原式。右边为正有两个分支，为零只有一个不同值，为负无实数分支。不能一律机械写“±某个数”，也不能重复执行补角却忘了右边再次改变。

工作台可以逐步撤回，帮助说明每一步依靠什么。先预测补多少，再显示真实角块；半系数为3时缺角面积9，而非3。家长可请孩子指认长度与面积、解释为何两边同加，并说明负根究竟被原题限制排除，还是仅在图中未显示。能说清这些理由，比背一句“移项配方开方”更能迁移。

# Completing the square visually

## Two strips and one corner

First make the quadratic coefficient one. For x²+2px with positive illustrative lengths, start with an x-by-x square. Split the linear term into two px strips of width p and length x, placing one to the right and one below without overlap. Their outer frame lacks a p-by-p corner. Adding p² completes a square of side x+p: x²+2px+p²=(x+p)².

The picture explains an identity. Positive x and p help the illustration; they do not impose a positive-root domain on the equation. The algebraic identity holds for every real x. For negative p, use signed algebra rather than drawing positive-area tiles with negative widths. Changing illustrative x changes the picture, not the problem.

### Example 1: the missing corner is nine

For x²+6x=7, split 6x into two 3x strips. The corner has side three and area nine. Add nine to both sides to obtain x²+6x+9=16, or (x+3)²=16. Both real branches are needed: x+3=4 or −4, giving x=1 or −7.

The original checks are 1+6=7 and 49−42=7. Keep the negative root because the equation states no positive-length restriction. A drawing that only uses positive lengths cannot reject it. Adding nine only on the left changes the equation; adding three does not complete (x+3)².

### Example 2: fractional and negative half-coefficients

For x²+3x=1, half the linear coefficient is 3/2. Add its square 9/4 on both sides: (x+3/2)²=13/4. Thus x+3/2=±√13/2 and x=(−3±√13)/2. Fractions and radicals retain exact values; do not introduce rounded decimals midway.

For x²−4x=1, the signed half-coefficient is −2, whose square is four. Adding four gives (x−2)²=5 and roots 2±√5. No negative-width picture is needed. By contrast x²+4x=−5 becomes (x+2)²=−1, impossible for a real square. It has no real roots, rather than a root of −2.

## Respect the dependencies

Normalise using a nonzero quadratic coefficient, move the constant, add the half-coefficient squared to both sides, rewrite the square, take every permitted real branch, isolate x and substitute into the original. A positive target gives two branches, zero one distinct value, and a negative target none. Repeating the corner step without changing both sides is not valid.

The laboratory allows steps to be undone. Predict the added area before revealing it, distinguish lengths from areas and explain the same operation on both sides. A parent should ask whether a rejected root fails an original restriction or is merely absent from a positive-length illustration.
