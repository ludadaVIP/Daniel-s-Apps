# 空间向量的线性运算

空间中的加减与数乘沿用平面规则，但第三分量必须同步处理。**三分量逐项运算与真实箭头首尾相接**说的是同一件事，不是两套无关算法。

## 从位移合成理解分量

若u=(a,b,c)、v=(d,e,f)，先走u再走v，总位移为(a+d,b+e,c+f)。箭头可以平移到首尾相接的位置，自由向量分量不变。交换先后次序终点相同，对应交换律。

减v是加−v，−v=(−d,−e,−f)。数乘ku=(ka,kb,kc)，k>0保同向，k<0反向，长度变成|k||u|；k=0给零向量，没有确定方向。若u本来是零向量，任意倍数仍为零，不谈与原方向同反向。

线性组合αu+βv必须先把倍数作用所有分量，再合并。它是向量，不是长度。一般|u+v|不等于|u|+|v|；后者只在同向共线（含相应零向量边界）等条件下取等号。

### 例1：加、减与逆运算核对

u=(1,2,−1)、v=(2,−1,3)。u+v=(3,1,2)，第三分量为−1+3=2。u−v=(−1,3,−4)，第二项是2−(−1)=3，不能漏掉减负数。

2u−v=(2,4,−2)−(2,−1,3)=(0,5,−5)。用逆运算验证：w+v=(2,4,−2)=2u。若第三项错写−4，逆运算便无法恢复−2。

首尾图中减法应画−v，从u的终点出发，终点为u−v。2u−v则先画2u再接−v。箭头的起点变化不等于分量变化；精确表同时列起点、分量与终点供核对。

### 例2：负倍数、零与长度反例

(−1/2)u=(−1/2,−1,1/2)，全部分量都乘同一数，方向反转、长度减半。0u=(0,0,0)，不能把零画成仍有箭头方向的短线。

取e₁=(1,0,0)、e₂=(0,1,0)。两模各1，和为2；但e₁+e₂=(1,1,0)的模为√2。向量相加并不是把模直接相加。点积课会解释角度如何影响合向量长度；本课先用三项平方和检验。

## 工作台中的问题与显示

选择u、v以及u+v、u−v、ku或2u−v。k有正、负、分数和0。先给结果三元组，再求结果平方模；核验后显示合成箭头。ku不依赖v，改变未使用的v不会清空结果；同一个结果下切换相机与核验显示也保留预测。

图中颜色与名字共同标识箭头，不能只看颜色决定方向。正投影可能使非零箭头看起来很短，仍需检查三分量。数学上的零向量判断以全部分量为0为准。

出口用一般分量公式与具体逆运算共同解释2u−v。家长可要求孩子逐项读出“这个负号作用谁”，再检查最后一项是否与前两项遵守同一规则。

# Operations on 3D vectors

Spatial addition, subtraction and scaling extend the planar rules to a third component. **Component calculations and head-to-tail arrows** describe the same displacement.

## Components follow displacement composition

For u=(a,b,c),v=(d,e,f), travelling by u then v gives (a+d,b+e,c+f). Translate an arrow to attach it head to tail without changing its components. Reversing travel order reaches the same endpoint, matching commutativity.

Subtracting v adds −v=(−d,−e,−f). Scaling gives ku=(ka,kb,kc). Positive k preserves direction, negative k reverses it, and length becomes |k||u|. Zero scaling gives the zero vector, with no direction. Scaling an already zero vector does not create a direction either.

In αu+βv, scale every component before combining. The result is a vector, not a length. Norm is not linear: |u+v| generally differs from |u|+|v|. Same-direction collinearity gives equality, with the relevant zero-vector boundary cases.

### Example 1: Addition, subtraction and inverse checking

With u=(1,2,−1),v=(2,−1,3), addition gives (3,1,2). Subtraction gives (−1,3,−4): the second component is 2−(−1), not 2−1.

Next, 2u−v=(2,4,−2)−(2,−1,3)=(0,5,−5). Adding v back gives (2,4,−2)=2u. An incorrect third entry would fail this inverse check.

For subtraction, attach −v at u’s head. For 2u−v, first draw 2u, then attach −v. Moving an arrow’s tail does not change its components; the audit lists tail, components and head separately.

### Example 2: Negative and zero scalars, and a norm counterexample

Multiplying u by −1/2 gives (−1/2,−1,1/2). All entries scale, direction reverses and length halves. Multiplying by zero gives (0,0,0), which has no arrow direction.

Let e₁=(1,0,0),e₂=(0,1,0). Their lengths sum to two, but their sum (1,1,0) has length √2. Adding vectors is not generally adding their lengths. The next lesson explains how angle affects the combined length; here verify using squared components.

## Mathematical tasks versus display changes

Choose vectors and an operation, including positive, negative, fractional and zero scalars. Predict the result triple and squared norm before revealing the resultant. Scaling u does not use v, so changing an unused v preserves that result. Camera and audit changes also preserve predictions.

Arrow names and colours identify the construction. A projection may make a nonzero arrow look short; inspect its components. A mathematical zero requires all three entries to vanish.

The exit explains 2u−v using general components and a concrete inverse check. Ask which terms each minus sign acts on, and whether z follows the same rule as x and y.
