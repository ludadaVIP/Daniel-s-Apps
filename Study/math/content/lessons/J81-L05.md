# 平方差公式

## 一样的首项，相反的次项

`(A+B)(A−B)` 中，两个首项都是完整的 A，两个次项是互为相反数的 B 与 −B。逐项展开为 `A²−AB+BA−B²`。因为 BA=AB，中间两项相消，留下 `A²−B²`。这就是平方差公式。

要识别的是整体结构，不是只看某两个相同数字。A 可以是 2x，也可以是 x²，平方时整个 A 都要平方。若两首项不同，或次项不是相反数，交叉项通常不消失，就应重新列全配对，不能强套公式。

### 例 1：与差的平方比较

`(x+3)(x−3)=x²−3x+3x−9=x²−9`。但 `(x−3)²=x²−6x+9`，前者中间项异号相消，后者两中间项同为负。两个式子的常数符号也不同，不能因都看见一个减号就用同一个结果。

对 `(2x+5)(2x−5)`，令 A=2x、B=5，得到 `(2x)²−5²=4x²−25`。若写 2x²−25，就没有平方整个首项。再看 `(x²+2)(x²−2)`，A=x²，A²=x⁴，所以结果 x⁴−4，不是 x²−4。

### 例 2：保持两块面积的重排

取 x>b>0，从边长 x 的大正方形去掉右下角边长 b 的小正方形，余面积 x²−b²。把余下 L 形沿横线切成两块：上块宽 x、高 x−b；左下块宽 x−b、高 b。

上块不动，将左下块旋转九十度，变成宽 b、高 x−b，接到上块右边。它的两边只是交换，面积仍为 b(x−b)，没有拉伸。新外框宽 x+b、高 x−b，所以面积也等于 `(x+b)(x−b)`。

例如 x=5、b=2，两块面积十五与六，合计二十一；重排后七乘三仍为二十一。被去掉的四不参与重排，不能为了铺满新图再把它补进去。这个正长度图解释面积守恒，而代数分配给出对任意实数 A、B 的一般恒等式。

## 识别结构可以帮助准确计算

`99×101=(100−1)(100+1)=10000−1=9999`。共同整体是一百，偏移是一，不必把九十九和一百零一各自平方。类似 `102×98=(100+2)(100−2)=10000−4=9996`。先找共同中心和相反偏移，再计算完整平方。

若是 `(2x+3)(x−3)`，首项不相同，不能直接写成某个平方差；全配对得到 `2x²−3x−9`。公式节省重复步骤，却不免除结构检查。一次数值巧合也不能替代一般证明。

工作台保留两个图块的编号和面积，重排只旋转一块；改变图中 x 只改变正长度实例，不改变符号答案。改变 b 则重算代数任务并清空旧答案。家长请孩子指出相同整体、相反项、两个交叉项的抵消，以及每块面积为何不变。

# Difference of squares

## Matching first terms and opposite second terms

In (A+B)(A−B), both first terms are the same whole A, and B and −B are opposites. Distribution gives A²−AB+BA−B². Commutativity makes the middle terms cancel, leaving A²−B².

Identify complete terms rather than a superficial pair of numbers. A may be 2x or x²; square the entire A. If the first terms differ or the second terms are not opposites, the cross terms generally remain. Return to every term pair instead of forcing the formula.

### Example 1: compare a squared difference

(x+3)(x−3)=x²−3x+3x−9=x²−9. In contrast, (x−3)²=x²−6x+9. The conjugate factors have opposite middle products; the squared difference has two negative middle products. Their final constants also have different signs.

For (2x+5)(2x−5), take whole terms A=2x,B=5 to obtain 4x²−25. Writing 2x² would fail to square the coefficient. For (x²+2)(x²−2), A=x², so A²=x⁴ and the result is x⁴−4.

### Example 2: rearrange two pieces without changing area

For x>b>0, remove a corner square of side b from a square of side x. The remaining L-shaped area is x²−b². Cut it into a top rectangle of width x and height x−b, and a lower-left rectangle of width x−b and height b.

Keep the top piece fixed. Rotate the lower piece ninety degrees to make it width b and height x−b, and place it to the right. Swapping its dimensions preserves area b(x−b); it is not stretched. The new rectangle has width x+b and height x−b, giving the same area (x+b)(x−b).

At x=5,b=2, the pieces have areas fifteen and six, totalling twenty-one. The rearranged seven-by-three rectangle also has area twenty-one. The removed four-unit corner is not included among the pieces. This geometric explanation requires positive lengths; distribution proves the general identity for all real A,B.

## Recognise structure for exact calculation

99×101=(100−1)(100+1)=10000−1=9999. The shared whole is one hundred and the opposite offsets are one. Similarly, 102×98=(100+2)(100−2)=10000−4=9996. Identify the common centre before calculating the complete squares.

The factors (2x+3)(x−3) do not share a first term. Their full expansion is 2x²−3x−9, rather than a direct difference of two squares. A formula saves repeated work but does not remove structural checks. Numerical coincidence at one input does not prove an identity.

The workshop preserves both piece IDs and their areas, rotating one without stretching it. Changing illustration x changes only the positive-length instance and preserves the symbolic task. Changing b resets that task. A parent asks for the matching whole, opposite terms, cancellation and conserved piece areas rather than only a final expression.
