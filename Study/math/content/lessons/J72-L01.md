# 代数式与代入

## 从字母关系走向完整的代数式

在 P65 中，字母已经可以表示变化的数量。现在我们同时保留一次项、平方项、常数与两个字母，问它们组成什么关系。`x²+2x−3` 是代数式，不是一个等待猜答案的等式；`x=−2` 则是一条赋值条件，告诉我们本次每个 x 都替换成哪个数。

`2x` 表示二乘 x，`xy` 表示 x 乘 y，`x²` 表示 x 乘自身。省略乘号不等于省略运算。相同字母在同一次计算中取相同值，不同字母可以有各自给定值；没有条件时，不能擅自认为 x 与 y 相等。代数式还可包含有理数系数，如 `(1/2)x²−2x`。

代入前先圈字母与指数，保留原来的运算结构；把负数或分数作为完整的数放进括号，再按 J71 的运算次序计算。代入不是只改一个 x，也不是让给定值旁边的负号自动消失。

### 例 1：负数要作为整个底数代入

给定 `x=−2`，求 `x²+2x−3`。

1. 每次出现的 x 都改为负二，原式成为 `(−2)²+2×(−2)−3`。
2. 平方得四，乘法得负四，最后 `4−4−3=−3`。
3. 若求的是 `−x²`，外部负号仍保留，得到 `−[(−2)²]=−4`，不能与 x² 的值混为一谈。

再把 x 改成 `−3/2`，`x²−x=(−3/2)²−(−3/2)=9/4+6/4=15/4`。精确分数可直接保留，不必先舍入为小数。工作台每改一次赋值都会清空旧预测，提醒我们旧数值只属于旧条件。

### 例 2：两个字母一致替换

给定 `x=−1`、`y=3`，求 `2xy+x`。

先写完整替换 `2×(−1)×3+(−1)`，算成 `−6−1=−7`。前面的 x 与末尾 x 都是负一，y 是三。若只替换乘积里的 x 而保留末项 x，就还没得到这个赋值下的数值。

同一组赋值下 `x²+y²=1+9=10`；换成 x 为负一、y 为二，值变为五。式子的关系保留，数值随赋值变化。表达式和这一次算出的数是不同层次的对象。

## 单次相等不等于恒等

x² 与 x 在 x=0 时都为零，在 x=1 时都为一，仍不能断言两式相同。令 x=2，一个是四，一个是二，反例已经推翻“对所有 x 相等”。几次试值可以检错、反驳普遍断言，却不能仅凭吻合就证明恒等。

后续课程会用字母部分和精确系数比较一般表达式，说明为什么 `3x²+4x²` 等于 `7x²`，而不是靠挑几个 x 试出来。这里先把代入做准确，并学会区分“给定赋值下的结果”和“所有允许赋值下都保持的关系”。

## 给出可核查的理由

家长可让孩子依次指出赋值条件、每个字母出现的位置、代入后的括号与完整运算顺序。最后问：“如果换一个值，哪一部分保留，哪一部分会变？”能展示替换过程和一个明确反例，比只给出最终数值更能说明理解。

# Expressions and substitution

## Preserve the relationship, then assign values

P65 used letters for changing quantities. We now include linear terms, squares, constants and two letters in one expression. The expression `x²+2x−3` is not an equation waiting for a guessed solution. The condition `x=−2` assigns the value used for every occurrence of x in this calculation.

Here `2x` means two times x, `xy` means x times y and `x²` means x times itself. Omitting a multiplication symbol does not omit the operation. One letter has one value within a calculation; different letters may have different assigned values. Rational coefficients are allowed, as in `(1/2)x²−2x`.

Identify letters and powers before replacing them. Bracket a negative or fractional value as one number and preserve all outside operations. Substitution does not mean changing one occurrence or discarding a sign.

### Example 1: substitute the whole signed base

At `x=−2`, evaluate `x²+2x−3`.

1. Replace every x to obtain `(−2)²+2×(−2)−3`.
2. The square is four and the product negative four, so `4−4−3=−3`.
3. If the requested expression were `−x²`, the outside minus would remain: `−[(−2)²]=−4`, unlike the value of x².

At `x=−3/2`, the expression `x²−x` becomes `9/4−(−3/2)=15/4`. Keep exact fractions instead of rounding intermediate quantities. Changing a workshop assignment clears the old numerical prediction because that prediction belonged to a different condition.

### Example 2: substitute two letters consistently

At `x=−1` and `y=3`, evaluate `2xy+x`. The complete replacement is `2×(−1)×3+(−1)=−6−1=−7`. Both x occurrences receive negative one, while y receives three. Leaving the final x unchanged would leave the substitution unfinished.

For the same assignment, `x²+y²=1+9=10`. Changing y to two gives five instead. The expression’s relationship remains, but the numerical value changes. An expression and one of its evaluated values are different kinds of objects.

## Agreement at a few values is not identity

The expressions x² and x agree at zero and one. They are still different: at x=2 they give four and two. That counterexample disproves agreement for every x. Trial values can reveal errors or refute a universal claim; a few matches do not prove an identity.

The following lessons compare variable parts and exact coefficients to explain identities such as `3x²+4x²=7x²` generally. For now, make substitution reliable and distinguish a value under one assignment from a relationship valid for all permitted assignments.

Ask the learner to identify the assignment, every letter occurrence, the brackets after replacement and the operation order. Then change a value and explain what remains and what changes. A complete replacement and a specific counterexample provide stronger evidence than a bare numerical answer.
