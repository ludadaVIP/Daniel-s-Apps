# 图论与最短路径

顶点表示地点或状态，边表示允许的连接，边权表示成本。最短路径在本课指从A到E的权重和最小，并不要求图上画得最短，也不等于边数最少。无箭头的边可双向，箭头边只能顺向。工作台限定非负整数边权，含零权边；按不重复顶点的简单路径作答。

## 暂定距离不是眼前一条边

记d(v)为当前找到的从A到v最便宜成本。初始d(A)=0，其余∞，表示尚未找到路径。每轮从全体未确定顶点选暂定距离最小者u，确定它，再对相邻边更新d(v)=min(d(v),d(u)+w)。这个更新叫松弛。选择的是从起点累计的总成本，而不是当前地点最便宜的出边。

### 例1：逐轮确定成本6

无向边为AB2、AC5、BC1、BD5、CD1、DE2、CE7。从A开始，确定A后得到B2、C5。选B2，得到C3、D7；选C3，得到D4、E10；选D4，得到E6；最后确定E6。沿着改进来源还原A-B-C-D-E，成本2+1+1+2=6。

路线A-C-E只走两条边，却成本5+7=12，说明不能数边数代替加权。工作台核验表记录每轮确定顶点后的全部标签；∞不是一条极长的边，更不是可以拿来相加的已找到路线。若需路径本身，必须确认每对相邻顶点真有允许方向的边。

### 例2：局部便宜、并列和不可达

换成AB1、AC3、BE9、CD1、DE1。局部沿最便宜边选A-B，会以A-B-E成本10结束；A-C-D-E成本3+1+1=5更优。Dijkstra先确定B后仍保留C的暂定标签3，下一轮选C，随后发现更便宜的E。算法没有把路线锁死在上一步选中的顶点上。

菱形AB2、BE3、AC1、CE4有两条成本5的最短路A-B-E和A-C-E，两者都应接受。只有AB2、BC1、CD1而E孤立时，E不可达，应写unreachable，不能补造一条D-E。含零边AB0、BC0、CE2、AE2时，A-E与A-B-C-E也都最优；零成本并不是不存在的边。

## 为什么非负条件重要

全局最小暂定距离被确定时，经任何尚未确定顶点再绕到它的路线，不会因后续非负边而变便宜。这个论证依赖非负性；遇负边不能直接沿用“确定后不再改”的保证。单个小例子碰巧算对，不足以扩大算法的适用范围。

本课图最多六顶点，路线判题核开始、终点、无重复顶点、边存在、方向和总成本，接受全部并列最优简单路径。非负权图总能从一条最优通路中删去重复顶点的非负环而不增加成本，因此选简单路径不损失最优值。含零环时可能有更多最优游走，本课有意采用简单路径作答范围。

图旁数字是权重，线段长短只用于排版；颜色也不承担唯一信息。出口需要解释每轮标签、贪心反例和算法前提，由人复核。家长可问“比较的是边还是从A来的总成本”和“另一条同成本路线为什么也对”；先核图的定义，再谈算法步骤。

# Graphs and shortest paths

Vertices represent locations or states, edges represent permitted connections, and weights represent costs. Here “shortest” means minimum total weight from A to E. It does not mean the shortest-looking drawing or the fewest edges. Unmarked edges are bidirectional; arrows permit only their indicated direction. We use nonnegative integer weights, including zero, and answer with simple paths that do not repeat vertices.

## Tentative distance is a total from the start

Let d(v) be the cheapest A-to-v cost found so far. Initially d(A)=0 and other labels are infinity, meaning no route has yet been found. Choose the globally smallest tentative distance among all unsettled vertices, settle that vertex u, and relax adjacent edges using d(v)=min(d(v),d(u)+w). The choice compares accumulated costs from A, not individual outgoing edges from the current location.

### Example 1: Settle a minimum cost of six

Use undirected edges AB2, AC5, BC1, BD5, CD1, DE2 and CE7. Settling A gives B2 and C5. Settling B2 improves C to three and gives D7. Settling C3 improves D to four and gives E10. Settling D4 improves E to six; finally settle E6. Following the improving connections reconstructs A-B-C-D-E, with cost 2+1+1+2=6.

The two-edge route A-C-E costs twelve, so fewer edges need not be cheaper. The audit table records every label after each settled vertex relaxes its neighbours. Infinity is not an actual long edge. A claimed route must use existing edges in permitted directions, even when its listed total happens to match the optimum.

### Example 2: A local trap, ties and disconnection

Replace the graph by AB1, AC3, BE9, CD1, DE1. Locally choosing AB because it costs one leads to A-B-E costing ten. A-C-D-E costs only 3+1+1=5. Dijkstra still retains C’s tentative label three after settling B. It can next settle C and later improve E; it does not commit the final route to the vertex selected in the previous round.

In the diamond AB2, BE3, AC1, CE4, both A-B-E and A-C-E cost five and both are optimal. If the only edges are AB2, BC1 and CD1 while E is isolated, the goal is unreachable. Do not invent D-E. With zero-weight edges AB0 and BC0, plus CE2 and AE2, both A-E and A-B-C-E are shortest. A zero-cost edge is still an existing connection.

## Why nonnegative weights matter

Once the globally smallest unsettled distance is chosen, a detour through other unsettled vertices cannot lower it by adding nonnegative remaining costs. Negative edges invalidate this settling argument. Getting one small negative-edge example right by coincidence would not establish the algorithm’s general validity there.

The workbench uses at most six vertices. Route checking verifies the endpoints, distinct vertices, existing connections, direction and total cost. Every tied minimum simple path is accepted. In a nonnegative graph, deleting a repeated-vertex cycle cannot increase cost, so restricting answers to simple paths preserves the optimum value. Zero-cost cycles may give additional optimal walks, but those are outside the stated answer format.

Drawn lengths serve layout only; read edge numbers for cost. The exit asks for the sequence of labels, the local-greedy counterexample and the algorithm’s assumptions, all reviewed by a person. Ask whether a comparison concerns a single edge or total distance from A, then ask why another equal-cost route should also be accepted. Establish the graph’s meaning before treating the algorithm as a memorised procedure.
