# 坐标形式的向量

点B的位置是(3,4)，从A到B的位移也一定是(3,4)吗？只有A在原点时才可以这样读。坐标形式要记录“改变了多少”，而不是只看最后在哪里。

## 一条核心想法

**AB=(xB−xA,yB−yA)，即终点减起点。** 这里把点的坐标对参与运算，得到位移分量。BA交换了起终点，因此BA=−AB。相同坐标系中，B的位置可由A的位置加AB恢复。

点坐标和向量分量都能写成数对，却描述不同对象。共同平移使点坐标改变，差向量中的平移项互相抵消。中点仍是一个点，其位置随平移改变，不应因为向量不变就误判中点也不动。

### 例1：差向量、反向与恢复终点

A=(−2,1)、B=(3,4)，则AB=(3−(−2),4−1)=(5,3)。若算成(−5,−3)，得到的是BA。若直接抄(3,4)，则把B的坐标当成位移，忽略了非零起点。

另取A=(1,−2)、AB=(3,4)，终点B=A+AB=(4,2)。将B−A再算一次，(4−1,2−(−2))=(3,4)，可独立核对。已知位移与已知终点的两种问题，所问对象不能混淆。

### 例2：中点移动，向量不变

A=(−2,1)、B=(3,4)的中点为M=((−2+3)/2,(1+4)/2)=(1/2,5/2)。注意不是AB/2=(5/2,3/2)：后者是从A走到中点的位移，不是中点本身的位置。

同时平移t=(2,−1)，得A′=(0,0)、B′=(5,3)。A′B′=(5,3)与原AB相同，因为(B+t)−(A+t)=B−A。中点变为M′=(5/2,3/2)=M+t。此例中A′恰为原点，所以新中点位置与半位移相同；这是一种特殊起点，不能倒推所有情况都如此。

## 坐标工作台

改变共同平移，先解释为什么向量预测保持，而中点位置需要重新算。反转AB/BA时，向量分量改号，中点保持。选择B=A时，箭头变成零向量，图上合并重合点的字母，不另外画一个假方向。

图的两轴同单位刻度，精确分数在表中保留。比如模长|(2,−3)|=√(4+9)=√13，不把负分量在平方时丢掉括号，也不把13当成长度。图形用于观察，数对和根式用于确切核验。

## 家长核对与迁移

出口同时问AB与中点如何平移，要求用消去平移项的等式说明，而不是只报两串数。家长可以临时更换坐标原点，再让孩子核对起终点差不变。下课用分量计算数量积，最后用点和向量之间的关系恢复平行四边形第四点。

# Coordinate vectors

If B has position (3,4), must displacement from A to B also be (3,4)? Only when A is the origin. Components describe how position changes, not merely where it finishes.

## One central idea

**AB=(xB−xA,yB−yA): endpoint minus start.** Arithmetic on the two point-coordinate pairs gives displacement components. BA reverses the endpoints and equals −AB. In one coordinate system, adding AB to position A recovers position B.

Point positions and vector components can both be written as pairs but describe different objects. Translating both points changes their positions while the common shift cancels from their difference. A midpoint remains a point and moves under translation; an unchanged vector does not make every position unchanged.

### Example 1: Difference, reversal and endpoint recovery

For A=(−2,1), B=(3,4), AB=(3−(−2),4−1)=(5,3). The answer (−5,−3) would be BA. Copying (3,4) instead would confuse the endpoint with displacement and ignore the nonzero start.

For A=(1,−2) and AB=(3,4), the endpoint is B=A+AB=(4,2). Rechecking B−A gives (4−1,2−(−2))=(3,4), independently auditing the result. A known displacement and a known endpoint lead to different questions, so identify the requested object first.

### Example 2: The midpoint moves while the vector stays fixed

The midpoint of A=(−2,1), B=(3,4) is M=((−2+3)/2,(1+4)/2)=(1/2,5/2). It is not AB/2=(5/2,3/2): that pair describes displacement from A to the midpoint, not the midpoint’s position.

Translate both points by t=(2,−1), obtaining A′=(0,0), B′=(5,3). Then A′B′ remains (5,3), because (B+t)−(A+t)=B−A. The midpoint becomes M′=(5/2,3/2)=M+t. Since the new A′ happens to be the origin, the new midpoint position equals half the displacement. That special start cannot justify the same claim for every pair of points.

## Coordinate workbench

Change the common translation and explain why the vector prediction stays while midpoint position resets. Reversing AB to BA reverses components but leaves the midpoint unchanged. Selecting B=A gives the zero vector; coincident labels combine without inventing a direction.

Both axes use the same length scale and exact fractions remain available in the audit table. For instance |(2,−3)|=√(4+9)=√13: square the entire negative component and do not report 13 as a length. The diagram supports observation while pairs and radicals provide exact checks.
