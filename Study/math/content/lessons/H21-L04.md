# 共轭复数与模

实轴反射保留距离，却不等于把整个复数取负。本课用**z与其共轭的乘积**连接代数与原点距离。

## 一条核心想法

对z=a+bi，共轭conj(z)=a−bi，只改变虚部系数。点(a,b)变(a,−b)，关于实轴反射；负数−z=−a−bi却把两个系数都取反，通常是另一个点。

乘积(a+bi)(a−bi)=a²−b²i²=a²+b²为非负实数。定义模|z|=sqrt(a²+b²)，取非负平方根，它正是点(a,b)到原点的距离。因此z·conj(z)=|z|²，而不是一般的|z|或z²。

### 例1：3−4i的反射、平方模与模

z=3−4i的共轭为3+4i；其负数为−3+4i，实部不同，两者不相等。复平面点(3,−4)反射为(3,4)，横坐标保留，纵坐标变号。

z·conj(z)=(3−4i)(3+4i)=9−16i²=25。这个25是平方模。再开非负根得|z|=5，不能把25当距离，也不能给“±5”作为模。方程t²=25有两个实根，但距离定义只取非负的一个。

直角三角形的两条直角边长是|3|=3和|−4|=4，斜边5。把虚部取负不改变平方，故|3+4i|也为5。注意z²=(3−4i)²=−7−24i，与实数平方模25完全不同。

### 例2：无理距离、零模与共轭等于取负的条件

z=1+i对应(1,1)，平方模2，模√2。这个距离不是复数1+i，也不是平方模2；正式题接受sqrt(2)等精确根式，而有限小数仅为近似。

对z=0，有a=b=0，模0，共轭0。反过来，若|z|=0，则a²+b²=0。两个非负平方的和为0，只能a=b=0，所以z=0。不能把“模非负”误读成“模严格正”。

若问什么时候conj(z)=−z，比较a−bi与−a−bi，只须a=−a，即a=0，虚部条件自动一致。因此纯虚数会有这个性质，0也有；它不适用于所有复数。

## 反射与距离工作台

先预测共轭、平方模和精确模，再展开核验。共轭反射开关只改图示，原答案保留。把虚部从−4换成4时，共轭问题改变，但平方模和模不变，相关预测继续保留，帮助解释平方为何消除这个符号差。

两轴采用同单位标尺，原点到z的线段才可按欧氏距离理解。三角形在a=0或b=0时退化成线段，0时为点；不画成有虚构面积的三角形，也不给零箭头制造方向。实轴点与其共轭重合，两个名字仍保留。

# Conjugate and modulus

Reflection in the real axis preserves distance but generally differs from negating a whole number. This lesson connects algebra and origin-distance through **the product of z and its conjugate**.

## One central idea

For z=a+bi, the conjugate is a−bi, changing only the imaginary coefficient. Point (a,b) reflects to (a,−b). Negation −z=−a−bi reverses both coefficients and usually gives a different point.

The product (a+bi)(a−bi)=a²−b²i²=a²+b² is a nonnegative real number. Modulus |z|=sqrt(a²+b²) is its nonnegative root and the distance from (a,b) to the origin. Thus z·conj(z)=|z|², not generally |z| or z².

### Example 1: Reflection, squared modulus and modulus of 3−4i

The conjugate of 3−4i is 3+4i, while its negative is −3+4i. Their real parts differ. Geometrically (3,−4) reflects to (3,4), retaining the horizontal coordinate and reversing the vertical one.

Multiplication gives (3−4i)(3+4i)=9−16i²=25. This is the squared modulus. Its nonnegative root gives |z|=5. Twenty-five is not the distance, and ±5 is not the modulus. Although t²=25 has two real roots, a distance definition selects the nonnegative one.

The right triangle has leg lengths |3|=3 and |−4|=4, giving hypotenuse five. Negating the imaginary coefficient leaves its square unchanged, so 3+4i also has modulus five. By contrast, z²=(3−4i)²=−7−24i, quite different from the real squared modulus twenty-five.

### Example 2: Irrational distance, zero modulus and a special equality

The number 1+i corresponds to (1,1), with squared modulus two and modulus √2. Its distance is neither the complex number 1+i nor the squared value two. Exact tasks accept equivalent radical forms such as sqrt(2); a finite decimal is only an approximation.

Zero has both coefficients zero, modulus zero and conjugate zero. Conversely, if |z|=0 then a²+b²=0. The sum of two nonnegative squares vanishes only when both coefficients are zero, so z=0. Nonnegative does not mean strictly positive.

To find when conj(z)=−z, compare a−bi with −a−bi. This requires a=−a, hence a=0; imaginary coefficients already agree. Purely imaginary numbers satisfy this condition, as does zero, but general complex numbers do not.

## Reflection and distance workbench

Predict the conjugate, squared modulus and exact modulus before opening the audit. Reflection is a display choice and preserves answers. Changing b from −4 to four changes the conjugate but not squared modulus or modulus; those unaffected predictions remain, illustrating the role of squaring.

Both axes use equal unit lengths, supporting a Euclidean distance interpretation. If a or b is zero the right triangle degenerates to a segment, and at zero to a point. It is not displayed as a triangle with invented area or a zero arrow with direction. A real point and its conjugate coincide while retaining both identities.
