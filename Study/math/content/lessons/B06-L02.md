## 让问号在图里有一个位置

条形模型用长度表示数量关系，帮助看清整体、部分、两量的差距或相等组。本课同一幅图里的条形共用一个比例；长度代表题中给定的数量，不是家里的实际测量尺。图只是关系的表达，最后仍要用给定数值列式。

整体与部分的图把两段首尾相接。比较图让两条起点对齐，多出来的一段表示差距。相等组用同样大小的段，每段代表每组数量。三种图的摆法不同，是因为它们表达的关系不同，而不是任意换一个漂亮布局。

### 例 1 · 整体已知，另一部分未知

1. 两段步道合计 **24 m**，其中一段 **9 m**，另一段未知。
2. 先画整体，再把九米部分标在里面；问号放在另一部分。
3. 关系为 **9+另一部分=24**。
4. 未知部分是 **24−9=15 m**，核查 **9+15=24**。
5. 如果已知两部分九米与十五米而问整体，就用九加十五。关系没有变，改变的是未知的位置。

### 例 2 · “多四张”不一定做加法

1. Mina 有 **9 张**卡，比 Noah **多 4 张**，问 Noah 有几张。
2. Mina 是较大量；两条起点对齐，超出 Noah 的部分是四张。
3. 关系为 **Noah+4=9**，所以 **Noah=9−4=5 张**。
4. 核查 **5+4=9**，符合 Mina 比 Noah 多四张。
5. 做九加四会求出一个比 Mina 还大的量，不符合本题的较小量。单词“多”描述关系，不能直接代替判断未知。

### 比较与相等组

十七米和九米两条步道相差八米，图里九米的共同部分先对齐，差距是 **17−9=8 m**，不是两条合计二十六米。若给九米和差距八米，问较长步道，则要做 **9+8=17 m**。同一比较关系也能有不同未知。

四袋每袋六张卡，是四个相等的六张段，**6+6+6+6=4×6=24 张**。这里四是袋数，六是每袋张数，不能把它们当两个卡片部分做四加六。图中组数标签与每组数量标签都要保留。

工作台可以切换三种关系、调整给定量，并在整体或部分、差距或较大量上放问号。调整之后请重新列式，再展开数值核对。图按同一比例生成，不把零数量画成有正长度的条；即使画得很短，读数仍以标签为准。

你也可以用纸笔画简略条形，不必把毫米都量准确，但必须标清量的名称、已知数、单位和问号。不要把明显不同的已知量画成“同样大所以相等”；图形相似不能代替题目条件。

**带走一个发现：** 我先说“这几段怎样联系”，再指出问号在哪里；运算来自关系与未知的位置，不来自一个孤立关键词。

## Give the unknown a place in the diagram

A bar model represents a relationship among a whole and parts, compared quantities or equal groups. Bars within one workshop diagram share a scale. Their lengths represent supplied quantities rather than an actual household ruler; use stated numerical data for calculation.

Part-whole bars join end to end. Comparison bars align their starts, with the excess representing a difference. Equal-group segments have matching sizes, each representing quantity per group. These layouts differ because their relationships differ.

### Example 1 · Known whole, unknown part

1. Two track sections total **24 m**, with one section **9 m** and the other unknown.
2. Draw the whole and label the nine-metre part inside it. Place a question mark on the other part.
3. The relationship is **9+other part=24**.
4. Find **24−9=15 m**, checked by **9+15=24**.
5. If both parts nine and fifteen were known and the whole were asked, addition would apply. The relationship remains; the unknown moves.

### Example 2 · “Four more” need not instruct addition

1. Mina has **9 cards**, **4 more than Noah**. Find Noah’s count.
2. Mina has the larger quantity. Aligned bars show four cards beyond Noah’s bar.
3. **Noah+4=9**, so **Noah=9−4=5 cards**.
4. Check **5+4=9**, consistent with Mina having four more.
5. Nine plus four would find a quantity larger than Mina’s, contrary to the requested smaller quantity. “More” describes a relationship, not an automatic operation.

### Comparison and equal groups

Tracks of seventeen and nine metres differ by **17−9=8 m**, excluding the aligned common nine metres. Their total twenty-six metres answers another question. Given nine metres and an eight-metre difference, the longer track is instead **9+8=17 m**.

Four bags of six cards contain four equal six-card segments: **6+6+6+6=4×6=24 cards**. Four counts bags and six counts cards per bag; they are not two card-count parts to add. Keep labels for both group count and quantity per group.

Switch workshop relationships, adjust givens and move the unknown between whole and part or gap and larger quantity. Make an expression before checking numerically. Zero quantities retain zero bar width; read labels rather than estimate from tiny screen lengths.

A hand-drawn sketch need not be measured perfectly, but it should name quantities, known values, units and the unknown. Drawing different known quantities as identical cannot establish equality. Visual resemblance is not a replacement for given conditions.

**Takeaway:** describe how quantities connect and locate the unknown. Operations follow that relationship and unknown position rather than one isolated word.
