# 向他人解释完整推理

完整解释要让没看过题目的人知道你问什么、用什么前提、为什么结论成立。本课只论证一个主张：在已声明名义雨水模型、容量可取任意非负整数升时，最小够用容量是90L。这里暂不加入目录尺寸与预算，那是下一课的新约束。

## 把主张和范围写在前面

题设为屋顶20m²、收集率3/4，五天雨量[6,0,0,6,0]mm，每日需求30L，初始存水0，无蒸发备用水，先收后用。进水I=15r，日末水量为max(0,min(C,s+I)−30)，并另记实际供水和缺口。证明“最小”需要两个方向：更小者都不行，以及90确实可以。

### 例1：必要下界和充分构造

前三天只有首日有进水。当天收集之后水箱最多存C升，随后这三天合计需3×30=90L，中间没有新进水。因此C≥90是必要条件，排除了所有小于90的容量，不只是某几个试过的数。

取C=90。首日收90供30余60；第二日余30；第三日余0；第四日收90供30余60；第五日余30。每天供水均30，无缺口，证明90充分。必要与充分合在一起，才得到最小90。C89交叉检查：首日余59，次日余29，第三日缺1L，符合下界预测。

### 例2：正确计算，也可能支持不了所写结论

若只列C100每天够用的账本，就证明了100可行，尚未证明它最小；90的可行构造直接反驳“100最小”。只说“图上90看起来刚好”也不能排除89.5等候选；本课虽限定整数，仍需说明排除范围与理由，不能把画图窗口当作完整数学域。

再看“这个水箱在任何天气都保证够用”。原证明使用固定五天雨量与固定收集率，根本没有覆盖任意天气。更长干期、较小收集率或更高需求会改变输入。正确的范围内结果，不应被扩大成范围外保证；需要改变条件时明确重建模型，而不是悄悄沿用结论。

## 让证据链能被别人检查

一段可读论证依次包含问题、变量单位、假设、模型、必要性、充分性、核验和局限。顺序可以调整，但每个结论要说明依赖哪一项证据。账本是充分构造，下界是对全部更小者的论证，边界89是额外交叉核验；三者职责不同。

工作台可选60、89、90、100或120，分别判断“每天够用”和“最小”两个命题。核验列账本及前一个整数容量的缺口。测试某个候选和它前一项，对任意模型并不自动证明全域最小；本课还使用已证明的供水单调性与必要下界，不能把局部邻点比较当成通用证明术。

书写时用“在这些假设下”限定结论，标明所有数据为虚构。若读者无法辨别给定事实、选择变量和新增假设，再漂亮的算式也难以审查。指出模型没有包含蒸发、维护和真实天气，不是削弱推理，而是说明它究竟证明了什么。

出口交给人阅读完整论证，不靠自动匹配“必要、充分、90”几个词判对。家长可追问“你证明的是够用还是最小”和“没看过题目的人知道前提吗”；若只会报答案，回到H28的假设与结果说明，再用自己的话串起证据链。

# Presenting a complete argument

A complete explanation lets a reader new to the task identify the question, assumptions and reasons for the conclusion. Prove one claim here: in the stated nominal rainwater model, allowing any nonnegative integer-litre capacity, the minimum sufficient tank is 90 L. Catalog sizes and budget are not yet constraints; those enter the next lesson.

## State the claim and its scope

Use roof 20 m², capture fraction 3/4, rainfall [6,0,0,6,0] mm, demand 30 L/day, initial zero, no evaporation or backup, and collection before use. Inflow is I=15r. Closing storage is max(0,min(C,s+I)−30), with supplied volume and deficit recorded separately. “Minimum” requires both excluding every smaller candidate and showing ninety works.

### Example 1: A necessary bound and sufficient construction

Only day one brings inflow for the first three days. After that collection at most C litres are stored, while three demands total ninety and no further inflow arrives during the span. Thus C≥90 is necessary. This excludes all smaller capacities, not just a handful of tested values.

Set C=90. Day one collects ninety, serves thirty and retains sixty. Subsequent closing stores are thirty, zero, sixty and thirty. Every day supplies thirty without deficit, proving sufficiency. Combining both directions establishes the minimum. A boundary check at C89 gives closing stores fifty-nine and twenty-nine, then a one-litre deficit on day three, consistent with the bound.

### Example 2: Correct arithmetic, unsupported conclusion

A full ledger showing C100 works proves feasibility, not minimality. The feasible C90 construction directly refutes “100 is minimal.” Saying ninety looks just right on a plot does not exclude other permitted values. This lesson uses integers, but its domain and exclusion argument must still be explicit; a viewing window is not the mathematical domain.

The statement “this tank works in every weather” is also unsupported. The proof uses a fixed five-day rainfall sequence and fixed capture fraction. A longer dry spell, poorer capture or higher demand changes the inputs. A valid scoped result must not be expanded into an all-weather guarantee. Revise conditions and recompute explicitly when asking a different question.

## Make the evidence chain inspectable

An argument identifies question, variables and units, assumptions, model, necessity, sufficiency, checks and limitations. Each conclusion should show what supports it. The ledger supplies a construction; the lower bound excludes every smaller choice; the C89 boundary calculation cross-checks the result. Their roles differ.

The workbench offers candidate capacities 60,89,90,100 and120 and separates “supplies every day” from “is minimal.” The audit includes the daily ledger and the preceding integer capacity’s deficit. Testing one candidate and its neighbour is not a universal optimality method. Here the established monotone supply property and necessary bound provide the missing global reasoning.

Use “under these assumptions” and identify fictional data. Readers must distinguish supplied facts, decisions and additional assumptions. Stating that evaporation, maintenance and real weather are outside this model clarifies what the proof actually establishes. The exit requires human review of the full argument rather than keyword matching. Ask whether feasibility or minimality was proved and whether a new reader can find the assumptions. Return to the earlier communication lesson if the learner can report a number but cannot connect its supporting evidence.
