# 标准正态与z分数

原值减去均值、再除以正标准差，得到z=(x−μ)/σ。它表示有方向的“离均值几个标准差”，没有单位。z可以负、为零或大于1；它不是概率。这个代数变换适用于标准差存在且为正的变量，并不会自动让分布变成正态。

## 标准化与查表分两步

若X确实服从N(μ,σ²)，则Z=(X−μ)/σ服从N(0,1)。先标准化，把区间端点转换；再用标准正态累计函数Φ(z)=P(Z≤z)求概率。本课的Φ定义为整段左尾面积，不是从0到z的一小段。

有的参考表给P(0≤Z≤|z|)。对正z要加1/2才成左尾；对负z用对称性。查表前必须看标题、图示与定义，不能只记行列读数。原量可由x=μ+σz反算；σ=0时不能除，需另按退化分布解释。

### 例1：原值、方向与逆变换

假设X~N(100,10²)。原值85对应z=(85−100)/10=−3/2，表示低于均值1.5个标准差。115对应1.5，而z=2对应原值100+10×2=120。

原值85在均值不同或标准差不同的另一组中，z可能不同。即使两组z相同，也只表示相对于各自均值和标准差的位置相同；若要说相同百分位，还要知道分布形状。正态模型中z1左尾约0.8413，其他分布不必如此。

比较不同测量目标还需说明“更大”是否代表同一意义。例如某种误差越小越好，成绩通常越大越好，仅移除单位不使两者可直接排名。数学标准化不能替代情境判断。

### 例2：左尾、区间与右尾

同一正态模型，P(X≤115)=Φ(1.5)≈0.9332。NIST表若给0到1.5的面积约0.43319，要先加0.5，不能直接把0.43319当左尾。

P(85≤X≤115)=Φ(1.5)−Φ(−1.5)≈0.8664。利用对称性Φ(−1.5)=1−Φ(1.5)也可核验。区间作累计差，不是把两个左尾相加。

P(X>120)=1−Φ(2)≈0.0228。0.9772是另一侧左尾；两数加起来约1。先用足够精度算完整式，再按题意保留四位小数，避免先舍入每个中间量引入额外误差。

## 换坐标不换事件

工作台可显示原坐标或标准正态坐标，着色对应同一个事件。原坐标密度f_X(x)=φ(z)/σ，而dx=σdz，因此f_X(x)dx=φ(z)dz，概率面积保持；不能只把横轴变窄却保持原高度不变。

改变μ与σ时，选中位置按相同相对标准差数变化，原值标签随之更新，所以z和相对事件概率可保留。若只改变原值位置，则重新核z及尾方向。对σ0，工作台采用正标准差选择，数学模型明确拒绝除零。

参考[NIST标准正态表及其定义](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/eda/section3/eda3671.htm)。出口报告要保留原模型、标准化式、表定义、事件方向和近似标记，并说清“标准化”本身不证明正态性。

# Standard normal and z-scores

The score z=(x−μ)/σ measures signed distance from the mean in units of positive SD. It is unitless and can be negative, zero or greater than one. It is not a probability. Standardisation does not turn arbitrary data into a normal distribution.

## Separate transformation from probability lookup

If X follows N(μ,σ²), then Z=(X−μ)/σ follows N(0,1). Convert endpoints first, then use Φ(z)=P(Z≤z). Here Φ denotes the full left-tail probability.

Some tables instead give area from zero to|z|. For positive z, add one-half to obtain a left tail; for negative z, use symmetry. Check the table definition before reading numbers. The inverse transformation is x=μ+σz. Zero SD prevents division and requires a separate degenerate-distribution analysis.

### Example 1: Direction and inverse conversion

Assume X~N(100,10²). The value85 gives z=(85−100)/10=−3/2, or1.5 SDs below the mean. The value115 gives1.5. A z-score of two corresponds to 100+10×2=120.

The same raw value can have a different relative position in a group with another mean or spread. Equal z-scores do not by themselves establish equal percentiles: distribution shape matters. Under normality, z=1 has left-tail probability approximately0.8413; other distributions can differ.

Comparisons across measurement goals also need interpretation. A larger score and a larger error may have opposite meanings. Removing units does not automatically justify ranking unrelated quantities.

### Example 2: Left tail, interval and right tail

For the same model, P(X≤115)=Φ(1.5)≈0.9332. A table reporting the zero-to 1.5 area, approximately0.43319, requires adding0.5 before it becomes a left-tail probability.

The interval85 through115 has probability Φ(1.5)−Φ(−1.5)≈0.8664. Symmetry gives Φ(−1.5)=1−Φ(1.5). Subtract cumulative probabilities for an interval rather than adding the two left tails.

For values above120, use1−Φ(2)≈0.0228. The value0.9772 describes the opposite left tail. Complete the calculation with sufficient precision before rounding to four decimal places; prematurely rounded intermediate values can add error.

## Preserve the event when coordinates change

Switching between original and standard coordinates preserves the probability of the corresponding shaded event. The density transforms as f_X(x)=φ(z)/σ and the width as dx=σdz, so their product and integrated probability agree. Changing horizontal units alone without the density adjustment would misrepresent area.

In the workbench, changing mean or SD moves the selected raw value with the same relative position. The raw labels update while z and the corresponding relative probability remain valid. Moving the raw position itself requires a new score and event check. Positive-SD controls avoid division by zero, which the model also rejects explicitly.

The [NIST standard-normal table](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/eda/section3/eda3671.htm) states its zero-to-z convention. A complete report should state the original model, transformation, table convention, event direction and approximation. Standardisation alone is never evidence of normality.
