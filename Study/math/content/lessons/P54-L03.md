## 先让份一样大，再数总份数

两个三分之一与一个四分之一，都是“份”，却不是同样大的份。不能直接把分子相加，也不能把分母相加。先确认同一整体，再通过**通分**把各量写成相同单位的份；通分保持原来的量，只增加分割线。

共同分母必须是两个分母的公倍数。最小公倍数通常让格子最少，但其他正公倍数也有效。每个分数有自己的倍数：分母乘几，分子也乘几。两分母原先不同，不应机械地把两个分子都乘同一个数。

### 例 1 · 三分之一与四分之一对齐

1. 求 **2/3+1/4**。3 与 4 的最小公倍数是 **12**。
2. 每个三分之一分成四小份：**2/3=8/12**，分子分母同乘四。
3. 每个四分之一分成三小份：**1/4=3/12**，分子分母同乘三。
4. 数同样大小的十二分之一：**8/12+3/12=11/12**。
5. 核查 **11/12−3/12=8/12=2/3**。和小于 1，也大于各个非零加数，符合图形。

### 例 2 · 非最小共同分母也有效

1. 求 **1/4+1/6**，先用 **12**：**3/12+2/12=5/12**。
2. 特意改用 **24**，仍可用，因为 24 同时被 4 与 6 整除。
3. **1/4=6/24**，**1/6=4/24**，所以和为 **10/24**。
4. **10/24=5/12**，总量没变。24 份的每份更小，份数相应更多。
5. 如果题目要求“分母为 24”，写 10/24；如果要求“最简”，写 5/12。两者不能被误说成数量不相等。

### 估计、超过一与单位检查

3/4+2/3：共同分母 12，得 9/12+8/12=17/12。它大于 1，因为两量都大于 1/2；也小于 2，因为各量小于 1。可写最简假分数 17/12，或在允许时写 1 5/12。不能只保留多出的 5/12 而丢掉一整个整体。

2/5 km+1/10 km=4/10 km+1/10 km=5/10 km=1/2 km。测量单位一致与分数单位一致是两次检查：先都为千米，再都用十分之一千米。2/5 km 与 1/10 m 若不先转换实际长度单位，直接通分并相加会得到错误情境答案。

工作台让你选择候选共同分母，并解释有效与无效选择。通过核对才显示细分，之后才合并。大分母会产生真实数量的细格，图可横向滚动，不用画少数宽格假装已经切出全部份。观察 A、B 各自的涂色总宽度；同倍细分应保留端点。

**带走一个发现：** 通分是在同一整体上换一把更细的计数尺。各分子和自己的分母同倍改变；先等份，再相加，最后按题意表示。

## Equalise piece size before counting the total

Two thirds and one quarter are pieces of different sizes. Do not directly add their numerators or denominators. Check a shared whole, then use a **common denominator** to count each amount in the same fraction unit. Conversion retains the amount while adding partition lines.

A common denominator must be a positive common multiple. The least common multiple normally uses fewer tiles, but other common multiples also work. Each fraction has its own scale factor: multiply its numerator and denominator together. Different original denominators may need different factors.

### Example 1 · Align thirds and quarters

1. Find **2/3+1/4**. The least common multiple of 3 and 4 is **12**.
2. Split each third into four: **2/3=8/12**, scaling numerator and denominator by four.
3. Split each quarter into three: **1/4=3/12**, scaling both by three.
4. Count twelfths: **8/12+3/12=11/12**.
5. Check **11/12−3/12=8/12=2/3**. The sum is below one and above either nonzero addend.

### Example 2 · A nonleast denominator also works

1. For **1/4+1/6**, denominator **12** gives **3/12+2/12=5/12**.
2. Deliberately use **24**, divisible by both 4 and 6.
3. **1/4=6/24** and **1/6=4/24**, so the sum is **10/24**.
4. **10/24=5/12**: the amount is unchanged, with more smaller pieces.
5. If denominator 24 is requested, write 10/24. If simplest form is requested, write 5/12. These are equivalent quantities.

### Estimation, sums above one and measurement units

3/4+2/3=9/12+8/12=17/12. Each addend exceeds one half, so their sum exceeds one; each is below one, so the sum is below two. Use simplest improper fraction 17/12 or, where allowed, mixed number 1 5/12. Do not discard the full whole and retain only 5/12.

For a path, 2/5 km+1/10 km=4/10 km+1/10 km=1/2 km. Check measurement units first, then fraction units: kilometres, followed by tenths of a kilometre. Kilometres and metres need conversion before addition even if their written fractions can be given a common denominator.

Choose a candidate denominator in the workshop. Only a valid common multiple reveals the refinement, followed separately by joining. High denominators have the actual number of fine tiles, with horizontal scrolling rather than a misleading few large tiles. Inspect each known amount’s shaded width: equivalent refinement retains its endpoint.

**Takeaway:** a common denominator gives a finer counting ruler on one whole. Scale each numerator with its own denominator, count equal-size pieces, then represent as requested.
