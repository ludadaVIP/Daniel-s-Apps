## 先知道问题要找什么

应用题不是“把出现的数字做一次运算”。先把故事中的量分开：哪些已经给出，哪个还不知道，每个数在计什么。最后的问题句通常指出未知；同一句故事可以问全长、已走长度或剩余长度，答案不会总是同一个数。

“已知”也不等于“这次一定要用”。四只鸟是已经给出的事实，但它不能帮助求步道剩余长度。整理时可以把卡片分成与目标有关的已知、要找的量、本题不用的信息。先组织关系，再选择加减乘除。

### 例 1 · 步道的全长与剩余

1. 步道全长 **18 m**，已经走了 **7 m**，草地上另有四只鸟。
2. 问的是剩余长度，单位是米；十八米是整体，七米是其中一部分。
3. 关系为“已走+剩余=全长”，也就是 **7+剩余=18**。
4. 因此剩余 **18−7=11 m**。不能把全长与它的一部分又相加。
5. 核查 **7+11=18**；鸟数不参与长度关系，换成六只鸟，剩余仍为十一米。

### 例 2 · 未知换成剪下的部分

1. 纸带总长 **31 cm**，剪下一段后剩 **13 cm**。
2. 这次未知是剪下的长度，不是“还有多少”。
3. “剪下+剩余=原总长”，所以 **剪下=31−13=18 cm**。
4. 用 **18+13=31** 回到原纸带；这个核查也确认了各数的角色。
5. 十八厘米应解释为剪下的部分，不能只写“十八”让读者猜它是什么。

### 单位帮助建立关系

长度可以用厘米或米计，张数数的是卡片，钱数计的是元。即使两个数都写四，它们也可能是四盒与每盒四张，角色不同，不能直接相加。先给数字配上“对象+单位”，能发现许多不合理的算式。

同一种量换单位后才能直接合并。资料写 **3 m 40 cm** 时，先用 **1 m=100 cm**，整理为 **300+40=340 cm**；不是三加四十得到四十三厘米。换单位没有改变纸带或步道本身。整理的是同一个量的表示方式，不是创造一个新长度。

也有题目只要求读取已知。例如“总长十八厘米，剪去七厘米，给出的总长是多少？”答案直接是十八厘米，不需要做减法。每次重新确认未知，避免看见相同数字就重复上一题的运算。

工作台里请先给四张信息卡分角色，再说单位。图的问号放在剩余部分；没有直接写出结果，是让你先说明关系。遇到阅读困难，可由家长读故事，但仍由你指出未知在哪里。

**带走一个发现：** 我可以先说“我知道什么、我要找什么、这些量用什么单位”，再写关系；这比从关键词猜运算更可靠。

## Know what the question asks before calculating

A word problem is not an instruction to combine every printed number. Separate quantities in the story: what is given, what is unknown, and what each number counts. The question sentence usually identifies the unknown. One story can ask for full length, walked length or remaining length, with different answers.

A given fact is not necessarily relevant. Four birds are a known fact, but do not determine remaining track length. Sort cards into relevant knowns, the requested unknown and information unused here. Organise the relationship before choosing an operation.

### Example 1 · Full and remaining track length

1. The full track is **18 m**, **7 m** have been walked, and there are four birds on the grass.
2. Remaining length in metres is unknown. Eighteen metres is the whole; seven metres is one part.
3. Walked plus remaining equals full length: **7+remaining=18**.
4. Therefore **18−7=11 m** remain. Do not add a whole to a part already included within it.
5. Check **7+11=18**. Changing the bird count to six leaves the remaining length unchanged.

### Example 2 · Ask for the removed part instead

1. A strip originally measures **31 cm**, with **13 cm** remaining after a piece is cut off.
2. The unknown is removed length rather than remaining length.
3. Removed plus remaining equals original, so **removed=31−13=18 cm**.
4. Check **18+13=31**, recovering the original strip and confirming each quantity’s role.
5. Eighteen centimetres describes the removed piece. A bare “eighteen” does not identify what was found.

### Units help organise the relationship

Lengths may use centimetres or metres, counts may refer to cards, and money may use dollars. Four boxes and four cards per box have different roles even though both numbers are four. Naming each number’s object and unit helps expose unsuitable expressions.

Matching kinds of quantity need matching units before direct addition. Express **3 m 40 cm** using **1 m=100 cm**, giving **300+40=340 cm**, not forty-three centimetres. Conversion changes the representation, not the length itself.

Some questions ask only for a given fact. If a strip is eighteen centimetres and seven are cut off, “What full length was given?” still asks for eighteen, without subtraction. Re-identify the unknown each time instead of repeating an earlier operation because the numbers look familiar.

In the workshop, sort the information cards and name units first. The bar’s question mark identifies remaining length without printing its result. A parent may read the story aloud; you can still identify which quantity is unknown.

**Takeaway:** state what you know, what you seek and the units, then form a relationship instead of guessing an operation from a keyword.
