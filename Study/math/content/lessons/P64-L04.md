## 起点、变化、终点与距离各有角色

从 −3 出发加五，会到二。这里 **−3 是起点，+5 是带符号变化，2 是终点，五格是移动距离**。同一道题出现的这些量不能混写。看到终点二，不能说“只走了两格”。

在本课数轴中，加正数向右，加负数向左；减去一个变化是在撤销它。先用方向与完整步数解释，再写算式。到零只是可能的途中位置，尚有剩余步数就必须继续。

### 例 1 · 从负三跨零到二

1. 起点 **−3**，变化为 **加 +5**，所以向右五格。
2. 先右走 **三格**到 **0**。
3. 已走三格，还剩 **5−3=2 格**。
4. 再向右两格到 **+2**，所以 **−3+5=2**。
5. 核回：终点减变化 **2−5=−3**，恢复起点。移动距离五格与终点到零二格不是同一个距离。

工作台会把这段移动拆成两个按钮。先到零后，还会说明剩余步数；不能因为第一次点击停在零就把答案写零。箭头的方向与格数都要保留，不只看最后点的数码。

### 例 2 · 从正四减七到负三

1. 起点 **+4**，减去七对应向左七格。
2. 先左走 **四格**到零，还剩 **三格**。
3. 再向左三格到 **−3**，因此 **4−7=−3**。
4. 带符号变化为 **−7**，移动距离为 **7**，终点为 **−3**。
5. 加回七可核查 **−3+7=4**；不能把距离七写成 −7，也不能漏终点的负号。

**加负数：** −4+(−3) 表示从 −4 向左三格，得到 −7。括号帮助看清“加号是运算，−3 是带符号的项”。不能把负号忽略后向右走三格。

**减负数：** −3−(−5) 是撤销一个向左五格的变化，反向向右五格，得到二。这是加减的逆关系。核回可看 **2+(−5)=−3**；不需要把“负负得正”的乘法口号当作解释，因为这里正在讨论减法。

**到零与不移动：** −5+5=0 恰好到零，没有剩余步数跨到正侧。−3+0=−3 或 −3−0=−3 都不移动，不能画成一格。零本身没有正负类别，也不需要另造一个负零终点。

假想温度从 −2°C 升高七度，先两度到零，再五度到 +5°C。最终温度五度、升高七度是不同问题。读题必须确认问“最后是多少”“变化多少”还是“相隔多少”。这里只是整数刻度任务，没有要求测量真实温度。

改变起点、运算或带符号项时，工作台重置演示与反馈。结果超出原显示窗时，数轴会沿相同单位间隔扩展，在图内滚动，不把 −18 等结果截成边界数。先预测再逐步走，最后核对终点、变化、距离和撤销关系。

**家长陪学：** 让孩子先说方向与总步数，再问“到零用了多少，还剩多少”。允许箭头、数格或口头说明；答对却把变化与终点混为一谈，仍需继续讨论。**带走一个发现：** 带符号移动跨零时方向不丢、步数不丢，并用逆变化恢复起点。

## Start, change, endpoint and distance play different roles

Adding five from −3 reaches two. Here **−3 is the start, +5 the signed change, 2 the endpoint, and five units the travelled distance**. These quantities cannot replace one another. An endpoint of two does not mean only two steps were taken.

Adding a positive moves right and adding a negative moves left. Subtracting a change undoes it. Explain direction and complete step count before recording an equation. Zero may be intermediate; any remaining steps must still be completed.

### Example 1 · Cross zero from negative three to two

1. Start at **−3** and add **+5**, moving five right.
2. Move **three** rightward units to **0**.
3. Three are complete, leaving **5−3=2**.
4. Move two more right to **+2**, so **−3+5=2**.
5. Check endpoint minus change: **2−5=−3**, restoring the start. The five-unit travel differs from the endpoint’s two-unit distance to zero.

The workshop splits this move into two buttons. After reaching zero it identifies the remaining steps, so the first stop is not the final answer. Preserve both arrow direction and total distance rather than reading only the final digits.

### Example 2 · Subtract seven from positive four

1. Start at **+4**; subtracting seven moves seven left.
2. Move **four** left to zero, leaving **three**.
3. Continue three left to **−3**, so **4−7=−3**.
4. The signed change is **−7**, travelled distance **7**, and endpoint **−3**.
5. Check **−3+7=4**. A distance of seven is not negative seven, and the endpoint still needs its minus sign.

Adding a negative, as in −4+(−3), moves three left from −4 to −7. Parentheses separate the addition operation from the signed term −3. Discarding the term’s sign would incorrectly move right.

Subtracting a negative, as in −3−(−5), undoes a five-unit leftward change. Move five right to reach two. Check **2+(−5)=−3**. This is inverse addition/subtraction reasoning, not an explanation based on a multiplication slogan.

The calculation −5+5=0 reaches zero exactly, without steps remaining to cross to the positive side. In −3+0=−3 or −3−0=−3, there is no movement at all. Do not draw an extra unit step or invent a separate negative-zero endpoint.

A fictional temperature rising seven degrees from −2°C reaches zero after two degrees and +5°C after five more. The final five-degree reading and seven-degree increase answer different questions. Decide whether a question asks for a final value, a signed change or a separation; no real-temperature measurement is required.

Changing the start, operation or signed term resets the demonstration. If a result lies outside the initial window, the axis extends with equal units and scrolls locally rather than clipping a value such as −18 to a boundary. Predict, reveal stages, then check endpoint, change, distance and the inverse relation.

**For a parent:** ask for direction and total steps first, then how many reach zero and how many remain. Arrows, counted intervals and spoken reasoning are all valid. A correct answer still needs discussion if change and endpoint are confused. **Takeaway:** preserve direction and every step across zero, then undo the change to recover the start.
