# 负数相乘为何改变不等号

## 反射使顺序相反

数轴上 a<b 表示 a 在 b 左边。同乘正数会按正比例缩放，但不颠倒左右。同乘负数可以分成“乘绝对值缩放，再关于零反射”：反射把原来左边的位置放到右边，所以 a<b 在同乘非零负数 k 后变成 ka>kb。

另一种解释是差：若 a<b，则 b−a 为正。k 为负时，k(b−a) 为负，即 kb−ka<0，所以 kb<ka。这里依据的是 k 的符号，不是表达式中随便出现一个负号。

### 例 1：−2<3 的乘积

先预测：两数同乘 −2，原左数变成 `(−2)×(−2)=4`，原右数变成 `3×(−2)=−6`。数轴上四在负六右边，所以新关系为 `4>−6`，方向反转。

若只同乘正二，得到 −4<6，方向不变。若两数同加负二，得到 −4<1，也不变。加一个负数只是同向平移，不是反射；不能看到“负二”就把不等号翻转。

若同乘零，两个积都变成零，原来不相等的数合并。得到的相等不能让我们恢复原来的左右顺序，所以乘零不是求解中的可逆等价步骤。除零则没有定义，不能作为另一种选择。

### 例 2：负除数与端点

解 `−4x≤5`。两边同除 −4，相当于同乘负倒数 −1/4，因此把 ≤ 反向为 ≥，得 `x≥−5/4`。反向只改变方向，原来允许相等的性质保留。

核边界 x=−5/4：左边 −4×(−5/4)=5，五≤五，成立。取零得零≤五，成立。取负二得八≤五，为假。故应向负四分之五的右边涂色并实心；截断边界为 −1.2 会改变解集，不能替代精确值 −1.25 或 −5/4。

再看 `x/(−3)>2`。同乘负三反向，得 x<−6。负九代回得三>二成立，负六只给相等而被排除。正乘、负乘、负除都能通过数轴顺序解释，而不必背互相冲突的口令。

## 未知符号不能跳过

若有人把 a<b 两边同乘 x 后总写 ax>bx，必须先问 x 的符号。x>0 时保持；x<0 时反向；x=0 时两积相等、丢失大小信息。没有这些条件，统一反向不成立。含未知字母的除法还要先排除零，并考虑符号；本单元不使用这种无条件操作。

实验里改变 a、b 与 k，先预测再显示。原数与乘积分别画在相同刻度的数轴，标签 a/b 与 ka/kb 保留对应身份，避免只把两个积从小到大重新排序而看不出反转。若 a=b，任何共同常数仍使两积相等，这与原两数严格有序时的反射讨论要分清。

家长请孩子分别举一个“有负号但不反向”与“同除负数须反向”的例子，并核边界是否仍包含。解释应落到具体数、操作与顺序，不只说“数学规定如此”。

# Why inequality signs reverse

## Reflection reverses order

On a number line, a<b puts a left of b. Positive multiplication scales positions without reversing them. Negative multiplication scales by the absolute value and reflects about zero. Reflection sends the formerly left position to the right, so for negative nonzero k, a<b becomes ka>kb.

The difference explains the same fact. If a<b, then b-a is positive. Multiplying this difference by negative k makes kb-ka negative. Therefore kb<ka. The sign of the common factor matters, rather than the mere presence of a minus sign.

### Example 1: multiplying -2<3

Predict, then calculate: (-2)×(-2)=4 and 3×(-2)=-6. Four is right of minus six, so the new comparison is 4>-6. The direction reverses.

Positive multiplication by two instead gives -4<6. Adding minus two gives -4<1, also without reversal. Adding a negative is translation, not reflection. Seeing “minus two” alone is not a reason to reverse.

Multiplication by zero makes both products zero, collapsing distinct positions. Equality of the products cannot recover their original order, so this is not a reversible solving step. Division by zero is undefined.

### Example 2: negative division and inclusion

Solve -4x≤5. Division by minus four is multiplication by the negative reciprocal -1/4. Reverse ≤ to ≥, obtaining x≥-5/4. Direction changes but equality inclusion remains.

At -5/4 the original left side equals five, so the boundary passes. Zero passes, while minus two gives 8≤5 and fails. Fill the endpoint and shade right. Replacing the exact boundary -1.25 or -5/4 with a truncation such as -1.2 changes the set.

For x/(-3)>2, multiplication by negative three gives x<-6. Minus nine gives 3>2 and passes. Minus six gives equality and is excluded. Common multiplication and division can be understood through order rather than disconnected rules.

## Unknown signs need conditions

If someone always transforms a<b into ax>bx, ask about x. A positive x preserves direction, a negative x reverses it, and zero makes the products equal while losing order. An unrestricted variable cannot be assigned one rule. Variable division also requires excluding zero and considering signs; this unit does not apply it without those conditions.

In the experiment, change a, b and k, predict, then reveal. Original numbers and products use matching axis scales, with identity labels a/b and ka/kb. Retaining those labels makes reversal visible rather than silently reordering products. If a=b, common multiplication keeps equality, a separate case from strictly ordered originals.

A parent asks for one operation involving a negative that keeps direction and one negative division that reverses it. Check endpoint inclusion too. Ground the explanation in values, operations and order.
