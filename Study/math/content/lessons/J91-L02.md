# 无理数与实数轴

## 用定义分类，不看外观猜

有理数能写成 p/q，其中 p、q 是整数且 q≠0；整数、有限小数和无限循环小数都属于有理数。无理数是不能写成这种整数比的实数，它的小数展开无限且不循环。两类共同组成实数，负数、零、正数都要按定义区分，不能把“无理”理解成“不合理”或“不可表示”。

根号是一种精确表示方式，不保证所表示的数无理。例如 √9=3、√(1/4)=1/2 都有理。反过来，−√2 仍然无理，负号不会把它变成整数比。√(−2) 在本课的实数域没有值，不能误归为“无理数”；无理数首先必须是实数。

### 例 1：√2 为什么无理

假设 √2=p/q，其中 p、q 是互质正整数，已经约到最简。平方得 p²=2q²，所以 p² 偶。奇整数的平方仍奇，因为 (2k+1)²=4k²+4k+1；因此 p² 偶只能说明 p 偶。设 p=2k，代回得 4k²=2q²，即 q²=2k²，因此 q 也偶。

这使 p、q 同时有因子二，与最简互质矛盾，所以最初的有理假设不成立。√2 是实数而非有理数，故无理。证明用了整数奇偶与最简分数，不是“试了很多分数都没找到”，也不是计算器输出了一长串不整齐的小数。

单位正方形的对角线由勾股得 √2，提供精确几何长度。把这段长度从零转到实数轴正向，可理解它有确定位置；证明无理性则仍需上面的整数理由，不能量到很多小数位就替代证明。

### 例 2：相似的显示，不同的精确数

按字面写的 1.414213562 是有限小数，等于 1414213562/1000000000，是有理数。它接近 √2，但不是 √2 的另一种精确写法。若题目给的是 √2 的屏幕近似，就应保留精确名称与近似关系，不能把显示字面数和原数混成一个对象。

0.(3) 在本课明确表示三无限循环，等于 1/3。可设 x=0.333…，则 10x=3.333…，相减得 9x=3，所以 x=1/3。它不终止，却仍有理。“无限”少了“不循环”这个条件，不能直接推出无理。

若只看到前几位 0.101001… 而没有规定后续规律，这个前缀不足以证明有理或无理；它可接成很多不同实数。只有明确知道整个展开或有其他数学证据，才能据定义分类。

## 数轴承载实数，像素显示近似

每个实数对应实数轴上唯一一点。分数与无理数都可出现在整数之间，数轴不是只由整数刻度组成；屏幕上两个标签接近，也不使精确值相等。比如 1.41²<2<1.42² 在非负区间推出 1.41<√2<1.42，给严格位置范围，不是把 √2 定义成某个截断小数。

改变窗口会改变像素距离与标签疏密，不改变数本身。工作台区分有限字面小数、明确循环小数、可化简根式、无理根式与无实数值的表达式。家长应请孩子先给整数比或证明依据，再看图；只回答“根号”“很多位”“不是整数”都不是充分的无理判断。

# Irrational numbers and the real line

## Classify by definition

A rational number is p/q for integers p,q with q≠0. Integers, terminating decimals and nonterminating repeating decimals are rational. An irrational is real but not an integer ratio, with a nonterminating nonrepeating expansion. Together these classes form the real numbers.

A radical symbol does not guarantee irrationality: √9=3 and √(1/4)=1/2 are rational. -√2 remains irrational, while √(-2) has no value in this real domain. An irrational must first be real; undefined real radicals are not another kind of irrational number.

### Example 1: why √2 is irrational

Suppose √2=p/q for coprime positive integers. Squaring gives p²=2q², so p² is even and therefore p is even. An odd integer’s square is odd since (2k+1)²=4k²+4k+1.

Write p=2k. Substitution gives q²=2k², making q even too. Both share factor two, contradicting lowest terms. Thus the rational assumption fails. This uses parity and coprimality, not many unsuccessful trial fractions or an untidy calculator display.

A unit square’s diagonal has exact length √2 by Pythagoras. Transferring that segment onto the positive real line explains a definite location; its irrationality still needs the integer argument, not a measurement with many digits.

### Example 2: similar displays, different exact values

The literal decimal 1.414213562 terminates and equals 1414213562/1000000000, an integer ratio. It approximates √2 but is not its exact alternative spelling. A displayed approximation and the original exact number are different objects.

Here 0.(3) explicitly repeats threes forever and equals 1/3. If x=0.333…, then 10x−x=3, giving x=1/3. Nontermination without the nonrepeating condition does not establish irrationality.

A displayed prefix such as 0.101001… without a specified continuation proves neither class: many different reals can share it. Use a known full expansion or other mathematical evidence.

## Exact reals, approximate pixels

Each real number corresponds to one real-line point. Fractions and irrationals can lie between integer ticks; the line is not a collection of integers only. Nearby labels do not make exact numbers equal.

On nonnegative numbers, 1.41²<2<1.42² implies 1.41<√2<1.42. This gives a strict location interval rather than defining the root as a truncated decimal. Changing the display window alters pixels, not the number.

The workshop distinguishes finite literals, explicitly repeating decimals, simplifiable roots, irrational roots and expressions without real values. A parent asks for an integer ratio or proof before looking at the plotted point. “A radical”, “many digits” or “not an integer” alone cannot establish irrationality.
