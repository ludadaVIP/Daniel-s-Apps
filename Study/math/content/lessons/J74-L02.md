# 相交线与对顶角

## 先认位置，再算度数

两条不同直线相交于一点，会围出四个角。对顶角来自同一个交点、同两条直线，两个角的边分别是反向射线，没有公共边。只说“共享一个顶点”还不够，因为相邻角也共享顶点；只说“度数相等”也不够，不同位置的角同样可能相等。

邻补角共享顶点和一条边，另外两条边是反向射线，所以合起来是平角，和为 180°。一般“补角”只说度数和为 180°，不要求相邻；“邻补”多了位置结构。例如纸上分开画的 70° 与 110° 互补，却不是同一个交点的邻补角。

### 例 1：用两个平角说明对顶相等

图中按顺序把四角标 A、B、C、D。A/B 与 B/C 都是邻补：

1. 由一条直线的反向射线，`A+B=180°`。
2. 由另一条直线的反向射线，`B+C=180°`。
3. 两个总量相等，即 `A+B=B+C`。
4. 两边同减 B，得到 `A=C`。

这条理由链不依赖图画是否准确，也不依赖量角器读数。若 A=42°，则 C=42°；B=180°−42°=138°，D=138°。四个角和为 360°，可作为计算检查，但不是因为“总和三百六十”就能单独推得某一对相等。

### 例 2：结构决定该相等还是相加

两直线相交，A=112°。对顶 C 同样是 112°；邻补 B、D 都是 68°。若误把对顶角相加到 180°，会把 C 错算成 68°。先指明公共边与反向射线，再决定写等式还是和式。

若已知一对对顶角分别为 `(3x+10)°` 与 `(2x+40)°`，应列 `3x+10=2x+40`，解得 x=30；两个角都是 100°，不是三十度。若两直线垂直，四个角都是 90°，邻补角也可以相等。因此“这两个角相等”仍不能单独用来认定对顶位置。

## 旋转图形，关系保持

工作台可改变 A 的度数，也能把两直线一起旋转。旋转后上、下、左、右的外观变了，A/C 的边仍分别反向，对顶关系不变；A/B 的另外两边仍组成直线，邻补关系不变。按角的实际位置关系判断，而不是背某张固定朝向的图片。

填写 C 和 B 之前，各说一条理由。改变已知 A 后要重新计算，旧答案会清空；只整体旋转时，同一对角的数值不变，原推理仍可使用。37.5° 的邻补是 142.5°，精确小数无需舍入成整数。

家长可以让孩子画一对相等却不对顶的角作为反例，再画一对互补却不相邻的角。能给出反例说明定义中的每个条件都在起作用，比只背“对顶角相等”更深入。

# Intersecting lines and vertically opposite angles

## Identify positions before calculating

Two distinct intersecting lines form four angles. Vertically opposite angles share the same intersection and come from the same lines, with opposite rays as their respective arms and no shared arm. A common vertex alone is insufficient because adjacent angles also share one. Equal measures are insufficient because equality occurs elsewhere too.

Adjacent supplementary angles share a vertex and arm; their remaining arms are opposite rays, so they form a straight angle and total 180°. Supplementary angles merely total 180° and need not be adjacent. Separately drawn 70° and 110° angles are supplementary without forming an adjacent pair.

### Example 1: derive opposite equality from two straight angles

Label the angles around the intersection A, B, C and D. Opposite rays give `A+B=180°` and `B+C=180°`. The totals are equal, so `A+B=B+C`. Subtracting the same B from both sides gives A=C.

This reasoning does not depend on drawing precision or protractor readings. If A=42°, C=42°, while B and D are 138°. All four total 360°, a useful arithmetic check. That total alone, however, does not independently establish which pair must be equal.

### Example 2: decide between equality and addition

With A=112°, its opposite C is 112°, while adjacent supplementary B and D are 68°. Making the opposite pair sum to 180° would incorrectly give C=68°. Locate shared and opposite arms before choosing an equation.

If opposite angles measure `(3x+10)°` and `(2x+40)°`, set the expressions equal. This gives x=30 and both angles 100°; x is not itself the angle measure. When the two lines are perpendicular, all four angles are 90°. Adjacent supplementary angles can therefore be equal, reinforcing why equal measures alone do not identify opposite positions.

## Rotation preserves the relationships

The workshop changes A and rotates both lines together. Their apparent top, bottom, left and right positions may change, but A/C retain opposite arms and A/B retain a straight-angle structure. Reason from geometry rather than memorising one orientation.

Before entering C or B, state the relevant relationship. Changing A clears old answers and requires new arithmetic; a whole rotation alone preserves the angles and the reasoning. Decimal angles need not be rounded: the adjacent supplementary angle to 37.5° is exactly 142.5°.

A parent asks for equal angles that are not vertically opposite and for supplementary angles that are not adjacent. These counterexamples reveal the role of each definition condition and show more understanding than repeating a theorem name alone.
