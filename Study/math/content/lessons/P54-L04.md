## 通分保留原量，相减保留顺序

异分母减法同样需要相同整体。先把不同大小的份通分成同样大的小份，再划去指定数量。通分不是把原量变大，也不是把两个分母相减。每个分子随自己的分母同倍改变，减号两侧的先后保持不变。

在本单元的非负实物模型里，先比较原有量与去掉量，确认有足够的量可划去。去掉一个正量后，剩余应小于原量。若两量等值，即使分母不同，差也可以为零。估计和加回核查可以发现符号、顺序或通分错误。

### 例 1 · 四分之三减六分之一

1. 求 **3/4−1/6**。共同分母选 **12**。
2. **3/4=9/12**；**1/6=2/12**，原有量与去掉量都保持不变。
3. 从九个十二分之一中划去两个：**9/12−2/12=7/12**。
4. 7/12 已是最简分数。差小于 3/4，而且大于 1/2，与画出的剩余段一致。
5. 加回：**7/12+1/6=7/12+2/12=9/12=3/4**，恢复原有量。

### 例 2 · 原量跨过一整条

1. 有 **7/6**，去掉 **3/4**。原量大于 1，但这不妨碍减法。
2. 通分为 **14/12−9/12**；注意不是把 7−3 直接放到十二分母上。
3. 剩 **5/12**。原有十四份、去掉九份，都以十二分之一计数。
4. 改用共同分母 **24**：**28/24−18/24=10/24=5/12**，结果等值。
5. **5/12+9/12=14/12=7/6**，恢复原量。交换成 3/4−7/6 会改变结果，不属于当前非负模型的同一个任务。

### 等值两量与整数减分数

2/3−4/6=4/6−4/6=0。不同写法并不保证不同数量，不能只凭分母不一样就说“不能等于零”。零差保留实际单位，例如用完 2/3 L 水后剩 0 L；不要写成 0/0。

1−3/8=8/8−3/8=5/8。整数 1 需要表达成八个八分之一，不是把 1 与分子 3 生硬相减。5/6−1/4=10/12−3/12=7/12，通分倍数分别为二和三；若把分子都不变，会得到错误的 4/12。

工作台先核对共同分母，再显示两量的细格；第二步才出现划去叉号和剩余。选择另一有效分母会重置旧步骤。去掉量超过原量时，不自动调换量，也不暗中缩小去掉量。高分母图保留相同整体宽度与真实格数，可以用键盘进入图的滚动区域查看。

**带走一个发现：** 通分不改变两量，相减不交换先后。剩余加去掉量应回到原量；零差合法，超过可划去量需要说明模型范围。

## Retain amounts under conversion and order under subtraction

Unlike-denominator subtraction still requires one common whole. Rewrite different-size pieces as equal-size smaller pieces, then remove the required count. Conversion does not enlarge amounts or subtract denominators. Scale each numerator with its own denominator and retain operand order.

In this unit’s non-negative material model, compare the original and removed amounts first. Removing a positive amount should leave less than the original. Equivalent amounts may have different denominators yet a zero difference. Estimation and adding back help detect sign, order and conversion errors.

### Example 1 · Three quarters minus one sixth

1. Find **3/4−1/6**, choosing denominator **12**.
2. **3/4=9/12** and **1/6=2/12**, retaining both quantities.
3. Remove two twelfths from nine: **9/12−2/12=7/12**.
4. The result is already simplest. It is below 3/4 and above 1/2, consistent with the remainder strip.
5. Add back: **7/12+1/6=7/12+2/12=9/12=3/4**.

### Example 2 · An original crossing one full strip

1. Start with **7/6** and remove **3/4**. The original may exceed one.
2. Convert to **14/12−9/12**; do not put 7−3 directly over twelve.
3. **5/12** remains. Both counts refer to twelfths.
4. Denominator **24** gives **28/24−18/24=10/24=5/12**, an equivalent result.
5. **5/12+9/12=14/12=7/6** restores the original. Reversing to 3/4−7/6 changes the task and lies outside this non-negative material model.

### Equivalent amounts and integers minus fractions

2/3−4/6=4/6−4/6=0. Different writing need not mean different quantities. Using exactly 2/3 L leaves 0 L; it does not produce 0/0.

Also, 1−3/8=8/8−3/8=5/8. Express the integer as eight eighths rather than directly subtracting the numerator three from one. For 5/6−1/4=10/12−3/12=7/12, the scale factors are two and three respectively. Leaving both numerators unchanged incorrectly gives 4/12.

The workshop checks the candidate denominator, reveals the refined known amounts, then separately shows crossed-out pieces and the remainder. Changing a denominator clears earlier steps. If removal exceeds the original, it does not secretly swap or shrink quantities. High-denominator diagrams retain equal whole widths and real tile counts; keyboard users can focus and scroll the diagram region.

**Takeaway:** conversion retains amounts; subtraction retains order. Remaining plus removed should recover the original. Zero is valid, and impossible removal needs an explicit model-boundary explanation.
