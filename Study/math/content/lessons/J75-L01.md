# 直角坐标系

## 两条数轴组成一个位置系统

一条数轴能定位一个方向上的数；在平面上定位，需要两个相互垂直的方向。横向 x 轴、纵向 y 轴共用原点 O，两轴都标正方向和刻度，组成直角坐标系。通常向右 x 增大、向上 y 增大，向左、向下分别用负数。

坐标写成有序数对 `(x,y)`。第一个数专门说明横向位置，第二个数专门说明纵向位置，顺序不是装饰。一个坐标为零也要写出来，不能把 `(0,−3)` 简写成负三，因为单个数没有说明哪个方向取零。两轴每格代表多少，要分别看标签，不能用屏幕上量出的厘米代替。

### 例 1：先横后纵读一个位置

从原点向右三单位、再向下二单位，点 P 的横坐标三、纵坐标负二，所以 `P=(3,−2)`。画投影线回到横轴和纵轴，可以分别读回三与负二。

若把顺序交换成 `(−2,3)`，点在左二、上三，是另一个位置，不只是另一种写法。描同一个点时可以用不同路径走到终点，但数对的两个位置仍固定对应 x、y；“先横后纵”是一种可靠定位方法，不是限制人只能走这一条实际路线。

### 例 2：轴上点与四象限

两轴把平面分成四个象限，从右上开始逆时针为第一、第二、第三、第四象限。符号依次是 `(+,+)`、`(−,+)`、`(−,−)`、`(+,−)`。例如 `(−2,5)` 在第二象限，`(−3,−2)` 在第三象限。

`(0,−3)` 的横坐标为零，位于 y 轴原点下方；它不属于第三或第四象限内部。`(5,0)` 位于 x 轴右侧，也不属于象限。`(0,0)` 是原点，同时在两轴上，不放进任何象限。象限的符号规则要求两个坐标都非零，不能见一个负号就直接选某象限。

## 图的位置与数据的位置

工作台用两个滑块分别改变 x、y，点在两条投影线的交点。先把其中一个数设成零，再把两个都设成零，检查轴与原点。用正负不同组合观察四象限，不要只背一张固定图片。

“只改横轴显示范围”会把横轴从负五至五改为负十至十，每格数值也改为二。P 的数据数对仍相同，只是同样的横坐标在屏幕上离原点近一些。两轴的单位长度不一定一样，正交不等于每单位像素数相同；以后比较图线陡峭程度更要先查刻度。

家长可让孩子一边指轴一边说“这个数控制哪一方向”，再改视窗询问数据是否改变。若孩子用屏幕格数答坐标，追问每格代表的数值；若漏写零，追问这个点究竟在横轴还是纵轴。位置、表示与显示尺度要连在一起理解。

# The Cartesian plane

## Two number lines locate a plane position

A number line locates a value in one direction. A plane needs two perpendicular directions: horizontal x and vertical y share origin O, with positive directions and labelled intervals. Conventionally x increases rightwards and y upwards; left and down use negative values.

The ordered pair `(x,y)` assigns the first slot to horizontal position and the second to vertical position. Zero entries must remain. Writing only negative three instead of `(0,−3)` loses which direction is zero. Read each scale independently rather than measuring centimetres on a screen.

### Example 1: locate horizontally, then vertically

Three units right and two down from the origin gives `P=(3,−2)`. Axis-parallel projections recover three on x and negative two on y. Swapping to `(−2,3)` gives a different point, two left and three up.

Different routes can reach the same position, but the coordinate slots still have fixed meanings. “Horizontal before vertical” is a reliable plotting method, not a claim that a traveller must physically follow only that route.

### Example 2: quadrants and their boundaries

Starting at upper right and moving anticlockwise, quadrants I, II, III and IV have signs `(+,+)`, `(−,+)`, `(−,−)` and `(+,−)`. Thus `(−2,5)` lies in quadrant II and `(−3,−2)` in III.

An x-coordinate of zero places `(0,−3)` on the y-axis, outside all quadrant interiors. Point `(5,0)` lies on the positive x-axis, also outside quadrants. Origin `(0,0)` belongs to both axes and no quadrant. The quadrant sign patterns require two nonzero entries; a single negative sign is insufficient to select one.

## Data position and display position differ

The two workshop controls vary x and y independently. Try one zero coordinate, then two, and investigate all sign combinations. The plotted position is the intersection of the coordinate projection lines.

Changing only the horizontal view expands it from −5…5 to −10…10 and changes its tick interval to two. The data pair stays fixed while its screen distance from zero changes. Perpendicular axes need not have equal pixels per numerical unit. Later, this distinction matters when interpreting the apparent steepness of a graph.

A parent asks which direction each entry controls and whether rescaling changed the data. If square counts are reported as coordinates, ask what each interval represents. If zero is omitted, ask which axis contains the point. Connect position, ordered representation and display scale.
