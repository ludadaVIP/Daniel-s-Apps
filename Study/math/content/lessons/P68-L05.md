# 综合项目：规划一次短途旅行

## 先写约束，再找方案

本项目把前四课连在一起。所有路线、价格与时长都是虚构课堂资料，不是现实出行建议。四人从 09:00 出发，交通按整组往返收费，门票按人收费且总票价减 25%，午餐每人 NZ$6、用时三十分钟；还必须留三十分钟机动，以及完整预算的 10% 不消费。机动时间与预留金分别保护时间和钱，不能混为一个量。

给定路线：直达单程四十分钟，整组往返 NZ$40；绕行单程六十分钟，整组往返 NZ$24。活动：展馆九十分钟、每人原票价 NZ$12；花园六十分钟、每人 NZ$8；步道九十分钟、票价零。先看清“单程”“往返”“每人”“整组”和“折前”，它们决定乘二、乘人数或乘付款比例的范围。

每份方案要同时通过两条约束：`全部支出+预留金≤预算`，以及`含机动的结束时间≤截止时间`。便宜但迟到，或按时但超预算，都不满足本题。可行也不自动代表唯一最优；在满足约束后，才根据任务目标比较活动时间、价格与偏好。

### 例 1：完整列账与时间表

四人、预算 NZ$160、最晚 15:00，选择直达展馆。

费用：折前门票 `4×12=48`，折后 `48×75%=36`；午餐 `4×6=24`；整组往返四十只计一次。总支出 `36+24+40=NZ$100`。预留 `160×10%=16`，可用一百四十四，所以保护预留后还可用四十四。账户剩六十，其中十六受保护，不要把两种余额混写。

时间表：09:00–09:40 去程；09:40–11:10 参观；11:10–11:40 午餐；11:40–12:20 返程；12:20–12:50 机动。合计 `40+90+30+40+30=230 min`，含机动在 12:50 结束，比 15:00 早一百三十分钟。这里把机动显示在末尾便于累计核验，它是可用于途中意外延误的保护时间，不是已经发生的延误记录。

### 例 2：更紧约束下的取舍

改为预算 NZ$80、允许四小时至 13:00，其余条件不变。直达展馆一百元超可用七十二；不能仅因时间足够就通过。

选择绕行花园：交通二十四，门票 `4×8×75%=24`，午餐二十四，总支出七十二；预算预留八、可用七十二，恰好满足。时间 `60+60+30+60+30=240 min`，09:00 至 13:00 也恰好满足。等于上限算通过，但已没有额外可用余量。

绕行步道没有门票，费用四十八更便宜，却需要 `60+90+30+60+30=270 min`，超过四小时上限三十分钟；所以不能只按最低价格选择。直达花园需要八十八，虽然更快，却超可用十六。每次换选项，都要重算两条约束。

还有一份可行方案：直达步道，交通四十加午餐二十四，共六十四；总时长二百三十分钟。它比绕行花园便宜且快，但活动内容不同。若任务特别要求参观花园，便不能拿步道替代。先声明目标，才能讨论哪种取舍更合适。

## 让方案承受一次变化

在工作台先复现两个例子，再把允许时长缩短三十分钟。原来恰好可行的绕行花园会变为时间不足；不要修改旧结果来维持“可行”。再改人数，门票和午餐按人数变化，整组往返不按人数重复乘。比较时一次只改一个条件，才能清楚说明变化从哪里来。

工作台草稿是临时的，刷新会清空；正式练习、出口说明和家长核验随学习记录保存。写理由时提供：选项、逐项费用、各百分比基数、逐段分钟数、两条约束、一个取舍，以及一个会让方案失效的改变。真实计划还需核实开放时间、可用路线和其他现实条件；这个课堂项目不把未给出的信息假装已知。纸笔即可完成，不需要购买或实际出行。

# Capstone: plan a short trip

## State both constraints first

This project combines the previous four lessons. Every route, price and duration is fictional classroom data. Four people leave at 09:00. Transport is one return fare for the group. Tickets are per person with 25% off their total. Lunch costs NZ$6 per person and takes thirty minutes. Protect thirty minutes of buffer and 10% of the full budget. Time buffer and money reserve protect different quantities.

The direct route takes forty minutes each way and costs NZ$40 per group return. The scenic route takes sixty each way and costs NZ$24 per group return. Museum: ninety minutes and NZ$12 per person before discount. Garden: sixty minutes and NZ$8 per person. Trail: ninety minutes and free tickets. Read one-way, return, per-person, per-group and before-discount labels before multiplying.

A plan must satisfy spending + reserve ≤ budget AND finish including buffer ≤ deadline. Cheap but late, or on time but too expensive, fails. Feasible does not mean uniquely best; compare preferences only after meeting the required constraints.

### Example 1: an itemised ledger and schedule

For four people, budget NZ$160 and deadline 15:00, choose direct transport and museum. Original tickets cost `4 × 12 = 48`; discounted tickets cost `48 × 75% = 36`. Lunch costs twenty-four. Add the group return fare forty once, giving total spending NZ$100.

Reserve is `160 × 10% = 16`, so 144 are available. Protecting reserve leaves forty-four still spendable. The account balance is sixty, including sixteen protected dollars; label these different quantities.

The schedule is outbound 09:00–09:40, visit 09:40–11:10, lunch 11:10–11:40, return 11:40–12:20 and buffer 12:20–12:50. Total duration is `40 + 90 + 30 + 40 + 30 = 230 min`. Including buffer, finish is 12:50, 130 minutes before the deadline. Buffer appears at the end for cumulative checking; it protects against delays during the trip rather than recording a delay that already happened.

### Example 2: tighter constraints change the choice

Change budget to NZ$80 and allowed time to four hours, ending at 13:00. Direct museum still costs one hundred, exceeding available money seventy-two.

Scenic garden costs twenty-four transport, `4 × 8 × 75% = 24` tickets and twenty-four lunch: total seventy-two. Reserve eight leaves exactly seventy-two available. Duration is `60 + 60 + 30 + 60 + 30 = 240 min`, reaching 13:00 including buffer. Equality satisfies both limits, with no additional headroom.

Scenic trail costs only forty-eight because tickets are free, but needs `60 + 90 + 30 + 60 + 30 = 270 min`. It exceeds four hours by thirty minutes. Direct garden is faster but costs eighty-eight, sixteen above the spendable limit. Neither lowest price nor shortest duration alone decides feasibility.

Direct trail is another feasible plan: transport forty plus lunch twenty-four costs sixty-four, and duration is 230 minutes. It is cheaper and faster than scenic garden but offers a different activity. If the task specifically requires a garden visit, a trail cannot replace it. State the objective before judging the trade-off.

## Test a change and explain a trade-off

Reproduce both examples in the workshop, then shorten allowed time by thirty minutes. The previously feasible scenic garden now fails its time condition. Change people next: tickets and lunches change per person, but group return fare is not multiplied again. Change one condition at a time so the source of each consequence is clear.

Workshop notes are temporary and clear on reload. Formal practice, exit explanations and parent checks use the saved learning record. A full justification includes choices, itemised costs, percentage bases, every time segment, both inequalities, one trade-off and a change that would break feasibility.

Real planning would also require verified opening times, available routes and other conditions not supplied here. Do not pretend the classroom model has checked those facts. Paper is sufficient; no purchase or actual trip is needed.
