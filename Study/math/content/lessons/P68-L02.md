# 工作量的直观理解

## 先看每一分钟完成多少

工作量是任务的总量，例如向空容器注入六十升水。效率在本课指每单位时间完成的工作量，例如每分钟三升，单位 `L/min`。它不是完成时间，也不是一个没有单位的“快慢分数”。基本关系是工作量=速率×时间；速率恒定时，一分钟、两分钟、三分钟分别完成三升、六升、九升。

本课使用理想水泵，是为了让工作量可连续分割。共同工作模型假设：同时启动、各自速率恒定、互不干扰、无漏水、同一容器有足够容量。这样每一分钟两泵各贡献的水量可相加。如果共享管道限制总流量，或一泵启停、漏水、容器已满，就不能沿用未修改的乘法模型。数学关系来自条件，不是见到“两人合作”就自动套公式。

### 例 1：完成时间不能相加

任务六十升。泵 A 单独二十分钟完成，泵 B 单独三十分钟完成。

1. 先把完成时间转为速率：A 为 `60÷20=3 L/min`，B 为 `60÷30=2 L/min`。
2. 同时恒速工作，每分钟合计 `3+2=5 L`。
3. 共同用时 `60÷5=12 min`。
4. 反查：A 十二分钟完成三十六升，B 完成二十四升，共六十升。

二十加三十等于五十分钟，是把两台各做完整任务的用时拼在一起，描述的不是“同时完成同一任务”。把二十直接除二也不成立，因为两泵效率不同。只有相同效率、同时运行且互不干扰时，两台的共同速率才是单台的两倍。

### 例 2：总量、完成量与剩余量

仍是六十升任务，A 每分钟三升、B 每分钟二升，同时运行四分钟。

共同速率五升每分钟，完成 `(3+2)×4=20 L`，剩余 `60−20=40 L`。四十是还要完成的量，不是已完成量。继续保持条件，还需 `40÷5=8 min`；已用四分钟加八分钟，恰好等于完整任务的十二分钟。

在工作台把运行时间改为二十分钟，水量条仍最多六十升。模型约定满时自动停止，因此不能画一百升装进六十升容器。显示的“经过时间”可以超过完成时间，但实际工作在任务完成时已结束；额外时间不会制造更多完成量。

## 两种重要边界

零分钟时尚未完成任何工作，余量等于全部任务，这是有定义的零完成量。若两台速率都为零，六十升任务在当前状态下无法完成，完成时间不能写 `60÷0`，也不能写零分钟。工作台会明确说明不能完成，而不是显示一个假结果。一台为零、另一台非零时，速率相加仍有效，退化为单台工作。

某些任务允许连续分割，例如水量或长度；另一些只能完成整件，例如封装成品。如果算出分数用时，要结合具体任务条件解释。本课十二升任务由每分钟二升和三升的泵共同完成，精确时间为 `12/5 min`，水量模型容许这个结果；不能把它误读为十二除五升。

## 给出可检查的理由

说明共同工作时，请逐项标出：同一个总任务、每台速率、共同时间、各自贡献和总和。最后问一个反例：“如果共享水管限制总流量，原来把速率相加还成立吗？”能指出假设比背诵合作公式更重要。家长可要求孩子把共同时间乘回每个速率，确认总和确为任务，而不是仅凭出现“效率相加”就判断掌握。

# Work-rate intuition

## Start with work in one minute

Work is the amount to be completed, such as filling an empty container with sixty litres. A rate describes work per unit time: three litres per minute, written `L/min`. It is neither a completion time nor a unitless score. At a constant rate, work = rate × time. A three-litre-per-minute pump produces three, six and nine litres in one, two and three minutes.

This lesson uses ideal pumps because water amounts are continuously divisible. The combined model assumes simultaneous operation, constant rates, no interference, no leaks and enough capacity. Under those conditions, add each pump’s contribution over the same minute. A shared pipe that limits flow, a changing rate or a full container requires a revised model. Cooperation words alone do not justify adding rates.

### Example 1: do not add completion times

The task is sixty litres. Pump A alone takes twenty minutes; pump B alone takes thirty.

1. Convert alone times into rates: A supplies `60 ÷ 20 = 3 L/min`, and B supplies `60 ÷ 30 = 2 L/min`.
2. Together they deliver `3 + 2 = 5 L` each minute.
3. The shared completion time is `60 ÷ 5 = 12 min`.
4. Check contributions over the same twelve minutes: A gives thirty-six litres and B twenty-four, totalling sixty.

Adding twenty and thirty gives the sequential time for each pump to complete an entire task, not the time for simultaneous contributions to one task. Halving twenty is also unjustified because the rates differ. Doubling a single pump’s rate requires another identical pump working simultaneously without interference.

### Example 2: distinguish completed and remaining work

For the same sixty-litre task, pumps at three and two L/min operate together for four minutes. The combined rate is five L/min, so completed work is `(3 + 2) × 4 = 20 L`. Remaining work is `60 − 20 = 40 L`.

Forty is what still needs doing, not what has been completed. Keeping the same conditions, the remainder needs `40 ÷ 5 = 8 min`. Four elapsed minutes plus eight remaining minutes agrees with the twelve-minute total from the first example.

Set workshop elapsed time to twenty minutes. The filled amount stays at sixty litres because this model automatically stops both pumps at capacity. Do not draw one hundred litres inside a sixty-litre container. Elapsed observation time may exceed completion time, but work has already stopped; extra elapsed minutes do not create more completed work.

## Check the boundaries and assumptions

At zero minutes, completed work is zero and remaining work equals the entire task. That is a defined zero amount. With both rates zero, a positive task cannot finish in that state. Neither `60 ÷ 0` nor zero completion minutes is a valid answer. With just one rate zero, the combined model reduces to the active pump alone.

Water and length can be continuously divided; completed packaged products may require whole items. Interpret fractional times in context. For a twelve-litre task using pumps at two and three L/min, the exact completion time is `12/5 min`. The continuous water model permits that value; it is a time, not a volume.

For a checkable explanation, identify the common total task, each rate, the shared time, each contribution and their sum. Then challenge an assumption: would rates still add if a shared pipe restricted the combined flow? Reconstructing the total and explaining a limitation shows more understanding than repeating a cooperation formula.
