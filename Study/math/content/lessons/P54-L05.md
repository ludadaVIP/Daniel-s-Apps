## 先读对象、整体和单位，再列分数式

应用题中的分数不能脱离对象。一壶容量一升，另一壶容量两升，各有半壶水：实际分别为 1/2 L 与 1 L，合计 3/2 L。“半壶加半壶”不一定是一升，因为两个壶不是相同大小的整体。把实际量转换到共同测量标尺，才有依据通分相加。

先说清所求的是合计量、剩余量，还是全程比例。合并各部分用加法；从原有量用去一部分求剩余用减法。一个“再”字不足以决定运算。两步题先写计划，记录中间量及单位，再完成最终问题。

### 例 1 · 先合并，再使用

1. 两壶各有 **3/4 L** 和 **2/3 L**，合并后用去 **1/2 L**，求剩余。
2. 计划：“先求共有水量，再减使用量”。所有量都以升计，每完整条是 **1 L**。
3. 共有 **9/12 L+8/12 L=17/12 L**。容器足够大，总量可大于一升。
4. 使用量 **1/2 L=6/12 L**，剩 **17/12 L−6/12 L=11/12 L**。
5. 核查：**11/12 L+6/12 L=17/12 L**；再减原第二壶的 8/12 L 得 9/12 L，恢复第一壶。最后剩余不是中间总量。

### 例 2 · 两次使用的两种有效计划

1. 有 **1 L** 水，先用 **1/3 L**，再用 **1/4 L**，求最终剩余。
2. 计划甲：先合计用量，**4/12 L+3/12 L=7/12 L**；再 **12/12 L−7/12 L=5/12 L**。
3. 计划乙：逐次减去，**1−1/3=2/3**，再 **8/12−3/12=5/12**，每步保留升。
4. 两种计划结果一致，因为两次都从同一升水中使用，没有加新水。
5. **5/12 L+1/3 L+1/4 L=12/12 L=1 L**，剩余和两次使用量恢复原量。图中划去四格与三格，剩五格。

### 不能只通分，还要看参照

“走了全程的 1/3，再走 1/4 km，共走全程的 7/12”缺少全程长度。若全程 2 km，1/3 全程是 2/3 km，加 1/4 km 得 11/12 km；它占全程 11/24，而不是 7/12。需要先把两段都换成千米，或都换成全程比例。

5/6 m 彩带剪去 1/4 m，剩 7/12 m；量是长度，不能输入 7/12 L。纸上假想有 NZ$3/4，花去 NZ$1/2，剩 NZ$1/4：钱数与硬币枚数不是相同对象。单位标注能暴露读题错误，不能只在最后随意添一个字母。

工作台提供合并水、剪彩带、假想钱数与用完水四种情境。先选择数量关系，核对后再通分和展开。两壶水合并后可继续预测用去 1/2 L 的第二步，区分中间总量与最终剩余。这里改变的是情境已知量，不会提前让你编辑或看到所求结果。家庭挑战只需纸画十二等份，不需要采购或实际倒水；先解释计划，再检查份数与单位。

**带走一个发现：** 整体与实际单位先一致，分数单位再一致。解释每一步所求对象，最后把剩余与使用量加回原量，不把“算过”当作“情境成立”。

## Read objects, reference wholes and units before writing an expression

A fraction belongs to a quantity and a reference. Half-filled jugs of capacities 1 L and 2 L contain 1/2 L and 1 L, totalling 3/2 L. “Half a jug plus half a jug” need not be one litre. Convert actual amounts to a common measurement reference before finding common fraction units.

Identify whether the unknown is a total, a remainder or a fraction of a route. Combining parts uses addition; using some of an original to find what remains uses subtraction. “Then” alone does not choose an operation. Plan both steps and record the intermediate amount with its unit.

### Example 1 · Combine, then use

1. Jugs contain **3/4 L** and **2/3 L**. Combine them, then use **1/2 L**.
2. Plan: find total water, then subtract the amount used. All quantities are litres; every complete strip is **1 L**.
3. Total: **9/12 L+8/12 L=17/12 L**. The container is sufficiently large, so a total above one litre is reasonable.
4. Use **1/2 L=6/12 L**, leaving **17/12 L−6/12 L=11/12 L**.
5. Check **11/12 L+6/12 L=17/12 L**, then subtract 8/12 L to recover the first jug’s 9/12 L. The final remainder differs from the intermediate total.

### Example 2 · Two valid plans for two uses

1. Start with **1 L**, use **1/3 L**, then **1/4 L**.
2. Plan A: used total is **4/12 L+3/12 L=7/12 L**; remainder is **12/12 L−7/12 L=5/12 L**.
3. Plan B: **1−1/3=2/3**, then **8/12−3/12=5/12**, retaining litres at every step.
4. Both plans agree because both uses come from the same original litre, with no new water added.
5. **5/12 L+1/3 L+1/4 L=1 L** restores the original. Cross out four and three tiles of twelve, leaving five.

### A common denominator does not repair a mismatched reference

“One third of a route, then 1/4 km, means 7/12 of the route” lacks the route length. If the route is 2 km, the first part is 2/3 km. Adding 1/4 km gives 11/12 km, or 11/24 of the route. Convert both distances to kilometres or both to fractions of the route before combining them.

Cutting 1/4 m from 5/6 m of ribbon leaves 7/12 m, not 7/12 L. An imagined NZ$3/4 minus NZ$1/2 leaves NZ$1/4; money amounts and coin counts are different objects. Units help detect reading mistakes rather than decorate an answer afterwards.

The workshop offers water combining, ribbon cutting, imagined money and complete water use. Choose the relationship first, then convert and reveal. After combining water, predict a second step using 1/2 L; distinguish the intermediate total from the final remainder. Only known quantities are provided before calculation. The family challenge needs paper, with no purchase or actual pouring: explain the plan before checking piece counts and units.

**Takeaway:** align reference and measurement units before fraction-piece units. Explain the unknown at each step and check remaining plus used against the original.
