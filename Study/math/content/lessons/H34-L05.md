# 统计显著与实际重要

统计证据回答的是相对于某个零假设与程序，数据表现出怎样的不相容；实际重要性还涉及效应多大、单位是什么、方向是否有益、成本如何以及目标如何定义。两者有关，但不能合成一个“p够小就值得采用”的开关。

## 先定义有益方向与阈值

本课虚构情境中，正数表示平均减少等待的分钟数，负数可能表示变慢。事先把至少改善1分钟设为实际目标Δ。零假设检验比较差异是否为0，实际目标比较的是是否达到Δ，这两个参照点不同。

为聚焦解释，采用iid正态、总体σ已知4分钟的均值差模型，SE=4/√n。双侧检验用z=估计差/SE与α0.05；约95%区间用z*=1.96。临界值已经舍入，所以覆盖写“约95%”，不能声称一个小数表达是无限精确。

### 例1：微小、精确而显著

估计平均改善0.2分钟，n10000，SE=4/100=0.04分钟，z=5。双侧p约0.00000057，四位小数显示0.0000，但这只是舍入，不表示真实尾概率恰0。

区间为0.2±1.96×0.04，即[0.1216,0.2784]分钟。它支持与0不同的正向差异，却整个低于1分钟目标。增加样本量让差异估计更精确，没有把0.2分钟变成更大的收益。

若成本极低或影响的人很多，0.2分钟是否值得仍需情境判断；这里的1分钟是明确预设的示例目标，不是所有问题的普遍阈值。不能看完小p后偷偷降低阈值，再宣称事先目标已经达成。

### 例2：大点估计、宽区间与负向结果

估计改善2分钟，但n4时SE2，约95%区间2±3.92=[−1.92,5.92]，z1、p约0.3173。点估计虽大，区间却同时容许负向、零附近与较大改善。不能只凭点估计宣布成功，也不能只凭不显著宣布毫无用处。

同样估计2分钟，若n100、σ仍4，则SE0.4，区间[1.216,2.784]，z5、小p。区间下限已超过1，在本模型与程序下支持达到目标；仍要报告模型、精度和设计，而非保证每个个体改善这么多。

再把估计改为−2分钟、n100，区间[−2.784,−1.216]。这可以显著偏离0，却是有害方向。它低于正向目标，不代表“无影响”或“不重要”；重要的不利变化同样需要完整报告。

## 让区间比较保持条件性

工作台的分类规则只描述区间位置：下限≥Δ写meets-target；上限<Δ写below-target；其余跨阈值写uncertain。它不是把参数变成已知真值，也不是给固定参数赋95%后验概率。

切换单例与全部对照，可以看到相同点估计因n变化而变得宽或窄，以及相同z值对应大小很不同的原单位效应。阈值控制用于比较假想预设方案，不是鼓励事后挑选成功标准。

[ASA说明](https://www.amstat.org/asa/files/pdfs/p-valuestatement.pdf)强调p或显著性不衡量效应大小与重要性。本课把这一点落实到可核对的数值对照。因果性与总体推广还需研究设计支持，不能由小p自动获得。

家长可以让学生先遮住p，解释单位、区间与目标，再把p放回报告。出口应包含方向、效应、区间、阈值、p近似、模型条件与限制；一个醒目的“显著”不能替代这些信息。

# Statistical versus practical significance

Statistical evidence concerns compatibility with a null model and procedure. Practical importance also concerns size, units, beneficial direction, costs and a defined goal. A small p-value is not an automatic adoption rule.

## Prespecify direction and the target

In this fictional example, positive differences mean minutes of waiting saved; negatives may mean deterioration. Let the prespecified practical target Δ be at least one minute. A null test compares with zero, while the target comparison uses Δ.

Assume iid normal observations of differences with known population SD four minutes. SE=4/√n. The two-sided test uses z=estimated difference/SE and α=0.05. Approximate 95% intervals use the rounded critical value 1.96; retain the approximation label.

### Example 1: Tiny, precise and significant

For estimated improvement 0.2 minutes and n=10000, SE is 0.04 and z=5. The two-sided p is approximately 0.00000057. A four-decimal display gives 0.0000, a rounding result rather than an exact zero probability.

The interval is 0.2±1.96×0.04=[0.1216, 0.2784] minutes. It supports a positive departure from zero while remaining wholly below the one-minute target. More data improved precision without making the underlying estimated benefit larger.

Whether 0.2 minutes is worthwhile can depend on costs and scale of use. One minute is a stated example target, not a universal standard. Do not lower a target after seeing a small p and present it as the original goal.

### Example 2: A large uncertain estimate and a harmful direction

With estimate two minutes and n=4, SE is two. The interval [−1.92, 5.92], z=1 and p≈0.3173 leave substantial uncertainty. The interval permits harm, near-zero difference and sizeable benefit. Neither the large point estimate nor non-significance alone settles usefulness.

Keep estimate two and σ four but use n=100. SE becomes 0.4, giving interval [1.216, 2.784], z=5 and a tiny p. The lower endpoint exceeds one, supporting the target under this model and procedure, without guaranteeing the same benefit for every individual.

An estimate −2 at n=100 instead gives [−2.784,−1.216]. It may be highly significant in a harmful direction. Falling below a positive goal does not mean no effect or no practical importance; adverse changes also need reporting.

## Interpret interval positions conditionally

The workbench uses a declared rule: lower endpoint≥Δ gives meets-target; upper endpoint<Δ gives below-target; otherwise uncertain. These labels describe interval positions, not known population truth or posterior probability.

Compare a single case with all cases to see how n changes precision at the same estimate, and how equal z-scores can coexist with very different original-unit effects. Threshold controls compare hypothetical prespecified plans rather than endorse post-hoc success criteria.

The [ASA statement](https://www.amstat.org/asa/files/pdfs/p-valuestatement.pdf) distinguishes significance from effect size and importance. Causal and population-generalisation claims additionally need suitable design support.

Explain units, direction, interval and target before revealing p. Then report all of them with model conditions, approximation labels and limitations. The word significant cannot replace that account.
