# 多解与定义域检查

一个代数结果可能只是候选，还不是原方程的解。完整求解要同时防止增根与丢根：平方可能扩大候选集，除以含未知数的因子可能排除真解，约分也可能隐藏原有的孔。本课把这几层检查分开写清。

## 审查顺序

先列原式定义域。再标变形方向：是等价、仅原式推出新式，还是需要分情况才能继续？求得候选后，先问是否在原域内，再代入原式两侧比较。最后解释候选集为什么包含全部原解，而不是只交出几个碰巧正确的数。

“在原域内”仅表示式子能计算，不表示等式成立。“不在原域内”与“式子有定义但两侧不等”是两种不同的拒绝理由。无解写∅或{}，不能写{0}，因为后者断言零是唯一解。

### 例1：平方后的负候选为什么不行

求sqrt(x+2)=x。原根式要求x≥−2，而左侧非负又迫使解满足x≥0。平方后x+2=x²，因式分解(x−2)(x+1)=0，得到候选2和−1。

x=−1属于根式原域，左侧sqrt(1)=1，右侧−1，因此不相等。它不是因为根号无定义而被拒绝，而是平方抹掉了两侧的符号差异。x=2代回两侧均2，保留。完整解集{2}；原解必满足平方后的二次式，两个候选已逐项检查，所以没有漏解。

把根式改为sqrt(x+6)=x时，候选−2与3，原域x≥−6，最后仅3保留。单纯检查根号内非负仍不足以筛掉−2。

### 例2：约分、分支与不能除掉的零

求(x²−4)/(x−2)=4。原式要求x≠2。在该域内可约为x+2=4，唯一候选2恰好被原域排除，因此解集∅。画出的直线在(2,4)处有孔，不能把它当成与水平线的交点。若右侧改5，则候选3合法，代回原分式得5，解集{3}。

再求x(x−3)=0。零乘积定律给x=0或x=3，完整集合{0,3}。若直接除以x，便默认x≠0并丢掉0；正确方法是先把x=0单独保留，再处理非零分支。

绝对值也须检查分支：|x−1|=2给x=−1或3；右侧为0时只有x=1一个不同的根；右侧为负时无解。不能机械地说所有绝对值方程都有两个解，也不把重合根重复计数。

## 图形怎样与代数互证

工作台画原方程左右两侧，而不是只画变形后的式子。紫线是左侧、虚线是右侧，空心点显示排除位置；只把经过原式核验的交点标成解。根式从原域端点开始画，不跨过无定义区连线。

候选表分别列原域资格、平方所需符号、原左侧、原右侧与是否保留。对不涉及平方的类型，符号列无额外限制；不能把该列当成所有问题都要x≥0。查看表中−1的原左右侧，就能区分它与分式孔2的拒绝原因。

图只显示有限窗口，曲线又以有限采样绘制，因此交点数量不能独立证明全实数解数。完整性来自代数分解、合法约分、分支讨论和逐个原式检查。数值图可发现错误，不能代替这些理由。

## 出口报告

用一份报告比较增根、丢根、重复候选和空集，至少写原域、变形方向、全部候选、拒绝原因及最终集合。家长可以故意说“−1能开根，所以就是解”，让学生展示两侧不同；再问除以x前必须保留哪个情形。

本课练习要求完整集合，少一个真根或多一个候选都不算完成。解释由人工复核，目的在于确认学生知道为何完整，而不是记住本页几个答案。

# Checking multiple solutions and domains

An algebraic result can be a candidate rather than an original root. Squaring may introduce roots, division by an unknown factor may lose roots, and cancellation can conceal a hole. A complete solution audit keeps these effects separate.

## The audit sequence

Record the original domain, then identify whether each step is reversible or merely gives a necessary condition. If a step requires cases, preserve every case. Check each candidate first for original eligibility and then by substituting into the unmodified two sides. Finally justify why the candidate set contains every original solution.

Domain membership means an expression can be evaluated, not that its equation holds. Being outside the domain differs from being defined but unequal. Write ∅ or {} for no solutions; {0} asserts a different claim, namely that zero is the only root.

### Example 1: A defined radical can still fail the equation

Solve sqrt(x+2)=x. The radical requires x≥−2. Its nonnegative output additionally means any solution must have x≥0. Squaring gives x+2=x², hence (x−2)(x+1)=0 and candidates two and −1.

At −1 the radical is defined: the left side is one while the right side is −1. Reject it because the sides differ, not because the radical is undefined. Squaring erased the sign difference. At two, both sides are two, so retain it. Every original root must satisfy the squared quadratic, and both candidates have been audited; the complete set is {2}.

For sqrt(x+6)=x, the squared candidates are −2 and three. Both lie in the radical’s domain x≥−6, but only three satisfies the original equation. Merely checking a nonnegative radicand would again leave an extra candidate.

### Example 2: Cancellation, branches and a lost zero

For (x²−4)/(x−2)=4, record x≠2 before cancelling. On that domain the equation becomes x+2=4, whose candidate two is excluded. The solution set is empty. The line has a hole at (2,4), so that missing point is not an intersection. Changing the target to five instead gives candidate three, and substitution into the original fraction confirms the solution set {3}.

In x(x−3)=0, the zero-product law gives x=0 or x=3. Dividing directly by x silently assumes x≠0 and loses a valid root. Retain the zero case before handling a nonzero branch.

Absolute values also need cases. The equation |x−1|=2 has roots −1 and three; target zero gives only one distinct root, one; a negative target gives none. An absolute-value equation does not automatically have two different roots, and coincident branches are not counted twice in a set.

## Connect the original graph and algebra

The workbench plots the original left side in purple and right side dashed, with holes shown as open points. Only verified intersections are marked as solutions. A radical curve begins at its original domain endpoint and does not connect across undefined inputs.

The candidate audit records original-domain membership, the sign condition needed for squaring, the two original values and the retention decision. Other equation families have no extra squaring sign condition; do not impose x≥0 on every equation. The row for −1 fails equality, whereas the rational candidate at two fails domain eligibility.

A finite graph window and finite curve samples cannot establish the total number of real roots. Completeness comes from algebraic candidate generation, legal cancellation, case splits and original substitution. Graphs can expose errors but cannot replace those arguments.

Report original domains, transformation directions, every candidate, rejection reasons and final sets. Compare extra roots, lost roots, coincident candidates and a legitimate empty set. Submit every distinct solution: omitting a root or retaining an invalid candidate changes the answer. A human reviews the explanation of completeness rather than rewarding a few remembered keywords.
