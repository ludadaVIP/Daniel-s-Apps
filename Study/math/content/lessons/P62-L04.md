## 从比例走向有单位的量

35% 描述一个比例，并没有升或克的单位。要问实际量，必须知道它作用于哪个整体。80 L 的 35% 与 40 L 的 35% 比例相同，实际升数却不同。先说“**100% 对应多少实际量**”，再计算，可以避免把百分数前面的数直接当作答案。

双数轴上方标百分数，下方标升数，相同位置表示同一个对应关系。100% 对应题目给定的整体；35% 对应待求量。图上的问号由你计算，在核对之前不会替你显示答案。

### 例 1 · 八十升的百分之三十五

1. 整体是 **80 L**，对应 **100%**。所求为 **35%** 的实际量。
2. 先预测：35% 小于 50%，所以答案应小于四十升。
3. **1%=80÷100=0.8 L**。
4. 三十五个这样的份：**0.8×35=28 L**。也可直接算 **80×35/100=28 L**。
5. 核回 **28÷80=0.35=35%**。答案二十八升有升单位，百分率 35% 本身没有升单位。

不能写 35 L，因为“每百份三十五”只有在整体恰好一百升时才对应三十五升。也不能算 80×35=2800 L，那漏掉了百分号的“除以一百”。数值和单位都要核查。

### 例 2 · 用熟悉分数求质量份额

1. 求 **240 g 的 12.5%**，先把整体二百四十克与 100% 对应。
2. **12.5%=12.5/100=1/8**。
3. 八等份取一份：**240÷8=30 g**。
4. 也可算 **240×0.125=30 g**。不同表示给同一个结果。
5. 核查 **30/240=1/8=12.5%**；三十克小于总质量的一半，量级合理。

**结果不必为整数：** 5 L 的 30% 为 5×0.3=1.5 L，不能取整成一升。连续量允许分数升；若对象是必须完整分配的卡片，出现非整数时应解释对象限制，而不是擅自截断数学结果。

**零和超过 100%：** 任意给定量的 0% 都是零。工作台整体为零时，乘法 0×35%=0 仍有意义，但不能倒过来用部分÷零整体定义占比。需求为参考 12 L 的 125% 时，12×1.25=15 L，表示需要比参考量多四分之一；这不表示原来只有十二升的桶会自动变出十五升水。

工作台可以独立改参考总量与所求百分数。先固定 35%，把总量八十升改四十升，预测结果减半；再固定总量，比较 0%、50%、100%、125%。修改后旧答案清空，双数轴始终明确哪一个实际量是 100%。

**家庭纸笔观察：** 画同样长度的两条线，分别标 100%=80 L 与 100%=40 L。在同样的 35% 位置写实际升数。图上比例位置相同，单位刻度不同，不能仅比较涂色长度就说实际量相同。

**家长陪学：** 看孩子是否先识别整体、把百分率写成除以一百、保留实际单位并做量级或乘回检查。正确数字但误把“35%”说成“三十五升”仍需讨论。**带走一个发现：** 求一个量的百分之几，就是指定整体乘给定比例；整体与单位不可省略。

## From a proportion to a measured amount

35% describes a proportion without litres or grams. To find an amount, identify the whole it acts on. The same 35% rate applied to 80 L and 40 L gives different litre amounts. Begin by saying **which actual quantity corresponds to 100%**, rather than treating the coefficient as an answer.

On a double number line, the upper scale shows percentages and the lower scale shows litres. Matching positions express the same relationship. The stated whole corresponds to 100%, while the requested amount corresponds to 35%. Work out the question mark before revealing a check.

### Example 1 · Thirty-five percent of eighty litres

1. The whole is **80 L**, corresponding to **100%**. Find the amount at **35%**.
2. Predict: 35% is below 50%, so the answer should be below forty litres.
3. **1%=80÷100=0.8 L**.
4. Thirty-five such shares give **0.8×35=28 L**, or directly **80×35/100=28 L**.
5. Check **28÷80=0.35=35%**. Twenty-eight litres has a measurement unit; the rate 35% is dimensionless.

35 L would be correct only if the reference whole were one hundred litres. Calculating 80×35=2800 L omits the percent sign’s division by one hundred. Check both the number and the unit.

### Example 2 · Use a familiar fraction for a mass share

1. Find **12.5% of 240 g**, matching the whole mass with 100%.
2. **12.5%=12.5/100=1/8**.
3. One of eight equal shares is **240÷8=30 g**.
4. Equivalently **240×0.125=30 g**. Different representations preserve the result.
5. Check **30/240=1/8=12.5%**. Thirty grams is below half of the total mass, as expected.

Results need not be integers. Thirty percent of 5 L is 5×0.3=1.5 L; do not truncate it to one litre. Continuous quantities allow fractional litres. If objects must remain whole, a noninteger result raises a separate interpretation issue rather than permission to discard part of the mathematical answer.

Zero percent of any stated amount is zero. With a zero reference amount, the multiplication 0×35%=0 is valid; the reverse part-divided-by-zero-whole calculation cannot define a proportion. A requirement equal to 125% of a 12 L reference is 12×1.25=15 L, one quarter beyond the reference. It does not mean that a container holding twelve litres produces extra water.

Change the reference amount and requested percentage independently. Hold 35% fixed and change eighty litres to forty: predict that the result halves. Then hold the whole fixed and compare 0%, 50%, 100% and 125%. Input changes clear old results, and the scales identify the 100% reference throughout.

Draw two equal-length lines on paper, labeling their 100% points as 80 L and 40 L. At the same 35% position, write the respective litre amounts. Identical proportion positions have different quantity scales, so matching shaded lengths alone cannot establish matching actual amounts.

**For a parent:** check identification of the whole, division by one hundred, retained units, and a size or inverse check. A correct number accompanied by “35% means thirty-five litres” still needs discussion. **Takeaway:** finding a percentage of an amount multiplies a specified whole by a proportion; retain its reference and unit.
