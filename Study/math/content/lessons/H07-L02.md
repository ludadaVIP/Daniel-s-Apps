# 等差数列

某个数列完整地遵守“每次增加同一个数”，才能称为等差。公差可以为负、为0或为分数；等差不意味着每项都是正整数，也不意味着数值一定越来越大。

## 一条核心想法

**对所有相邻项，aₙ₊₁−aₙ=d固定。** 因而aₙ=a₁+(n−1)d。第一项已经在那里，从a₁到aₙ只发生n−1次转移。

当题目给带序号的两项aₚ、aᵣ且p≠r时，aᵣ−aₚ=(r−p)d。两项之间包含多少个位置与走过多少步不同。例如第2至第5项包括4个位置，却只有3次转移。先认出间隔，再做除法。

### 例1：两个索引项恢复规则

已知完整数列等差，a₂=8、a₅=17。相差3步，所以d=(17−8)/(5−2)=3；不能把差9当公差，也不能按4个位置去除。

由a₂=a₁+d得a₁=5。于是a₆=5+(6−1)×3=20。独立代回a₂=5+3=8、a₅=5+4×3=17，检验两个原条件，而不只核对自己刚推出的公式。

若另题给a₄=11、a₉=26，同样先得5步的增量15，d=3，a₁=11−3×3=2，a₁₂=2+11×3=35。同公差不表示同一数列，仍需要一个起点。

### 例2：负、分数、零与重复条件

取a₁=6、d=−2，项为6、4、2、0、−2，所以a₅=−2。负公差跨过0后仍按同一规则，不取绝对值。若a₃=1、a₇=3，则d=(3−1)/(7−3)=1/2，回推a₁=0，分数公差同样有效。

当a₁=−3、d=0时，每项都为−3；零公差描述的是不再改变，不是项值为0。反过来，仅把a₃=7写两遍不能推出d=0：aₙ=7和bₙ=n+4都符合，却有不同公差。

若同一序号同时要求a₃=7和a₃=8，条件矛盾，不存在这样的数列。它与“条件不足、有许多可能”是不同状态。重复序号不能用0/0来恢复规则。

## 索引间隔工作台

工作台先明确要求整个数列等差，再由带序号的条件预测d、a₁和目标项。改变目标n保留已恢复的公差和首项，只重新判断目标项。

重复条件时，在已知的那个序号仍可回答给定值7，其他项却不唯一。矛盾条件只画原给定点，不伪造一条数列。图中离散点帮助比较，不能以连线外观代替“整个数列等差”的前提。

## 家长核对与迁移

让孩子用手指从第4项走到第9项，边走边数转移，而不是只数两端包含的项。再问d=0保持的是哪个量。下一课用同一通项先求末项，随后把末项与前n项和分开；分数和负号都要继续保留。

# Arithmetic sequences

A sequence is arithmetic when its complete rule gives the same addition at every step. The difference may be negative, zero or fractional. Arithmetic does not mean positive integer terms or automatic growth.

## One central idea

**Every adjacent difference aₙ₊₁−aₙ equals one fixed d.** Therefore aₙ=a₁+(n−1)d. Term one is already present, so reaching term n requires n−1 transitions.

For known indexed terms aₚ,aᵣ with p≠r, aᵣ−aₚ=(r−p)d. Included positions differ from completed transitions: indices two through five include four positions but only three steps. Identify the gap before dividing.

### Example 1: Recover a rule from indexed terms

The whole sequence is known to be arithmetic, with a₂=8,a₅=17. There are three steps, so d=(17−8)/(5−2)=3. The total change nine is not itself d, nor should it be divided by four included positions.

From a₂=a₁+d, recover a₁=5. Then a₆=5+(6−1)×3=20. Independently substitute back: a₂=5+3=8 and a₅=5+4×3=17. Audit both original conditions rather than only the formula you have just generated.

For another sequence with a₄=11,a₉=26, five steps change the value by fifteen, so d=3,a₁=2 and a₁₂=35. Equal differences do not make two sequences identical; an initial position still matters.

### Example 2: Negative, fractional, zero and repeated data

With a₁=6,d=−2, terms are 6,4,2,0,−2, hence a₅=−2. The same signed rule continues past zero. If instead a₃=1,a₇=3, then d=(3−1)/(7−3)=1/2 and a₁=0. Fractional differences are fully valid.

For a₁=−3,d=0, all terms are−3. Zero difference means unchanged values, not zero values. Conversely, repeating a₃=7 does not establish d=0: both aₙ=7 and bₙ=n+4 satisfy it with different differences.

Requiring both a₃=7 and a₃=8 is inconsistent: no sequence meets those conditions. That differs from insufficient conditions allowing many sequences. Never recover a rule by dividing zero by zero at repeated indices.

## Index-gap workbench

The workbench explicitly requires the complete sequence to be arithmetic. Recover d,a₁ and a requested term from indexed conditions. Changing only the target index preserves the recovered difference and first term.

With repeated information, the value at the given index remains known even while other terms are undetermined. Conflicting data stay as the original plotted givens without an invented sequence. Discrete points support comparison but do not replace the premise that the whole sequence is arithmetic.

## Parent check and transfer

Ask the learner to step from position four to nine, counting transitions rather than simply all included positions. Then ask which quantity remains fixed when d=0. The next lesson first obtains the last term and then separates it from a partial sum; negative signs and fractional values must remain intact.
