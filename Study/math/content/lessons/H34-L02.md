# 抽样分布模拟

先固定总体与抽样设计，再想象重复抽取同样大小的样本。每次得到一个样本均值，把这些均值看成一个随机变量X̄，就有了它的抽样分布。图中一个值不再是一位成员的等待时间，而是一整组样本算出的均值。

## n与R分别控制什么

n是每次抽几个人，R是重复几次实验。固定n与设计后，理论抽样分布已经确定；增大R只是让我们对这个分布的有限频率记录更充分。它不增加每组人数，不改变理论方差，也不使有限离散分布自动变正态。

本课继续使用六编号0、0、0、6、6、6的固定总体。每组内部简单随机不放回；抽完一组后重置总体，再独立构思下一组。因此不同组可以出现同一个人，不能把“组内不放回”误读成整个模拟一直删人。

### 例1：枚举得到精确抽样分布

n=2时共有C(6,2)=15个等可能编号子集。两位A有3个子集，均值0；一A一B有9个，均值3；两位B有3个，均值6。所以分布为0:1/5;3:3/5;6:1/5。

E(X̄)=0×1/5+3×3/5+6×1/5=3，与总体均值一致。其方差为(0−3)²/5+(3−3)²×3/5+(6−3)²/5=18/5，标准误SE=√(18/5)分钟。

原始单个人的总体方差为9、标准差3分钟。标准误则是统计量X̄的标准差，两者不能互换。不同编号但相同数值的子集仍各占一份概率，否则会把3、9、3错误改成等权三行。

### 例2：改变样本量，不等于增加模拟次数

n=3时20个等可能子集产生均值0、2、4、6，其质量为1/20、9/20、9/20、1/20，期望仍3，方差9/5。n=6普查只有一个样本，均值恒3、方差0。

作为独立核查，简单随机不放回的均值方差为σ总体²(N−n)/[n(N−1)]。这里N6、σ总体²9，n2给18/5。直接套独立有放回的9/n会得到9/2，忽略了有限总体修正。公式的设计条件不可省。

若固定n2只把R从10增至100，理论0、3、6三点分布不变。观测频率会波动，也不要求每次误差单调减小。若只从A框抽，每次均值都0，模拟再多也只更清楚地暴露这个偏差。

## 用轨迹核来源

工作台使用标明种子的伪随机字序列，并从全部等可能子集中选择。它展示前10组不同编号与均值，频率统计则使用全部R组。零组时频率与均值的模拟平均未定义，不能写成已经观测到0概率。

先预测理论期望、方差与标准误，再增加R核对经验频率。换种子会变轨迹，不改变理论分布。换n或抽样框则需要重建实验记录，不能把不同设计的样本混在一起。

家长可让学生说出横轴是观测还是均值，追问哪次改了n、哪次仅改了R。出口说明必须保留组内不放回、编号多重性、标准误的层级和有限模拟的限度，不能只说“多抽就更准确”。

# Simulating sampling distributions

Fix a population and sampling design, then repeat samples of the same size. Each sample produces one mean. The distribution of those means is the sampling distribution of X̄. One plotted value now represents an entire sample’s statistic, not one person’s waiting time.

## Sample size differs from repetitions

n counts people per sample; R counts repeated experiments. Once n and design are fixed, the theoretical sampling law is fixed. More repetitions refine empirical records without changing sample size, theoretical variance or a discrete law into a normal one.

Use the six distinct units valued 0, 0, 0, 6, 6, 6. Draw without replacement inside each group, then restore the population before the next group. A person may appear in multiple groups; the simulation does not keep permanently removing units.

### Example 1: Enumerate the exact distribution

For n=2 there are fifteen equally likely ID subsets. Three all-A subsets have mean zero, nine mixed subsets mean three, and three all-B subsets mean six. Thus the mean distribution is 0:1/5;3:3/5;6:1/5.

Its expectation is three. Weighted squared distances from three give variance 18/5 and standard error √(18/5) minutes. Individual population values instead have variance nine and SD three minutes. SE is the SD of the statistic, not of individual observations.

Retain distinct subsets even when their means agree. Replacing multiplicities 3, 9, 3 by three equal weights changes the model and its variance.

### Example 2: Change n rather than merely R

With n=3, twenty subsets produce means 0, 2, 4, 6 with masses 1/20, 9/20, 9/20, 1/20. Their expectation stays three and variance is 9/5. A census n=6 gives constant mean three and zero variance.

An independent check uses the SRS-without-replacement variance σ_population²(N−n)/[n(N−1)]. With N=6 and population variance nine, n=2 gives 18/5. Using the independent with-replacement formula 9/n would give 9/2 and omit the finite-population correction.

Increasing R from ten to one hundred at fixed n=2 leaves the three-point theoretical law unchanged. Frequencies fluctuate and need not improve monotonically. An A-only frame continues producing zero means however many repetitions are run; repetition does not repair its bias.

## Keep the simulation auditable

The workbench identifies a seed and pseudorandom word protocol. It displays the first ten ID groups while empirical frequencies use all R groups. At zero repetitions frequencies and the average of simulated means are undefined, rather than observed zero probabilities.

Predict theoretical expectation, variance and SE before drawing more samples. A new seed changes the trajectory without changing theory. Changing n or the frame rebuilds the experiment rather than mixing designs.

Explain what the horizontal axis records, which control changes n, and which only changes R. Preserve within-group sampling rules, distinct-ID multiplicity and the limits of finite simulation. “More draws are better” is not a substitute for these distinctions.
