# 空间数量积与夹角

数量积把两个空间向量联系到一个实数。它可以检验垂直、帮助求角，但**数量积不是余弦，也不是向量**，零向量还需要单独处理。

## 从三项乘积到角

u=(x₁,y₁,z₁)、v=(x₂,y₂,z₂)，u·v=x₁x₂+y₁y₂+z₁z₂。空间直角坐标轴的单位基向量互相垂直，所以展开时只保留对应坐标乘积，不出现跨轴交叉项。

若u、v均非零，定义夹角θ在0°至180°之间，有cosθ=(u·v)/(|u||v|)。分母为正：点积正对应锐角（同向边界0°），负对应钝角（反向边界180°），点积0对应90°。要区分一般锐钝与同向、反向的边界。

若有零向量，点积仍按分量算出0，但方向与夹角无定义，不能在分母为0时硬说cosθ=0。“点积0等价于垂直夹角”必须附上两个非零向量的前提。

### 例1：垂直与归一化是两个步骤

u=(1,2,−1)、v=(2,−1,0)，点积2−2+0=0。两向量非零，因此空间中垂直，即使投影图看起来不是直角也不改变结论。

换v=(1,0,0)，点积为1，不等于余弦1。|u|=√6、|v|=1，cosθ=1/√6=√6/6。只有特定单位向量情形，点积才恰与余弦数值相同。若把u数乘2，点积变2，模之积也变2√6，余弦不变，夹角也不变。

### 例2：向量角、较小线角与零边界

取u=(1,0,0)、v=(−1,1,0)，点积−1、模之积√2，cosθ=−√2/2，向量夹角135°。若它们作为两条相交直线的方向，直线本身不选朝向，可把v改成−v，较小线角为45°。所以较小线角的余弦用绝对点积，不能把它与指定方向的向量角混用。

再取u=(0,0,0)、v=(1,2,3)。点积0，但u没有方向，夹角分母0，没有90°这个结论。相反，v=−u且u非零时余弦−1、角180°；v=u且非零时角0°，这些边界都有定义。

## 工作台与空间角的条件

工作台分别预测点积、余弦和类型，允许undefined。选择同向、反向、垂直、一般锐角与零向量例子，核验表把平方模与点积分开列出。换相机不清答案；向量改变后，仅重做数学值改变的预测。

图的角不能直接量读，零箭头或投影重合也不能代替非零检查。可选迁移：直线与平面的夹角φ是直线和它在平面内投影的较小角，满足sinφ=|u·n|/(|u||n|)，与方向和法向的较小夹角互余。不要把法向量夹角直接当线面角。

出口必须给反例、补条件、再给有效例，家长据此人工核对。下一课还要把方向关系与位置关系分开：方向点积0不保证两条空间直线相交。

# Dot products and spatial angles

The dot product connects two spatial vectors to a scalar. It tests perpendicular directions and helps calculate angles, but **it is neither cosine nor a vector**. Zero vectors require a separate check.

## From three products to an angle

For u=(x₁,y₁,z₁),v=(x₂,y₂,z₂), define u·v=x₁x₂+y₁y₂+z₁z₂. The orthonormal coordinate basis has unit vectors with zero cross-axis dot products, leaving only matching component products.

For nonzero u,v, their vector angle θ lies from 0° to 180° and cosθ=(u·v)/(|u||v|). The positive denominator makes a positive dot product acute, with a zero-angle same-direction boundary; a negative one is obtuse, with an opposite-direction 180° boundary. Dot zero gives 90° only after the nonzero check.

A zero vector still has dot product zero by the component formula, but no direction or angle. Its zero denominator cannot be treated as cosine zero.

### Example 1: Perpendicularity and normalisation are separate steps

For u=(1,2,−1),v=(2,−1,0), the dot product is 2−2+0=0. Both are nonzero, so they are perpendicular in space regardless of the displayed projected angle.

For v=(1,0,0), the dot product is one, but cosine is not one. Norms are √6 and one, giving cosine √6/6. Doubling u doubles both dot product and norm product, preserving cosine and angle. The dot product equals cosine numerically only under particular norm conditions.

### Example 2: Directed vector angle, smaller line angle and zero

Vectors (1,0,0),(−1,1,0) give cosine −√2/2 and angle 135°. Lines have no chosen orientation, so reversing the second direction preserves the line. The smaller angle between the corresponding intersecting lines is 45°. Its cosine uses an absolute dot product, unlike the directed vector angle.

Now take u=0,v=(1,2,3). Dot zero remains meaningful, but the angle does not exist because the denominator vanishes. In contrast, nonzero opposite vectors have angle 180°, and identical nonzero vectors angle zero; those boundaries are defined.

## Conditions in the workbench and spatial angle transfer

Predict dot product, cosine and angle type separately, including undefined. Examples include same direction, opposite direction, perpendicularity, a general acute angle and zero. The audit separates dot product from squared norms. Camera changes preserve answers; changed vectors reset only changed mathematical predictions.

Do not measure the projection’s angle or infer direction from a collapsed arrow. Optional transfer: the smaller line-plane angle φ satisfies sinφ=|u·n|/(|u||n|), complementary to the smaller angle with the normal. A direction-to-normal angle is not automatically the line-plane angle.

The exit requires a counterexample, corrected conditions and a valid example, reviewed by a parent. Next distinguish direction from position: a zero direction dot product does not establish an intersection of spatial lines.
