# 简单递推关系

递推关系用已知项生成后面的项；初始条件负责启动。只给一个关系式，未必唯一确定一条数列。本课从n=0编号，目标是把规则、足够的初值和索引放在一起，逐项生成并核验答案。图上的方框都是离散项，不应在它们之间补一条连续曲线来“读出”非整数索引。

## 先数更新次数

一次规则u(n+1)=p u(n)+q配一个初值u(0)，便可按n=0,1,2,…向前生成。到u(4)需要四次更新，但表中有u(0)至u(4)五行。二阶相加规则u(n+2)=u(n+1)+u(n)需要两个相邻初值，下一项同时使用前两项。

### 例1：把仿射递推平移成等比

设u(0)=1、u(n+1)=2u(n)+1。依次得到u(1)=3、u(2)=7、u(3)=15、u(4)=31。若误从u(1)=1开始，会把31错放到第五项；写出完整的索引行能发现这种偏移。

令v(n)=u(n)+1，则v(n+1)=2u(n)+2=2v(n)，且v(0)=2。于是v(n)=2×2^n，原数列u(n)=2^(n+1)−1。必须核回原题：n=0给1；若u(k)=2^(k+1)−1，则2u(k)+1=2^(k+2)−1，满足下一项规则。猜到前几项相同不够，初值和任意k的递推都要核。

若乘数p=1，规则变为u(n+1)=u(n)+q，是等差数列，直接得u(n)=u(0)+nq。不要把含p−1的通用推导在p=1处硬除零。例如u(0)=3、q=2，五次更新给u(5)=13；p=0时从第一次更新起所有项都是q，也不需要特别发明不存在的除法。

### 例2：相同规则，第二初值决定后续

给u(0)=0、u(1)=1、u(n+2)=u(n+1)+u(n)。逐项得0,1,1,2,3,5,8，所以u(6)=8。这里两项初值已足够，不能把相加规则误读成“每次加1”。

现在删去u(1)。令它为t，后续为0,t,t,2t,3t,…，u(4)=3t。取t=1得3，取t=2得6，两条数列都满足同一个关系和u(0)=0。因此u(4)不能唯一确定，填insufficient，而非无解。u(0)仍已知是0，资料不足并不意味着每一个量都未知。

## 规则和样本不同

工作台能改变初值、乘数和加数，显示当前规则与逐项核验表。先预测所求项，再打开表检查哪一步首次偏离。负乘数可产生交替，零乘数可让后续稳定；这些行为要由明确规则判断，不能只凭“数列应该递增”的印象排除。

有限前缀可以由不同规则继续延伸，所以“观察前五项”与“已声明递推”是两类资料。通项是另一种描述，需要证明与递推描述给出同一条数列。一次确定性更新配一个初值时，可归纳证明唯一性；二阶规则也要让两个初值都匹配，才能逐步传递相同的项。

出口把平移、通项、归纳核验和缺初值反例连成一段，由人审查。家长可问“到u(4)做了几次更新”和“换第二初值哪一项先变”；若不清楚，回到H07的序列、递推与通项，将索引与值分成两行，不先背复杂公式。

# Simple recurrence relations

A recurrence generates later terms from earlier ones; initial conditions start it. A relation alone may not identify a unique sequence. This lesson uses indices starting at zero and combines the rule, sufficient seeds and careful indexing. The boxes represent discrete terms; drawing a continuous curve between them would invent information at non-integer indices.

## Count updates separately from terms

The first-order rule u(n+1)=p u(n)+q and one initial value u(0) determine successive terms. Reaching u(4) takes four updates, although the table contains five rows from zero through four. The second-order addition rule u(n+2)=u(n+1)+u(n) needs two adjacent seeds because each new term uses two previous terms.

### Example 1: Shift an affine recurrence

Let u(0)=1 and u(n+1)=2u(n)+1. The terms at indices zero to four are 1,3,7,15,31. Relabelling the initial one as u(1) would shift every answer. Write indices alongside values before calculating a distant term.

Define v(n)=u(n)+1. Then v(n+1)=2u(n)+2=2v(n), and v(0)=2. This geometric sequence gives v(n)=2×2^n and u(n)=2^(n+1)−1. Verify both requirements: at zero the expression gives one; assuming it at k, the update produces 2u(k)+1=2^(k+2)−1. Agreement at a few sample terms alone would not verify the general formula.

When p=1, the recurrence is arithmetic: u(n)=u(0)+nq. Do not divide by p−1 in a formula at that parameter. For u(0)=3 and q=2, five updates give u(5)=13. At p=0, all terms after the initial one equal q. These cases are defined and deserve direct treatment rather than an invalid division.

### Example 2: Why the second seed matters

With u(0)=0, u(1)=1 and u(n+2)=u(n+1)+u(n), the terms are 0,1,1,2,3,5,8. Hence u(6)=8. The rule adds the two preceding terms, not a fixed one each time.

Remove u(1) and call it t. The sequence begins 0,t,t,2t,3t, so u(4)=3t. Choosing t=1 gives three; choosing t=2 gives six. Both sequences meet the stated relation and u(0)=0. The requested fourth-index term is therefore not uniquely determined: enter insufficient. This does not mean there is no solution, nor that every term is unknown. The given u(0) is still zero.

## A declared rule differs from a pattern guess

Change the affine seed, multiplier or addition in the workbench. Predict a term, then reveal the indexed table to locate the first incorrect update. A negative multiplier can produce alternating behaviour and a zero multiplier can produce a constant tail. Determine behaviour from the rule rather than assuming every sequence should increase.

Many different rules can extend the same finite list. A proposed nth term is another description that must satisfy the initial data and the original recurrence. A deterministic first-order update with one fixed seed is unique because matching terms give matching next terms. A second-order argument must start with two matching seeds and carry both forward.

The exit connects the shifted sequence, its formula, an induction check and two different completions of missing initial data. A person reviews the argument. Ask how many updates reach u(4), then ask which term first changes when the second seed changes. If indexing is uncertain, return to the earlier sequence lesson and write a row of indices above a row of values before introducing any general formula.
