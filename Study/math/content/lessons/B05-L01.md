## 长度是在数两端之间的等长间隔

尺上刻度表示位置，物体长度表示它占了多少个等长单位。物体从零开始时，终点数字恰好等于长度；起点不在零，就要用终点减起点。不能把物体前面的空白也当成物体的一部分。

测量先对齐起点、看清单位，再读终点。同一把尺的小间隔应等长；不是把印出来的数字或刻度线条本身数一遍。七个间隔有八个端点记号，这也说明“数线条”与“数长度单位”不同。

### 例 1 · 纸条从三到十

1. 纸条左端对齐 3 cm，右端对齐 10 cm。
2. 它占据三到十之间的长度，零到三不属于纸条。
3. **10−3=7 cm**。
4. 不拉伸纸条，把左端移到零，右端会在七厘米；物体没有变长。
5. 若把两端写成毫米，是 30 mm 与 100 mm，差 70 mm，仍表示同一长度。

### 例 2 · 两米三十五厘米统一单位

1. 1 m=100 cm，所以 2 m=200 cm。
2. 200 cm 与 35 cm 单位相同，可以合并。
3. **2 m 35 cm=235 cm**，不是 2+35=37 cm。
4. 检查：235 cm 中有两个完整的一百厘米与余下三十五厘米，换回 2 m 35 cm。

### 单位变小，计数常变大，物体不伸缩

常见关系：**1 cm=10 mm，1 m=100 cm=1000 mm，1 km=1000 m**。一厘米与一毫米的数字都是一，但长度不同。相同长度改用小单位，需要更多单位来计数；改用大单位，数值可能变小。

4 cm=40 mm，是四个十毫米。750 mm=75 cm，是七百五十里面有七十五个十。假想步道 1500 m=1.5 km，包含一千米与半千米；小数并没有把步道“缩短”。

不要只背“乘十、除一百”。先写单位关系，再决定是在合并小单位还是拆成小单位。只有单位一致，才能直接相加比较；7 cm 与 7 mm 不是同一长度。

工作台用固定的示意尺。起点滑动时，物体长度保持；切换 cm 与 mm 时，图示物体占的位置与宽度保持，刻度数字改变。屏幕比例不是一比一实际尺，不能把屏幕上的像素当家里的厘米；练习依给定刻度测量。

**小检查：** 纸条从 3 cm 到 11 cm，先预测长度，再用毫米刻度核对。若两个端点重合，长度应是多少？

## Length counts equal intervals between endpoints

Ruler labels record positions; object length records how many equal units it occupies. If an object starts at zero, its endpoint happens to match its length. Otherwise subtract start from end. The space before the object is not part of the object.

Align the start, identify the unit and read the end. Small ruler intervals must be equal. Do not simply count printed numbers or tick marks: seven intervals have eight endpoint marks, so counting marks is not counting length units.

### Example 1 · A strip from three to ten

1. Align the left end with 3 cm and the right end with 10 cm.
2. Only the interval from three to ten belongs to the strip; zero to three does not.
3. **10−3=7 cm**.
4. Move the strip without stretching until its start is at zero. Its end is now at seven centimetres, with unchanged length.
5. Millimetre endpoints are 30 mm and 100 mm. Their difference, 70 mm, represents the same length.

### Example 2 · Express two metres and thirty-five centimetres in one unit

1. Since 1 m=100 cm, two metres are 200 cm.
2. Combine 200 cm and 35 cm because their units match.
3. **2 m 35 cm=235 cm**, not 2+35=37 cm.
4. Check by separating 235 cm into two complete hundreds and thirty-five centimetres, recovering 2 m 35 cm.

### Smaller units change the count, not the object

Common relationships are **1 cm=10 mm, 1 m=100 cm=1000 mm and 1 km=1000 m**. One centimetre and one millimetre both use the number one but have different lengths. Measuring the same length with smaller units needs more units; larger units may give a smaller number.

Four centimetres are forty millimetres: four amounts of ten. Seven hundred and fifty millimetres make seventy-five centimetres. An imaginary 1500 m track is 1.5 km, comprising a kilometre and half a kilometre. The decimal does not shorten the track.

Do not memorise only “multiply by ten” or “divide by a hundred”. Write the unit relationship and identify whether small units combine or a larger unit is split. Direct addition and comparison need matching units; 7 cm and 7 mm are not the same length.

The workshop uses a fixed schematic ruler. Moving the start retains object length; switching cm and mm keeps the object's drawn width and position while labels change. The screen is not an actual-size measuring tool, and pixels are not household centimetres. Read the stated scale.

**Quick check:** predict the length from 3 cm to 11 cm and check using millimetre labels. What length results when both endpoints coincide?
