## 同样的扇形重排，面积保留

圆面积测内部覆盖的大小，用平方单位。把圆分成偶数个相同扇形，再上下交错排列；每片只旋转和平移，半径、圆心角和弧都不变。没有拉伸、重叠或丢弃，因此总面积仍是原圆面积。

**有限片数时，长边仍然是弧。** 模型的虚线平行四边形只作辅助，不能把外侧弓形删掉后说它就是整个圆。份数增加时，弧形波浪越来越细，形状趋近长方形：一条长边来自半个圆周，趋近的底为 **πr**，高趋近 **r**，从而支持 **A=πr×r=πr²**。

### 例 1 · 半径三的面积与有限弧边

1. 半径 **3 cm**，精确面积 **A=π×3×3=9π cm²**。
2. 分成八份，每份面积为整个圆的八分之一；重排后仍是原来的八份。
3. 上下长边各来自半周弧，不能把整周 6π 当成一条底。
4. 虚线沿弦围出的辅助平行四边形比真实扇形总面积小；差额仍在弓形里，没有被切掉。
5. 改成十六、三十二或六十四份，原圆仍为 9π cm²，波浪变细而面积不变。

在有限图中，半径是扇形斜边，虚线高度并不已经恰好等于 r。模型分别报告有限辅助底、高，避免把近似形状的标注当精确相等。这里的重排解释支持公式，不是有限图突然把曲线变成直线。

### 例 2 · 直径先折半，再平方

1. 给 **直径 8 cm**，先得 **r=4 cm**。
2. 精确面积 **16π cm²**。
3. 若明确按 π≈3.14 计算，得 **A≈3.14×16=50.24 cm²**。
4. 把八直接平方会得到四倍的错误面积；把周长 8π 当面积则混淆测量对象。
5. 若半径由二变四，半径倍数二，面积由 4π 变 16π，倍数为 **四**，不是二。

平方来自两个长度因素：底随 r 变，高也随 r 变。因此所有长度放大两倍时，覆盖面积放大四倍；这与上一课周长只放大两倍不同。缩放比较的两个圆须使用相同单位。

工作台先看原圆，再按按钮重排同一批片；改变份数不改变 r。核对时说清“哪一半圆周作为一条长边”“为什么面积没丢”“有限辅助形与真实弧边哪里不同”。颜色只用于追踪片，片号和原弧也保留在图形结构中，不靠颜色猜公式。

纸笔可以画粗略六份或八份示意，不需用剪刀切硬材料。家长允许孩子指出有限图不精确，那正是重要观察，而不是必须忽略的瑕疵。带走一个发现：**每片面积精确保留；越来越细的重排说明 πr 与 r 的乘积，面积必须用平方单位。**

## Rearrange the same sectors without changing area

Circle area measures the interior in square units. Split a circle into an even number of equal sectors and alternate their orientations. Rotation and translation retain each radius, central angle and arc. With no stretching, overlap or discarded pieces, the total area remains the original circle’s area.

**Finite arrangements still have curved long edges.** The dashed parallelogram is a guide, not the entire collection of sectors. Do not discard the curved segments. As sectors become finer, the waves flatten toward a rectangle. Each long edge comes from half the circumference, giving limiting base **πr** and height approaching **r**, supporting **A=πr²**.

### Example 1 · Radius three and the remaining arcs

1. With **r=3 cm**, exact area is **9π cm²**.
2. Divide into eight equal sectors; each is one eighth of the whole. Rearrangement retains all eight.
3. Each long edge comes from half the circumference, not the full 6π.
4. The dashed chord-based parallelogram has less area than the sectors. The difference remains in curved segments rather than having been cut away.
5. With sixteen, thirty-two or sixty-four sectors, total area stays 9π cm² while the waves become finer.

For a finite arrangement, the radius forms a sloping sector edge and the dashed guide height is not yet exactly r. The model reports its finite guide dimensions separately. This prevents an approximate outline from being mistaken for an exact rectangle; finite rearrangement does not turn arcs into straight lines.

### Example 2 · Halve diameter before squaring

1. A **diameter of 8 cm** gives **r=4 cm**.
2. Exact area is **16π cm²**.
3. Using the requested π≈3.14 gives **A≈50.24 cm²**.
4. Squaring the diameter directly would produce four times the correct area. Using circumference 8π would confuse the quantity.
5. Doubling radius from two to four changes area from 4π to 16π, a factor of **four**, not two.

Two length factors explain the square: both the limiting base and height scale with r. Doubling all lengths therefore quadruples area, while circumference only doubles. Use matching units when comparing circles.

The workshop first displays the original circle, then rearranges the same pieces. Changing their count retains radius. Explain which half-circumference supplies a long edge, why no area is lost, and how the dashed guide differs from the actual arcs. Colours help track pieces, with their identifiers and original arc geometry retained as well. A paper sketch of six or eight pieces is sufficient; no rigid material needs cutting. A parent should welcome the observation that finite outlines are not exact rectangles. The takeaway distinguishes exact area conservation from the limiting shape that explains πr times r, with square units required for area.
