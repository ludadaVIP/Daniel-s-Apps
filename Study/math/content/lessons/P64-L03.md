## 比较完整的位置，不能先丢掉负号

正数比较时八大于三，但 **−8 与 −3** 是另外两个数。负号不是可以先删去的装饰。把它们放在同一条从左到右增大的数轴上，位置顺序会给出可靠判断。

本课只用同一个零点、同样单位与同一方向。**右边的数更大，左边的数更小；重合才相等。** 温度从下到上增大的刻度表达同样顺序，但画数轴时仍遵守这里的向右约定。

### 例 1 · 为什么负八小于负三

1. −8 位于零左八格，−3 位于零左三格。
2. **−3 在 −8 右边**，所以 **−8<−3**。
3. 从 −8 向右走五格，就能到 −3，沿数轴是在增大。
4. “八比三大”只比较了两个正数，不能推出 −8>−3。
5. 核查：两者都小于零，而 −3 比 −8 更接近零，是两个负数中较大的那个。

对两个不同的负数，“离零较近的较大”可以作为数轴顺序的结果，但不能把它扩成所有数字的规则。正数二比正数五更接近零，却是二小于五。根本依据始终是同一方向上的位置。

### 例 2 · 跨过零比较与相同位置

1. 比较 **−2 与 +1**，先定位零的两侧。
2. −2 在左、+1 在右，所以 **−2<+1**。
3. 比较 **−1 与 0**，零在右，故 **−1<0**；零比负数大不代表零是正数。
4. 两个点若都表示 **−4**，它们重合，应写 **−4=−4**，不是看到负号就选小于。
5. 核查问题到底要“较大”还是“较小”：−9 与 −5 中，较小的是 −9，较大的是 −5。

假想温度 −2°C 比 −6°C 暖，因为 −2 在较高刻度。两者仍都是零下；选“较暖读数”时答案应保留 −2°C 的负号，不能写成 +2°C。数字相近不是可省符号的理由。

工作台让你调整 A、B 的整数，再选择 `<`、`=` 或 `>`。先指哪一点靠右，最后核对。改任一个数会清空旧选择和旧反馈，避免拿上一组的关系回答新问题。两点重合时标签 A=B 合并显示，图不会故意错开造成两个位置的假象。

**用反例检查口号：** “负数总比正数小”适用于这一类比较，但不能回答两个负数谁更大；“到零距离较大就数值较大”又会在 −8、−3 这组失败。后面学习绝对值时，我们会把“到零距离”与“带符号大小”明确分开。

**家长陪学：** 先问孩子比较对象与目标，再请他用左、右或重合解释。数值答对却说“因为八比三大”需要继续回看；允许用温度、数轴或逐格移动说明，不凭单个关键词判理解。**带走一个发现：** 带符号的数按数轴位置比较；负号和数字必须一起保留。

## Compare complete positions without discarding signs

Eight exceeds three among positive numbers, but **−8 and −3** are different values. A minus sign is not decoration to remove before comparing. Place both values on one rightward-increasing number line to obtain reliable order.

Use one zero, equal units and one direction. **Farther right is larger, farther left smaller, and coincident positions equal.** A vertically increasing temperature scale expresses the same order, while this horizontal axis uses the stated rightward convention.

### Example 1 · Why negative eight is below negative three

1. −8 is eight intervals left of zero; −3 is three left.
2. **−3 lies right of −8**, so **−8<−3**.
3. Five rightward moves connect −8 to −3, increasing the value.
4. “Eight exceeds three” compares positive numbers and cannot prove −8>−3.
5. Both values are below zero, with −3 the nearer and larger of these two negatives.

For two distinct negative numbers, nearer zero means larger. Do not extend that shortcut to every pair: positive two is nearer zero than positive five but is smaller. The underlying rule remains position along one increasing direction.

### Example 2 · Compare across zero and recognize coincidence

1. Locate **−2 and +1** on opposite sides of zero.
2. −2 is left and +1 right, giving **−2<+1**.
3. Zero is right of −1, so **−1<0**. Being larger than a negative does not make zero positive.
4. Two points both representing **−4** coincide, so **−4=−4**. A minus sign alone does not dictate a less-than symbol.
5. Check the requested direction: of −9 and −5, −9 is smaller and −5 larger.

A fictional reading of −2°C is warmer than −6°C because it is higher on the scale. Both are still below zero. Keep the minus sign when reporting the warmer −2°C; +2°C would be a different reading.

Adjust A and B in the workshop, decide which lies right, then choose `<`, `=` or `>`. Changing either number clears the previous relation and feedback. Coincident points share an A=B label rather than being artificially separated into apparently different locations.

Use counterexamples to examine shortcuts. “A negative is smaller than a positive” does not decide which of two negatives is larger. “The larger absolute value means the larger signed number” fails for −8 and −3. The absolute-value lesson later separates distance from zero from signed order.

**For a parent:** ask what quantities and target are being compared, then request a left/right/coincidence explanation. A correct number justified by “eight exceeds three” still needs revision. Temperature, an axis or unit moves can all support reasoning. **Takeaway:** compare signed values by their positions, keeping each sign with its digits.
