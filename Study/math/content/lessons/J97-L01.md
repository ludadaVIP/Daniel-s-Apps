# 独立事件的直观意义

## 知道一件事，会改变另一件事的机会吗

把问题限定在一个明确随机模型中：先说基本结果、每种结果的概率以及事件是什么。独立不是“两个事件名字不同”，也不是“它们不能一起发生”。直观上，知道A已经发生，不改变B的概率，才体现独立。在P(A)>0时，可以比较P(B|A)和P(B)；竖线表示“已知A”的条件。

更一般的乘积定义是P(A且B)=P(A)P(B)。且表示两个事件同时满足，分子来自交集。工作台四格依次记录A且B、仅A、仅B、皆非，它们互不重叠且覆盖整个空间。格子里是等可能编号结果的数量，四个格子本身不一定等可能。求边际概率要合并一行或一列，不能把单格数误作整个事件数。

### 例 1：两枚独立公平硬币

模型明确两枚公平硬币独立，完整结果HH、HT、TH、TT各有1/4机会。A为首枚H，包含HH、HT；B为次枚H，包含HH、TH。因此P(A)=P(B)=1/2，P(A且B)=P(HH)=1/4，恰是1/2×1/2。若已知A，只保留HH、HT，两者等可能，B的条件概率仍1/2。

A与B能够同时发生，HH就是共同结果，所以独立不等于互斥。换成九个等可能编号、四格2、4、1、2，P(A)=6/9、P(B)=3/9，交集2/9，乘积也2/9。独立也不要求两事件的概率相等。先把分母与交集找对，再比较精确分数。

### 例 2：同一枚骰子的奇偶

公平六面骰子，A为偶数{2,4,6}，B为奇数{1,3,5}。各自概率1/2，但没有同时奇偶的点数，交集概率0，不等于边际乘积1/4。它们互斥而不独立；若已经知道偶数，奇数的机会由1/2变为0。

另一个八编号模型的四格为3、1、1、3。两边际仍各1/2，但交集3/8，条件P(B|A)=3/4，均显示不独立。这解释了为什么只知道边际概率相等不够；还要知道两个事件怎样共同出现。

## 零概率与证据的边界

如果A不可能，P(A)=0，P(B|A)不能写成0/0。乘积定义仍有意义：交集为空，P(A且B)=0=P(A)P(B)，因此零概率事件与任意事件按此定义独立。这个边界用来防止条件式零除，不是让孩子机械记口号；主要练习使用正概率条件。

完整等可能模型与现实抽样表不同。模型把所有基本结果列齐，比例直接定义模型概率。有限观测表只是样本，即使其中频数恰好满足乘积关系，也不能单凭这一批证明现实机制独立。抽样波动、遗漏和测量方式仍需考虑；一次不吻合也不自动证明装置不公平。

先预测独立性和交集概率，再揭示边际与条件概率。交换表的行列只改变显示，应保留结论；更换实际模型则重新判断。家长核验完整空间、条件分母、乘积比较与互斥反例，数值判对不替代这些理由。

# Independence of events

## Does learning one event change another chance?

Specify the random model first: basic outcomes, their probabilities and the events. Different names do not establish independence, and being unable to occur together describes disjointness. When P(A)>0, the intuitive independence question compares P(B|A) with P(B). The vertical bar means given A.

The general product definition is P(A and B)=P(A)P(B). Intersection means both requirements hold. The lab table has four disjoint cells: overlap, A only, B only and neither. Together they exhaust equally likely numbered outcomes. The cells need not have equal probability. Marginals require a whole row or column, rather than just one cell.

### Example 1: independent fair coins

The stipulated model has independent fair coins with HH, HT, TH, TT each of probability 1/4. A=first H contains HH and HT; B=second H contains HH and TH. Both marginals are 1/2 and their intersection HH has probability 1/4, equal to the product. Given A, only HH and HT remain, equally likely, so the conditional B probability remains 1/2.

These independent events can occur together: HH belongs to both. For another model with nine equally likely IDs and cell counts 2,4,1,2, P(A)=6/9 and P(B)=3/9, while overlap and product both equal 2/9. Independence does not require equal marginals. Identify denominators and intersection before comparing exact fractions.

### Example 2: even and odd on one die

On a fair six-sided die, A=even and B=odd each have probability 1/2, but their intersection is empty. Zero differs from the marginal product 1/4, so they are disjoint and dependent. Knowing even changes the probability of odd to zero.

Eight equally likely IDs with cells 3,1,1,3 also have marginals 1/2, but overlap 3/8 and conditional B given A equal to 3/4. Equal marginals alone do not determine how events occur together.

If A is impossible, P(B|A) is undefined: never calculate 0/0. The product definition still gives zero intersection equal to P(A)P(B), so impossible events are independent under that definition. This is a domain boundary, while core practice uses positive-probability conditions.

A complete equally likely model is not a finite observed sample. Model counts exhaust outcomes and define its probabilities. A sample can accidentally satisfy the product relation without proving real-world independence; variation, coverage and measurement remain relevant. One mismatch likewise does not prove an unfair device.

Predict classification and intersection before revealing the audit. Transposing the display preserves the mathematics; choosing a new model requires a new judgement.
