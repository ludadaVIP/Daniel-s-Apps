# 矩阵的直观意义

本课把二阶矩阵看作二维线性变换，采用列向量约定。写M=[a,b;c,d]时，逗号分开同一行，分号换到下一行。矩阵作用于(x,y)得到(ax+by,cx+dy)。目标是从两根基向量的像读懂这个规则，并通过复合操作理解顺序。

## 两列是两根基向量的像

设e1=(1,0)、e2=(0,1)。因为(x,y)=x e1+y e2，线性变换保数乘与加法，必有T(x,y)=xT(e1)+yT(e2)。因此第一列是T(e1)，第二列是T(e2)。这不是凭矩阵外形硬背的乘法规则，而是从线性性得到的计算。所有这样的二阶线性变换都保持原点。

### 例1：先旋转，再水平伸长

取A=[2,0;0,1]表示水平坐标乘2，B=[0,−1;1,0]表示逆时针90°旋转。列向量约定下ABv=A(Bv)，所以右边B先作用。先把e1经B变为(0,1)，再经A仍为(0,1)；把e2经B变为(−1,0)，再经A为(−2,0)。两像按列放置，得AB=[0,−2;1,0]。

对v=(1,2)，Bv=(−2,1)，再A得(−4,1)。也可直接算ABv=(0×1−2×2,1×1+0×2)=(−4,1)。顺次作用和复合矩阵得到同结果，是对列向量约定的核验；不能把A与B的对应位置逐项相乘，那个运算不是变换复合。

### 例2：交换顺序与丢失信息

BA先做A再做B。e1先为(2,0)，后为(0,2)；e2先为(0,1)，后为(−1,0)，所以BA=[0,−1;2,0]。作用(1,2)得(−2,2)，不同于AB的(−4,1)。由一个反例就足以否定“所有矩阵都交换”；也不应反过来说没有任何矩阵会交换，恒等矩阵就是容易核验的例子。

再看P=[1,0;0,0]，它把(x,y)投到(x,0)。输入(3,5)和(3,−2)都变成(3,0)，因此仅给输出无法唯一找回原输入。行列式ad−bc=0，原平面面积压为零；这是奇异变换。零矩阵更把所有向量送到原点。不能对每个矩阵都随意写一个逆矩阵。

## 从操作核验复合

工作台可切换伸长、旋转、错切、投影、零和恒等变换，再改变AB/BA。先预测整个复合矩阵和向量结果，之后打开核验表对照两列与行列式。图中画复合变换后的e1、e2和v，图的两轴等单位；共点标签合并，不把投影后重合的向量画成不同终点。

det的绝对值是面积缩放倍数，符号还记录取向。这里不要求背一般逆矩阵公式，而要能识别丢失信息。即使两个不同的矩阵在某一个向量上结果相同，也不能据此认定两变换完全相同；应核两根基向量。固定平移通常不保原点，超出本课二阶线性矩阵的表示范围。

# Intuition of matrices

Treat a two-by-two matrix as a linear map of the plane, using column vectors. In M=[a,b;c,d], a semicolon separates rows. Its action on (x,y) is (ax+by,cx+dy). The main goal is to understand this rule from two basis images and then use that meaning to interpret composition order.

## Columns are basis images

Let e1=(1,0) and e2=(0,1). Every vector has the form (x,y)=x e1+y e2. A linear map preserves addition and scalar multiplication, so T(x,y)=xT(e1)+yT(e2). The first column is therefore T(e1), and the second is T(e2). Matrix multiplication is grounded in this linearity, not merely in a pattern of symbols. These linear maps all preserve the origin.

### Example 1: Rotate, then stretch horizontally

Let A=[2,0;0,1] stretch horizontal coordinates by two and B=[0,−1;1,0] rotate anticlockwise by ninety degrees. Under the column-vector convention, ABv=A(Bv), so B acts first. Send e1 through B to (0,1), then through A to (0,1). Send e2 through B to (−1,0), then through A to (−2,0). Place these images as columns to obtain AB=[0,−2;1,0].

For v=(1,2), first Bv=(−2,1) and then A(Bv)=(−4,1). Directly applying AB gives (0×1−2×2,1×1+0×2), the same result. This checks the meaning of the composite. Multiplying corresponding entries of A and B instead would not compute transformation composition.

### Example 2: Reverse the order and lose information

BA applies A before B. The image of e1 is now (0,2), and the image of e2 is (−1,0), giving BA=[0,−1;2,0]. It sends (1,2) to (−2,2), different from AB’s (−4,1). One counterexample disproves a universal commutativity claim. It does not mean no matrices ever commute; an identity matrix provides an easy case to check.

Projection P=[1,0;0,0] sends (x,y) to (x,0). The different inputs (3,5) and (3,−2) both yield (3,0), so the output cannot uniquely recover the input. The determinant ad−bc is zero, reflecting collapse of plane area. This is a singular map. The zero matrix collapses everything to the origin. An inverse cannot simply be assumed for every matrix.

## Check composition through its action

Choose stretch, rotation, shear, projection, zero or identity maps in the workbench, then switch between AB and BA. Predict the whole composite matrix and its action on a vector before opening the audit. Compare both basis-image columns and the determinant. The plot uses equal coordinate units and shows the transformed basis vectors and v. Coincident endpoints share labels rather than being drawn as different geometric points.

The determinant’s magnitude gives area scale, while its sign records orientation. This lesson focuses on recognising loss of information rather than memorising an inverse formula. Two matrices agreeing on a single vector need not be the same transformation; verify both basis images. A fixed translation usually moves the origin and is outside the two-by-two linear representation used here.

The exit connects the column meaning, right-to-left composition and a projection with no unique inverse. A person checks the reasoning. Ask which basis vector gives the first column, then whether the map loses any input information. If rows and columns are confused, return to vector decomposition and apply the map separately to e1 and e2 before using a multiplication recipe.
