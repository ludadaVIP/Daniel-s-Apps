## 直线与原点要连同比值一起看

本课的假想价格规则已经给定，不要求询价或购买。x 表示 0–8 L 的连续水量，y 表示新西兰元总价。规则 **y=3x** 表示每升三元且没有固定费用；两升六元、四升十二元，水量扩大几倍，总价也扩大同样几倍。

这样的关系称为**正比例**。对正的 x，y/x 保持常数；零输入对应零输出，图像是经过原点的直线。不能把“是直线”或“过原点”单独当作充分证据，也不能用零行计算 0/0。

### 例 1 · 给定规则产生恒定比值

1. 给定 **y=3x**，x 单位升、y 单位新西兰元。
2. 取 **1 L、2 L、4 L**，总价分别为 **NZ$3、NZ$6、NZ$12**。
3. 正输入对应的比值为 **3/1=6/2=12/4=3 NZ$/L**。
4. 当 **x=0**，由原规则直接得 **y=0**；看零点对应，不去算 0/0。
5. 因为给定公式对本题整个范围都成立，所以正输入比值恒定，图线经过原点且为直线。反向总价三十元、每升五元的规则 y=5x，则可用 30÷5=6 L 求水量。

图中比例常数的数值依赖轴单位。若把升换成毫升，系数的数字也要随单位换算；不能只看屏幕“陡不陡”判断哪个真实单位价格更大。工作台只改变纵轴显示范围时，数据和实际每升系数都不变，线段看起来的倾斜程度会改变。

### 例 2 · 两类反例各缺什么

1. 给定 **y=3x+4**，每升三元外另有四元固定费用。
2. x=1、2 时总价七元、十元，y/x 分别为 **7 与 5 NZ$/L**，不恒定。
3. x=0 时 y=4，图线虽然直，却不经过原点，因此不是正比例。
4. 再看给定反例 **y=x×x**：x=0 时 y=0，确实过原点。
5. 但 x=1、2、4 时 y/x 分别为 **1、2、4 NZ$/L**，不恒定，图线弯曲，也不是正比例。平方价格只是用于比较数学关系的假想规则，不代表真实报价。

**已知规则与观察证据：** 本课公式明确给定，代入表格能核查它的结果；不能反过来仅凭有限几个点就证明所有输入都遵循唯一规律。几个点可能被不同公式同时经过，还需要更多条件或题目明确给定的规则。图像课继续保持前面“有限样例不是通用证明”的要求。

连续升数允许 1.5 L、2.5 L 等非整数。给定 y=2x，2.5 L 对应五元；若真实对象是完整件数，不能随意把件数之间连线解释为半件交易。这里的连续定义来自题意，不是所有图都能任意插值。

工作台切换三种规则，并列出零、一、二、四升的结果。先预测分类，再查看核对说明。改变每升系数或显示范围会清空旧反馈，正比例依据仍应来自规则、正值比值与零点。平方反例用精确曲线绘制，标记点与该规则相符。

**家庭纸笔挑战：** 设计假想饮水站：25 L 按 A:B=2:3 分区；原图 1:50000，路线三厘米再两厘米；补水价 y=3x，算四升费用，再解释加四元固定费后为何不正比例。无需出行、购物或配制饮料。

**家长陪学：** 核查比值、零点、轴单位与给定条件，不用“直”“原点”等关键词自动评分。**带走一个发现：** 正比例把同倍缩放、恒定比值和过原点的直线联系起来；每个证据都要放在明确规则与单位中解释。

## Connect shape and the origin with a constant quotient

The fictional price rules are given, with no price inquiry or purchase needed. Let x be a continuous volume from 0–8 L and y its price in NZ dollars. **y=3x** means three dollars per litre with no fixed fee. Doubling or tripling volume doubles or triples price.

This is **direct proportion**. For positive x, y/x stays constant, zero maps to zero, and the graph is a straight line through the origin. Neither being straight nor passing through the origin alone is sufficient. Do not calculate 0/0 in the zero row.

### Example 1 · A given rule produces a constant quotient

1. Use **y=3x**, with litres on x and NZ dollars on y.
2. For **1 L, 2 L and 4 L**, the prices are **NZ$3, NZ$6 and NZ$12**.
3. Their positive-input quotients are **3/1=6/2=12/4=3 NZ$/L**.
4. At **x=0**, the rule directly gives **y=0**. Inspect the correspondence without dividing zero by zero.
5. The stated formula applies throughout the domain, so it establishes a constant quotient and a straight graph through the origin. An inverse calculation under rule y=5x would recover 6 L from thirty dollars by dividing by five.

The numerical proportionality constant depends on axis units. Changing litres to millilitres requires a corresponding coefficient conversion. Apparent screen steepness alone cannot compare real unit prices. Changing only the displayed vertical range leaves all data and the actual price per litre unchanged, even though the line looks steeper or shallower.

### Example 2 · Two different counterexamples

1. Rule **y=3x+4** adds a fixed four-dollar fee to three dollars per litre.
2. At x=1 and 2, prices are seven and ten dollars, giving quotients **7 and 5 NZ$/L**, not a constant.
3. At x=0, y=4. The line is straight but misses the origin, so it is not direct proportion.
4. For another given rule **y=x×x**, zero maps to zero and the curve passes through the origin.
5. However, quotients at x=1, 2 and 4 are **1, 2 and 4 NZ$/L**, so it is not direct proportion either. This quadratic price is a mathematical counterexample, not a real quotation.

Given rules and observed evidence play different roles. Substituting into the stated formula checks its table values. Conversely, a finite set of points alone cannot prove a unique rule for every input: different formulas can share those points. A universal conclusion needs the given rule or sufficient additional conditions, continuing the earlier lesson about examples and general claims.

Continuous litres permit values such as 1.5 L and 2.5 L. Under y=2x, 2.5 L gives five dollars. If an object were an indivisible item count, connecting integer observations would not automatically justify half-item transactions. Continuity here comes from the stated setting, not from arbitrary interpolation.

Switch among the three workshop rules and inspect the zero, one, two and four-litre values. Predict the classification before checking. Changes to the coefficient or vertical display range clear feedback while preserving the need to inspect the rule, positive-input quotients and zero point. The quadratic counterexample uses an exact curve with matching marked points.
