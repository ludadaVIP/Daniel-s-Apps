# 百分比与预算

## 给每个百分比找自己的整体

百分比不是独立的金额。百分之二十五必须附着在某个参照量上，才能算出多少钱。原门票金额是折扣的基数，完整预算可以是预留金的基数，两者通常不同。题目只说门票减价，不能把交通、餐费和预留金一起打折。

本课的货币 NZD 表示新西兰元，所有价格是明确给定的虚构课堂数据，不是当前报价。金额运算按分记录。题目选数确保结果精确到分，暂不需要舍入；如果另造数据出现分以下的金额，应先声明按每张票还是整笔交易舍入，以及舍入规则，不能悄悄修改金额。

## 折扣率与付款率

减价百分之二十五表示减去原价的四分之一，还支付原价的百分之七十五。原价乘 `25%` 算的是省下的钱，乘 `75%` 才是折后票价。先写清每笔费用，再合计，比把整题所有钱相加后随意乘百分比更可靠。

### 例 1：只有票价能减价

虚构门票总价 NZ$80，减价 25%；交通 NZ$20 不打折。

1. 省下 `80×25%=NZ$20`。
2. 折后门票 `80−20=NZ$60`，也可算 `80×75%`。
3. 全部支出 `60+20=NZ$80`。

若把 `(80+20)×75%=75` 当成支出，就给了交通不允许的五元折扣。若只报二十，则把节省量当支出。答案恰好等于原门票总价八十，也不能因此省略过程；数值相同未必代表数量角色相同。

### 例 2：有余额仍可能不可行

预算 NZ$100，要求保护 NZ$10 预留金，某方案总支出 NZ$94。

可用于支出的金额 `100−10=NZ$90`。方案需要九十四，超过四元，因此不满足这个预算条件。支付后账户有六元，不是负数，却不足以保护十元预留。

用不等式检查：`支出+预留金≤预算`，即 `94+10≤100` 不成立。也可比较 `支出≤预算−预留金`。两种写法表达相同约束。预留金不是已经花掉的消费，但为了保护它，必须从可用额度里扣除；“账户余额”与“扣除预留后仍可支出的金额”要分别命名。

## 基数变化时，百分比不能抵消

NZ$80 先减价 20%，成为 NZ$64；再增加新价的 20%，得到 `64×1.2=NZ$76.80`。第二次的 100% 是六十四，不是八十，所以相同百分比的减与增不会自动抵消。先把每一步的新基数写出来，能避免只盯住两个“20%”。

预算 NZ$200，要求预留完整预算的 15%，预留三十，可用一百七十；支出一百四十六后，仍可用二十四。账户余额五十四，其中三十受保护。工作台也分别显示支出、预留和可用金额，短缺时显示正的短缺量，不把负数余额误标为“剩余”。

## 用边界检查账目

折扣零时付完整票价；折扣百分之一百时门票免费，交通仍需支付。预留零时可用金额等于预算；若支出恰好等于可用金额，费用约束成立，剩余可用金额为零。刚好成立不代表还有额外余地，也不保证其他时间或人数约束成立。

给家长解释：逐项指出每个百分比基数，说明哪些费用不能折扣，再写总支出和预留不等式。不要只说“省钱了所以能买”；需要具体差额和完整条件。

# Percentages and budgets

## Attach each percentage to its own whole

A percentage is not a money amount on its own. Twenty-five percent needs a reference amount before it can produce a saving. Original ticket cost may be the discount base, while the entire budget is the reserve base. If only tickets qualify, transport, lunches and reserve do not receive that discount.

NZD means New Zealand dollars. Every price here is supplied fictional classroom data, not a current quote. Money is tracked in cents. Chosen inputs produce exact cents without rounding. If another scenario creates fractional cents, specify whether rounding happens per ticket or on the transaction total and state the rule first.

### Example 1: discount only eligible items

Tickets total NZ$80 with 25% off. Transport costs NZ$20 and is ineligible.

1. Saving is `80 × 25% = NZ$20`.
2. Tickets after discount cost `80 − 20 = NZ$60`, also `80 × 75%`.
3. Total spending is `60 + 20 = NZ$80`.

The calculation `(80 + 20) × 75% = 75` gives transport an unauthorised five-dollar discount. Reporting twenty instead mistakes saving for spending. The final total happens to equal the original ticket amount; identical numbers do not make their roles identical. Keep an itemised explanation.

## A reserve protects money without spending it

A reserve is money deliberately left available for another purpose. It is not an already purchased item. Protecting it nevertheless reduces what the present plan may spend. Distinguish the account balance after payment from the amount still spendable after reserve protection.

### Example 2: a positive balance can still fail

Budget is NZ$100, protected reserve NZ$10 and planned spending NZ$94. Available spending money is `100 − 10 = NZ$90`. The plan needs four more dollars than that limit.

After payment, six dollars would remain in the account. That balance is positive, but cannot protect ten dollars. The check `spending + reserve ≤ budget` fails because `94 + 10 > 100`. Equivalently, compare spending with `budget − reserve`. Both inequalities describe the same requirement.

## A changed base prevents cancellation

An NZ$80 price falls 20% to NZ$64. Increasing the new price by 20% gives `64 × 1.2 = NZ$76.80`. The second one-hundred-percent amount is sixty-four, not eighty, so equal percentage decreases and increases do not cancel automatically.

For a budget of NZ$200 with 15% reserved, reserve is thirty and available money is 170. Spending 146 leaves twenty-four still spendable. The account balance is fifty-four, including thirty protected dollars. The workshop reports these quantities separately and names a positive shortfall when a plan exceeds the protected limit.

## Use boundaries to check the ledger

At zero discount, pay the full ticket price. At one hundred percent discount, tickets are free but ineligible transport still costs money. With no reserve, available money equals the budget. Spending exactly the available amount satisfies the money constraint with zero headroom; it does not guarantee that a time constraint also passes.

Explain the base of every percentage, identify ineligible items and state the total-spending inequality. “It is cheaper” is insufficient evidence of affordability. A complete argument includes the exact remaining amount or shortfall and all conditions.
