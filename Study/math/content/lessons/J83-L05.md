# 实际情境建模

## 先定义两个量，再写两种关系

方程组建模从数量和单位开始。明确 x、y 分别表示什么，再找两条同时成立的关系，例如总张数与总金额。每条等式两边要量纲一致：票价乘张数才是金额，不能把票的总张数与总价直接互换。

还要先记录取值范围。票数通常是非负整数，连续液体体积可以是非负实数。解方程得到的是代数数对，是否在实际范围允许，要另做核验。不能为了让现实“看起来可行”，把分数解随意四舍五入。

### 例 1：保留总量与加权金额

虚构社区活动，成人票 x 张每张 NZ$5，儿童票 y 张每张 NZ$3，共七张、总价 NZ$29。张数关系为 `x+y=7`，金额关系为 `5x+3y=29`，并注明 x、y 为非负整数。

可将总量关系乘三后从金额式减去，得到二 x 等八，所以 x=4、y=3。回查总张数七、金额二十加九为二十九，且张数满足范围。写模型时不能只交 `x=4;y=3`：那是最后的数值，未展示两条原数量关系如何建立。允许逐式等价改写，但不能把两式全部换成消元后的最后结果。

### 例 2：同一代数数对，不同情境结论

若仍七张票，总价改成 NZ$30，方程为 x+y=7、5x+3y=30。代数解为 `x=9/2、y=5/2`，代回总数七、金额三十都成立。然而四点五张成人票与两点五张儿童票不满足整数张数，所以该情境没有允许解；方程组本身仍有唯一代数解。

若同样的方程描述两种虚构果汁：x 升每升五元、y 升每升三元，共七升、总价三十，则 (9/2,5/2) 为允许的非负连续量。单位由张变升，数域条件改变，代数步骤没有改变。把票数取整为五与二，会使总价变成三十一，不能修复原问题。

## 求出数对以后还要回到原题

核验分三步：同一个数对代入两条原关系；检查每个量的单位和解释；检查非负、整数或其他已给范围。只有三步都通过，才能作为情境方案。若数据不支持整数方案，可以说明矛盾来源并重新检查假设或记录，不凭空修改条件。

应用图像时，也要区别连续直线与允许的离散票数点。无限实数直线帮助解代数关系，但真实票数仅允许整数格点。图窗与标尺影响显示，不改变价格、总量或原关系。这里所有活动和价格都是教学虚构，不是实际购票或商业建议。

工作台提供二十九元票数、三十元票数和三十元升数三种情境。先写模型，再主动展开原关系，最后代数与情境分开核验。家长问“这条等式两边单位一样吗？”“该数对是满足方程，还是也满足现实条件？”解释清楚两层结论，比只报两个数更有意义。

# Modelling a real-world system

## Define both quantities before writing relationships

State what x and y mean and their units. Then identify two simultaneous relationships, such as total count and total cost. Each equation needs matching dimensions: price per ticket times count gives money. Count and cost cannot simply exchange places.

Record the domain first. Ticket counts are nonnegative integers, while continuous liquid volumes may be nonnegative real amounts. Solving produces an algebraic pair; context admissibility requires another audit. Arbitrary rounding does not repair a fractional count solution.

### Example 1: preserve total and weighted cost

At a fictional community event, x adult tickets cost NZ$5 each and y child tickets NZ$3 each. Seven tickets total NZ$29. Write x+y=7 and 5x+3y=29, with nonnegative integer counts.

Subtract three times the count equation from the cost equation to obtain 2x=8. Thus x=4,y=3. Both original totals and the count domain pass. Submitting only x=4;y=3 does not show the two original modelling relationships. Equivalent rewrites of each are allowed, but replacing both with final solved values erases their quantity origins.

### Example 2: identical algebra, different domains

Change the cost to NZ$30 while keeping seven tickets. The equations give x=9/2,y=5/2, satisfying both sum seven and weighted cost thirty. Fractional tickets fail the integer domain: there is no allowed contextual solution, despite a unique algebraic solution.

If the same equations describe two fictional juices priced at five and three dollars per litre, the pair is allowed as nonnegative continuous litres. The algebra is unchanged but the quantities and domains differ. Rounding tickets to five and two would change cost to thirty-one, failing the source data.

## Return to the original problem after solving

First substitute the same pair into both original relationships. Then check units and the meaning of each coordinate. Finally check nonnegative, integer or other stated restrictions. All three are required for a contextual plan. If integer counts are impossible, explain and recheck assumptions or records rather than silently changing them.

A continuous line can help solve the algebra while actual ticket counts occupy only allowed integer grid points. A window or scale changes display rather than prices, totals or relationships. All prices and activities here are fictional teaching examples, not requests to purchase anything.

The workshop contrasts twenty-nine-dollar tickets, thirty-dollar tickets and thirty-dollar litres. Build the model first, explicitly reveal the originals when needed, then audit equations and context separately. A parent asks whether units match and whether the pair satisfies only algebra or also the real restrictions, seeking the two-layer explanation beyond the numerical result.
