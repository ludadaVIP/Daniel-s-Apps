# 面积累积的思想

在水箱旁记录“每分钟几升”，得到的是流入率；问水箱里增加了几升，则要把率沿时间累加。图中的面积首先是一种乘法记录：横轴分钟乘纵轴升/分钟，结果为升。这里讨论没有流出的理想模型；初始体积必须另外给出。

## 把时间切成有意义的段

设q(t)在0≤t<1为2、1≤t<3为4、3≤t≤4为1升/分钟。每段恒定，所以增量恰好是率乘实际经历时长，不是近似。A(t)表示从0到t新增的升数，A(0)=0。若初始V₀升，则实际体积V(t)=V₀+A(t)，初始量不属于这一段流入。

本课用右侧值定义跳点，如q(1)=4；刚到1分钟前的率仍趋近2。只改变有限个时刻的率值，不改变积分，因为单点没有时间宽度。真实开关过程可能有短暂过渡，那是另一个模型，不能用“单点不影响”忽略有正时长的过渡。

### 例1：部分时段的精确累积

到t=5/2分钟，第一段完整经历1分钟，第二段只经历3/2分钟，第三段尚未开始。A(5/2)=2×1+4×3/2=8升。不能把第二段完整的2分钟都计入，也不能把当前率4直接当累计量。

若最初有5升，V(5/2)=5+8=13升。此刻q(5/2)=4升/分钟，A(5/2)=8升，V(5/2)=13升：同一个时刻可以有三个不同数，因为所指数量不同。

可以用表格独立核查：已历宽度依次1、3/2、0，乘对应率得2、6、0。图的矩形也只着色已历宽度。换到累积图时，A从零连续上升，各段斜率依次2、4、1；流入率跳变没有使体积瞬间跳跃。

### 例2：平均率需要时间权重

到4分钟共新增A(4)=2×1+4×2+1×1=11升。全程平均流入率是11/4升/分钟，不是(2+4+1)/3=7/3，因为率4维持的时间是另两段各自的两倍。

从1到3分钟的增量既可算4×2=8，也可算A(3)−A(1)=10−2=8。初始体积在两端相减时抵消。对于t=0，累积量0、当前率2都明确，但从0到0的平均率公式是0/0，未定义；不能为了填表写成0或2。

## 图形、解释与自检

工作台可比较分段率、恒定率和含停流段的模型。停流时率为零，累积图保持水平，已有水不会消失。改变初始量只平移实际体积图，不改变A或平均率；改变视图不应清除预测。

先预测新增量、实际量、平均率，再打开逐段核验。说清每段到底经历多久，比背“面积等于积分”更有用。若出现流出，应建立有符号净率；只把本课流入模型硬套过去可能把总流入误当末体积。

家长可追问：“你的每个乘积单位是什么？”以及“5升初始水在哪一步加入？”出口解释还应区分点值和时间区间：单点定义不改变面积，但一整段率改变会改变累积。图示和精确分段运算互相核查，有限演示本身不建立一般积分理论。

# Accumulation and area

A record of litres per minute describes inflow rate. To find litres added, accumulate that rate across time. The area represents a product of quantities: minutes times litres per minute gives litres. Assume no outflow; initial volume is separate information.

## Split time where the rate changes

Let q(t) be two on 0≤t<1, four on 1≤t<3 and one on 3≤t≤4 L/min. Each constant-rate contribution is exactly rate times elapsed duration. Define A(t) as added litres since zero, so A(0)=0. With initial volume V₀, actual volume is V(t)=V₀+A(t). Initial water is not inflow during this interval.

Use the right-sided value at a jump, such as q(1)=4. The left-hand rate tends to two. Changing finitely many point values changes no integral because isolated instants have no time width. A physical transition lasting a positive duration is different and must be included in the model.

### Example 1: Accumulate a partial segment

At t=5/2 minutes, the first segment has lasted one minute, the second only3/2 minutes and the third has not begun. Thus A(5/2)=2×1+4×3/2=8 L. Including all two minutes of the second segment would count future inflow. Taking the current rate four as the accumulated volume would confuse different units.

With five litres initially, V(5/2)=5+8=13 L. At the same instant, q is4 L/min, A is8 L and V is13 L. Different numerical values describe different quantities.

An independent table uses elapsed widths1,3/2,0 and products2,6,0. The shaded rectangles include only these elapsed widths. On the accumulation plot, A is continuous and has successive slopes2,4,1. A jump in rate does not produce an instantaneous jump in volume.

### Example 2: Average rate needs duration weights

By four minutes, added volume is2×1+4×2+1×1=11 L. Average inflow is11/4 L/min. The unweighted mean(2+4+1)/3=7/3 is wrong because the rate four lasts twice as long as either other rate.

From minute one through three the increment is4×2=8 L. Equivalently, A(3)−A(1)=10−2=8. Initial water cancels in a difference between actual volumes. At zero elapsed duration, A(0)=0 and q(0)=2 are defined, but the interval-average formula gives0/0 and is undefined. Neither zero nor two should be inserted as that average.

## Use the representations to check reasoning

Compare the piecewise model with a constant rate and a model containing a pause. During a zero-rate segment, accumulated volume stays level; existing water does not vanish. Changing initial volume translates the actual-volume curve while preserving added volume and average inflow. Switching views preserves the mathematical task and predictions.

Predict the three quantities before opening the audit. Explain actual elapsed widths and units in each product. If water can leave, define a signed net rate; total inflow alone cannot determine final volume without the outflow information. Parent prompts should ask which terms represent new water and which term represents the initial state. The picture checks the finite piecewise calculation; it is not a proof of general integration theory.
