# 分解复杂问题

复杂问题往往不是算式长，而是量之间有依赖。先把“组织一次活动”改写成几个可以核验的小问题，再决定顺序。本课只练一个主要目标：把目标拆成任务，保留任务之间的输入与输出。

## 先说清规则，再拆任务

虚构纸模工作坊使用筹码计价。每人用3张卡片，正活动另备5张，库存4张；新材料每包8张，只能整包买。方案A收固定7筹码，再收每包6筹码。预算50筹码，房间容量20人。人数n是非负整数；题设明确n=0取消，需求和费用都为0。这里的取消规则是一条约定，并非所有实际活动都如此。

正人数时依次定义总需求D=3n+5，采购缺口R=max(0,D−S)，最少包数k=⌈R/8⌉，费用C=7+6k。S为库存张数。符号⌈u⌉表示不小于u的最小整数。不要把“需求”和“缺口”混成同一个数：库存改变缺口，却不改变参与者及备用所需总量。

依赖链是需求→缺口→包数→费用。容量检查n≤20不依赖采购，可以先做。最终可行要求费用≤预算且人数≤容量。依赖图允许多种有效顺序，并不要求把可独立检查的容量固定放在某一个位置。

### 例1：18人需要多少包

需求D=3×18+5=59张。已有4张，净缺口R=55张。55/8=6.875说明六包多，不能把6.875包当成可购买数量。

六包只有48张，连库存共52张，少于需求59；七包有56张，连库存共60张，足够。故k=7且是最少。费用7+6×7=49筹码，在50内；18≤20，也通过容量。供给60−需求59=1张剩余。备用5张已经包含在59内，不能再把备用当剩余加回。

每一步都有不同单位：人、张、包、筹码。工作台每个材料格严格代表1张，购买来源每8格组成一包；参与者、备用与剩余分色。图可核数量，取整不等式解释为何最少。

### 例2：一个可行方案是否已经最大

预算50下，7+6k≤50，所以最多7包。库存4加56张新料，共60张。正活动3n+5≤60，得到n≤55/3；n为整数，所以n≤18。容量20没有给出更紧限制。

再核相邻边界：18人费用49；19人需求62、缺58张，需8包，费用55超预算。需求与最少费用随正人数不减，所以更大人数也不会恢复可行。这样才证明最大18，而不只是找到一个可行值。若把容量改10，则还须取两个上界较小者。

## 检查拆分有没有漏项

若库存改60张，18人的需求仍59，缺口0、采购0包，但活动举行仍收固定7筹码。库存够用不等于取消。n=0才按单独分支需求0、包数0、费用0；不能机械代入3n+5或7+6k收取费用。

探索时先尝试提前点“合并决策”，观察缺少哪些前置任务，再构造一条有效任务顺序。改变库存后，预测需求为何可以保持，而包数可能改变。出口题需要画依赖并解释这些边界；家长核验完整推理，数值正确不自动代表会分解问题。

# Breaking down complex problems

A difficult problem often contains dependencies rather than one long calculation. Turn “organise a workshop” into smaller questions with checkable inputs and outputs. The central goal is to preserve those dependencies when choosing an order.

## State the rules before splitting the work

A fictional paper-model workshop uses toy credits. Each participant needs three cards and a positive-attendance event needs five additional spares. Four cards are in stock. New cards come only in whole packets of eight. Plan A charges seven credits plus six per packet. Budget is fifty and room capacity is twenty people. Attendance n is a nonnegative integer. The stated cancellation rule makes both demand and cost zero at n=0; this is an explicit modelling agreement, not a claim about every real event.

For positive attendance define demand D=3n+5, shortage R=max(0,D−S), minimum packet count k=⌈R/8⌉ and cost C=7+6k. S is stock in cards. The ceiling symbol means the least integer greater than or equal to its argument. Demand and purchase shortage are different: stock changes the shortage, not the cards required by participants and spares.

Demand feeds shortage, shortage feeds packet count, and packets feed cost. Capacity n≤20 is independent of this chain and can be checked first. The final decision requires both cost within budget and attendance within capacity. Several task orders are valid; the dependency diagram does not force the independent capacity check into one position.

### Example 1: Supply eighteen people

Demand is 3×18+5=59 cards. Subtract four in stock to get shortage 55. Dividing by eight gives 6.875, which is not a purchasable packet count.

Six packets provide 48 new cards, or 52 with stock, below demand 59. Seven provide 56 new cards, or sixty in total, which suffices. Thus seven is both sufficient and minimal. Cost is 7+6×7=49 credits, within fifty, and eighteen fits capacity twenty. Supply sixty minus demand 59 leaves one surplus card. The five spares are already included in demand; do not add them back as surplus.

Keep units attached to the chain: people, cards, packets, credits. Every workbench material tile is exactly one card. Purchased cards are grouped by packet number, eight per packet, and colours distinguish participant allocation, spares and surplus. Counting audits this example; the least-integer inequality explains minimality.

### Example 2: Establish a maximum, not just a feasible value

Budget requires 7+6k≤50, allowing at most seven packets. Four stocked cards plus 56 new cards supply sixty. For a positive event, 3n+5≤60 gives n≤55/3. Whole attendance therefore satisfies n≤18. Capacity twenty does not tighten this bound.

Check adjacent inputs: eighteen cost 49; nineteen need 62 cards, shortage 58, eight packets and cost 55. Demand and minimum cost are nondecreasing for positive attendance, so still larger attendances cannot become affordable again. This proves maximum eighteen. If capacity were ten, the smaller of the material/budget bound and capacity would control.

## Look for missing branches

With stock sixty, eighteen still demand 59 cards, but shortage and purchases are zero. A positive event still pays seven credits. Stock coverage is not cancellation. Only the n=0 branch sets demand, packets and cost to zero; applying the positive-event formulas there would incorrectly retain spares or a fee.

In Explore, try the final decision too early and identify missing prerequisites. Then find a valid sequence of tasks. Change stock and explain why a demand prediction can remain valid while packet count changes. For the exit, draw dependencies and explain these boundaries. A parent reviews the reasoning; a correct number alone does not show that a learner can break down the problem.
