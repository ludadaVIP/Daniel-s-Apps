# 相关系数

相关系数把配对数据的线性方向和程度压缩成一个无量纲数。压缩方便比较，却会丢掉形状、异常点和研究设计。先看原始散点，再算r，最后回到问题解释。

## 配对、中心化与范围

一行(x,y)必须属于同一个对象，ID随整行保留。记Sxx=Σ(x−x̄)²、Syy=Σ(y−ȳ)²、Sxy=Σ(x−x̄)(y−ȳ)，Pearson相关系数r=Sxy/√(SxxSyy)。两列都必须有变异；任一平方和为0，分母为0，r未定义，不把它补成0。

当r有定义时−1≤r≤1，正负表示线性方向，绝对值表示线性贴近程度。|r|=1意味着这些点落在同一条非水平、非竖直直线上，并不保证总体也如此。r²去掉方向，不是另一个有符号相关系数。比较“强弱”需结合样本大小和完整图形，不给所有情境硬套统一阈值。

### 例1：手算一个不完美的相关

三对数据(1,1)、(2,3)、(3,2)，两列均值都是2。中心差分别为x：−1,0,1，y：−1,1,0。平方和均为2，交积和为1，因此r=1/√4=1/2，r²=1/4。

最小二乘斜率是Sxy/Sxx=1/2，这次恰巧与r同数，但角色不同。把x变为2x，Sxx变8、Sxy变2，r仍为1/2，斜率却变1/4。正向单位转换或加常数不改r；若把y变为6−y，交积反号，r变−1/2。数值相同不能让斜率与相关成为同一个概念。

### 例2：零、未定义与被压缩的形状

配对(1,4)、(2,1)、(3,0)、(4,1)、(5,4)有均值(3,2)。两侧交积抵消，Sxy=0，Sxx与Syy都正，所以r=0；散点却呈明确U形。零线性相关不代表没有关系，也不推出独立。

相反，(1,2)、(2,2)、(3,2)的y恒为2，Syy=0，r未定义。含截距OLS仍能给唯一水平线y=2，因为x有变异；“相关未定义”与“斜率不能识别”并不相同。若x都为2，才无法从这些输入唯一识别斜率。

## 保留身份与研究条件

分别排序x、y再重配会造出原本没有的对象组合。可以按整行重排显示，不可拆配以追求更大的r。有影响的第五点也不能为了让r漂亮而删掉；工作台的临时排除只是敏感性对比，真正更正或排除需来源依据。

工作台用精确有理数核交积和与r²；开平方只有需要时才显示精确根式。显示坐标自动容纳正向单位转换后的点，不改记录本身。先预测r再预测r²，转换只在答案真的改变时清相应预测。

出口应给中心化计算，并逐一解释正向单位、反射、常数列与U形。家长可追问“这些配对来自谁”“为什么没有因果结论”。即使样本r=1，也可能有混杂、选择偏差或范围限制；相关本身不提供因果、总体推广和范围外预测的许可证。

# Correlation coefficient

Correlation compresses the direction and strength of a linear pattern into a dimensionless number. That helps comparisons but discards shape, influential observations and study design. Inspect the original scatterplot, calculate r, then interpret it in context.

## Preserve pairs and centre both columns

Each (x,y) row must belong to the same unit, with its identity retained. Define Sxx=Σ(x−x̄)², Syy=Σ(y−ȳ)² and Sxy=Σ(x−x̄)(y−ȳ). Pearson correlation is r=Sxy/√(SxxSyy). Both columns must vary. A zero square sum makes the denominator zero and r undefined, rather than zero.

When defined, r lies between −1 and one. Its sign describes linear direction; its magnitude describes linear closeness. A magnitude of one places these observations on a common nonhorizontal, nonvertical line, without making the same claim about a population. Squaring loses direction. Interpret strength alongside sample size and the full graph, rather than imposing one universal cutoff on every setting.

### Example 1: Calculate an imperfect correlation

For pairs (1,1), (2,3), (3,2), both means are two. Centred x values are −1,0,1 and centred y values −1,1,0. Both square sums equal two and the cross-product sum equals one. Thus r=1/√4=1/2 and r²=1/4.

The least-squares slope Sxy/Sxx also equals one half here, but coincidence does not make it correlation. Replacing x by 2x changes Sxx to eight and Sxy to two. Correlation remains one half while slope becomes one quarter. Positive unit changes and shifts preserve r. Reflecting y to 6−y reverses the cross-product sign, giving r=−1/2. Keep the roles of a dimensionless coefficient and a units-dependent slope separate.

### Example 2: Zero differs from undefined

The pairs (1,4), (2,1), (3,0), (4,1), (5,4) have means (3,2). Opposite cross-products cancel, so Sxy=0 while both square sums remain positive. Correlation is zero, yet the points follow a clear U shape. Zero linear correlation does not establish absence of association or independence.

In contrast, pairs (1,2), (2,2), (3,2) have constant y and Syy=0, making correlation undefined. Intercept OLS can still identify the unique horizontal line y=2 because x varies. Undefined correlation and unidentified slope are different issues. Constant x, rather than constant y alone, prevents unique slope identification.

## Identities and design remain part of the evidence

Separately sorting x and y creates pairings that were never observed. Reorder complete rows if useful, but do not break pair identities to increase r. Similarly, temporarily excluding an influential fifth point is a sensitivity comparison, not evidence that the record is erroneous. Actual correction or exclusion needs a source-based reason.

The workbench audits centred sums and r² using rational arithmetic and displays an exact radical when needed. Its numerical x scale accommodates unit changes while keeping source values intact. Predict r and then r²; transformations preserve predictions whose mathematical answers remain unchanged.

For the exit, show the centred calculation and explain positive unit changes, reflection, constant columns and the U shape. A parent can ask whose observations were paired and why no causal conclusion follows. Even sample correlation one can coexist with confounding, selection bias or limited scope. Correlation alone authorises neither causation, population generalisation nor extrapolation.
