# 标准差的意义

同样均值的数据，可能挤在一起，也可能分得很开。本课用**离均差的平方贡献**解释标准差，而不是把它记成计算器上的一个键。

## 一条核心想法

先求均值，再对每项计算“原值减均值”。带符号离均差相加总是0，不能用它量波动；要先逐项平方，使左右两侧都贡献非负量。把列出的n个值当完整有限总体时：方差σ²=Σ(x−μ)²/n，标准差σ为方差的非负平方根。

方差若用cm²，开根后的标准差用cm，与原测量同单位。它概括离均值的分散程度，并非平均绝对距离，也不是极差。一个离差从2变成10，平方贡献从4变为100，因此尾部会有很大影响。

### 例1：从有方向的线段到平方

完整总体2、4、4、6 cm，总和16，均值4 cm。离均差为−2、0、0、2 cm，带符号相加0；但第一个和最后一个确实离均值2 cm。

平方贡献为4、0、0、4 cm²，平方和8 cm²；总体方差8/4=2 cm²，标准差√2 cm。工作台用边长|离均差|的正方形表示贡献，负离差不画负面积。√2约为1.414只作观察，正式精确题可填sqrt(2)或等值根式，不能用有限近似冒充确切值。

若所有值同加10，新值12、14、14、16，均值14，每个离差仍为−2、0、0、2，因此标准差仍√2。若改为同乘−2，离差乘−2，平方贡献乘4，标准差乘绝对比例2，成为2√2，不是−2√2。平移与缩放有不同作用。

### 例2：同一数据，不同的分母约定

若2、4、4、6只是样本，用样本方差约定s²=Σ(x−x̄)²/(n−1)，分母为3，得到s²=8/3、s=√(8/3)。它与完整总体的√2是不同问题的答案；不能写样本标题却仍除4。

对独立同分布、有限方差的随机样本，n−1使样本方差成为总体方差的无偏估计；这不意味着其平方根s也无偏，更不能修复偏差抽样。本课只要求识别已声明的约定，不把分母选择当作有代表性的证明。

单个值4若视为完整单元素总体，均值4、总体方差与标准差都是0；若要用样本n−1约定估计，分母0，样本方差和标准差无定义。两种叙述不可合并成“一个值的标准差总是0”。四个4在两种约定下都得0，因为分母均正、每个离差均0。

## 平方贡献工作台

先预测均值、平方和和标准差，再展开逐项核验。切换n/n−1不改变均值、离差或平方和，只改变方差的分母。共同平移保留平方和与标准差答案；共同缩放按绝对因子改变标准差，按平方因子改变方差。

把末项6改为14时，均值也要从4更新为6；离差变为−4、−2、−2、8，平方和88，总体方差22，标准差√22。不能只改最后一个平方却仍围绕旧均值4计算。这个点若真实有效，应保留并讨论敏感性，而不是为减小标准差把它删掉。

## 家长核对与迁移

出口不只问四个4的标准差为0，还要解释反向结论：非空有限总体标准差为0，说明非负平方之和为0，因此每项都等于均值。下一课用标准差作共同单位；σ=0时不能作除数，本课的零波动边界会再次出现。

# Meaning of standard deviation

Datasets with equal means may be tightly packed or widely dispersed. This lesson explains standard deviation using **squared contributions from deviations**, rather than treating it as a calculator button.

## One central idea

Find the mean, then subtract that same mean from every observation. Signed deviations sum to zero, so that sum cannot measure spread. Squaring each deviation first gives nonnegative contributions from both sides. When the n listed values are the entire finite population, variance is σ²=Σ(x−μ)²/n, and SD is its nonnegative square root.

For measurements in cm, variance has units cm² and SD returns to cm. SD summarizes spread about the mean; it is neither mean absolute distance nor range. A deviation increasing from two to ten increases its squared contribution from four to one hundred, explaining sensitivity to tails.

### Example 1: Directed deviations become square areas

The finite population 2,4,4,6 cm totals sixteen and has mean four. Its deviations are −2,0,0,2 cm. Their signed sum is zero, but the first and last measurements remain two centimetres from the mean.

Squared contributions are 4,0,0,4 cm², totaling eight. Population variance is 8/4=2 cm², and SD is √2 cm. The workbench uses squares with side |deviation| to show these contributions. Negative deviations do not produce negative areas. The decimal 1.414 is only an approximation; an exact answer may use sqrt(2) or an equivalent radical expression.

Adding ten to every observation produces 12,14,14,16 with mean fourteen. Deviations remain −2,0,0,2, so SD remains √2. Multiplying every observation by −2 instead multiplies deviations by −2, squares by four and SD by the absolute factor two. The new SD is 2√2, never negative. Translation and scaling affect spread differently.

### Example 2: The same values under different divisor conventions

If 2,4,4,6 are a sample rather than the whole population, the conventional sample variance is s²=Σ(x−x̄)²/(n−1). Divisor three gives variance 8/3 and SD √(8/3). This differs from the finite-population answer √2 because the question and convention differ. A sample heading cannot silently use divisor four.

For independent identically distributed random observations with finite variance, divisor n−1 makes sample variance an unbiased estimator of population variance. It does not make its square root unbiased and does not repair biased sampling. Here the main task is to follow the stated convention, not to treat a divisor as proof of representativeness.

A single observation of four, treated as an entire singleton population, has population SD zero. Under the sample n−1 convention, however, the divisor is zero and both sample variance and SD are undefined. Do not merge these statements into a universal claim that one observation always has SD zero. Four fours give zero under either convention because all deviations vanish and both divisors are positive.

## Squared-contribution workbench

Predict the mean, squared deviation sum and SD before revealing the audit. Changing n to n−1 does not change the mean, deviations or squared sum, only the variance divisor. A common translation preserves squared-sum and SD answers. Scaling changes SD by the absolute factor and variance by its square.

Replacing the final six with fourteen also changes the mean from four to six. Deviations become −4,−2,−2,8, squared sum 88, population variance 22 and SD √22. Updating only the last square around the old mean would be wrong. Retain a genuine valid extreme observation and discuss sensitivity rather than deleting it to reduce spread.

## Parent review and transfer

The exit asks for both directions of the zero-spread argument. Four fours clearly give SD zero. Conversely, in a nonempty finite population, zero variance is a sum of nonnegative squares equal to zero, forcing every observation to equal the mean. The next lesson divides by SD to create a common unit; zero spread will make that standardization undefined.
