## 分子数小份，分母说明每个整体有几份

本节讨论分母为正整数的**正分数**。分子数取了几个相同小份；分母说明一个整体由多少个这样的小份组成。比较分子与分母，就是把所取份数与凑成一个整体所需的份数比较。

- 分子小于分母：不足一个整体，是 **真分数**。
- 分子大于或等于分母：至少一个整体，是 **假分数**。

“假”是分类名称，不是说数量不存在或算错了。零表示没有取任何份额，工作台会单独说明零的情形；它不用于本节正分数的分类判断。

### 例 1 · 七个四分之一

1. 一个整体含 4 个四分之一。
2. 七份可以分成 4 份和剩下的 3 份。
3. 一整条涂满，另一条涂三个四分之一，合计 **7/4**。
4. 因为 7>4，数量大于 1，是假分数。
5. 第二条仍以相同大小整体为基准；不能把分母改成两条的总格数 8。

### 例 2 · 等于一个整体的边界

1. 五个五分之一合起来，正好是 **5/5=1**。
2. 分子与分母相等，所以没有剩下小份，也没有不足。
3. 假分数的规则包含“或等于”，5/5 属于这个边界。
4. 不能把“至少一个整体”误说成“一定严格大于一个整体”。

### 两种问题要分别回答

7/3 含两个完整整体和一个三分之一，因为 7=3+3+1。如果问“完整整体有几个”，答案是 **2**。如果问“至少画几条完整轮廓才装得下全部数量”，答案是 **3**：最后一条只涂了一部分。

因此，9/4 也需要三个整体轮廓，却只包含两个完整整体。读清楚题目是在数涂满的整体，还是数需要的轮廓，不要靠猜整数答案。

分母始终表示每个同样大小整体的等份数，分子可以继续增加，跨过一个、两个或更多整体。八个三分之一升的总量是 8/3 升：一升作为统一计量整体，每瓶只贡献其中一个三分之一。

工作台允许改变小份总数和每整体等份数，并显示多个同样大小的条形。改变分母而不改分子会改变每份大小与总量；观察边界“分子等于分母”，解释图为何恰好涂满一条。

**小检查：** 7/5 的分母为何还是 5，而不是表示两条轮廓的 10？

## The numerator counts parts; the denominator counts parts per whole

This lesson considers **positive fractions** with positive whole-number denominators. The numerator counts selected equal parts; the denominator tells how many such parts make one whole. Comparing them compares the selected count with the count needed for a whole.

- Numerator below denominator: less than a whole, a **proper fraction**.
- Numerator at least denominator: at least a whole, an **improper fraction**.

“Improper” is a classification, not a claim that the quantity is unreal or wrong. Zero means no parts are taken; the workshop labels it separately rather than use it in this lesson's positive-fraction classifications.

### Example 1 · Seven quarters

1. Four quarters make one whole.
2. Seven parts split into four parts and three remaining parts.
3. Shade one complete strip and three quarters of another to show **7/4**.
4. Seven exceeds four, so the amount exceeds one and is improper.
5. The second strip uses the same-sized whole. Do not change the denominator to eight by counting all cells in both outlines.

### Example 2 · The equality boundary

1. Five fifths make exactly **5/5=1**.
2. Matching numerator and denominator leave neither a shortfall nor extra parts.
3. The improper-fraction rule includes equality, so 5/5 is on this boundary.
4. “At least one whole” must not become “strictly greater than one whole”.

### Distinguish two different questions

7/3 contains two complete wholes and one third, because 7=3+3+1. Asked for the number of **complete wholes**, answer two. Asked for the minimum number of **whole outlines to hold all the shading**, answer three: the last outline is only partly shaded.

Likewise, 9/4 needs three whole outlines but contains only two complete wholes. Identify whether the question counts filled wholes or necessary outlines rather than guess an integer.

The denominator keeps counting equal parts per whole of the same size. The numerator can keep growing across one, two or more wholes. Eight one-third-litre amounts total 8/3 litres: one litre is the shared measurement whole, and each bottle contributes one third of it.

The workshop changes selected part count and parts per whole, showing several equal-sized outlines. Changing only the denominator changes part size and total value. Observe the equality boundary and explain why one strip is exactly full.

**Quick check:** why does 7/5 retain denominator five rather than ten, even though it needs two outlines?
