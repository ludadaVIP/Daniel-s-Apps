# 有理数减法

## 减法号与数的负号角色不同

`−3/4−(−1/2)` 中，第一个负号属于被减数，居中的减号指定运算，括号里的负号属于减数。它们看起来相似，却不能一次性删掉。减法可用加法的逆关系解释：从结果加回原减数，应还原被减数。

因此 `a−b=a+(−b)`。保留第一个 a 不变，把减法改为加法，把第二个数换成相反数。如果 b 已经负，取相反数后变正；如果 b 为正，取相反数后变负；零的相反数仍零。这条关系对整数、分数和有限小数统一适用。

### 例 1：撤销负变化

计算 `−3/4−(−1/2)`。

1. 被减数 `−3/4` 保留。
2. 原减数 `−1/2` 的相反数是 `1/2`。
3. 改写为 `−3/4+1/2=−3/4+2/4=−1/4`。
4. 加回原减数核验：`−1/4+(−1/2)=−3/4`。

数轴中撤销一个向左半单位的变化，相当于从起点向右半单位。终点比原数大，但仍为负。这已经反驳了“减法一定变小”以及“减负数后结果一定正”的说法。

对比 `−3/4+(−1/2)=−5/4`，这里增加一个负变化，应继续向左，不是同一运算。只看两个负数而不看中间运算符，会把本来方向相反的过程混为一谈。

### 例 2：同一改写，结果可跨零

计算 `−2/3−(−5/6)`。改为 `−2/3+5/6`，通分为 `−4/6+5/6=1/6`。此次向右五个六分之一，足以越过零。

反查 `1/6+(−5/6)=−4/6=−2/3`。这个加回法使用原来的减数 `−5/6`，不能误加它的相反数。再看 `−0.4−0.6`，原减数是正 0.6，应加负 0.6，结果负一；不是看到减号便向右走。

## 保留顺序与精确数量

`1/3−5/6=−1/2`，但反过来 `5/6−1/3=1/2`，说明减法通常不交换。把减法统一写成带符号的加法后，可以按加法性质处理这些完整加数；不能先删符号再随意挪动。

减零保持原位置，所以 `−7/4−0=−7/4`，不能因为看见一个减号就再取相反数。`−1/2−(−5/4)=3/4`，单位仍是四分之一，移了五格而不是把原单位放大。计算结果若是分数，保留精确形式；数轴只帮助说明位置，不能靠肉眼接近来判断精确相等。

## 自己检查与家长提问

工作台先要求写减数的相反数，再写结果。改变任一分子或分母后，旧预测会清空，避免把前一个正确状态当新题证据。展开后同时显示原式、改写后的加法和有向线段，核对三种表示是否一致。

给家长说明时，圈出被减数、减法运算符和带符号的减数；说清哪一项保持不变、哪一项取相反数。最后把结果加回原减数，展示还原原量的等式。一个能同时解释减正、减负、减零与跨零的关系，比“负负得正”这种没有说明符号角色的短句更可靠。

# Subtracting rational numbers

## A subtraction sign and a number’s sign have different roles

In `−3/4−(−1/2)`, the first minus belongs to the minuend, the middle minus specifies subtraction, and the bracketed minus belongs to the subtrahend. Similar symbols do not have interchangeable roles. Subtraction reverses addition: adding the original subtrahend to the result should recover the minuend.

Thus `a−b=a+(−b)`. Keep a unchanged, switch the operation to addition and replace only b by its opposite. A negative b becomes positive; a positive b becomes negative; zero remains zero. This relationship handles integers, fractions and terminating decimals together.

### Example 1: undoing a negative change

Calculate `−3/4−(−1/2)`.

1. Keep the minuend `−3/4`.
2. The opposite of the original subtrahend `−1/2` is `1/2`.
3. Rewrite `−3/4+1/2=−3/4+2/4=−1/4`.
4. Add back the original subtrahend: `−1/4+(−1/2)=−3/4`.

Undoing a leftward half-unit change corresponds to moving right by a half-unit. The result is larger than the starting value but still negative. Subtraction need not decrease a number, and subtracting a negative need not produce a positive result.

Compare `−3/4+(−1/2)=−5/4`. This adds another negative change, moving left. The two processes have opposite directions despite sharing the same signed numbers. Read the operation between them.

### Example 2: a rightward change can cross zero

Rewrite `−2/3−(−5/6)` as `−2/3+5/6`. With sixths, `−4/6+5/6=1/6`. Moving right by five sixths now crosses zero.

Check `1/6+(−5/6)=−4/6=−2/3`. The add-back check uses the original negative subtrahend, not its positive opposite. For `−0.4−0.6`, the subtrahend is positive, so add negative 0.6 and obtain negative one. Subtraction alone does not always indicate a rightward movement.

## Preserve order and exactness

The difference `1/3−5/6` is negative one half, while `5/6−1/3` is positive one half. Subtraction is not generally commutative. Once rewritten as addition, properties of addition apply to the complete signed terms, not unsigned values stripped of their signs.

Subtracting zero changes nothing: `−7/4−0=−7/4`. In `−1/2−(−5/4)=3/4`, quarters remain quarter-units; the model moves five intervals without enlarging the unit. Keep exact fractions instead of using visual closeness as equality evidence.

## Explain the invariant

The workshop asks for the opposite of the subtrahend and the result. Changing either operand clears the old prediction. Revealing the model shows the original subtraction, rewritten addition and directed segment together.

For a checkable explanation, identify minuend, operation and signed subtrahend; state which part remains and which changes. Finish by adding the original subtrahend back. A relationship that handles positive, negative and zero subtrahends is more reliable than a sign slogan with no identified roles.
