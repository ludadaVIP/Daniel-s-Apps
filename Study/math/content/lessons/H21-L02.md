# 复数的实部与虚部

复数的两部分不是两个都含i的量。本课抓住**a+bi中的a和b都是实数**，用这两个系数理解表示、分类与相等。

## 一条核心想法

z=a+bi，记Re(z)=a、Im(z)=b。a叫实部，b叫虚部；含i的整项bi可称虚数项，不能把它与虚部b混为一谈。3−2i写成3+(−2)i，故Re=3、Im=−2，而不是Im=−2i。

每个复数有唯一的一对实系数。实数5就是5+0i，所以实数也属于复数；−4i就是0−4i。b=0表示实数，b≠0表示非实数。本单元纯虚数要求a=0、b≠0，因而0是实数但不叫纯虚数。

复平面中a+bi对应点(a,b)，两个坐标也都是实数。虚轴的纵坐标是系数b，不是bi；例如2i对应(0,2)，不写成(0,2i)。

### 例1：补足省略项，保留负号和分数

对z=3−2i，先明确a=3、b=−2；负号属于虚部系数。对z=1/2+3i，Re=1/2、Im=3，实部不要求整数。对z=−4i，Re=0、Im=−4；对实数5，Re=5、Im=0。

z=0时两部分都是0，原点没有新造的方向。它既在实轴也在虚轴，但“位于虚轴”不等于本单元“纯虚数”的全部条件，因为后者还要求非零虚部。

只改b会使点上下移动，不改变实部a；只改a会使点左右移动，不改变虚部b。图窗缩放与分量投影是显示选择，不会改写两个系数。

### 例2：相等必须同时核对两部分

x、y为实数，若2x+(y−1)i=6+3i，比较系数得2x=6、y−1=3，所以x=3、y=4。代回得到6+3i，两部分都一致。

若只检查实部，x=3而y=0也会通过第一条，但左边成为6−i，并不等于6+3i。只核一部分不是完整的相等证据。

另一个等式(x+1)+(2y)i=3−4i，给x+1=3、2y=−4，得到(x,y)=(2,−2)。有序数对在这里记录两个未知实数的解，不能把它误写为2−2i后当作(x,y)。在复数相等中，实部与虚部不是可以互相补偿的两个位置。

## 两部分工作台

选择a与b，先预测Re、Im、real/nonreal与是否pure-imaginary。需要时打开水平/竖直投影，数清各轴的实系数。改变b只清与虚部有关的问题，保留已经正确读出的Re；改变a不抹掉未受影响的Im或real/nonreal判断。

原点箭头是这个复数的一种表示。自由平移一支几何箭头可保持位移分量，但复数对应的点始终以同一个原点定位，不能把任意终点坐标当原数。两轴同单位，避免把角度或长度变化误当代数变化。

## 家长核对与迁移

追问“虚部是不是实数？”以及“另一部分有没有核对？”出口要求解两个实系数方程并代回。若只出现正确x、y而没有两部分理由，应继续追问。下一课运算仍保留两部分，但乘法会通过i²把部分虚数项乘积变成实数贡献。

# Real and imaginary parts

The two parts of a complex number are not both quantities containing i. This lesson uses **the real coefficients a and b in a+bi** to understand representation, classification and equality.

## One central idea

For z=a+bi, write Re(z)=a and Im(z)=b. The imaginary part is b, not the entire term bi. Thus 3−2i=3+(−2)i has real part three and imaginary part −2, rather than −2i.

Each complex number has one pair of real components. The real number five is 5+0i and is therefore also complex; −4i is 0−4i. A zero imaginary coefficient gives a real number, and a nonzero one gives a nonreal number. Here purely imaginary requires a=0 and b≠0, excluding zero from that category.

On the complex plane a+bi corresponds to (a,b), with both coordinates real. The vertical coordinate is coefficient b, not the term bi. For example, 2i corresponds to (0,2), not (0,2i).

### Example 1: Implicit zeros, signs and fractions

For z=3−2i, identify a=3 and b=−2; the negative sign belongs to the coefficient. For 1/2+3i, Re=1/2 and Im=3, so components need not be integers. For −4i, Re=0 and Im=−4. For the real number five, Re=5 and Im=0.

Zero has both components zero and no newly invented arrow direction. The origin lies on both axes, but being on the imaginary axis alone is insufficient for the stated purely imaginary category: the imaginary coefficient must also be nonzero.

Changing only b moves a point vertically without changing its real part. Changing only a moves it horizontally without changing its imaginary part. A different viewing window or component projection changes no coefficient.

### Example 2: Equality checks both components

For real x,y, suppose 2x+(y−1)i=6+3i. Matching the two real coefficients gives 2x=6 and y−1=3, so x=3,y=4. Substitution gives 6+3i and verifies both parts.

Checking only the real part would also accept x=3,y=0 for the first condition, yet the resulting number 6−i is not 6+3i. Agreement of one component does not establish complex equality.

For the separate equation (x+1)+(2y)i=3−4i, coefficient equations x+1=3 and 2y=−4 give (x,y)=(2,−2). This ordered pair records two real unknowns. Writing the complex number 2−2i is not a substitute for the requested pair. One component cannot compensate for a mismatch in the other.

## Components workbench

Choose a and b, then predict Re, Im, real/nonreal and whether the number is purely imaginary. Reveal horizontal and vertical projections when useful, reading real coefficients on both axes. A change to b preserves an unaffected Re answer; a change to a preserves Im and a real/nonreal classification that depends only on b.

An arrow from the origin represents this complex number. Translating a geometric arrow preserves its displacement components, while the complex point is still located relative to the fixed origin. Do not identify an arbitrary translated arrow's endpoint coordinates with the original number. Equal axis units keep the geometric interpretation faithful.

## Parent review and transfer

Ask whether the imaginary part is a real number and whether the other component has also been checked. The exit requires two coefficient equations and substitution, not merely the final x,y. The next lesson preserves the two-component representation while multiplication uses i² to turn some imaginary-term products into real contributions.
