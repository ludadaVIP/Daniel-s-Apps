## 先读标签、单位、零点与刻度

条形图把同一份数据画成可比较的高度。类别标签说明在计谁，纵轴单位说明在计人数、长度还是别的量，刻度说明一个间隔代表多少。只看条形跨了几格，还不知道人数。

本课用假想通勤数据，每人只记一种主要方式，所以类别之间没有重复计人，可以把人数相加。数据不是实际班级调查，也不能直接证明哪种交通方式更好。

### 例 1 · 一格两人，不是一格一人

1. 表格记录步行 8 人、骑车 6 人、公交 10 人。
2. 图从零开始，刻度依次为 0、2、4、6、8、10 等。
3. 公交条形跨五个间隔，每间隔两人，所以 **5×2=10 人**。
4. 公交比步行多 **10−8=2 人**；两根条形合计 18 人回答的是另一问题。
5. 骑车人数为六，表格与对应柱条必须一致。人数不能直接说明哪种方式更好。

### 例 2 · 同一数据换纵轴范围

1. 原图纵轴从零到二十，公交条形对齐十，达到图示范围的一半。
2. 改成从零到四十，仍然画公交十人，条形只达到范围的四分之一。
3. 它看起来变矮，但**人数仍是 10**，表格没有改变。
4. 改为每间隔五人，也只是用两格表示十人，不是改成两人。
5. 比较不同图之前必须读各自标尺；不能把屏幕高度变化当成真实人数变化。

### 用表格核查未知量

三类总人数是 8+6+10=24。若问题只问步行与公交，则只合并那两类，不加入骑车人数。差距则用较多减较少，不把两数相加。先说明要找整体、两个部分的合计还是差。

零也是明确的数据。一类零人，对应条形高度零，不能为了让它“看得见”而画成一人高；类别标签仍保留，读者才知道它存在而数值为零。四、零、六三类合计十人，不是十一人。

本工作台固定零起点，用同一表格生成每根条形。滑块改人数，图与表同步；只换刻度密度或纵轴上限，人数保持。刻度间距在屏幕上不同，不影响它们代表的数量关系。

不能从这些人数直接推出公交速度、费用或某种方式更健康，也不能概括所有新西兰学校。图表只支持给定的比较；若要回答新问题，需另有合适数据。学习时说明“我能看出什么，也不能看出什么”，比猜一个漂亮的结论更重要。

**家庭挑战：** 十张纸卡代表十位假想通勤者，每张只选一种方式。做人数表，画零起点条形图，再用不同间隔画同一数据。说明合计、差距与不能推断的事项，不需真实调查、出行或计分。

## Read labels, units, zero and tick spacing first

A bar chart draws the same data as comparable heights. Category labels identify what is counted, vertical units distinguish people from lengths or other quantities, and ticks state how much one interval represents. Counting intervals alone does not tell a people count.

These illustrative travel data give each imaginary person one main mode. Categories do not double-count people, so totals can be added. They are not a real class survey and cannot establish which mode is better.

### Example 1 · Two people per interval

1. The table records Walk 8, Bike 6 and Bus 10 people.
2. The axis begins at zero with labels 0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 and so on.
3. Bus spans five intervals of two people, giving **5×2=10 people**.
4. Bus exceeds Walk by **10−8=2 people**. Combining both to get eighteen answers a different question.
5. Bike has six people; its table entry and corresponding bar must agree. Counts alone do not establish which mode is better.

### Example 2 · Change the axis range, retain the data

1. On an axis from zero to twenty, the ten-person Bus bar reaches halfway up the range.
2. Change the axis to zero through forty. The same ten people reach only one quarter of the range.
3. The bar looks shorter, but **the count remains 10** and the table is unchanged.
4. Ticks of five instead represent ten people with two intervals, not two people.
5. Read each chart's scale before comparing charts; changed screen height is not automatically changed population.

### Check the unknown against the table

All three categories total 8+6+10=24. Asking only for Walk and Bus includes those two and excludes Bike. A difference instead subtracts the smaller from the larger. Identify whether the unknown is a total, a selected combination or a difference.

Zero is an explicit data value. A zero-count category needs zero bar height, not a one-person bar to make it visible. Retain the category label so readers see that it is included with count zero. Counts four, zero and six total ten rather than eleven.

The workshop uses a zero baseline and generates every bar from the same table. Changing counts updates chart and table together; changing tick density or the axis maximum retains the data. Different drawn spacing does not change the represented quantitative relationship.

These counts do not establish journey speed, cost or health benefits, and cannot describe all New Zealand schools. The chart supports the stated comparisons. A new question requires appropriate additional data. Explaining both what can and cannot be inferred is more useful than guessing an attractive conclusion.

**Family challenge:** ten cards represent ten imaginary travellers, each assigned one mode. Make a count table and zero-baseline chart, then redraw the same data with another tick spacing. Explain totals, differences and unsupported conclusions. No real survey, travel or score is needed.
