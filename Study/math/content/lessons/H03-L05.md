# 对数运算法则

指数把乘法中的重复因子变成指数的相加。反过来，对数能把正真数的乘积变成对数的和。但它没有把所有运算都“拆开”的魔法。

## 一条核心想法

**从同底指数律推导对数律，同时保留原来每个真数的条件。** 设a>0、a≠1，u,v>0，令u=aᵖ、v=aᑫ，则uv=a^(p+q)。由唯一逆指数，logₐ(uv)=p+q=logₐu+logₐv。

同理logₐ(u/v)=logₐu−logₐv；u>0、实数r时logₐ(uʳ)=r logₐu。换底公式为logₐb=log_cb/log_ca，要求a,c>0且均不等于1、b>0。分母log_ca不为0，因为a≠1。计算器上的log通常指以10为底，ln指以e为底；换底只需分子分母使用同一合法底数。

### 例1：可合并的是乘积，不是和

log₂8+log₂4=3+2=5；合并得log₂(8×4)=log₂32=5。log₃81−log₃3=4−1=3；合并为log₃(81/3)=log₃27=3。

反例取u=v=4：log₂(4+4)=log₂8=3，而log₂4+log₂4=4。因此不存在通用的“和的对数等于对数的和”法则。一个反例足以否定全称等式；几个巧合相等的例子不能证明它。换底例log₈4=log₂4/log₂8=2/3，不要把分子分母颠倒。

### 例2：变形不能悄悄扩大原域

解log₂(x−1)+log₂(x+1)=3。原式要求x−1>0且x+1>0，即x>1。合并后log₂(x²−1)=3，故x²−1=8，x²=9，候选x=±3。

只有3符合原域；代回log₂2+log₂4=1+2=3。−3虽能使合并式有意义，原来两个真数却为−4和−2，无实数对数。因此它不是原方程的解。

另一个域例：log₂(x²)=2log₂|x|对所有x≠0成立；若写成2log₂x，就只在x>0成立。写绝对值并非装饰，而是保留负输入时的合法正真数。

## 结构对照实验

令u=2ᵖ、v=2ᑫ，切换乘积、商与和，先预测再揭示结构。乘积和商可由p、q直接推导；和先算真数，必要时用计算器并标近似号。实验只核数值，不把某组巧合当通式证明。

## 家长核对

出口方程要求写原域、变形、全部候选、筛选与代回，由家长逐项审核。先问“原来每个对数分别要求什么”，再看最后答案；只报x=3不足以展示排除−3的依据。后续模型课将复习换底与单位。

# Logarithm laws

Exponent laws turn multiplication of repeated factors into addition of exponents. Reversing that structure turns products of positive arguments into sums of logarithms. It does not permit splitting every operation inside a logarithm.

## One central idea

**Derive logarithm laws from same-base exponent laws while retaining every original argument condition.** Let a>0, a≠1, u,v>0 and write u=aᵖ, v=aᑫ. Then uv=a^(p+q). The unique inverse exponent gives logₐ(uv)=p+q=logₐu+logₐv.

Similarly logₐ(u/v)=logₐu−logₐv. For u>0 and real r, logₐ(uʳ)=r logₐu. Change base using logₐb=log_cb/log_ca, where a,c>0, neither base is one, and b>0. The denominator is nonzero because a≠1. A calculator’s log commonly means base ten and ln means base e; change of base works when numerator and denominator use the same valid base.

### Example 1: Combine a product, not a sum

log₂8+log₂4=3+2=5. Combining gives log₂(8×4)=log₂32=5. Also log₃81−log₃3=4−1=3; combining gives log₃(81/3)=log₃27=3.

For a counterexample choose u=v=4. Then log₂(4+4)=log₂8=3, but log₂4+log₂4=4. There is no general law splitting the logarithm of a sum into a sum of logarithms. One counterexample disproves a universal identity; a few accidental agreements cannot prove one. For change of base, log₈4=log₂4/log₂8=2/3. Reversing the numerator and denominator gives the wrong result.

### Example 2: Transformation must not enlarge the original domain

Solve log₂(x−1)+log₂(x+1)=3. Both original arguments must be positive: x−1>0 and x+1>0, hence x>1. Combining gives log₂(x²−1)=3, then x²−1=8 and x²=9, producing candidates x=±3.

Only three satisfies the original domain. Substitution yields log₂2+log₂4=1+2=3. Although −3 makes the combined expression meaningful, its two original arguments are −4 and −2, whose real logarithms are undefined. It is not a solution of the original equation.

Another domain example is log₂(x²)=2log₂|x| for every x≠0. Replacing the right side with 2log₂x works only for x>0. The absolute value preserves a positive argument for negative original inputs; it is part of the mathematics, not decoration.

## Structure-comparison experiment

Set u=2ᵖ and v=2ᑫ, switch among product, quotient and sum, and predict before revealing the comparison. Product and quotient values follow directly from p and q. For a sum, evaluate the argument first and use a calculator if necessary, marking approximation. The experiment checks numbers; it never treats an accidental match as proof of a law.

## Parent check

The exit equation requires the original domain, transformations, all candidates, filtering and substitution. A parent reviews each part. Ask what each original logarithm requires before inspecting the final answer. Reporting only x=3 does not demonstrate why −3 was excluded. The modelling lesson retrieves change of base alongside time units.
