# 期望值的意义

期望是整个概率分布的加权中心，不是下一次必须出现的值。离散变量的E(X)=∑xP(X=x)，每个取值都乘它自己的概率。这里分布有限，所有和都明确存在。

## 三种平均要分清

理论期望由给定分布决定；一次结果是实际抽到的一个值；样本均值是有限多次结果之和除次数。三者有联系，却不能相互替代。零次实验没有样本均值，因为不能除以零；这不妨碍理论期望已经由模型确定。

独立同分布重复且期望存在时，大数定律描述样本均值接近期望的收敛性质。它不承诺每一步误差减小，也不要求偏小之后下一次必须偏大。有限模拟只能展示轨迹，不能证明这个一般定理。

### 例1：期望不是可取值

X取0或3，各概率1/2。E(X)=0×1/2+3×1/2=3/2。一次只能得到0或3，不能得到1.5，所以P(X=E(X))=0。期望落在两个可能值之间并不矛盾：它是加权中心，不是新增一种结果。

若十次每一次都有同样边缘分布，和S的期望为十个3/2相加，即15。期望的加法本身不要求独立；但若要解释重复均值的通常大数规律，还要说明适当的独立等条件。不要把两个定理的条件混成一条口诀。

十次实际总和可以0、3、6等，不保证恰15。即使过去九次都为0，在独立模型下第十次仍是0或3各半，不会为了补偿而改变概率。

### 例2：权重与线性变换

另一变量取0概率9/10、取10概率1/10。期望为1，不是把不同取值直接平均得到的5。较罕见的大结果仍会影响长期均值；频率高的零也不意味着期望一定零。

回到等概率0或3，定义Y=2X+1，则Y取1或7，各半，E(Y)=1/2+7/2=4。也可用E(2X+1)=2E(X)+1=4。一般E(aX+b)=aE(X)+b；常数b每次都加，不应再无故乘一次概率或次数。

若记录单位为虚构积分，则期望和样本均值也用积分，不是概率。对“每轮平均增加多少”的问题，先分清毛收益、固定费用与净变化，给出完整模型后再加权。

## 读模拟，保留来源

工作台用明确种子的伪随机序列，先显示零次时均值未定义，再逐批增加10次。理论期望可以先预测，模拟均值可以上下变化。换种子会换有限轨迹，不改变理论分布；改变数值变换会重建模拟样本，避免把两种随机变量混成一组记录。

核验表逐行列出值、概率及乘积，还列前12次生成状态和结果。它能复现本次演示，但伪随机轨迹不是独立性的经验证明。家长可以要求学生指出哪一列来自模型、哪一列来自有限样本。

出口解释要保留长期、有限、单次三层区别，说明期望为什么不必能取到，以及为何过去结果不强迫下一次补偿。把偶然接近期望的一批当必然规律，或者把一批偏离当理论错误，都是同一类层次混淆。

# Meaning of expected value

Expectation is the probability-weighted centre of a distribution, not a promised next result. For a finite discrete variable, E(X)=∑xP(X=x). Multiply each value by its own probability rather than averaging distinct values equally.

## Distinguish three quantities

The theoretical mean comes from the model. One outcome is a realised value. A sample mean averages a finite set of realised values. With zero observations the sample mean is undefined, while a theoretical expectation can already be known.

Under iid repetition with an existing expectation, the law of large numbers describes convergence of averages. It does not require decreasing error at every step or a compensating large draw after small results. A finite simulation illustrates one trajectory rather than proving the theorem.

### Example 1: An unattainable expectation

Let X be zero or three with equal probabilities. Then E(X)=0×1/2+3×1/2=3/2. No individual draw can equal1.5, so P(X=E(X))=0. A weighted centre between possible values is not a new outcome.

For ten draws, each having this marginal distribution, the expected sum is fifteen. Linearity of expectation itself does not require independence. Independence enters the usual repeated-sampling convergence interpretation, not this simple addition rule.

An actual ten-draw sum may be zero, three, six and so on; fifteen is not guaranteed. Even after nine zeros, an independent tenth draw remains zero or three with equal probabilities. The model does not alter its probabilities to compensate for the past.

### Example 2: Weights and affine transformations

A variable equal to zero with probability 9/10 and ten with probability 1/10 has mean one. The unweighted mean of its distinct values, five, is wrong. A rare large value still affects expectation, while a common zero does not force expectation to zero.

For the equally likely zero-or-three variable, set Y=2X+1. Its values are one and seven, each with mass 1/2, so E(Y)=4. The same result follows from E(2X+1)=2E(X)+1. In general, E(aX+b)=aE(X)+b. The added constant belongs to every outcome.

If values represent fictional points, the expectation and sample mean use points too; neither is a probability. Clearly define gross amounts, fixed costs and net changes before weighting a model.

## Read the simulation with its source intact

The workbench identifies a seed and uses a reproducible pseudorandom sequence. Begin with undefined sample mean at zero trials, then add batches of ten. Changing the seed changes the finite trajectory while preserving theoretical expectation. Changing the numerical transform rebuilds the sample, avoiding a mixture of different variables.

An audit lists values, masses, weighted products and the first twelve generated states and outcomes. Reproducibility checks the demonstration, not a general independence claim. Explain which numbers belong to the model and which belong to one finite experiment. A near match is not a guarantee; a deviation is not automatically a contradiction of theory.
