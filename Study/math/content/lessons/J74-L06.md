# 写清楚为什么

## 每一条关系都应有来处

一份几何说明需要读者能追踪：已知什么、要证明什么、由哪个条件和理由得到哪条关系、最后怎样到达结论。正确数值是结果的一部分，不是一整份证明。测量和外观能引出猜想，也能检查明显错误；不能补成题目没给的平行、相等或直角条件。

先把已知与目标分开。若平行是待证结论，不能在第一步就当已知使用平行线性质。若给定边长无法组成三角形，不能先画一个正常三角形，再套内角和把数算完。理由要满足适用条件，而不是见到某个词就套公式。

### 例 1：从两个平角连到对顶相等

已知两直线相交，A/B 与 B/C 是邻补角，要解释 A=C，且本条推导不直接引用待解释的对顶角定理。

1. 由反向射线组成平角，`A+B=180°`。
2. 同理，`B+C=180°`。
3. 两个和相等，`A+B=B+C`。
4. 从相等量中同减 B，得到 `A=C`。

每一步都交代关系与依据。这不表示以后任何题都不能引用对顶角定理；定理建立后可以使用。但当题目明确要求说明它为何成立时，重复“因为对顶角相等”并没有给出这条推导。

### 例 2：跨交点先用平行，再用局部平角

已知 ℓ∥m，A/E 是同位角，E/F 在下交点邻补，A=62°。先由平行同位相等得到 `E=A=62°`；再由反向射线得到 `E+F=180°`，于是 `F=118°`。

不能说 A/E 是对顶，因为它们在不同交点。也不能删去平行条件仍保持第一步。若只有“图看起来平行”，就没有足够依据确定 E；用假定平行推出同位相等，再反过来声称证明了平行，是循环论证。

三角形说明同样要连条件：过 C 作 AB 平行线，两组内错角把 A、B 搬到 C 的两侧，与 C 组成平角，因此内角和一百八十。若用已经建立的内角和求第三角，应写明所用定理与三个内角，不必每次重证定理；论证详略由问题要求决定。

## 遇到缺口，就如实停在缺口

给三边三、四、八厘米，先比较 `3+4<8`，说明条件不能围成非退化三角形。这时“求第三内角”的任务缺少合法对象，不能偷偷改八为五或画一张看似正常的图继续。条件不足与条件冲突也不同：前者可有多种可能，后者这些条件根本不能同时成立。

工作台的依据勾选只检查本条推理用到哪些规则，不声称自动读懂你的文字。写下完整链后由家长核对；改动文字会取消旧复核，换案例则清空旧条件与推理。正式课内解释、出口题与复习中也保留人工核对，不用选择题代替证明学习。

家长可逐行问“这行用了哪条已知？”“删掉它以后仍一定成立吗？”“能不能给一个反例？”孩子能指出缺条件的具体位置，承认无法确定或不可能，比凑出漂亮数字更接近严谨推理。

# Writing geometrical reasons

## Every relationship needs a source

A geometrical explanation lets a reader trace the givens, the target, the reason supporting each relationship and the final inference. A correct number is part of a result, not a complete proof. Measurement and appearance support conjectures and error checks, but cannot create missing parallel, equal or right-angle premises.

Separate givens from the target. If parallelism is the conclusion, do not begin by applying parallel-line properties. If proposed side lengths cannot form a triangle, a plausible sketch does not make the triangle-sum theorem applicable. Every rule has conditions.

### Example 1: link straight angles to opposite equality

Two lines intersect, with adjacent supplementary pairs A/B and B/C. To derive A=C without citing the theorem being explained, write A+B=180° from opposite rays, then B+C=180° for the other straight line. The sums are equal, so A+B=B+C. Subtract the same B to obtain A=C.

Each relationship now has a stated source. This does not forbid using the established vertically opposite theorem later. However, repeating its name is insufficient when the specific task asks why it holds.

### Example 2: use parallelism across intersections, then a local straight angle

Given ℓ∥m, corresponding A/E and adjacent supplementary E/F at the lower intersection, with A=62°, parallel corresponding equality gives E=A=62°. Opposite rays below give E+F=180°, hence F=118°.

A/E are not vertically opposite because they occur at different vertices. Removing the parallel premise invalidates the first inference. Assuming parallelism from appearance, deriving corresponding equality and then claiming to prove parallelism is circular.

A triangle argument similarly links its conditions: construct a parallel to AB through C, use two alternate-interior equalities, then form a straight angle with C. Once the triangle-sum theorem is established, it may be cited in a calculation without being re-proved every time. The detail required depends on the task.

## Report the gap where it occurs

Sides three, four and eight fail because 3+4<8. No nondegenerate triangle has those conditions. Do not change eight to five or invent a valid-looking figure merely to calculate an angle. Insufficient information allows several possibilities; contradictory conditions cannot all hold together.

Reason selection in the workshop checks only which rules directly support the specified argument. It does not automatically read the written proof. After writing a complete chain, a parent reviews it. Editing clears the preceding review, and changing cases clears the preceding conditions and reasoning. Formal lesson, exit and retrieval explanations retain human review rather than replacing proof with selections.

A parent asks which given supports each line, what happens if that premise is removed and whether a counterexample is possible. Identifying a precise missing condition and reporting undetermined or impossible conclusions demonstrates stronger reasoning than forcing a tidy number.
