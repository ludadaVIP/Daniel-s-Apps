## 已知的是部分，找回 100%

“18 L 是整体的 30%”与“18 L 的 30%”是两个不同问题。前一句十八升只对应 30%，整体未知；后一句把十八升当作 100%，求它的一个部分。读到百分数先找对应，能比看到数字就决定乘或除更可靠。

本课的目标是把**已知部分与给定百分率对应**，反求 100% 的实际量。既可以先求 1% 再扩大一百倍，也可以用分数除法。求完必须把整体乘原百分率，检查是否恢复题目给定部分。

### 例 1 · 十八升对应百分之三十

1. 已知 **18 L ↔ 30%**，在双数轴的 30% 位置写十八升，100% 下写问号。
2. 把三十个百分之一的量平均分：**1%=18÷30=0.6 L**。
3. 一百个这样的份为 **0.6×100=60 L**。
4. 合并计算：**整体=18÷(30/100)=18÷0.3=60 L**。
5. 核回 **60×30%=18 L**。若错算 18×30%=5.4 L，实际上求的是十八升自身的 30%，换错了参照整体。

除数小于一时，商可以大于被除数。这里三十份扩成一百份，整体当然比已知部分大。不要用“除法一定变小”的口号。用对应关系解释，比记住某类题永远用除法更稳定。

### 例 2 · 已知量超过参考整体

1. 假想需求为 **30 L**，它相当于参考量的 **125%**。
2. 这里三十升对应 125%，不是 100%；参考量反而较小。
3. **125%=5/4**，所以 **参考量=30÷(5/4)=24 L**。
4. 也可先算 **1%=30÷125=0.24 L**，一百份为二十四升。
5. 核查 **24×1.25=30 L**。若算三十乘 1.25，求的是另一个错误整体上的份额。

**不能随便取整：** 7 L 是整体的 40% 时，整体为 7÷0.4=17.5 L，不应截成十七升。再乘 40% 得回七升；十七乘 40% 只得 6.8 L，核回就能发现丢量。

**零百分率分情况：** 若零升对应 0%，任意正整体都符合，因此资料不足，不能唯一确定整体。若十八升这样的正量对应 0%，条件矛盾，因为正整体的 0% 必定为零。两种情况都不能用除以零制造答案。若正百分率却对应零部分，代数等式只给零整体；用部分除零整体解释占比又无法定义，因此工作台会要求换正的已知量。

探索时先保持十八升与 30%，预测整体再按两步核对：先展示 1%，再展示 100% 与乘回。修改部分或百分率会清空旧步骤。试试 25%、100% 与 125%，说明整体分别比已知量大、相等还是小；最后把百分率设零，读懂“资料不足”和“条件矛盾”的区别。

**家长陪学：** 让孩子画出已知量的正确位置，再判断运算。解释必须回答“为什么十八不是整体”“除以 0.3 表示什么”“怎么乘回”，不只背“已知部分求整体用除法”。**带走一个发现：** 反求整体是在已知百分率的基础上找回 100%；参照清楚、非零条件与核回缺一不可。

## Recover 100% from a known part

“18 L is 30% of the whole” differs from “30% of 18 L.” In the first statement, eighteen litres corresponds to 30% and the whole is unknown. In the second, eighteen litres is the 100% base. Identify correspondences before choosing multiplication or division.

The objective is to **match the known part to its given rate**, then recover the amount at 100%. Find 1% and scale to one hundred, or use fraction division. Finally, multiply the recovered whole by the original rate to restore the stated part.

### Example 1 · Eighteen litres corresponds to thirty percent

1. Mark **18 L ↔ 30%** on the double number line, with a question mark under 100%.
2. Share the thirty percentage parts equally: **1%=18÷30=0.6 L**.
3. One hundred such shares give **0.6×100=60 L**.
4. Combine the calculation: **whole=18÷(30/100)=18÷0.3=60 L**.
5. Check **60×30%=18 L**. The incorrect calculation 18×30%=5.4 L finds a part of eighteen litres itself, changing the reference whole.

Dividing by a number below one can produce a larger result. Expanding thirty shares to one hundred should increase the amount. A slogan that division always makes numbers smaller would fail here. Explain the relationship instead of memorizing a topic-specific operation.

### Example 2 · The known amount exceeds its reference

1. A fictional requirement is **30 L**, equal to **125%** of a reference amount.
2. Thirty litres belongs at 125%, rather than 100%; the reference is smaller.
3. **125%=5/4**, so **reference=30÷(5/4)=24 L**.
4. Alternatively, **1%=30÷125=0.24 L**, and one hundred shares give twenty-four litres.
5. Check **24×1.25=30 L**. Multiplying thirty by 1.25 would use the wrong whole.

Do not truncate results. If 7 L is 40% of a whole, the whole is 7÷0.4=17.5 L. Multiplying back by 40% gives seven litres. Truncating to seventeen would give only 6.8 L, exposing the lost amount.

A zero rate needs separate reasoning. If zero litres is 0%, every positive whole fits, so the information is insufficient for a unique result. If a positive amount such as eighteen litres is labeled 0%, the conditions conflict: 0% of a positive whole must be zero. Neither case permits division by zero. A positive rate with zero as the known part gives only a zero whole algebraically; a part-to-zero-whole proportion cannot be defined by division, so the workshop requests a positive known amount.

Keep eighteen litres and 30% initially. Predict the whole, then check in two stages: first reveal 1%, then 100% and multiplication back. Changing the known part or rate clears those stages. Compare 25%, 100% and 125%, explaining whether the whole is larger than, equal to or smaller than the known amount. Finally try a zero rate and distinguish insufficient information from inconsistent conditions.

**For a parent:** ask the learner to place the known amount correctly before choosing an operation. Listen for why eighteen is not the whole, what division by 0.3 does, and how multiplication checks it. **Takeaway:** recovering a whole means finding 100% from a known percentage, with a clear reference, nonzero conditions and an inverse check.
