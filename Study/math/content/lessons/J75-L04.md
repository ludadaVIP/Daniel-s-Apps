# 简单线性规律

## 每单位变化与零输入的输出

已知规则 `y=kx+b` 中，x 每增加一单位，y 改变 k；x=0 时，y=b。k 说明变化率，b 说明初始量或纵轴截距，不能把 b 也随 x 重复。若 x 变化不是一，就用 `Δy=kΔx`，其中 Δ 表示新值减旧值。

本课把含常数项的 `y=kx+b` 广义称为线性关系，也保留 k=0 的常数边界；中文严格的一次函数通常要求 k≠0，两个说法的范围要说明。正比例还要求没有额外截距，不能把任何直线都叫正比例，也不能认为只要经过原点就必是正比例直线。

### 例 1：已知线性前提下确定参数

已知关系形式为 `y=kx+b`，且经过 `(0,−1)`、`(2,3)`。第一点直接给 b=−1。x 增二，y 增四，所以 `k=4/2=2`，关系为 `y=2x−1`。再代入两点检验，零输入给负一、二输入给三。

这一步依赖“已知线性”的前提。两个样本足以确定这个模型里的 k、b，并不代表两个样本已经证明真实情况一定线性。用变化率公式时 Δx 不能为零；同一输入的两个输出也不能直接决定普通函数的唯一变化率。

对于 `y=3x+2`，x=4 得十四，不是把三与二先加后乘四得到二十。对于 k=−2，x 增三，`Δy=−6`，表示减少六；若没有给初始 y，就不能把负六当最后输出。

### 例 2：常数与反例

已知 `y=kx+b` 经过 `(0,3)`、`(2,3)`，输出没有变化，k=0，故 `y=3`。一般式可以是常数，不必强留一个 x。若 k=−1/2、b=2，则 `y=−x/2+2`，每单位 x 使 y 减半，零输入给二。

再比较两个明确给定的模型 `y=x`、`y=x²`。在 x=0 时都零、x=1 时都一，但 x=2 时分别二与四，所以它们不是同一个一般关系。一个反例可否定恒等，几个吻合却不能证明恒等。工作台先给匹配样本，再试新输入；曲线来自两个已给模型，而不是从有限点猜出的唯一图像。

## 变化率不等于屏幕上的倾角

把横轴显示范围扩大，同样 k 的图线会显得更陡；把纵轴范围扩大，又可能显得更平。数据的每单位变化没有因此改变。斜率是按两轴数值计算的比，不是直接量屏幕上一个角，尤其两轴单位不同更不能混淆。

工作台可给 k 正、负、零和不同 b，画出的线段会被显示窗口截取，窗口边界不是函数停止的数学原因。情境中的停止条件则由下一课的有效范围决定。家长请孩子解释每个参数、给两点检验，再指出一般关系的前提，避免只记“看起来直就线性”的口号。

# Simple linear relationships

## Rate and output at zero

Under `y=kx+b`, each unit increase in x changes y by k, while x=0 gives b. Rate and intercept have different roles; the intercept is not repeated for every input. For a general input change, use Δy=kΔx, where each change means new minus old.

Here linear is used broadly for the affine form y=kx+b, including the constant boundary k=0. A strictly first-degree function excludes zero k. Direct proportion also needs no added intercept. Not every straight relationship is proportional, and merely passing through zero is insufficient without the proportional straight form.

### Example 1: determine parameters under a linear premise

A relationship is known to have form y=kx+b and passes through `(0,−1)` and `(2,3)`. The first gives b=−1. Input increases two while output increases four, so k=2 and y=2x−1. Both supplied points check exactly.

This calculation depends on the given linear premise. Two samples determine the parameters within that model; they do not prove that reality must follow it. A rate quotient requires nonzero Δx. Two outputs at the same input do not provide an ordinary unique rate in this way.

For y=3x+2 at x=4, multiply first and add the intercept to obtain fourteen, not twenty. With rate −2 and input increase three, Δy=−6 means a decrease of six, not a final output unless an initial output is supplied.

### Example 2: constants and a counterexample

A known affine relationship through `(0,3)` and `(2,3)` has zero rate and constant expression three. A valid general expression need not contain x. Rate −1/2 and intercept two instead give y=−x/2+2.

The supplied models y=x and y=x² agree at zero and one, yet give two and four at x=2. One counterexample refutes their identity; a few matches cannot prove identity. The workshop starts with matching samples and tests another input. Its curves come from supplied models, not a claim that finitely many points uniquely determine a general curve.

## Numerical rate is not screen angle

Widening the horizontal viewing range can make the same rate look steeper; widening the vertical range can make it look flatter. The numerical ratio has not changed. Compute changes from axis values rather than measuring a screen angle, especially with different units on the axes.

Change k among positive, negative and zero values, and vary b. Visible line segments are clipped to the viewing window, whose edge is not itself a mathematical domain limit. Contextual limits in the next lesson have a different source. A parent asks for parameter meanings, point checks and the premise supporting the general relationship.
