## 先看关系，再看符号

加法可以合并两个部分，也可以描述数量增加。减法可以表示拿走、找缺少部分，还可以比较两个数量的差距。同一个减号出现在不同故事里，不代表故事中都真的移走了东西。

最重要的关系是 **部分 + 部分 = 整体**。知道两个部分，可以求整体；知道整体与其中一个部分，可以求另一个。比较时则要辨认两个独立的数量，不要把差距当成它们的总量。

### 例 1 · 两种颜色合在一起

1. 有 8 个绿球与 5 个蓝球，两组都是整体的部分。
2. 每一个球只数一次，合计 **8+5=13 个**。
3. 若从这 13 个中拿走 5 个蓝球，留下 **13−5=8 个**。
4. 两个算式说的是同一个整体与两部分，从不同方向找到未知量。

### 例 2 · 比较而不拿走

1. A 有 14 个球，B 有 9 个球。
2. 从两组各取一个在图中配对，九对之后 A 还有五个未配对。
3. 因此 A 比 B 多 **14−9=5 个**。
4. 这不是把 B 的九个球从 A 箱里拿走，两组原来的球都还在。
5. 检查差距：9+5=14；两组总量 14+9=23 是另一个问题。

### 缺少部分也可以用减法

总共有 19 个球，其中 7 个是红球，其余都是蓝球。19 已经包括红球，不能再做 19+7。写成 **7+蓝球数=19**，得到 19−7=12 个蓝球。反向检查 7+12=19，能看出两个部分恰好组成总量。

不要只看“多”字就选择加法。“A 比 B 多几个”是在求差距；“B 又得到几个”可能在求增加后的总量。先说清楚未知的是整体、部分还是差距，再选算式。

比较距离时要确认单位相同。7 km 与 8 km 两段路都走了，总距离是 15 km，而不是差距 1 km。20 米绳子剪去 8 米，剩下 12 米；剩余长度与剪去长度合起来恢复原总量。

工作台用小球显示合并、拿走和比较。每个小球代表一个单位；拿走的球有划线，比较的未配对球有独立标记。改变数量前先预测，操作后说明符号背后的关系，不只读出得数。

**小检查：** 给出一个没有物体被拿走、却仍适合用减法的故事。

## Identify the relationship before the symbol

Addition can combine two parts or describe an increase. Subtraction can remove an amount, find a missing part or compare a difference. Using the same minus symbol does not mean that objects are physically removed in every story.

The key relationship is **part + part = whole**. Knowing both parts finds the whole; knowing the whole and one part finds the other. A comparison instead involves two separate amounts. Their difference is not their combined total.

### Example 1 · Combine two colours

1. Eight green balls and five blue balls are both parts of the whole.
2. Count each ball once to obtain **8+5=13 balls**.
3. Removing the five blue balls leaves **13−5=8 balls**.
4. These expressions describe the same whole and parts from different directions.

### Example 2 · Compare without removing

1. A has fourteen balls and B has nine.
2. Pair one counter from each group. After nine pairs, A has five unmatched counters.
3. A therefore has **14−9=5 more balls**.
4. This does not remove B's balls from A's box; both original groups remain.
5. Check the difference with 9+5=14. Finding 14+9=23 as the combined total answers a different question.

### A missing part can also use subtraction

There are nineteen balls, seven red and all the rest blue. Nineteen already includes the red balls, so adding seven again double-counts. Write **seven + blue count = nineteen**, giving 19−7=12 blue balls. The check 7+12=19 confirms that both parts recover the whole.

Do not select addition just because a problem says “more”. “How many more does A have?” asks for a difference; “B receives some more” may ask for a new total. Identify whether the unknown is a whole, part or difference before choosing the expression.

Distances require matching units. Walking both seven and eight kilometres gives fifteen kilometres altogether, not their one-kilometre difference. Cutting eight metres from twenty leaves twelve metres; remaining and removed lengths recover the original.

The workshop uses one counter per unit to show combining, taking away and comparing. Removal has crossing marks; unmatched comparison counters have separate marks. Predict before changing counts, then explain the relationship behind the symbol rather than only read the answer.

**Quick check:** give a subtraction story in which nothing is actually taken away.
