# 隐函数求导入门

曲线不一定能在全平面写成唯一y=f(x)，却可能在一个点附近有可求导的分支。本课学习沿原关系求导，并先检查公式什么时候能用。

## 沿关系求导

圆x²+y²=25在一些x有上下两个y，所以不是全局单值y函数。选定上半圆某个非端点后，附近可以把y写成y(x)。对x²+y(x)²=25求导，链式法则给2x+2y·dy/dx=0。

y≠0时才可除得dy/dx=−x/y。一般地，对光滑关系F(x,y)=0有Fx+Fy y′=0。这里Fx表示只让x变化、固定y所得的偏导，Fy类似。Fy非0支持局部y(x)分支并给y′=−Fx/Fy。本课用圆和两直线的显式分支核这个条件，不把任意关系都当成自动满足。

### 例1：同一个圆，不同切线类型

P=(3,4)先代回：9+16=25，在圆上。Fy=2y=8非0，dy/dx=−3/4；切线可写y−4=−3(x−3)/4。它与半径的方向一致地垂直，也可作为几何核查。

在(0,5)，斜率−0/5=0，是水平切线y=5。在(5,0)，Fy=0而Fx=10非0，不能用比值给有限dy/dx。但附近可以把x写为√(25−y²)，其dx/dy在0为0，得到竖直切线x=5。无有限斜率不代表没有几何切线。

若给点(3,3)，先代回只有18≠25。尽管−x/y可以算出−1，它不是这个圆在该点的导数，因为这个点根本不在曲线上。输入核查必须在代公式前完成。

### 例2：分母零不保证竖直

交叉关系x²−y²=0等价于(y−x)(y+x)=0，是y=x与y=−x两条直线。在原点，Fx=2x=0、Fy=−2y=0，隐导数公式成为不可除的0/0。

实际两分支分别有斜率1与−1，没有一条可冒充唯一切线的竖直线。称该点为这个关系的奇点，表示正则局部分支条件失败；不是说每个奇点都必有这两条分支。这里能给明确结论，是因为已分解原方程。

在(1,1)，点在正分支，Fy=−2非0，斜率x/y=1。不能把圆的−x/y符号搬到另一条隐关系；每次须从当前F推导。

## 核验顺序比背公式重要

依次核：点是否属于曲线；F是否在附近光滑；Fy是否非0；若除法失败，Fx及实际分支还说明什么。Fy=0、Fx≠0可在这里识别竖直切线；两者同时0时需要进一步分析，不能单靠0/0决定几何。

工作台给点类型和精确Fx、Fy表，正则点显示有限斜率，竖直点显示竖直线，奇点不画假唯一切线。横纵保持同一单位刻度，点在圆外也保留原位置。出口需解释链式内导与三个不同失败原因；家长复核原关系和局部条件，不能只看最终比值。

# Introduction to implicit differentiation

A curve need not be a single global function y=f(x) to have a differentiable local branch. Learn to differentiate along the relation and check when the resulting formula applies.

## Differentiate along a relation

The circle x²+y²=25 has two y-values at some inputs, so it is not a global y-function. Near a selected non-endpoint on its upper half, write y=y(x). Differentiating x²+y(x)²=25 gives2x+2y·dy/dx=0 by the chain rule.

Only when y≠0 may we divide to obtain dy/dx=−x/y. More generally, a smooth relation F(x,y)=0 gives Fx+Fy y′=0. Fx denotes differentiation in x while holding y fixed; Fy is analogous. A nonzero Fy supports a local y(x) branch with derivative−Fx/Fy. Here explicit circle and line branches make the conditions inspectable; an arbitrary relation is not automatically regular.

### Example 1: Different tangent types on one circle

Check P=(3,4): nine plus sixteen equals25, so it lies on the circle. Fy=8≠0 and dy/dx=−3/4. The tangent is y−4=−3(x−3)/4. Its perpendicularity to the radius independently checks the geometry.

At(0,5), the slope is zero and the tangent y=5 is horizontal. At(5,0), Fy=0 but Fx=10≠0, so the ratio does not give a finite dy/dx. Locally express x=√(25−y²); its derivative with respect to y is zero there, giving vertical tangent x=5. No finite slope does not mean no geometric tangent.

At(3,3), substitution gives eighteen rather than25. The calculable ratio−1 is not a derivative of this circle at that point because the point is not on the curve. Check inputs before substituting into the slope formula.

### Example 2: A zero denominator does not guarantee verticality

The relation x²−y²=0 factors into the two lines y=x and y=−x. At their origin Fx=0 and Fy=0. The implicit slope formula would require division0/0.

The branches actually have slopes one and−1, with no unique vertical tangent. The origin is singular for this relation because the regular local-branch condition fails. This does not assert that every singularity has two branches; the specific conclusion follows from factoring this particular relation.

At(1,1), Fy=−2≠0 and slope x/y=1. Do not carry the circle’s negative ratio into a different implicit equation. Derive from the current F each time.

## A checking order is more useful than a memorised ratio

Check membership, smoothness near the point, and whether Fy is nonzero. If division fails, inspect Fx and the actual branches. Here Fy=0 with nonzero Fx identifies a vertical tangent. When both vanish, further analysis is needed;0/0 alone does not determine geometry.

The workbench audits exact partials and membership. Regular points show a finite slope, vertical points a vertical line, and singularities receive no falsely unique tangent. Both axes retain the same scale, including off-curve points. The exit should explain the inner derivative and distinguish these failure reasons. Parent review checks the original relation and local conditions, not merely the final ratio.
