## 第二步需要第一步得到什么

两步问题不是随便把两个运算连起来。先想最后要找的量需要哪些已知；如果其中一个还没有直接给出，就先把它求出来。这个暂时求出的量叫中间量，也要说明对象与单位，不能只在草稿写一个没名字的数字。

有些问题先乘后减，有些先加后乘，还有先减后除。并不存在所有应用题都“乘法先做”的故事规则。算式的运算顺序必须记录你已经理解的数量关系，括号用来表达这种依赖，不是为了让答案看起来复杂。

### 例 1 · 先求原有张数，再扣送出

1. **4 盒**，每盒 **6 张卡**，总共送出 **5 张**，问还剩几张。
2. 最后需要“原有张数−送出张数”；原有量没有直接给出。
3. 第一步 **4×6=24 张**，输出是原有卡片张数，不是二十四盒。
4. 第二步 **24−5=19 张**；合并算式为 **4×6−5=19**。
5. 核查 **19+5=24**，再由 **24÷4=6** 恢复每盒张数。

### 例 2 · 先合并人数，再按每人发卡

1. 第一组 **5 人**，第二组 **3 人**，每人需要 **4 张卡**。
2. 第一步求共有 **5+3=8 人**。
3. 第二步按八个相等的四张组计数，**8×4=32 张**。
4. 合并算式写 **(5+3)×4=32**；括号表示人数先合并。
5. **5+3×4=17** 只把第二组三人乘四，还把第一组人数直接加入张数，不符合每人都需要四张的关系。

### 单位沿着步骤走

“盒数×每盒张数”得到张数；“人数×每人张数”也得到张数。把张数减去送出的张数，仍得到张数。单位与角色帮助判断一个中间结果能否进入下一步，而不是做完才随便加一个单位。

三十张卡先留下六张，余下每袋四张：先 **30−6=24 张**，再 **24÷4=6 袋**。核查六袋每袋四张再加留下六张，恢复三十张。这里结果是袋数，与中间的张数不同。

原题只总共送五张，**4×(6−5)=4** 却表示每盒都送五张，共送二十张。括号改变了故事，不只是另一种写法。先确认每个动作作用于一组还是全部，避免把有效算式用于错误情境。

零也可能是合理结果。三盒每盒四张，全部十二张送出，剩余 **3×4−12=0 张**；零张不是资料不足，而是可以由关系确定的数量。工作台不允许送出超过现有的张数，改盒数或每盒数时会同时限制送出量。

**带走一个发现：** 我能给第一步结果命名，说明它怎样成为第二步的已知；再用加回或乘回去核查故事，而不只重复原计算。

## Ask what the second step needs from the first

A two-step problem is not an arbitrary chain of operations. Identify which known quantities the final result requires. If one is not directly supplied, find it first. Name this intermediate quantity and its unit rather than leave an unexplained number on the page.

Some stories multiply then subtract, others add then multiply, and others subtract then divide. There is no universal story rule saying multiplication must be the first step. Arithmetic order and parentheses should express the quantitative relationship already understood.

### Example 1 · Find original cards, then subtract given cards

1. **4 boxes** hold **6 cards each**, with **5 cards given away in total**.
2. Remaining cards require original minus given cards. Original count is not directly supplied.
3. First **4×6=24 cards**, the original card count rather than twenty-four boxes.
4. Then **24−5=19 cards**, combined as **4×6−5=19**.
5. Check **19+5=24**, then **24÷4=6** to recover cards per box.

### Example 2 · Combine people, then allocate cards per person

1. Groups of **5 people** and **3 people** each need **4 cards per person**.
2. First find **5+3=8 people**.
3. Eight equal groups of four cards give **8×4=32 cards**.
4. Write **(5+3)×4=32**; parentheses record combining people first.
5. **5+3×4=17** multiplies cards only for the second group and adds the first group’s people count directly to cards. That does not match every person receiving four cards.

### Follow units through the steps

Boxes times cards per box gives cards; people times cards per person also gives cards. Subtracting given cards from original cards retains the card unit. Units and roles help decide whether an intermediate result belongs in the next step rather than being decoration added afterwards.

Set aside six of thirty cards and pack the rest in bags of four. First **30−6=24 cards**, then **24÷4=6 bags**. Six bags of four plus six set aside recovers thirty. The final bag count differs from the intermediate card count.

**4×(6−5)=4** removes five from every box, giving away twenty overall. A story giving away five in total needs another relationship. Parentheses can change the story, not merely the appearance of an expression.

Zero can be a determined answer: three boxes of four with all twelve given away leave **3×4−12=0 cards**. Zero is not insufficient information. The workshop prevents removal beyond the available count and restricts given cards when boxes or cards per box change.

**Takeaway:** name the first result and explain why it is needed next. Check the story by adding or multiplying back rather than merely repeat the original calculation.
