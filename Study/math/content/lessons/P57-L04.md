## 同一组数，为什么能看起来差很多？

图表可以帮助发现关系，也可能让眼睛产生不合数据的印象。读图先查标题说的是谁、哪个时间段、什么单位，再查坐标起点、刻度间隔和原数值。最后问：这句话真的由这些观察支持吗？不要只凭柱子的长短下结论。

本课两个假想小组人数为 **A 四十、B 四十四**。数据始终不变，只切换纵轴从零还是从三十八开始。屏幕上柱高可以变很多，但同一次记录的人数不能因为画法而变化。柱形图用零基线时，柱高适合直接表达完整数量。

### 例 1 · 四个人的差，不是人数三倍

1. 读原数值：**A=40 人，B=44 人**。
2. 实际差 **44−40=4 人**；若 B 真是 A 三倍，应有 120 人，而不是四十四。
3. 若纵轴截到 **38**，两柱画出的高度分别表示 **40−38=2** 和 **44−38=6** 个刻度单位。
4. 六的确是二的三倍，但比较的是“高出三十八的部分”，不是完整人数。
5. 切回零基线，原数值仍是四十与四十四；重新解释为什么原先的视觉印象会夸大相对差异。

截断图不是永远不能出现。例如有些图要细看很小的变化，但必须清楚标出截断、刻度和目的，读者不能再直接把柱高比当总量比。本工作台保留轴断裂符号与明确提示，并始终标出原人数，让人能检查画法带来的影响。

### 例 2 · 局部时间不能代表全年

1. 海报只展示最后两天 **40→44 人**，标题却说“全年人数暴增三倍”。
2. 两个原数值只支持“这两次记录相差四人”，不支持三倍。
3. 时间范围只有两天，其他月份可能变化不同；需要完整时段或说明为何选择这两天。
4. 还需核对是否一直数同样的人群、是否换了问题或单位，以及有无漏报和缺失。
5. 若邀请五十人而只有三十人回答，应另报 **50−30=20 人无回应**，不能把三十人的意见说成五十人一致意见。

“今天人数”和“最喜欢的方式”不同，“次数”和“人数”不同。两幅图可能标题相似，却统计了不同对象。一个图画得很整齐，也不保证收集过程有代表性。小组志愿者只能支持该组已回答者的描述，不能自动推广全校或全新西兰。

工作台先预测切换坐标轴的效果，再选结论。正确结论应同时说出数据依据和范围：例如“这次记录中 B 比 A 多四人”。若选择三倍，回看原数值；若推广所有人，检查样本如何选取。改画法后反馈会清空，需要对当前图重新核对。

还可以用另一个例子练习：原数值为 28 与 35，纵轴从 25 起，柱高是三与十，但实际差为 **35−28=7**。**带走一个发现：** 让数值、标尺和样本一起支持结论。家长可要求指出一句明确事实，再说明图里还缺什么资料，不仅说“这张图骗人”。

## Why can identical data look very different?

A chart can reveal relationships while also encouraging an impression unsupported by data. Check whom the title describes, the time window and units. Then inspect the axis origin, tick intervals and original values. Only then ask whether the claim follows from the observations.

Our fictional groups contain **40 people in A and 44 in B**. The data remain fixed while the vertical origin switches between zero and thirty-eight. Bar heights may look dramatically different, but recorded counts cannot change with the drawing. A zero baseline lets bar heights represent whole quantities directly.

### Example 1 · Four more is not triple the people

1. Read original counts: **A=40, B=44 people**.
2. Actual difference is **44−40=4 people**. Triple forty would be 120, not forty-four.
3. An axis truncated at **38** makes the displayed heights represent **40−38=2** and **44−38=6** scale units.
4. Six is three times two, but these are the portions above thirty-eight, not whole counts.
5. Restore zero. Counts remain forty and forty-four. Explain how truncation exaggerated the relative visual difference.

A truncated scale can sometimes help inspect small changes, provided its origin, break and purpose are explicit. Readers must not then equate bar-height ratios with total-quantity ratios. The workshop displays an axis-break symbol, clear warning and original counts so the effect can be checked.

### Example 2 · Two days do not establish a whole year

1. A poster displays only the final two days, **40→44 people**, but claims “Tripled across the year”.
2. The original values support a four-person difference between these observations, not a threefold count.
3. Other months may differ. Seek the full time window or an explanation of the selection.
4. Check that populations, questions and units remain consistent, and whether records are missing.
5. If fifty were invited and thirty answered, report **50−30=20 missing responses**. Thirty responses do not establish fifty unanimous views.

Today’s count differs from a favourite method, and trips differ from people. Similar-looking chart titles may conceal different measurement objects. A tidy graph does not ensure representative collection. Volunteers describe their responding group, not automatically the school or New Zealand.

Predict the effect of changing the workshop axis before choosing a claim. A supported statement includes both evidence and scope: “B has four more people in this record.” For a triple-count claim, reread values; for a claim about everyone, inspect sampling. Changing the drawing clears feedback so the current display is considered again.

Try original values 28 and 35 on an axis starting at 25. Displayed heights are three and ten, but the actual difference is **35−28=7**. **Takeaway:** values, scales and sample scope jointly support conclusions. A parent can ask for one precise fact and one missing piece of evidence, rather than accepting only “the graph is misleading”.
