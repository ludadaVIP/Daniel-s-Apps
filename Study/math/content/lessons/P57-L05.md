## 先算需要多少，再算怎么买够

给一次家庭活动做纸上食谱计划。假想四份需要 **200 g 面粉、300 mL 牛奶**。商店只卖整包：面粉 **500 g/NZ$2.40**，牛奶 **1000 mL/NZ$3.00**。从零库存开始，价格完全假想且无额外费用；不需要购买或实际烹饪。

这项任务有两个阶段。第一阶段按同样配方算实际用量，份数改变时每份用量保持不变。第二阶段按包装规格买够，再核算付款、预算和余料。用掉的量不总等于买到的量，剩下的食材也不自动退回钱。

### 例 1 · 十份用量与整包付款

1. 四份用面粉二百克，所以每份 **200÷4=50 g**；牛奶每份 **300÷4=75 mL**。
2. 十份需面粉 **50×10=500 g**，牛奶 **75×10=750 mL**。
3. 面粉一包五百克刚好；牛奶仍要买一整盒一千毫升，不能只付四分之三盒的钱。
4. 付款用整数分核对：**240+300=540 分=NZ$5.40**。
5. 若预算 NZ$6，剩 **600−540=60 分=NZ$0.60**；牛奶余 **1000−750=250 mL**，面粉无剩余。

两种剩余单位不同：钱是新西兰元，面粉是克，牛奶是毫升。不能把六角钱与二百五十毫升相加，也不能把余料的估值当购物时已退的钱。若真实任务已有库存，先扣库存再计划采购；本题明确库存为零。

### 例 2 · 十二份跨过一个包装边界

1. 十二份面粉需 **50×12=600 g**，奶需 **75×12=900 mL**。
2. 一包面粉只有五百克，缺一百克；只卖整包就要买两包，一盒奶仍足够。
3. 总费用 **2×240+300=780 分=NZ$7.80**。
4. NZ$8 预算剩二角；若只有 NZ$7，则**短缺八角**，应说明“不足”，不能说“剩负八角”。
5. 余料面粉 **1000−600=400 g**、牛奶 **1000−900=100 mL**；买到两包并不表示必须把一千克全倒进食谱。

整包数要向上买足，不用四舍五入。例如六百除五百为一点二包，四舍五入成一包会不够；一又五分之一包也不符合整包销售条件。先验证“购买总量至少等于所需量”，再问是否有多买了一包。这同时检查够用和必要性。

比较四份、十份、十二份：四份与十份都只需一包面粉、一盒奶，付款都是 NZ$5.40，但余料不同；十二份才需要第二包面粉，付款跳到 NZ$7.80。食谱用量按份数成比例，整包采购费用却有台阶，不能把付款也一律乘同一个倍数。

工作台先显示四份到目标份数的关系，把用量留成问号。自己完成计划后再核对数量、包数与费用；修改份数或预算会清空旧核对结果。预算不足时明确显示短缺的正金额，不把负数叫余额。

**家庭挑战：** 在纸上列三种份数的用量、买到的量、花费、余料和 NZ$8 余额。每一列标单位，再解释为什么份数增加不总马上加钱。家长核查每份不变、整包够用、总价和预算关系；不要求唯一一种排版。出口题换为八份，独立说出需要与剩余。

## First calculate requirements, then buy enough

Plan a fictional family activity on paper. Four servings need **200 g flour and 300 mL milk**. Only whole packs are sold: flour **500 g/NZ$2.40**, milk **1000 mL/NZ$3.00**. Start with no stock; prices are fictional and no extra charges apply. No purchase or cooking is required.

There are two stages. First scale actual ingredient requirements while keeping the recipe unchanged. Then use pack sizes to buy enough and check payment, budget and leftovers. Quantity consumed need not equal quantity bought, and unused ingredients do not automatically produce a refund.

### Example 1 · Ten servings and whole-pack payment

1. Each serving needs **200÷4=50 g flour** and **300÷4=75 mL milk**.
2. Ten need **50×10=500 g flour** and **75×10=750 mL milk**.
3. One flour pack is exact. Milk still requires a whole 1000 mL carton; paying for three quarters of a carton is not permitted by the stated conditions.
4. In integer cents, **240+300=540 cents=NZ$5.40**.
5. From NZ$6, **600−540=60 cents=NZ$0.60** remains. Milk left is **1000−750=250 mL**, with no flour left.

Keep leftover units separate: dollars, grams and millilitres. Money and milk cannot be added. An estimated value of leftover ingredients is not money already refunded at checkout. Real tasks with existing stock would subtract it before planning purchases; this problem specifies zero stock.

### Example 2 · Twelve servings cross a pack boundary

1. Twelve need **50×12=600 g flour** and **75×12=900 mL milk**.
2. One 500 g flour pack is short by one hundred grams, so buy two whole packs. One milk carton remains enough.
3. Cost is **2×240+300=780 cents=NZ$7.80**.
4. An NZ$8 budget leaves twenty cents. An NZ$7 budget has an **eighty-cent shortfall**, which should be labelled insufficient.
5. Leftovers are **1000−600=400 g flour** and **1000−900=100 mL milk**. Buying one thousand grams does not mean putting all of it into the recipe.

Whole-pack counts must cover requirements. Six hundred divided by five hundred is 1.2 packs, but rounding to one is insufficient, while buying one and one fifth violates whole-pack conditions. Verify purchased quantity is at least the required quantity, then check whether a pack was unnecessary.

Compare four, ten and twelve servings. Four and ten both buy one flour pack and one milk carton, costing NZ$5.40, with different leftovers. Twelve require the second flour pack and cost NZ$7.80. Recipe consumption is proportional to servings; whole-pack payment increases in steps, so it does not always share that same multiplier.

The workshop initially leaves ingredient requirements as question marks. Complete a plan before checking quantities, packs and payment. Changing servings or budget clears the old result. Insufficient budgets display a positive shortfall explicitly.

**Family challenge:** tabulate required and purchased quantities, cost, leftovers and remaining NZ$8 budget for three serving counts. Label every unit and explain why more servings need not immediately cost more. A parent checks the fixed recipe, sufficient packs and budget relationship rather than a unique layout. Transfer the reasoning independently to eight servings in the exit ticket.
