# 黎曼和的直观表示

曲线下的区域不总是长方形。先把区间分成小格，在每格选一个采样点，用该点函数值作矩形高度。每个乘积f(ξᵢ)Δxᵢ再相加得到黎曼和。高度可以是负数，乘积便带符号；本课先用连续多项式观察。

## 三个对象不要混淆

分割给出边界x₀<x₁<…<xₙ，每格宽Δxᵢ=xᵢ−xᵢ₋₁。采样点ξᵢ必须在该格内，可取左端点、右端点或中点。网格宽度是全部Δxᵢ的最大值，不是格数，也不是宽度平均值。

给定同一格，函数最小值乘宽形成下贡献，最大值乘宽形成上贡献。所有下贡献之和≤任何合法采样和≤上贡献之和。若函数连续在闭区间上，它有界且均匀连续；网格宽度趋0时上下和之差趋0，因此合法黎曼和趋向同一积分。有限的几张图只能核查当前情形，不证明这个一般定理。

### 例1：同一曲线，三种采样

取f(x)=x²，区间[0,2]，等分4格，各宽1/2。左端点0、1/2、1、3/2给和(0+1/4+1+9/4)/2=7/4。右端点1/2、1、3/2、2给和(1/4+1+9/4+4)/2=15/4。

中点1/4、3/4、5/4、7/4给和(1+9+25+49)/32=21/8。精确积分为8/3，所以三个有限和都不精确；中点误差21/8−8/3=−1/24。

因为x²在此区间递增，左和恰为下和、右和恰为上和。但“左端点总低估”不是普遍规则：对4−x²这样的递减函数，角色会交换；对(x−1)²，还要检查格内最小值，不能只比较区间两个总端点。

### 例2：不等宽就要逐格加权

仍取x²，但分割边界0、1/4、1、2。宽度1/4、3/4、1，中点1/8、5/8、3/2。三项贡献分别为1/256、75/256、9/4，总和652/256=163/64。最大格宽为1，不是1/4，也不是2/3。

若错误地统一乘1/2，得到的是另一组并不对应此分割的乘积。正确程序和纸笔都应保留“这格的宽”与“这格的高”的配对。把第一格继续细分无数次但保留最后宽1的格，格数虽增大，网格宽度仍至少1，不能援引网格宽度趋0的定理。

## 用反例限制直觉

中点对一次函数精确，可以由对称性或梯形面积说明；这不推出它对所有曲线精确。x²的中点和低估，而4−x²在同区间、同分割的中点和高估。误差方向还依赖函数形状，不能只看采样名字。

工作台可以切换函数、采样与分割。图中矩形保留真实宽度，点标明采样位置，核验表逐项列宽、点、高与乘积，再给上下和、精确积分及误差。改变采样点不会改变既定分割的网格宽度，因此该预测应保留。

家长重点问“哪一格决定最大宽？”和“你声称的精确或低估是否有条件？”出口解释应能把有限核验、一般定理和假设分开。多画一些矩形可以提供证据，但极限结论需要控制整个分割的最大宽。

# Visual Riemann sums

Divide an interval into cells, choose a tag in each cell, and form a rectangle with height f at that tag. Sum f(ξᵢ)Δxᵢ. Heights and products may be negative. Here continuous polynomials make the construction visible and exactly checkable.

## Distinguish partition, tags and mesh

A partition consists of increasing boundaries x₀<x₁<…<xₙ. Its widths are Δxᵢ=xᵢ−xᵢ₋₁. A tag belongs to its own cell; left, right and midpoint tags are specific choices. Mesh is the maximum width, not the number of cells or their average width.

Within each cell, minimum height times width gives a lower contribution and maximum height times width an upper contribution. Every tagged sum lies between the resulting lower and upper sums. A continuous function on a closed bounded interval is uniformly continuous, so their difference tends to zero as mesh tends to zero. Tagged sums then approach one integral. Finite displays check examples rather than prove this general theorem.

### Example 1: Three rules for the same curve

For f(x)=x² on[0,2], take four equal widths1/2. Left tags0,1/2,1,3/2 give7/4. Right tags1/2,1,3/2,2 give15/4. Midpoint tags1/4,3/4,5/4,7/4 give(1+9+25+49)/32=21/8.

The exact integral is8/3, so none of these finite sums is exact. Midpoint error, defined as sum minus integral, is−1/24. Because x² increases here, the left sum equals the lower sum and the right sum the upper sum.

This ordering is conditional. For decreasing4−x² on the same interval, left and right estimates reverse their roles. For(x−1)², a cell containing one has an interior minimum that must be included in its bound. Endpoint comparisons alone are insufficient when monotonicity changes inside a cell.

### Example 2: Weight unequal widths separately

Keep x² but use boundaries 0,1/4,1,2. Widths are1/4,3/4,1; midpoint tags are1/8,5/8,3/2. Products are1/256,75/256,9/4 and their sum is163/64. Mesh is one, the largest width.

Multiplying every sampled height by 1/2 would describe a different construction. Preserve each actual width-height pairing. Refining only the first cell while retaining the final width-one cell increases the cell count without making mesh tend to zero. The convergence theorem therefore cannot be invoked on the basis of count alone.

## Limit a claim with counterexamples

Midpoint sums are exact for affine functions, as symmetry or trapezoid area shows. This does not extend to every curve. For x² the midpoint sum underestimates; for4−x² on the same interval and partition it overestimates. The tag rule alone does not determine the sign of error.

The workbench shows actual rectangle widths and tagged points. Its audit lists each width, tag, height and product before giving lower and upper sums, exact integral and signed error. Changing tags leaves a fixed partition’s mesh unchanged, so that prediction remains valid. Explain which cell controls mesh and state the conditions behind any exactness or error claim. More rectangles are useful evidence, but a limit argument requires control of the largest gap throughout the interval.
