# 方差与标准差

均值告诉我们分布中心，却不足以描述波动。方差用概率加权的平方偏差：Var(X)=E[(X−μ)²]。标准差σ为其非负平方根。这里讨论一个已知理论分布，不是在用样本估计未知总体；因此不引入样本估计里的n−1分母。

## 为何先平方再加权

∑(x−μ)p=E(X)−μ=0。有符号偏差会抵消，无论分布窄还是宽，都得到零，不能衡量离散程度。平方后每项非负，离中心更远的值贡献更大，再按其发生概率加权。

展开平方也有Var(X)=E(X²)−[E(X)]²，可作独立核验。不能把E(X²)误写成[E(X)]²；两者的差恰是我们要保留的波动。若X以米计，平方偏差和方差用平方米，标准差回到米。

### 例1：同均值，不同波动

分布A取0或2，各1/2，均值1。两个偏差−1、1，平方均1，所以方差1、标准差1。

分布B取−1或3，各半，均值也1。偏差−2、2，平方均4，所以方差4、标准差2。两种均值相同，但B更分散；有符号偏差均为零无法区分，平方偏差则区分得很清楚。

两次公平硬币正面数的分布0、1、2带权1/4、1/2、1/4，也有均值1，但方差1/4+0+1/4=1/2，标准差√(1/2)=√2/2。不能把三个取值按等权计算，也不能直接把方差1/2称作标准差。

### 例2：平移与负缩放

对分布A定义Y=−2X+7。Y取7或3，各半，均值5。相对新均值的偏差2与−2，方差4、标准差2。平移7移动中心，不额外增加方差；负缩放反转方向，却不产生负标准差。

一般E(Y)=aμ+b，Y−E(Y)=a(X−μ)，故Var(Y)=a²Var(X)，SD(Y)=|a|SD(X)。这由偏差关系直接推得，不需要逐个例子猜规则。

若a=0，则Y恒为b，方差和标准差都0。一般方差0意味着以概率1取均值，即所有正概率质量集中在该值；表中保留的零概率槽不改变结论。这种分布仍合法，但不能用σ作分母求z。

## 精确值与单位一起保留

工作台可比较不同原分布，再选择正、负、零缩放和平移。先预测新方差、标准差与均值，再看逐项偏差表。只改平移时方差与标准差答案不应丢；改缩放则按平方与绝对值规则复核。

标准差输入支持sqrt(2)/2等精确根式，也接受确实等价的形式；不把截断小数当精确根。平方单位不是几何面积测量，而来自原量偏差的平方，解释时需要说明这一点。

家长可追问哪个运算防止正负抵消、为什么负a要取绝对值。出口说明应覆盖理论概率权重、单位、平移和缩放，并分清方差0与资料缺失：一个是确定分布的结果，另一个是尚未知道分布，不能都写成零。

# Variance and standard deviation

A mean locates the centre but does not determine spread. Distribution variance is Var(X)=E[(X−μ)²], and standard deviation is its nonnegative square root. We are computing from a known theoretical distribution, not estimating an unknown population from a sample; no n−1 adjustment belongs here.

## Square before weighting

Weighted signed deviations sum to E(X)−μ=0. Positive and negative distances cancel for both narrow and broad distributions. Squaring prevents that cancellation, and probability weights retain how often each deviation occurs.

Expanding gives Var(X)=E(X²)−[E(X)]², an independent check. These two expectations are not generally equal. For metre-valued X, variance has units m² and standard deviation metres.

### Example 1: Equal centres, different spreads

Distribution A takes zero or two, each with mass 1/2. Its mean is one; deviations are−1 and one, so variance and SD are both one.

Distribution B takes−1 or three with equal masses. Its mean is also one, but deviations−2 and two give variance four and SD two. Their signed average deviations both vanish, while squared deviations distinguish their spreads.

The head count from two fair independent coins also has mean one. Its masses1/4,1/2,1/4 at 0,1,2 give variance 1/2 and SD√2/2. Do not treat the three distinct values equally or confuse variance 1/2 with its square root.

### Example 2: Translation and negative scaling

For distribution A, let Y=−2X+7. Values become seven and three, each equally likely, with mean five. Deviations from five are two and−2, so variance is four and SD two. Translation moves the centre without adding spread. A negative scale reverses order without producing negative SD.

In general, E(Y)=aμ+b and Y−E(Y)=a(X−μ). Squaring and averaging gives Var(Y)=a²Var(X), and taking the nonnegative root gives SD(Y)=|a|SD(X). This derivation explains the rule rather than relying on examples alone.

When a=0, Y is constant b with zero variance. More generally, variance zero concentrates probability one at the mean; retained zero-mass table slots do not change this. Such a distribution is valid, but its zero SD cannot be used as a divisor for a z-score.

## Preserve exactness and units

The workbench compares distributions and affine transforms with positive, negative or zero scale. Predict mean, variance and SD, then inspect exact weighted squared deviations. Translation alone preserves valid spread predictions.

Use exact radicals such as sqrt(2)/2. A truncated decimal is not the same exact number. Squared units here arise from squaring deviations, rather than measuring a geometric surface.

Explain the role of weights, why squaring avoids cancellation, and why absolute scale appears in SD. Distinguish zero variance from missing information: one describes a specified distribution, while the other does not establish any variance at all.
