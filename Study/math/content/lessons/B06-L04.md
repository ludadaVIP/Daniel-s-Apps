## 信息有没有用，要看这次问什么

应用题可能有多余资料，也可能少了必要资料。这是两种不同情况：多余资料可以不用，保留必要关系仍能确定答案；缺少必要资料时，不能编一个看似合理 的数来补。准确说出“目前不能确定，还缺……”也是有依据的数学回答。

判断信息时先说未知是什么。求本子总价，要用本数与每本价钱；求买后余钱，还要用预算。相同的一段故事换了问题，所需数据也会改变，不是某个数字永远有用或永远没用。

### 例 1 · 只买笔记本的余钱

1. 预算 **NZ$30**，买 **4 本**笔记本，每本 **NZ$5**。另有每包 NZ$3 的铅笔，但这次不买。
2. 求余钱需要预算、笔记本本数、笔记本单价。
3. 总价 **4×5=NZ$20**；余钱 **30−20=NZ$10**。
4. 铅笔价格也是钱数，但它属于没购买的商品，不能因为同单位就减进去。
5. 若只问笔记本总价，预算三十元也不必使用；答案仍为二十元。

### 例 2 · 同一资料却不能求质量

1. 仍只有四本笔记本与每本五元的价格，问题改成“四本总质量多少？”
2. 四本是数量，五元是单价；**4×5=20** 表示元，不表示千克。
3. 还需每本质量，或直接给四本的合计质量。当前资料没有这些关系。
4. 因此结论是**信息不足，不能确定总质量**，不是二十千克，也不是四千克。
5. 不能因商店通常卖相似本子就猜重量；新资料到来后，才可继续计算。

### 不要因为数字很大就删掉

地图印于 2018 年、办公室九点开门；买两张每张六元的地图，总价 **2×6=NZ$12**，印刷年与时间不用。它们是否有用仍取决于问题：若问地图印了几年，年份会很重要。我们的判断只针对当前目标，不能说日期永远是无关信息。

同样，“铅笔每包六支、每包三元、买四包”若问支数，用 **4×6=24 支**，价格不用；若问花钱，则用 **4×3=NZ$12**，每包支数可以不用。数值相同或单位不同都不能独自决定相关性，要说明数量关系。

工作台提供余钱、总价和总质量三个目标。每换一个目标就重新选信息。选择检查不会把“所有卡都选了”当成充分理由；质量题明确提示缺少的量。不要把未知质量误写为零：零质量与没有给质量不是一回事。

假想购物资料只是练习关系，没有实际采购要求。解释时可以说：“我用这两项，因为它们决定总价；这项没有进入关系；如果改问另一种量，还需新的资料。”这比把数字划掉却说不出理由更清楚。

**带走一个发现：** 我允许不用多余数字，也允许指出信息不足；选择来自目标和关系，而不是把所有数字用完。

## Relevance depends on the current question

A story may contain extra data or lack necessary data. These are different situations. Extra facts can be left unused while the required relationship still determines an answer. Missing necessary information cannot be replaced by an invented plausible number. Explaining “this cannot yet be determined; we need…” is a supported mathematical conclusion.

Name the unknown first. Notebook cost needs count and unit price; money remaining also needs budget. Changing the question can change which facts matter within the same story.

### Example 1 · Remaining money when buying only notebooks

1. Budget **NZ$30**, **4 notebooks** at **NZ$5 each**, plus pencil packs priced NZ$3 that are not bought.
2. Remaining money needs budget, notebook count and notebook unit price.
3. Cost is **4×5=NZ$20**; remaining money is **30−20=NZ$10**.
4. Pencil price also uses dollars, but describes unbought goods. Sharing a unit does not make it relevant.
5. Asking only for notebook cost would leave the budget unused, with cost still twenty dollars.

### Example 2 · The same facts cannot determine mass

1. Keep four notebooks and their five-dollar unit price, but ask for total mass.
2. Four is a count and five is a price. **4×5=20** represents dollars, not kilograms.
3. Mass per notebook or total mass is needed, and no such relationship is supplied.
4. Conclude **insufficient information: total mass cannot be determined**, rather than twenty or four kilograms.
5. Similar-looking products do not justify assuming a mass. Calculation can continue when suitable information arrives.

### Do not discard numbers merely because they look unusual

Maps printed in 2018 and an office opening at nine do not enter the cost of two maps at six dollars each: **2×6=NZ$12**. A question about years since printing would make the date relevant. This judgement belongs to the current goal, not a rule that dates never matter.

For packs of six pencils costing three dollars, four packs contain **4×6=24 pencils**, without using price. Their cost is instead **4×3=NZ$12**, without needing pencils per pack. Explain the relationship rather than decide relevance from value or unit alone.

Switch the workshop between remaining money, total cost and total mass, selecting information afresh. Selecting every card does not establish sufficiency; the mass question identifies missing data. Unknown mass is not zero mass: absent information differs from a determined zero.

These fictional purchases require no real shopping. Say which facts determine the goal, which are outside the relationship, and which additional information another question would need.

**Takeaway:** allow extra numbers to remain unused and necessary missing information to remain unknown. Selection follows the goal and relationship, not a requirement to use every number.
