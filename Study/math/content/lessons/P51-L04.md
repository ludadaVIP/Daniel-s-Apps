## 分类要有因数证据

大于一的整数，如果恰有两个不同正因数——一与它自己——就是质数。如果还有其他正因数，就是合数。这两类的定义都要求大于一；零与一都不属于这两类。

“非平凡因数”指除了一和数本身之外的正因数。找到这样的因数，已经足以证明大于一的数是合数。反过来，某一次试除有余数，只说明那一个试除数不适合，不能证明所有其他因数都不存在。

### 例 1 · 一与二的边界

1. 一的正因数只有 **1**。
2. “一与自己”在这里是同一个数，不是两个不同因数，而且一不大于一。
3. 因此一既不是质数，也不是合数。
4. 二的不同正因数为 **1、2**，恰有两个且二大于一，所以二是质数。
5. 二还是唯一偶质数：任何大于二的偶数都有因数二，而二既不是一也不是这个较大的数。

### 例 2 · 四十九不是由一次失败判质数

1. 四十九试除二有余数，只说明二不整除四十九。
2. 它是奇数，也不能因此确定为质数。
3. **49=7×7**，七是非平凡因数，所以四十九是合数。
4. 不同正因数为 **1、7、49**，七不重计，共三个。
5. 一个正确非平凡因数足以否定质数；一次失败试除没有同样强的证明力。

### 奇偶不是分类规则

九是奇数，但 9=3×3，有因数三，是合数；十三只有一与十三两个正因数，是质数。两个数都奇，分类不同。二则是偶质数，因此“奇数一定质、偶数一定合”两边都有反例。

0 不满足大于一的前提，不是质数也不是合数。它的整除情况与正整数的有限因数表不同，工作台不会给零贴一个空列表后声称“没有因数”。先看定义适用范围，避免把未使用的列表当成数学结论。

二十一可以排成三行七个，给出 21=3×7；三与七都大于一且小于二十一，说明它有非平凡因数。图形是依据明确数量生成的，不能只因画出来像“长方形”就判合数：1×13 也是长方形排列，但只有平凡配对。

工作台先选待分类数与试除数，再核对商与余数。没有装入完整组的计数格单独保留，不藏掉余量。最后可展开全部不同正因数与分类，用完整证据核查预测。分类提示只是对给定整数的数学检查，不给孩子贴能力标签。

**带走一个发现：** 我先检验大于一，再看不同正因数；找到非平凡因数可以证明合数，不能用奇偶或一次失败代替质数证据。

## Classification needs factor evidence

An integer greater than one with exactly two distinct positive factors, one and itself, is prime. Other positive factors make it composite. Both definitions require greater than one, excluding zero and one.

A nontrivial factor is a positive factor other than one or the number itself. Finding one proves an integer greater than one composite. A failed trial division only rules out that particular divisor, not every possible factor.

### Example 1 · The boundary between one and two

1. One has only positive factor **1**.
2. “One and itself” refers to the same factor, not two distinct factors, and one is not greater than one.
3. One is therefore neither prime nor composite.
4. Two has distinct factors **1 and 2**, exactly two, and exceeds one, so it is prime.
5. Two is the only even prime: every larger even integer has nontrivial factor two.

### Example 2 · A failed trial does not make forty-nine prime

1. Forty-nine leaves a remainder when divided by two, excluding two as a factor.
2. Being odd does not establish primality.
3. **49=7×7** gives nontrivial factor seven, proving forty-nine composite.
4. Distinct factors are **1,7,49**, three altogether without duplicating seven.
5. One nontrivial factor disproves primality; one failed trial does not provide equally strong positive evidence.

### Parity is not the classification rule

Nine is odd but 9=3×3 makes it composite. Thirteen has only one and thirteen as factors and is prime. Both are odd but differ in classification; two provides an even-prime counterexample.

Zero fails the greater-than-one premise and belongs to neither class. Its divisibility situation differs from a finite positive-integer factor list. The workshop does not label an unused empty list “zero has no factors”. Check a definition’s domain before interpreting a missing display as a conclusion.

Twenty-one in three rows of seven gives 21=3×7, with nontrivial factors. The picture follows stated counts; merely looking rectangular cannot prove compositeness. A 1×13 arrangement is also rectangular but supplies only a trivial pair.

Choose an integer and trial divisor, then check quotient and remainder. Counters outside full groups remain visible rather than disappear. Finally reveal all distinct positive factors and classification to check the prediction against complete evidence. This is a mathematical check on a given integer, not a label for a learner’s ability.

**Takeaway:** test greater than one and distinct factor counts. A nontrivial factor proves composite; parity or one failed trial cannot replace evidence of primality.
