# 绝对值与距离

## 先说清楚在问哪种量

点的位置可以带正负号，有向变化也可以带正负号，距离却一定非负。`−3/2` 描述零左侧一点五单位的位置；`|−3/2|=3/2` 描述它到零的距离。竖线是绝对值符号，不是把一个数随便改成另一个数的装饰。

绝对值可以等于零，所以“绝对值总是正数”不准确，正确说法是非负。`|a|=0` 当且仅当 a 为零。两个数绝对值相同，也未必是同一个数，例如 `|−3/2|=|3/2|`，但两点在零的两侧。给任何距离都保留同一单位长度，不因为某边的数是负的就把刻度反着缩放。

### 例 1：到零距离与两点距离不同

A 在 `−3/2`，B 在 `1/2`。

到零距离分别 `|A|=3/2`、`|B|=1/2`。从 A 到 B 的有向变化是 `B−A=1/2−(−3/2)=2`；两点距离为这个变化的绝对值，仍是二。反向从 B 到 A，有向变化变为 `A−B=−2`，但两点没有换位置，距离依旧二。

可以跨零分段检查：从 A 到零走一点五单位，再到 B 半单位，共两单位。这里绝对值之和可用，是因为两点在零的不同侧；不是任意两点都能这样算。

### 例 2：同侧不能重复计路程

A 改到 `−5/4`，B 改到 `−1/4`。两点都在左侧。

从 A 到 B，变化 `−1/4−(−5/4)=4/4=1`；反向变化是负一。两点距离均为一。若把到零距离 `5/4+1/4=3/2` 相加，就把零附近那段来回重复计算，得到的不是点间最短间隔。

同侧可用到零距离之差的绝对值，这里 `5/4−1/4=1`。更统一的关系是 `两点距离=|B−A|`，它也适用于跨零、同侧、某点为零和两点重合。不要先假设顺序，再把一个可能为负的差直接写成距离。

## 分母改变时，单位不变

三分之一与六分之五同在零右侧，把三分之一写为六分之二，距离是 `5/6−2/6=1/2`。改为负三分之二与正六分之五时，距离 `|5/6−(−4/6)|=9/6=3/2`。通分让每小份同样大小，不意味着原来一单位跟着改变。

在工作台移动 A、B 的分子，再切共同分母。每单位会等分成对应的小格，图注保留精确位置；如果两个值能约成更简分母，图可以使用足够表示这些点的较粗等分。原单位间距始终相同。两点重合时合并一个标记并列出 A、B，距离零，不画一个假的非零移动箭头。

## 用反向检查理解

在输入前分别说：“位置是……，从 A 到 B 的变化是……，距离是……。”交换 A、B 后，再说明哪些变、哪些不变。对重合点，变化与距离都为零；这不等于没有定义。家长可用同侧的两点质疑“所有绝对值都相加”，也可用一正一负同距点质疑“相同绝对值就是相同数”。能举出并解释反例，比简单说“去掉负号”更能表明理解。

# Absolute value as distance

## Name the quantity before calculating

Position may be signed, and directed change may be signed, but distance is non-negative. The number `−3/2` locates a point one and a half units left of zero; `|−3/2|=3/2` measures its distance to zero. Absolute-value bars specify a quantity, not a decorative instruction to alter signs arbitrarily.

Absolute value may equal zero. “Always positive” is therefore inaccurate; use non-negative. The equation `|a|=0` holds exactly when a is zero. Equal absolute values need not mean equal numbers: `|−3/2|=|3/2|`, but those positions differ. Keep a common unit on both sides of the origin.

### Example 1: zero-distance and pairwise distance

Let A be `−3/2` and B be `1/2`. Their distances to zero are `3/2` and `1/2`. Directed change from A to B is `B−A=1/2−(−3/2)=2`; pairwise distance is its absolute value, two.

Reversing the trip gives directed change `A−B=−2`. The separation remains two because the endpoints have not changed. Check by splitting at zero: one and a half units from A to zero, then a half-unit to B. Adding magnitudes works here because the points lie on opposite sides, not because it is a universal distance formula.

### Example 2: avoid counting overlap twice

Move A to `−5/4` and B to `−1/4`, both left of zero. Forward directed change is `−1/4−(−5/4)=1`; reverse change is negative one. Each distance is one.

Adding their distances to zero gives `5/4+1/4=3/2`, counting an overlapping segment twice. On this same side, subtract the magnitudes: `5/4−1/4=1`. The unified relationship is pairwise distance = `|B−A|`. It handles same-side, across-zero, zero-endpoint and coincident cases without assuming an endpoint order.

## Preserve units when changing denominators

Between `1/3=2/6` and `5/6`, separation is `3/6=1/2`. Between `−2/3=−4/6` and `5/6`, separation is `9/6=3/2`. Common denominators make the parts comparable without changing the original unit length.

In the workshop, change both point numerators and the common denominator. Unit distances stay fixed. Reduced fractions may permit coarser subdivisions than the selected denominator, but each point retains its exact position. Coincident A and B share one marker; zero separation does not create a false movement arrow.

## Check by reversing endpoints

State position, directed change and distance separately before entering answers. Swap A and B and explain which quantities change sign. Coincident points have a defined zero change and zero distance. A same-side example refutes the claim that all magnitudes should be added; opposite points refute the claim that equal absolute values guarantee identical numbers. Explain the counterexample rather than relying only on a remove-the-minus slogan.
