## 折线连接的是有时间的观察

同一地点的温度会变化。把每次测量的“时刻、温度”成对记录，先读表再作图。横轴表示从开始后经过的小时，纵轴表示摄氏温度 °C。每个圆点是一条实际观察；连接线帮助看变化，但不等于每一个中间时刻都测过。

本课用假想资料：开始 12 °C，一小时后 16 °C，三小时后 14 °C，六小时后 20 °C。时刻从同一起点算，0、1、3、6 是经过小时数，不是四个相邻的整点。整张图必须沿用同一时间标尺和同一温度标尺。

### 例 1 · 首尾变化与分段变化

1. 开始为 **12 °C**，第 1 小时为 **16 °C**，升高 **16−12=4 °C**。
2. 第 1 到第 3 小时，从十六降到十四，降低量 **16−14=2 °C**；若用带方向的变化表示，是 −2 °C。
3. 第 3 到第 6 小时，从十四到二十，升高 **20−14=6 °C**。
4. 最后比开始高 **20−12=8 °C**。中间有下降，不代表总变化就是下降。
5. 对照三段有方向变化：**4−2+6=8 °C**，与首尾差一致。

注意问题问的是“多少摄氏度”还是“经过几小时”。第 3 到第 6 小时经过三小时，温度升高六度，这两个数分别回答时间和温度问题，不能混用。若问降低量，本课明确填正数二；若问带符号变化则是负二，先读清题意。

### 例 2 · 不等时间要有不等距离

1. 把 **0、1、3、6 小时**放在同一横轴，间隔依次为 **1、2、3 小时**。
2. 一小时若画同样长的一小段，则 1→3 要画两小段，3→6 要画三小段。
3. 不能只因有四条记录就等距摆四个点，那会把不等时间误画成相等。
4. 第 1 到第 3 小时经过 **3−1=2 小时**，需要按真实时间比较变化过程。
5. 若纵轴上限由 24 改成 40 °C，点的屏幕高度会改变，但第 1 小时的记录仍是 **16 °C**。

工作台提供表格与图形，换标尺后先回到表中核对。更陡的线段不一定只是温度变化更大，也可能因为画面时间尺度不同；同图也要考虑实际经过时长。暂时不需要背速度公式，先把横纵两种量读清。

如果第 3 小时没有测到，要写“缺测”，保留断开。零度是一个具体测量值，不是资料缺失的代号。即使知道一小时和六小时的温度，中间也可能先升再降、突然变化或保持一段时间；不能用连接直线宣称第 3 小时确切是多少。直线插值只能是额外假设下的估计，需要明确说明。

最后一段上升也不保证下一次继续上升。图形描述已经记录的观察，预测未来需要新证据或有依据的模型。**带走一个发现：** 先读时间与单位，再用两个观察值求变化；缺测保持未知。家长请孩子指出哪些是真正观察点，再听其解释线段的限制。

## A line joins observations that have times

Record each temperature together with its time, then read the table before plotting. The horizontal axis shows hours elapsed since a common start; the vertical axis shows temperature in °C. Each dot is an observation. Connecting lines help reveal change, but do not mean every intervening moment was measured.

Our fictional readings are 12 °C at the start, 16 °C after one hour, 14 °C after three hours and 20 °C after six hours. Times 0, 1, 3 and 6 are elapsed hours, rather than four consecutive hourly readings. Keep one consistent time scale and temperature scale throughout the chart.

### Example 1 · Endpoint and segment changes

1. From **12 °C** at the start to **16 °C** at hour 1, the rise is **16−12=4 °C**.
2. From hour 1 to hour 3, the reading falls from sixteen to fourteen. The positive fall amount is **16−14=2 °C**; signed change is −2 °C.
3. From hour 3 to hour 6, the rise is **20−14=6 °C**.
4. The final reading is **20−12=8 °C** above the first, despite an intervening fall.
5. Check the signed segment changes: **4−2+6=8 °C**, matching the endpoint difference.

Distinguish “how many degrees” from “how many hours”. Hour 3 to hour 6 lasts three hours and has a six-degree rise. These quantities answer different questions. This lesson explicitly requests a positive fall amount when appropriate; a signed change would use a negative value.

### Example 2 · Unequal times need unequal gaps

1. Times **0, 1, 3 and 6 hours** have successive gaps of **1, 2 and 3 hours**.
2. If one small horizontal length represents an hour, the second gap needs two such lengths and the third three.
3. Four observations do not justify four equally spaced dots. That would falsely display equal elapsed times.
4. From hour 1 to hour 3, **3−1=2 hours** pass. Interpret the process using actual elapsed time.
5. Changing the vertical maximum from 24 to 40 °C changes a dot’s screen height, but the hour-1 reading stays **16 °C**.

Use the workshop’s table to check observations after changing scale. A steeper-looking line is not necessarily evidence of a larger change when different charts use different time scales. Even within one chart, consider elapsed duration. No new rate formula is required here; first identify the two quantities being plotted.

If hour 3 was not measured, keep a Missing label and a gap. Zero degrees is a specific value, not a code for absent data. Endpoints do not establish the exact intermediate path: temperature could rise, fall, change suddenly or remain steady. A straight-line interpolation is only an estimate under extra assumptions and must be labelled accordingly.

A final upward segment also does not guarantee the next reading will rise. The chart describes recorded observations; prediction needs further evidence or a justified model. **Takeaway:** read times and units, compare observed values and retain unknown readings as unknown. A parent can ask which points were actually measured and what the connecting lines cannot prove.
