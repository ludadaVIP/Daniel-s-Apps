# 方程的解与检验

## 解是让原等式成立的值

代数式描述关系，方程用等号提出条件。`2x+3=11` 的意思不是“把 x 随意换成数”，而是寻找使左边与右边相等的 x。本课在有理数范围讨论；应用题还要说明允许的值。能使原方程成立的一个值叫一个解，所有允许解组成解集。

检验时保留原方程，用同一个候选替换两边的每个 x，分别算出精确结果。两边相等才通过。只算左边得到了某个数，还没有完成比较；只代回一个可能写错的中间式，也不能保证满足原条件。没有经过要求的近似约定，就不能把相差一点说成精确相等。

### 例 1：同一个候选要过两边

解 `2x+3=11`。两边同减三，得 `2x=8`；再同除非零的二，得 `x=4`。代回原式：左边 `2×4+3=11`，右边十一，所以四是解。

换候选三：左边 `2×3+3=9`，右边仍是十一，九不等于十一，三不是解。右边不含 x 也要保留、计算和比较。工作台的等量板用 `=` 与 `≠` 直接表达比较，不靠图形看起来平衡来猜。

另如 `3x−2=−3`，同加二得 `3x=−1`，因此 `x=−1/3`。代回 `3×(−1/3)−2=−3`，负分数完全可以是有理数方程的解。`−0.33` 只是近似值，代回得 `−2.99`，不等于负三。

### 例 2：零、空集与任意值

比较三条条件：

1. `3x+1=1` 化为 `3x=0`，同除非零三得 `x=0`。零代回两边都是一，是唯一的有效解。
2. `2x+1=2x+3` 两边同减 `2x` 后为 `1=3`。不管取什么 x 都不能让这个矛盾成立，所以无解。
3. `2(x+1)=2x+2` 展开后两边完全相同，再同减 `2x+2` 得 `0=0`。每个有理数都满足，解集是所有有理数，不是只解出零。

标准一元一次方程能整理成 `ax+b=0`，其中 `a≠0`。后两条用来辨认系数抵消的退化边界，不能把它们也当作有非零一次系数的方程继续除法。没有 x 项时，要读剩下的等式，不要除以零。

## 一次检验的证据有多大

代入四通过，证明四是这一原方程的解；它不自动证明没有别的解。证明唯一解还依靠前面每一步可逆，因此原条件与 `x=4` 有相同解集。同样，给恒等式代入零或一通过，不能靠这一次试数证明所有数都通过；恒成立来自通用展开与消去。

先在工作台输入一个错误候选，再改为精确解，读出原式两边。切换到零解、恒等与矛盾案例，用“零”“无解”“任意有理数”分别表达。家长请孩子指认原式、解释两边数值，并说出一次检验能证明和不能证明的结论。

# Solutions and checking

## A solution makes the original equality true

An expression describes a relationship; an equation imposes a condition using an equal sign. In `2x+3=11`, we seek values making the left and right sides equal. This lesson works over rational numbers. A word problem may impose an additional domain. One permitted value making the original equation true is a solution; the complete collection is its solution set.

Keep the original equation when checking. Replace every x on both sides by the same candidate and calculate each side exactly. A left-side value alone is not a comparison. A candidate satisfying a mistaken intermediate equation may still fail the original. Without an agreed approximation task, nearly equal values do not establish exact equality.

### Example 1: one candidate, two original sides

For `2x+3=11`, subtract three from both sides to obtain `2x=8`, then divide both by nonzero two: `x=4`. In the original, the left is `2×4+3=11` and the right is eleven. Four passes.

Try three instead. The original left becomes nine and the right remains eleven, so three fails. A side without x still needs to be retained and compared. The workshop board displays `=` or `≠` from exact values rather than asking you to guess from a picture.

For `3x−2=−3`, adding two gives `3x=−1`, hence `x=−1/3`. This checks as `3×(−1/3)−2=−3`. A negative fraction can be a valid rational solution. The rounded value `−0.33` instead gives `−2.99`, which is not negative three.

### Example 2: zero, none and every rational

The equation `3x+1=1` reduces to `3x=0`. Division by nonzero three gives the unique solution zero. Substitution produces one on each original side.

In `2x+1=2x+3`, subtracting 2x gives `1=3`, a contradiction. No value can meet this condition, so the solution set is empty.

In `2(x+1)=2x+2`, expansion makes the sides identical. Subtracting 2x+2 gives `0=0`. Every rational satisfies this identity; zero is not the unique solution.

A genuine first-degree equation in one variable can be written `ax+b=0` with a nonzero. The last two examples are degenerate cancellation cases used to recognise the boundary. They do not have a nonzero remaining x coefficient to divide by. Interpret the constant statement instead of dividing by zero.

## What one check establishes

A successful substitution proves that candidate is a solution. It does not by itself prove uniqueness or that every value works. Reversible transformations explain why the original and `x=4` have exactly the same solutions. General expansion explains an identity; one or two matching substitutions do not.

Try a failing candidate, then the exact solution, and read both values. Switch among the zero, identity and contradiction cases and use the distinct answers zero, no solution and all rational numbers. A parent should ask which original equation was checked, what each side became and which conclusions the evidence supports.
