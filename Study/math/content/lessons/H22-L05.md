# 椭圆的几何定义

圆固定到一个中心的距离；椭圆固定的是**到两焦点的距离和**。每一段可以改变，和保持不变。图像像一个扁圆，并不足以给出这个定义。

## 先检查常数是否可能

焦点F1(−c,0)、F2(c,0)，c>0，要求PF1+PF2=2a。三角不等式给距离和≥2c，所以a<c无点，a=c只得到两焦点间的闭线段。非退化且两焦点不同的椭圆须a>c>0。

若c=0，两焦点重合，条件变为2PC=2a，是半径a的圆；圆也是更广义的椭圆特例。工作台用不同焦点，帮助分清a与c，不把这个选择误当数学上禁止圆。

令r1=PF1、r2=PF2。r1+r2=2a，而r1²−r2²=(x+c)²−(x−c)²=4cx，所以r1−r2=2cx/a，进而r1=a+cx/a，r2=a−cx/a。把r1平方代回距离式，消去交叉项，得到b²=(b²/a²)x²+y²，其中b²=a²−c²>0。因此标准式为x²/a²+y²/b²=1。

反过来，标准式给|x|≤a，所以a±cx/a≥a−c>0；两距离的平方恰是这些正数的平方，距离和恢复为2a。这个正号检查使推导没有因平方而扩大点集。

### 例1：固定和，单段并不固定

焦点(−3,0)、(3,0)，距离和10。2a=10，a=5，c=3，b²=25−9=16，故x²/25+y²/16=1。P(5,0)的两距离为8、2；Q(0,4)的两距离为5、5。单段改变，和都是10。

长半轴a=5，短半轴b=4；全长分别10、8。焦点间距是2c=6，不是和10。x轴端点±5和y轴端点±4均可直接代方程验证。

### 例2：长轴可以竖直，也可以平移

中心C(1,−2)，竖直长轴，a=5、c=4，所以b²=25−16=9。方程为(x−1)²/9+(y+2)²/25=1；焦点(1,−6)、(1,2)。a²=25放在长轴对应的y位移下，不是永远放在x下。

右侧短轴端点P(4,−2)到两焦点距离均为√(3²+4²)=5，和10；上侧长轴端点Q(1,3)的距离为9、1，和仍10。平移只改变坐标基准，不改变距离和。不能把x、y分母互换后还说同一方向的椭圆。

## 工作台与人工论证

选择a,c、中心、轴向与描点P。F1在所选轴的负方向、F2在正方向，标签固定。分别预测方程、PF1及距离和，再展开精确核验。移动P可观察单段距离变化；只改中心或轴向，不改变常数2a，独立的常数预测保留。

工作台用勾股数组让b和示范点保持精确有理数；椭圆定义不限于这些参数。t只是有理描点工具，不是方程中的x。有限图窗与示范点不证明全部轨迹；完整理由来自距离条件与上面的双向推导。

出口必须用两个位置给出不同的分段长度与相同的和，并区分半轴、全轴、焦距。家长还可让孩子解释和小于、等于焦距的边界。下一课将“和”换成“绝对差”，条件与符号都要重新检查。

# Geometric definition of ellipses

A circle fixes distance from one centre. An ellipse fixes the **sum of distances to two foci**. Each segment can change while its sum stays fixed; a flattened circular appearance is not a definition.

## Check whether the constant is possible

Let F1(−c,0),F2(c,0), with c>0, and require PF1+PF2=2a. The triangle inequality gives a sum at least 2c. For a<c the locus is empty; for a=c it is the closed segment between the foci. A nondegenerate ellipse with distinct foci requires a>c>0.

If c=0, the foci coincide and 2PC=2a gives a circle of radius a. A circle is an ellipse special case under the broader definition. Distinct workbench foci help distinguish a and c; they do not forbid the circular case mathematically.

Put r1=PF1,r2=PF2. Since r1+r2=2a and r1²−r2²=4cx, we obtain r1−r2=2cx/a. Hence r1=a+cx/a and r2=a−cx/a. Substituting the first square into its coordinate distance formula and cancelling cross terms gives b²=(b²/a²)x²+y², where b²=a²−c²>0. Thus x²/a²+y²/b²=1.

Conversely, the standard equation implies |x|≤a, so a±cx/a≥a−c>0. Their squares match the squared focal distances, restoring sum 2a. This sign check prevents squaring from enlarging the locus.

### Example 1: The sum stays fixed, not each segment

For foci (−3,0),(3,0) and sum ten, a=5,c=3,b²=16. The equation is x²/25+y²/16=1. At P(5,0) the distances are eight and two; at Q(0,4) they are five and five. Their sum stays ten.

Semi-axis lengths are a=5,b=4; full lengths are ten and eight. Focal separation 2c=6 differs from sum ten. Substitute the axis endpoints to check the equation.

### Example 2: A translated vertical major axis

For centre (1,−2), vertical major axis, a=5,c=4, we have b²=9. The equation is (x−1)²/9+(y+2)²/25=1 and the foci are (1,−6),(1,2). Denominator a²=25 follows the major axis, not always x.

At minor-axis endpoint P(4,−2), each focal distance is √(3²+4²)=5. At upper major-axis endpoint Q(1,3), the distances are nine and one. Both sums are ten. Translation changes the coordinate reference, not the invariant. Swapping the denominators changes orientation.

## Workbench and reasoning

Choose a,c, centre, orientation and trace point P. F1 is on the negative axial side and F2 on the positive side. Predict the equation, PF1 and distance sum before opening the audit. Moving P changes individual distances; translations and axis changes preserve 2a and its independent prediction.

The workbench uses Pythagorean triples for exact rational examples, but the definition is not limited to them. Trace parameter t is not x. A finite window and sampled points do not prove the whole locus; the distance condition and two-way derivation do.

The exit compares two positions with changing segment lengths and the same sum, while distinguishing semi-axes, full axes and focal separation. Explain the smaller/equal-sum boundaries too. Next, replacing the sum by an absolute difference changes both conditions and equation signs.
