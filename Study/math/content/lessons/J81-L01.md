# 单项式乘单项式

## 把系数与字母因子分开看

单项式乘法并不是把所有看见的数字都乘在一起。系数回答“这一组有多少倍”，指数回答“同一个字母作为因子出现几次”。先乘系数，再把全部字母因子连接、按字母分别计数。系数中的负号属于系数，不能掉在一边。

同底数幂相乘，指数相加，来自重复因子的个数相加：两个 x 因子再接三个，一共有五个。不是把二与三相乘得到六。这里讨论正整数指数，x 可以为任意实数，包括零；推导没有除以 x，所以没有偷偷排除零。

### 例 1：负系数与重复因子

整理 `(−2x²)(3x)`。把它写成 `(-2)×3×x×x×x`，系数为负六，字母部分为 x³，所以结果 `−6x³`。

同一个乘积也可先用交换律、结合律把数放一起、字母放一起；这些操作不改变任何因子。不能写成 −6x²，因为后一个 x 也要计数；也不能写成 x³ 而丢掉系数。若 x=−1，原乘积与结果都为六，这次代入能检错，但一般关系的依据仍是乘法的结构。

### 例 2：两个字母与分数系数

`(3xy)(−2x)` 的系数为 −6，字母为 `x·y·x`，整理成 `−6x²y`。只有 x 出现两次，y 只一次，所以不是 −6x²y²。字母写成 yx 与 xy 表示同一乘积，但不同字母的指数不能并成一个总指数。

`((1/2)x²)(−4x²)` 中，半乘负四为负二；两个 x 因子接两个，共四个，所以结果 `−2x⁴`。分数系数按精确分数相乘，不需要先写近似小数。

## 乘法与加法的规则不能混用

`x²·x³=x⁵` 是连接因子；`x²+x³` 是两个不同次数的项相加，不能写 x⁵。类似地，`2x+3x=5x` 是同类项系数相加，字母部分不变；`(2x)(3x)=6x²` 才是系数相乘、因子个数相加。先读清运算符，比背一句“指数怎么算”更可靠。

若某个系数为零，整个乘积为零，最简结果写 `0`。保留 `0x³` 仍显示了应当省去的零项。本课要求完成乘积：写出 `−6x²·x` 虽然等值，却仍把同底因子留给下一步，需整理为 `−6x³`。输入可用 x^3 或 x³，系数写前，每个字母用一个幂表示。

工作台可改两个系数、两个指数及第二个字母。先算，再展开重复因子核对；改条件会清空旧答案。家长问“这是相乘还是相加？”“每种字母究竟出现几次？”孩子应能解释系数、符号与字母，而不是只报一个幂。

# Multiplying monomials

## Separate coefficients from repeated letter factors

A coefficient records a multiplier; an exponent counts repeated occurrences of the same base. Multiply the coefficients, then concatenate and count letter factors separately. Keep a negative sign with its coefficient. Do not multiply every visible number indiscriminately: a coefficient and an exponent have different jobs.

For positive integer exponents, the same-base product rule comes from counting factors. Two x factors followed by three make five, not six. The letter may represent any real number, including zero. No division by x is used, so zero is not excluded.

### Example 1: negative coefficients and repeated factors

For `(−2x²)(3x)`, write `(-2)×3×x×x×x`. The numerical coefficient is −6 and the letter part is x³, giving `−6x³`.

Commutativity and associativity let us group the numerical and letter factors without losing any. The answer is not −6x² because the last x also counts, and not x³ because the coefficient matters. At x=−1 both expressions equal six. This checks a particular case; the factor-based reasoning establishes the general relationship.

### Example 2: separate letters and a fractional coefficient

In `(3xy)(−2x)`, coefficients give −6 and the factors x·y·x give x²y. Thus the product is `−6x²y`, not −6x²y². Only x occurs twice. The order xy or yx may change, but exponents of different bases cannot be merged into one exponent.

For `((1/2)x²)(−4x²)`, the coefficient is −2 and four x factors give x⁴, so the product is `−2x⁴`. Keep the fractional coefficient exact; a rounded decimal is unnecessary.

## A multiplication rule is not an addition rule

The product x²·x³ equals x⁵ because factors concatenate. The sum x²+x³ adds unlike terms and does not equal x⁵. Likewise, 2x+3x=5x adds like-term coefficients while retaining x, whereas (2x)(3x)=6x² multiplies coefficients and concatenates factors. Identify the operation before choosing a rule.

A zero coefficient makes the entire product zero. Write 0 rather than a redundant zero letter term. This lesson also asks for completed products: −6x²·x is equivalent but still needs the same-base multiplication completed. Write −6x³. Enter the coefficient first and each letter as one power, using x^3 or x³.

Change the workshop’s coefficients, exponents and second letter, predict a result and reveal its repeated factors. New conditions clear old answers. A parent asks which operation is being used and how often each letter appears, checking the explanation of coefficients and signs rather than a memorised exponent alone.
