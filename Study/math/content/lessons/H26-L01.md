# 导数的正负与增减

函数的高度回答“现在有多少”，导数的符号回答“输入往右走，输出怎样变化”。本课要用完整规则解释区间增减，不能用几个数值或曲线看起来的方向代替证明。

## 先说清区间

在一个区间内部每处可导且f′>0，函数在该区间严格递增；f′<0则严格递减。这里的严格递增是：区间中任意a<b，都有f(a)<f(b)。若讨论闭区间，还需要端点连续，才能把内部结论延伸至端点。背后的理由是两点平均变化率等于某个内点的导数，即平均值定理；它要求整段连续、内部可导，不能跨定义域缺口。

f′≥0只直接保证非递减。零导数孤立出现时，函数仍可能严格递增；但若导数在一整段恒为0，函数在那一段恒定。不要把充分条件“每处导数正”误背成严格递增的必要条件。函数值为正也可以递减，如x²在(−∞,0)为正且递减，说明高度和斜率有不同角色。

### 例1：从因子符号得到整段结论

f(x)=x³−3x，先求f′(x)=3x²−3=3(x−1)(x+1)。零点−1与1把实数轴分三段。

当x<−1，两因子均负，乘积正，f严格递增。当−1<x<1，一负一正，导数负，f严格递减。当x>1，两因子均正，f严格递增。导数在−1、1为0，这两个单点没有“区间长度”，不能叫作恒定区间。我们能在每段取一个点检查计算，但整段符号的依据是因子不在段内过零，不是样点数量多。

在x=2，f(2)=2而f′(2)=9。2是高度，9是斜率。选择工作台的某个区间时，应先给区间结论，再单独回答a处导数，不把一个点的斜率当全段证明。

### 例2：零斜率与缺口的两个反例

g(x)=x³在0有g′(0)=0，但整条函数严格递增。对任意a<b，b³−a³=(b−a)(a²+ab+b²)。第一因子正，第二因子在不同的a、b时也正，因此差为正。0处水平只是瞬间放平，没有回头，也没有恒定小段。

另看h(x)=−1/x，定义域排除0，两侧导数都为1/x²>0。结论只能分别用于(−∞,0)和(0,∞)。取−1<1，却h(−1)=1>−1=h(1)，所以不能声称它在整个不连通定义域严格递增。连接两支画一条线会伪造函数并掩盖定理缺少的条件。

## 操作、解释与迁移

工作台支持转折三次、平坦三次、正负平方、常数与绝对值。绝对值在0不可导，左右斜率分别−1与1；不要在0制造绿色导数点。符号表按代数规则生成，图形是帮助理解的近似绘制；换视窗只改变看见的范围，不改函数定义域。

先预测区间增减，再给a处导数，最后展开核验表。出口需要三个区间、导数符号与规则依据。家长可问：“你说的是所有输入对，还是只看了两张数值卡？”以及“这个区间有没有缺口？”若孩子只会代值，先回H25基本求导，再回H02函数性质，避免用记箭头代替定义。

# Derivative sign and monotonicity

Function height answers “how much now?” The derivative sign describes how output changes as input moves right. Use a complete rule to justify interval behaviour; a few readings or the apparent direction of a sketch are not a proof.

## Specify the interval first

If a function is differentiable throughout an interval and its derivative is positive there, it is strictly increasing. A negative derivative gives strict decrease. Strict increase means that every pair a<b in the interval satisfies f(a)<f(b). To extend an interior conclusion to included endpoints, continuity at those endpoints is also needed. The mean value theorem explains the connection: a secant rate equals a derivative at some interior point when the function is continuous on the whole closed segment and differentiable inside it. A domain gap invalidates that argument.

A nonnegative derivative directly guarantees nondecrease, allowing equal outputs. An isolated zero slope can coexist with strict increase. By contrast, a derivative identically zero on an interval makes the function constant there. Positivity everywhere is a sufficient condition, not a necessary condition for strict increase. Output sign plays a different role from derivative sign: for example, x² is positive and decreasing on (−∞,0).

### Example 1: Factor signs justify whole intervals

For f(x)=x³−3x, differentiate to f′(x)=3x²−3=3(x−1)(x+1). Its zeros −1 and one split the real line into three open intervals.

Left of −1, both factors are negative, so the derivative is positive and f increases. Between −1 and one, the factors have opposite signs, giving a negative derivative and decrease. Right of one, both factors are positive and f increases. At −1 and one the derivative is zero; these individual inputs do not form constant intervals. A test point can check arithmetic, but the justification for an entire interval is that no factor crosses zero inside it.

At two, the function value is two while the derivative value is nine. One measures height; the other measures slope. After selecting an interval in the workbench, give its behaviour separately from the derivative at the observed input a. One derivative value cannot establish a whole interval conclusion.

### Example 2: A horizontal instant and a domain gap

The function g(x)=x³ has g′(0)=0 and remains strictly increasing everywhere. For arbitrary a<b, the difference b³−a³ factors as (b−a)(a²+ab+b²). The first factor is positive, and the second is positive for distinct a and b. Thus the difference is positive. The horizontal tangent at zero creates neither a reversal nor a constant segment.

Now consider h(x)=−1/x, with zero excluded from its domain. Its derivative 1/x² is positive on each side of zero. Increase follows separately on (−∞,0) and (0,∞). Nevertheless −1<1 while h(−1)=1>−1=h(1), so the function is not increasing across its entire disconnected domain. Joining the branches would invent values and conceal the missing interval condition.

For |x| at zero the one-sided slopes are −1 and one, so there is no derivative there. The sign table follows algebraic rules.
