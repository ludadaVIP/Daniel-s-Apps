# 指数方程与对数方程

解方程需要保持原解，最好让每一步可逆；暂时扩大候选集时，要在结尾回到原式筛选。本课把底数条件和各个真数条件放在运算前面，再使用同底指数的一对一性和对数运算法则。

## 先记录合法条件

实指数函数b^x在b>0、b≠1时严格单调，因而一对一。b>1时递增，0<b<1时递减；两种情况都满足b^u=b^v当且仅当u=v。等式比较不需要翻转等号，若改成不等式才须注意递减时的方向。

实对数log_b A还要求A>0。这个条件属于真数A，不是对数的值必须正，也不是未知量x必须正。合并log_b A+log_b B=log_b(AB)之前必须A>0且B>0；只检查AB>0会错误地放进两个负真数的情况。

### 例1：负指数与负对数值

解2^(x−1)=8。先把8写成2³，利用同底一对一得x−1=3，因此x=4。原指数在所有实x都有定义，代回2³=8核验。

再解log₃(x+1)=−2。原条件x+1>0。改成x+1=3^(−2)=1/9，得x=−8/9。代回真数为1/9，确为正；对数值−2也完全合法。这里的负解不应因“对数不能负”的误解被拒绝。底数1不可用，因为1的实指数恒1，不能唯一还原指数。

### 例2：合并后有两个候选，不等于两个原解

解log₂(x−1)+log₂(x+1)=3。先列x−1>0、x+1>0，交集为x>1。保留这个条件后才合并，得到log₂(x²−1)=3，继而x²−1=8，得到候选x=±3。

x=3使原式真数为2与4，对数和1+2=3，因此保留。x=−3虽然让乘积8为正，却使两个原真数为−4与−2，原式无定义。因此完整解集是{3}，不是{−3,3}。这说明合并式若擅自使用其更大自然定义域，会改变方程。

改为log₂(x−3)+log₂(x+3)=4时，原域x>3，候选±5，只留5。代回真数2和8，对数和1+3=4。反例−5被拒绝的原因仍是原式无定义，而不是负数普遍不能做方程的解。

## 图形怎样服务于推理

工作台只在原定义域绘制紫色左侧函数，虚线是右侧目标。指数曲线在所选大于1的底数下递增；对数乘积例子在x>h上递增，因此观察到交点后还能用代数和单调性说明完整性。

底数0<b<1也合法，但本台控制只选2、3、4方便观察；控制范围不是数学定义。图被裁在有限窗口，交点可能在窗口外，不能把“没画到”写成无解。对数不在不合法区域画一条连线，也不把数值近似交点当作精确根。

## 输出完整报告

按“条件—变形—候选—代回—集合”书写。先让学生逐项说出真数，避免只盯着乘积。家长可追问为什么−8/9能作为单对数方程的解，而−3不是前述和式的解；两题比较能检验学生是否在检查真正的条件。

出口同时说明底数为何可用、对数在哪些输入有定义，以及每个候选被保留或拒绝的原因。只展示一个正确答案，不足以证明没有遗漏其他解。

# Exponential and logarithmic equations

A solution method must retain original roots and justify every transformation. Record base and argument restrictions before applying exponent or logarithm laws. If a step produces a larger candidate set, return to the unmodified equation for the final audit.

## Conditions before calculations

For b>0 and b≠1, the real exponential b^x is strictly monotone and therefore one-to-one. It increases when b>1 and decreases when 0<b<1. In either case b^u=b^v exactly when u=v. Decrease changes inequality directions, not the validity of exponent comparison in an equation.

A real logarithm log_b A additionally requires A>0. Positivity belongs to its argument, not necessarily to x or to the logarithm’s value. Combining log_b A+log_b B into log_b(AB) requires both arguments individually positive. A positive product alone also allows two negative factors and is insufficient.

### Example 1: Negative powers and negative logarithms

Solve 2^(x−1)=8. Write eight as 2³, use injectivity to obtain x−1=3 and conclude x=4. The original exponential is defined at every real x; substitution gives 2³=8.

Now solve log₃(x+1)=−2. Record x+1>0, then convert to x+1=3^(−2)=1/9. Thus x=−8/9. Its argument is positive, and a negative logarithm is legitimate. Rejecting this root merely because x is negative confuses the unknown with its argument. Base one is excluded because its exponential is constant and cannot uniquely recover an exponent.

### Example 2: Two algebraic candidates, one original root

Consider log₂(x−1)+log₂(x+1)=3. Require x−1>0 and x+1>0, giving x>1. On that domain, combine to log₂(x²−1)=3 and then obtain x²−1=8. The quadratic candidates are −3 and 3.

At three, the original arguments are two and four, giving logarithms one and two. The original equation holds. At −3, both separate arguments are negative. Their product is eight, but the original logarithms are undefined. The complete solution set is {3}. Using the combined expression on its larger natural domain would change the original problem.

For log₂(x−3)+log₂(x+3)=4, the original domain is x>3. Algebra gives candidates ±5, of which only five survives. Its arguments two and eight give logarithms one and three. The negative candidate is rejected because of this equation’s domain, not because negative numbers never solve equations.

## Use graphs without surrendering exactness

The workbench plots the original left side only where it is defined, with a dashed horizontal target. Its controls use bases two, three and four for inspection; that restriction is not the full mathematical definition. Decreasing bases between zero and one remain valid.

A plot window can miss an intersection outside its bounds. Numerical sampling cannot replace exact algebra or prove completeness. In the product examples, the original left side is increasing on x>h, and the candidate audit establishes the full solution. No graph segment should connect logarithm values across forbidden inputs.
