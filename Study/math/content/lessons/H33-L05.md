# 正态分布

正态分布是一族连续概率模型，写作N(μ,σ²)，其中μ为均值，σ>0为标准差，第二参数σ²是方差。密度在μ两侧对称，定义在整个实轴上。它是需要依据的模型假设，不是所有数据的自动归宿。

## 高度和面积是不同的量

密度f(x)=exp[−(x−μ)²/(2σ²)]/(σ√(2π))。区间概率是∫ₐᵇf(x)dx，整条曲线在实轴上的总面积1。某点密度f(x)不是P(X=x)；连续模型中单点宽度0，单点概率0，即使该处密度最高也一样。

如果X以厘米计，密度单位是1/厘米，乘横向宽度厘米后才得无量纲概率。密度高度甚至可以大于1：例如标准差0.1厘米时中心密度约3.989每厘米，仍不违反概率≤1，因为高度不是概率。

### 例1：均值左右一个标准差

假设某虚构长度模型X~N(50,2²)，均值50厘米，标准差2厘米，方差4平方厘米。区间48到52正是μ±σ，其概率约0.68268949，保留四位小数0.6827。

P(X=50)=0是连续模型的精确结论，不是把一个很小的正概率舍入成零。由对称性，P(X<50)=1/2；单点概率0使小于与小于等于在此没有差别。

μ±2σ的面积约0.9545，μ±3σ约0.9973。这些是正态积分的近似数，不是任意分布或任意有限样本必须满足的精确比例。不能看一组数据大致对称，就规定它的每个相应区间都有这些频率。

### 例2：中心与尺度各有作用

把均值从50改为10、标准差仍2，曲线沿横轴平移；相对中心的区间概率不变。把标准差从2改为3、均值仍50，分布更宽、中心高度更低，但总面积仍1。相同的“均值±一个自身标准差”概率仍约0.6827。

若保持绝对区间48到52不变而把σ改为3，它不再是μ±σ，概率也应重新计算。区间是否跟随参数变化必须讲明，不能把相对区间的不变量误套到固定端点上。

工作台在改变σ时保留同一横纵显示尺度，便于比较宽度与高度；改变μ则显示窗口随中心平移，刻度仍标原数值。有限窗口外有尾部，未画出的部分不是概率0，左尾事件还包括窗外那一小段。

## 使用模型要有证据

现实长度通常不能负，而正态模型有无限两尾，因此最多是需要检查精度与适用范围的近似。数据收集过程、异常点、偏态与尾部都可能影响适用性。钟形直方图还受分组影响，单凭外观不足以证明正态。

本课的数值概率由标准密度数值积分得到，先完成累计或区间差，再按题意舍入四位小数；图中有限采样只用于展示。家长可要求学生指明高度、区间宽和面积的单位，并说清哪句话依赖正态假设。

公式核对参考[NIST正态分布说明](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/eda/section3/eda3661.htm)。出口要解释密度与概率的关系、σ²记号和图窗外尾部，不能仅记住68%这个数。

# Normal distribution

A normal distribution is a continuous model written N(μ,σ²), with mean μ and positive SD σ. Its second parameter is variance. The density is symmetric about μ and extends across the real line. Normality is a model assumption requiring evidence.

## Distinguish height from area

The density is exp[−(x−μ)²/(2σ²)]/(σ√(2π)). Integrating it over an interval gives probability, and its total real-line area is one. A density value is not the probability of exactly that point. A singleton has zero width and zero probability even at the peak.

For centimetre-valued X, density has units1/cm. Multiplying by horizontal width yields unitless probability. A density height may exceed one: with SD 0.1 cm, the central density is approximately3.989 per centimetre. This violates no probability rule because height is not probability.

### Example 1: One standard deviation around the mean

Assume a fictional length model X~N(50,2²). Mean is50 cm, SD 2 cm and variance 4 cm². The interval48 through52 equals μ±σ and has probability approximately0.68268949, or0.6827 to four decimal places.

P(X=50)=0 is exact in this continuous model, not a small positive value rounded to zero. Symmetry gives P(X<50)=1/2. Including the endpoint changes no probability because its point mass is zero.

The areas within two and three SDs are approximately0.9545 and 0.9973. These rounded normal-model probabilities are not exact rules for every distribution or finite sample. A roughly symmetric dataset does not force these observed proportions.

### Example 2: Centre and scale have separate roles

Changing the mean from fifty to ten while keeping SD two translates the curve. Probabilities in corresponding intervals relative to the centre stay unchanged. Increasing SD from two to three at fixed mean makes the curve wider and lower, preserving total area one.

An interval following mean±one current SD still has mass approximately0.6827. A fixed original interval48 through52 does not: with SD three, those endpoints no longer lie one SD from the mean. State whether endpoints move with the parameters.

The workbench keeps both display scales fixed while changing SD, revealing width and height changes. Changing the mean recentres the window while retaining original-value tick labels. Infinite tails remain outside any finite window; left-tail probabilities include their unshown parts.

## Justify the model and retain approximation labels

Physical lengths cannot usually be negative, whereas a normal model has two infinite tails. Check approximation quality and scope rather than applying normality unconditionally. Generating processes, skewness, outliers and tails matter; histogram appearance also depends on binning.

Numerical integration gives the displayed probabilities, rounded only after the calculation. Finite sampled graphics illustrate the model rather than establish the integral. Ask which statements require normality, and identify the units of height, width and area.
