# 数据结论的局限性

一个数字必须和“谁、怎样取得、还有什么未知”一起报告。数据多、相关高或拟合好都不能单独消除设计限制。本课用有限资料的逻辑界，把“看见什么”和“推断什么”分开。

## 分母先对应对象

目标总体是想谈论的完整群体，观察对象是实际有资料的成员。两者可能不同。自愿回应、便利渠道和未回应都可能改变覆盖；不能把未回应自动写成否，也不能只因为样本大就宣称代表性。

本课二元例子假定总体N个不同成员，各有固定的是/否结果；已知n人的明确结果，其中y人是。其余N−n个未知，没有额外分布假设。把未知全部设否得到下界y/N，全部设是得到上界(y+N−n)/N。它们是与资料相容的逻辑界，不附带95%或其他置信水平。

### 例1：回应者75%，总体仍有很宽可能范围

200人中20人明确回应，15人是、5人否。回应者比例15/20=3/4=75%；总体已知的是人数只有15，所以最小比例15/200=3/40=7.5%。

若未知180人全是，最大比例(15+180)/200=39/40=97.5%。两端可由具体补全实现，因此不是随意保守猜测。全体75%是一个与资料相容的可能值，却不是被这些资料唯一确定的结论。若有40人明确结果、30是10否，逻辑范围变为15%到95%；若200人全部已知且150是，范围才收成75%一个值。改变覆盖不同于反复重抽同一批已知资料。

### 例2：随机抽样不等于随机分配

设恰当简单随机抽取20名成员，且所抽人员全部给出可靠结果。这样的设计可支持总体比例估计及抽样不确定性的分析，但一次样本75%仍不等于总体真值；本课不凭空给置信区间或假设检验结论。

另一个独立研究招募志愿者，再随机分配到两种处理。适当随机分配在依从、无干扰、可靠测量等条件下支持参与者的处理效果比较，却不自动使志愿者代表所有人。还需各组结果才能算效果；只给总计15/20不能凭随机分配四个字构造处理差。样本量有限时随机化也不保证每个背景变量恰好平衡，因果结果仍需不确定性分析。

观察性相关则可能有混杂或反向因果，不由r很大直接变成处理效果。两类设计分别解决推广与比较的不同困难，都不能用漂亮的训练SSE替代。

## 报告适用范围和未解决的问题

若观测x只覆盖[1,5]，用模型算x=6是外推；算得出不代表机制仍成立。内插也不自动可靠，仍要查形状、测量与资料设计。训练集误差小不等于新资料误差小，可用独立验证检查预测，避免把同一训练表现当成证实。

工作台的200格只用于核对身份数量，颜色分别是已知是、已知否、未知，不编码随机性或成员排序。下方三张独立设计卡不改变上方固定资料，也不把汇总比例冒充因果效果。

出口报告须同时包括目标、已观察对象、分母、逻辑界、设计、未知和范围；家长可问“你把哪条假设加进结论了”。完整论证由人核验；不以术语匹配自动通过，也不把工程测试记录当成孩子掌握统计推理的证据。

# Limits of data conclusions

Report a statistic together with whom it describes, how evidence was obtained and what remains unknown. More observations, stronger correlation or better training fit do not independently remove design limitations.

## Match denominators to people

The target population is the full group of interest; observed units are members with actual data. Voluntary response, convenience channels and nonresponse can alter coverage. Nonresponse is not automatically no, and a large sample alone does not establish representativeness.

For this binary example, N distinct members each have a fixed yes/no outcome. Outcomes are known for n of them, including y yes, and the remaining N−n are unknown. Without further assumptions, all unknowns no gives lower bound y/N, and all unknowns yes gives upper bound (y+N−n)/N. These are logical bounds compatible with the information, not intervals with a stated confidence level.

### Example 1: Respondents give 75%, not a unique population truth

Of 200 members, twenty respond clearly: fifteen yes and five no. The respondent proportion is 15/20=75%. The smallest possible population proportion is 15/200=3/40=7.5%.

If all 180 unknowns say yes, the largest proportion is 195/200=39/40=97.5%. Both extremes are achievable completions, rather than arbitrary cautious guesses. A population proportion of 75% is compatible with the evidence but not uniquely determined by it. Forty known outcomes with thirty yes narrow the logical range to 15% through 95%. Observing all 200, with 150 yes, collapses it to 75%. Better coverage is different from repeatedly resampling the same known records.

### Example 2: Sampling differs from assignment

Properly draw a simple random sample of twenty members and obtain complete, reliable responses. That design supports population estimation with an analysis of sampling uncertainty. A realised sample proportion of 75% still need not equal population truth. This lesson does not invent a confidence interval or significance result without the required analysis.

A separate study recruits volunteers and randomly assigns two treatments. Under conditions including adherence, no interference and reliable measurement, assignment supports treatment comparisons among participants. It does not make volunteers automatically representative of everyone. Group outcomes are needed to calculate a treatment contrast; an aggregate fifteen of twenty cannot supply it. Finite randomisation also does not guarantee exact balance on every background variable, so causal estimates still require uncertainty analysis.

Observational correlation may reflect confounding or reverse causation. A large r alone is not a treatment effect. Sampling and assignment address different obstacles, and neither can be replaced by attractive training SSE.

## State scope and remaining unknowns

If observed x lies only in [1,5], predicting at six is extrapolation. Algebraic evaluation does not establish that the mechanism persists. Interpolation also needs checks of shape, measurement and design. Small training error does not guarantee performance on new evidence; use independent validation rather than treating the same training fit as confirmation.

The workbench’s two hundred tiles audit identities and counts. Colours mean known yes, known no and unknown; their arrangement does not encode random selection. The independent design cards below do not change the fixed outcomes above or turn an aggregate proportion into a causal estimate.

For the exit, report target, observed units, denominator, logical bounds, design, unknowns and domain together. A parent can ask which assumptions entered the conclusion. Human review assesses the full argument; matching terminology does not earn automatic approval, and engineering test records do not demonstrate a child’s statistical mastery.
