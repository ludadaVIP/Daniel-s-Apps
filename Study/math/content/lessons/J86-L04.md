# 等腰三角形性质

## 等边所对的角相等

非退化三角形 ABC 中若 AB=AC，A 是两腰相交的顶角，BC 为底边，B 与 C 为底角。等边对等角，所以 B=C；结合内角和，两个底角的和是 180°−A，每个底角为其一半。不能只看图“好像对称”，需要等腰的已知条件。

等边三角形属于至少两边相等的等腰大类，三个角都是六十度。图形是否倾斜、顶角是否画在最上方，都不改变腰与底角的身份。若换成 BC=BA，就要重新认顶角在 B，不沿用原来的 A。

### 例 1：计算与证明不混淆

已知 AB=AC、A=50°。先由等边对等角得 B=C，再算 B+C=180−50=130°，所以 B=C=65°。一百三十是两角的总和，不是每一个的数值。

若反过来已给两个底角各七十，则顶角 A=180−70−70=40°，剩下的是一个顶角，不再除二。顶角一百时，两个底角各四十，同样可以是合法等腰三角形，不因顶角为钝角而否定。

为何等腰能推出底角相等？作 A 的内角平分线 AD 交 BC。已知 AB=AC，构造给 BAD=CAD，公共边 AD=AD，因此 △ABD≅△ACD（SAS），随后由对应角得 B=C。这里不能先以底角相等充当判全等的理由，又说全等证明底角相等。

### 例 2：逆命题不能偷用待证边

现在只给 B=C，想证 AB=AC。这是相反方向，需要一条独立证明，不能一开始就把 AB=AC 当作已知套 SAS。

仍作顶角平分线 AD：小三角形中 BAD=CAD 来自构造，ABD=ACD 来自原给等角及 D 在 BC 上，AD=AD 为公共边。两角与非夹边给 AAS（也可补第三角转 ASA），得到小三角形全等，才取 AB=AC。

辅助线是增加一个明确构造，不是凭空增加想要的相等关系。画 AD 时还不能直接写 BD=DC；等分底边是随后由全等推出的结论。由两小三角形对应的 D 角相等且它们组成平角，还能得各九十度；这属于进一步推导，不作为前面 SAS 的原始前提。

## 工作台与检查

改变已知顶角后，图按同一长度单位重新构造，两腰仍相等；底角数值主动揭示。显示角平分线时明确列出新增的是角平分条件和公共边，而不是预先标上待证底边相等。隐藏辅助线只是显示变化，不清空当前计算答案；换顶角才重置旧问题证据。

家长让孩子先指认两腰，再指出它们分别对着哪个角。计算正确还要解释为什么两个角能平均分；逆命题则要核每个条件来自“原始已知”“构造”还是“已经证明”。不能仅凭一个口诀把两个方向混为同一步。

# Properties of isosceles triangles

## Equal sides face equal angles

If AB=AC in a nondegenerate ABC, A is the apex and B/C are base angles. Equal sides face equal angles, so B=C. Each base angle is half of 180° minus the apex, not the whole remaining sum.

Equilateral triangles belong to the broad class with at least two equal sides. Orientation does not determine the apex: if BC=BA instead, the shared vertex is B and the roles must be relabelled.

### Example 1: calculate after justifying equality

With AB=AC and A=50°, the equal base angles sum to 130°, giving 65° each. The total 130 is not one angle.

If the base angles are each seventy, the one remaining apex is forty and is not halved again. An apex of 100 gives forty-degree base angles; an obtuse apex is allowed.

To justify the property, construct internal apex bisector AD meeting BC. Given AB=AC, constructed BAD=CAD and common AD=AD yield ABD≅ACD by SAS. Equal corresponding angles then give B=C. Assuming that target angle equality before congruence would be circular.

### Example 2: prove the converse independently

Now suppose only B=C is given and AB=AC is the target. Do not assume the target legs and apply SAS.

Construct AD again. Apex halves are equal by construction, ABD=ACD follows from the given B/C angles and D on BC, and AD is common. AAS, or ASA after a third-angle deduction, establishes congruence; matching legs then give AB=AC.

An auxiliary line adds a stated construction, not whichever equality is desired. BD=DC must be concluded later rather than assumed when drawing AD. Equal corresponding angles at D, together forming a straight angle, can subsequently show each is ninety degrees, but that is not an initial SAS premise.

## Investigate

Changing the apex reconstructs a genuine equal-leg triangle. Reveal base values separately. The bisector display states its new angle condition and common side without assuming equal base segments. Hiding the helper retains the answer; a new apex resets old evidence.

A parent checks which sides are legs, which angles they face and why a sum may be halved. For the converse, identify each premise as given, constructed or already proved instead of treating both logical directions as one unexplained rule.
