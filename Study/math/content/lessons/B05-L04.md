## 用开口与性质判断，不凭摆放方向

角描述两条线臂的相对开口，通常用度表示。线臂画得长些，角不一定更大；两臂一起转动，开口也不改变。顶点与两臂方向的关系才是关键，不是图画有没有“站正”。

一整圈为 360°，四分之一圈为 90°，半圈为 180°。本课工作台观察零到平角：零角为 0°，锐角大于零而小于 90°，直角为 90°，钝角大于 90°而小于 180°，平角为 180°。边界值不应被误放到相邻类别。

### 例 1 · 延长与旋转一个直角

1. 已知开口是 **90°**，两臂在同一顶点相交。
2. 保持方向，只把两臂延长，开口仍为九十度。
3. 再把整幅图一起转斜，两臂相对关系没改，仍为直角。
4. 若只转动其中一臂，才可能改变开口。必须区分“整图旋转”与“改变角度”。
5. 工作台的直角小方记号随方向一起转，不以水平或竖直作为直角定义。

### 例 2 · 斜放正方形的两个名字

1. 已知四条直边都相等，四个角都是直角，这确定一个正方形。
2. 旋转不改变边长与角度，因此斜放后仍是正方形。
3. 本课把长方形定义为有四条直边、四个直角的四边形，不要求四边都相等。
4. 正方形满足这个定义，所以**正方形也是一种特殊长方形**。
5. 一般长方形不一定是正方形：四边全等是额外条件。分类可以包含，并非每个物体只能有一个名字。

### 常见图形的直边与边界

三条直线段围成的封闭图形是三角形。三角形不必都有直角；工作台给的是一个直角三角形例子，不能拿一个例子的性质概括全部三角形。

圆没有直边，边界是曲线，圆不是多边形。“没有直边”不表示没有边界，也不表示周长为零。看清问题究竟在数直边、角、开口，还是边界长度。

同方向转 45°再转 45°，总转动为 90°；若题目改变转动方向，不能照搬相加。本课练习都明确方向，避免把读题困难当成不会角度。

图形看着像正方形，不等于已经证明四边相等与四角直角。要用题目给出的性质、测量或后续证明核查。工作台的数字开口与自绘线臂同步，用确定的几何构造表示性质；真实测量时还要看工具与精度。

**小检查：** 60°角只延长两臂后还是几度？半圈为什么是平角？各给出需要用到的关系，而不只说“看着像”。

## Use openings and properties rather than orientation

An angle describes the relative opening between two arms and is commonly measured in degrees. Longer drawn arms do not necessarily make a larger angle. Rotating both arms together retains the opening. The shared vertex and relative directions matter, not whether a drawing looks upright.

A full turn is 360°, a quarter turn 90° and a half turn 180°. This workshop covers zero through straight angles: zero is 0°, acute is greater than zero and less than 90°, right is 90°, obtuse is greater than 90° and less than 180°, and straight is 180°. Keep boundary values out of neighbouring categories.

### Example 1 · Extend and rotate a right angle

1. The stated opening is **90°**, with arms meeting at one vertex.
2. Extend both arms without changing their directions; the opening remains ninety degrees.
3. Tilt the whole drawing. Their relative relationship is unchanged, so it is still a right angle.
4. Turning only one arm could change opening. Distinguish rotating the whole picture from changing angle size.
5. The workshop's right-angle corner mark rotates with the arms; horizontal or vertical placement is not the definition.

### Example 2 · Two names for a tilted square

1. Four equal straight sides and four right angles establish a square.
2. Rotation preserves side lengths and angles, so the tilted figure remains a square.
3. Here a rectangle is a quadrilateral with four straight sides and four right angles; it need not have all four sides equal.
4. A square meets this definition, making **a square a special rectangle**.
5. An arbitrary rectangle need not be a square because equal sides are an additional condition. Categories may include one another; an object can have more than one valid name.

### Straight sides and boundaries of common shapes

Three straight segments enclose a triangle. Not every triangle has a right angle. The workshop draws one right-triangle example, whose additional property must not be generalised to all triangles.

A circle has no straight sides and has a curved boundary; it is not a polygon. No straight sides does not mean no boundary or zero perimeter. Identify whether the task counts straight sides, corners, opening or boundary length.

Turning 45° and another 45° in the same direction gives 90°. A change of direction would require reconsidering the relationship. These exercises state the direction so reading difficulties are not confused with angle understanding.

A picture looking square does not prove equal sides and right angles. Use stated properties, measurement or later proof. The workshop synchronises numerical openings and self-drawn arms through specified geometric constructions. Real measurements also depend on tools and precision.

**Quick check:** what happens to a 60° opening if both arms are merely extended? Why does a half turn form a straight angle? Use the relevant relationships rather than only visual resemblance.
