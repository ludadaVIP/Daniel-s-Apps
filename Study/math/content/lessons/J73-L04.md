# 含分数的一元一次方程

## 去分母是两边整体乘非零数

本课分母都是已知非零常数，如二、三、六，不含未知数。分数系数不会改变等式性质；为了便于整理，可以给两边整体乘分母的公倍数。用最小公倍数通常让数字较小，但任何已知非零公倍数都能做这一步。

“整体乘”意味着每项都得到相同因子，整数项也不例外。`x/2+1=3` 乘二后为 `x+2=6`，不是只删掉二得 `x+1=3`，也不是漏乘左边一得 `x+1=6`。公倍数非零，所以可以除回去，原方程与新方程同解。

### 例 1：负号覆盖整个分子

解 `(x−1)/2−(x+2)/3=1/6`。

1. 分母二、三、六的最小公倍数为六。两边整体乘六：`3(x−1)−2(x+2)=1`。
2. 展开：`3x−3−2x−4=1`。第二组的负二同时乘 x 与正二，常数贡献是负四。
3. 合并得 `x−7=1`，两边同加七，`x=8`。
4. 原式检验：`7/2−10/3=21/6−20/6=1/6`，与原右边相等。

若漏掉负号对第二项的作用，会写 `−2x+4`，改变了原数量。去分母后仍保留分子括号，再逐项展开，能减少这个错误。

### 例 2：整数项也要乘，解也可为分数

解 `(x+1)/3=x/2−1`。两边整体乘六，得到 `2(x+1)=3x−6`，整数负一也乘了六。展开 `2x+2=3x−6`，同减 `2x`、同加六得 `x=8`。代回原式左边三，右边四减一为三。

另解 `x/3=1/6`：两边乘六得 `2x=1`，所以 `x=1/2`。代回 `(1/2)/3=1/6`。分母消掉只表示改写后的系数是整数，不保证解一定是整数。出口式 `(2x−1)/3−(x+2)/4=1/6` 乘十二后为 `4(2x−1)−3(x+2)=2`，得到 `5x=12`，所以 `x=12/5`；原式 `19/15−11/10=1/6`。

## 检查范围与表达精度

与含 x 分母的方程相比，这里知道公倍数必非零，不需要隐含地假设 x 不等某个值。将来处理含变量的分母时，必须先研究定义范围，不能把本课规则无条件搬过去。此处也不靠消去分母来丢掉原式，求解后仍代回最初的分数关系。

工作台先展示整体乘，再展示每项分配，然后逐步合并与保解操作。每次点下一步前预测系数与符号。对原分母与公倍数说明为什么可逆，再写精确解。分数允许不约分的等值写法，但小数若有限且精确才能替代；例如三分之一不能写成零点三三。

家长请孩子为每一项指明公倍数从哪里来、分母约掉哪部分、负号作用到哪里。若孩子只说“把分母去掉”，用带整数项的例子追问，直到能说明等号两边每一项都做了什么。

# Linear equations involving fractions

## Clear denominators by scaling both whole sides

All denominators here are known nonzero constants, such as two, three and six. They do not contain x. Fractional coefficients obey the same equality properties. Multiplying both whole sides by a common multiple can make the arithmetic simpler. The least common multiple usually keeps numbers small, although any known nonzero common multiple works.

Every term receives the factor, including integers. Multiplying `x/2+1=3` by two gives `x+2=6`, not x+1=3 or x+1=6. Because the factor is nonzero, division can reverse the step and the solution set is preserved.

### Example 1: the minus acts on the complete numerator

For `(x−1)/2−(x+2)/3=1/6`, the least common multiple is six. Multiply both sides by six to obtain `3(x−1)−2(x+2)=1`. Expand to `3x−3−2x−4=1`. Negative two multiplies both x and positive two, contributing negative four.

Collect to `x−7=1` and add seven to both sides: x=8. Check the original: `7/2−10/3=21/6−20/6=1/6`. Writing −2x+4 would change the second group. Keeping numerator brackets until distribution makes the scope of the minus clear.

### Example 2: integers receive the factor too

For `(x+1)/3=x/2−1`, multiply both whole sides by six: `2(x+1)=3x−6`. The integer negative one becomes negative six. Expand, subtract 2x and add six on both sides to get x=8. In the original, the left is three and the right four minus one, also three.

In `x/3=1/6`, multiplication by six gives `2x=1`, hence x=1/2. This checks because `(1/2)/3=1/6`. Integer coefficients after clearing do not guarantee an integer solution. For `(2x−1)/3−(x+2)/4=1/6`, multiplication by twelve gives `4(2x−1)−3(x+2)=2`. Thus 5x=12 and x=12/5, checking as `19/15−11/10=1/6`.

## Keep the domain and precision visible

Unlike a variable denominator, our common multiple is known to be nonzero. We do not silently assume restrictions on x. Later equations with variable denominators require a separate domain analysis; do not transfer this procedure without its conditions. Keep the original fractional equation for the final substitution.

The workshop first scales the whole equation, then distributes to each term, then collects and solves. Predict coefficients and signs before revealing a step. Explain why the factor is nonzero and why division can reverse it. Equivalent unreduced fractions are accepted. A decimal is an exact replacement only if it represents the same rational number; 0.33 cannot replace one third.

A parent asks where each multiplier came from, which denominator factors cancelled and which terms the minus affects. If the learner says only “remove the denominators”, use the integer-term example until every term on both sides has an explicit explanation.
