# 判别式与根的个数

## 不必先算每个根，也能知道个数

对 ax²+bx+c=0 且 a≠0，判别式 Δ=b²−4ac 决定实数根的情况。Δ>0 时根号有正值，公式的正、负两分支不同，有两个不同实根；Δ=0 时两分支重合为 −b/(2a)，是二重根、一个不同实根；Δ<0 时根号在实数域没有值，所以没有实根。这里计数的是不同实数，不是公式上写了几个符号。

这条规则来自上一课配方后的平方方程，不能只记“正二零一负零”而忘记 a≠0。分母2a非零时，非零根式的两分支才保证不同；a=0时实际次数已变，推导不成立。判别式为零不表示 x=0，根的具体位置仍由 −b/(2a) 决定。

### 例 1：只改常数，根数改变

比较 x²−2x+c=0。c=0时，Δ=4，因式 x(x−2)=0，根为0、2，两不同值。c=1时，Δ=0，式子为 (x−1)²=0，唯一不同值1。c=2时，Δ=−4，配方得 (x−1)²=−1，没有实根。c=3时同样Δ<0，配方右边为−2。

图上可观察两个交点靠近、重合、然后消失的联系，但应由代数条件确认。重合时曲线接触横轴，只有一个位置，不应画两个间隔点假装两根。“零个根”的零在计数位置；把它填入求解题成为 x=0，意义完全不同。

### 例 2：看不见，不等于不存在

(x−8)(x−10)=0 的根为8、10。展开为 x²−18x+80=0，Δ=324−320=4>0，也说明两不同实根。若横轴只显示−4到6，两个根都在窗外，此时图中没有可见交点，方程却仍有两根。扩大到12便能看到它们；方程与代数根集从未改变。

再看0x²+2x−4=0。若盲算 b²−4ac 得4，然后按“正二”断言两根，会与原式 x=2 冲突。原因是它不是二次方程，应按一次方程处理。0x²+0x+0=0是恒等式，所有实数成立；常数非零等于零则矛盾，无解。这些情况不能硬塞进二次判别式的三行表。

## 判别式能说什么，不能说什么

判别式符号告诉实根的不同值个数，不自动给出所有根值，也不自动完成应用题范围筛选。一个矩形模型可以有两个实根，其中只有正根作长度；一个卡数模型可以有实根但没有整数根。等式根数与情境可行答案数量属于不同问题。即使Δ是完全平方数，也应算根并检查整数条件，不能只由“平方数”断言每个根是整数。

工作台允许切换常数、显示核验和扩大窗口。先写预测，再改变视图；视图改变保留原答案，方程条件改变则要重新作答。家长可追问：“你数的是不同值还是两条分支？a为什么非零？没有看到的部分怎么办？”这些提问把图像观察与代数证据连起来。

# Discriminant and number of roots

## Count before calculating every value

For ax²+bx+c=0 with a≠0, Δ=b²−4ac determines the real-root cases. Positive Δ gives a positive radical and two different formula branches. Zero Δ makes both branches equal −b/(2a): a double root with one distinct value. Negative Δ gives no real radical and hence no real roots. Count distinct numbers, not printed signs.

This follows from the completed-square equation. The nonzero-a premise is essential: it makes the denominator nonzero and ensures that nonzero radical branches remain distinct. A zero discriminant does not mean x=0; the position still depends on −b/(2a).

### Example 1: change only the constant

In x²−2x+c=0, c=0 gives Δ=4 and factors x(x−2), with roots zero and two. At c=1, Δ=0 and (x−1)²=0 has one distinct root, one. At c=2, Δ=−4 and (x−1)²=−1 has no real roots. At c=3 the squared target is −2, again impossible.

The graph connects two crossings, one contact and no crossing, but algebra confirms the cases. At contact there is one position; drawing separated markers would misrepresent the repeated root. Zero roots as a count is different from a root whose value is zero.

### Example 2: invisible roots still exist

The equation (x−8)(x−10)=0 has roots eight and ten. Expansion gives x²−18x+80=0 and Δ=324−320=4>0, confirming two distinct real roots. A window from −4 to six contains neither. Widening it to twelve reveals them without changing the equation or solution set.

Now consider 0x²+2x−4=0. Blindly using b²−4ac=4 and the positive-discriminant rule would predict two roots, contradicting x=2. The premise fails: this equation is linear. If both variable terms vanish, zero constant gives an identity with all real solutions, while a nonzero constant gives a contradiction. These do not belong in the quadratic three-case rule.

## Scope of the conclusion

The discriminant gives the number of distinct real equation roots. It does not supply every value or filter a contextual domain. A rectangle can have two algebraic roots and one positive width. A card model can have real roots but no whole-number count. Even a perfect-square discriminant does not make every root integral; inspect the formula denominator.

Predict before revealing the audit or changing the window. A display change preserves the task, while changing the actual equation requires a new answer. A parent can ask whether the learner is counting values or branches, why a is nonzero, and what might lie outside the visible region.
