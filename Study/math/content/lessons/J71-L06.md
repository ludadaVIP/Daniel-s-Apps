# 有理数混合运算

## 顺序来自算式结构

混合运算不是把看到的数字按喜欢的顺序配在一起。先处理括号指定的子式，再乘方、乘除、加减；同级的乘与除从左到右，同级的加与减也从左到右。已知性质允许某些等值变形，但必须说明性质，不能偷偷添加改变含义的括号。

平方表示同一底数乘自身两次，立方表示三次。底数是哪个整体，需要先圈出来。`(−2)²` 的底数是负二；`−2²` 的平方底数只有二，外面再取相反数。这个外部负号与负底数不是同一种结构。工作台会把内部平方先算完，再处理外部取相反数，让过程看得见。

### 例 1：先解决括号，保留其余算式

计算 `(−3/4+1/2)×(−2/3)`。

1. 原式保留外面的乘负 `2/3`，先把括号换成 `−3/4+2/4=−1/4`。
2. 整个算式于是成为 `(−1/4)×(−2/3)`。
3. 精确乘得 `2/12=1/6`。

只写一个 `−1/4` 作为最终答案，会把尚未做的乘法丢掉。每一步写完整余下算式，能防止这种遗漏。也可以合法使用分配律另算后核对，但“先乘后加”不能直接越过题目中的括号而不做相应等值变形。

### 例 2：括号与同级顺序都重要

`−2²=−(2×2)=−4`，而 `(−2)²=(−2)×(−2)=4`。它们数值相反，不是一个排版差别。类似地，`−3²+(−3)²=−9+9=0`，不能把两项都算成正九。

再算 `(−6)÷2×(−3)`：同级从左，先得负三，再乘负三得九。如果先算 `2×(−3)` 并把它当整个除数，就改成 `(−6)÷[2×(−3)]=1`。一是另一个算式的正确结果，不能成为原式的等值步骤。工作台的括号开关会直接对照这两种结构。

## 精确中间量不能提前丢掉

`1/2−[−3/4−(−1/4)]` 先把内层算成负半，再算 `1/2−(−1/2)=1`。每个减号都要看它作用于哪个完整数或子式，不因外层还有减号就把内层符号乱改。

`(−1/2)²−(3/4)÷(−3/2)` 的平方为正四分之一，除法为负二分之一，最后是 `1/4−(−1/2)=3/4`。这里所有中间量都能精确保留；不必先写近似小数再靠四舍五入凑答案。

## 中间出现零除数要停止

对任意有理数 a，`1÷(a−a)` 先算括号为零，接下来就出现零除数，整个算式未定义。它不是结果零，也不是把两个 a “约掉”得到一。约去非零乘法因子与两个数相减是不同结构；有理数除法从来没有承诺对零除数也给一个数值。

工作台逐步展示每次处理的子式，以及替换之后的整个表达式。切换家族、参数或括号后，旧步骤和旧答案会清空。解释时说出本步允许的理由、其余部分如何保留、是否改变原结构。家长可以先让孩子指出故意错误的括号位置，再核查符号与精确数值；最终数字偶然相同，不能证明中间理由正确。

# Mixed operations with rational numbers

## Follow the expression’s structure

Mixed operations do not permit pairing numbers in any preferred order. Resolve bracketed subexpressions, then powers, multiplication/division and addition/subtraction. Equal-precedence multiplication and division follow the original left-to-right order, as do addition and subtraction. A justified property can produce an equivalent rearrangement, but silently inserting meaning-changing brackets cannot.

A square multiplies the same base twice; a cube uses three copies. Identify the whole base first. In `(−2)²`, the base is negative two. In `−2²`, only two is squared, followed by taking its opposite. The workshop evaluates the power before an outside minus, keeping these operations visible.

### Example 1: preserve the rest of the expression

Calculate `(−3/4+1/2)×(−2/3)`.

1. Retain the outside multiplication while evaluating `−3/4+2/4=−1/4`.
2. The complete remaining expression becomes `(−1/4)×(−2/3)`.
3. Its exact result is `2/12=1/6`.

Reporting only negative one quarter drops an unfinished multiplication. Rewrite the whole remaining expression at each step. Distributivity could provide another legitimate route, but skipping brackets without the corresponding equivalent transformation is invalid.

### Example 2: both brackets and equal precedence matter

The value of `−2²` is `−(2×2)=−4`, while `(−2)²=(−2)×(−2)=4`. Their difference is structural, not cosmetic. Similarly, `−3²+(−3)²=−9+9=0`; both terms are not positive nine.

Now evaluate `(−6)÷2×(−3)`. From the left, divide to get negative three, then multiply by negative three to obtain nine. Combining `2×(−3)` first as the entire divisor changes the expression to `(−6)÷[2×(−3)]=1`. One is a valid answer to a different expression, not an equivalent step for the original. Toggle workshop brackets to compare the structures directly.

## Keep exact intermediate quantities

For `1/2−[−3/4−(−1/4)]`, the inner bracket gives negative one half, then `1/2−(−1/2)=1`. Each minus acts on a particular number or subtree. An outer minus does not justify arbitrarily changing inner signs.

In `(−1/2)²−(3/4)÷(−3/2)`, the square is one quarter and the quotient is negative one half. The final result is `1/4−(−1/2)=3/4`. Preserve exact fractions rather than rounding intermediates and trying to recover an intended answer later.

## Stop at an intermediate zero divisor

For any rational a, `1÷(a−a)` first produces a zero denominator, making the entire expression undefined. It is neither zero nor one. Subtracting identical values is not cancellation of a nonzero common multiplicative factor.

The workshop reveals each completed subtree and the full expression after replacement. Changing family, parameter or brackets clears old steps and answers. Explain why each step is allowed, what remains unchanged and whether the original structure is preserved. A coincidentally correct final number cannot validate an invalid intermediate argument.
