## 除以每份量，是在数有多少份

2 L 水按每份 3/4 L 量取，问的是两升中包含多少个标准份量，不是“取两升的四分之三”，也不是“平均分给四个人”。先读单位：已有量是升，每份量也是升，商表示这样的份数。

有三个相关却不同的问题：全部水相当于几份，能装满几份，装满后剩多少升。精确商保留不足一份；只数完整份则取能实际装满的整数个；余量又回到升单位。题目问哪种，就给对应量，不能把这三种答案混写。

### 例 1 · 两升里装四分之三升的份

1. 已有 **2 L**，目标每份 **3/4 L**。
2. 用共同小份 **1/4 L**：总量八小份，每份需要三小份。
3. 精确份数为 **8÷3=8/3 份**，即两又三分之二份。
4. 两个完整份用 **2×3/4=3/2 L**，剩 **2−3/2=1/2 L**；第三个完整份还缺水。
5. 剩半升包含两个四分之一，而目标一份包含三个四分之一，因此剩下的是 **2/3 份**，不是余 2/3 L。

核查两种表达都恢复原水量：**(8/3)×(3/4 L)=2 L**，或 **2×3/4 L+1/2 L=2 L**。分数三分之二的参照是一份四分之三升标准量；余半升的参照是一升，两者虽描述同一剩余水，单位和整体不同。

### 例 2 · 一份比已有水还大

1. 只有 **1 L**，标准每份是 **3/2 L**。
2. 把两种量都改为半升小份：已有两个，标准一份需要三个。
3. 全部水相当于 **2/3 份**，精确商 **1÷3/2=2/3**。
4. 一个完整份也装不满，但不能因此把精确商说成零。
5. 核回 **2/3×3/2 L=1 L**，说明不足一份的关系合理。

对照除以半升：3 L 中每升含两个半升，合计 **3÷1/2=6 份**，份数比三大。这并不创造水；变小的是标准每份量，因而同样总水能数出更多份。不能说任何除法都使数值变小，也不能把升数三与份数六当同类实物大小比较。

工作台横轴始终用升，每个上方括框只表示一份除数的量。最后不足一份的地方保留虚框，不把它涂满。先写共同小份，再比较个数，最后核对精确份数、完整份数与余量。除数改为零时，不生成图或无穷大答案，而说明零份量不能确定一般商。

总量为零、每份为正则合法，答案零份；不能与除以零混淆。**带走一个发现：** 除法先看每份量和问题所求，精确商不自动取整。家长可问“剩半升是几分之一升，又是几分之一份”，听孩子说出不同参照。出口题换成三升与三分之二升，仍需保留不足一份。

## Dividing by group size counts groups

Measuring 2 L in 3/4 L groups asks how many standard amounts the total contains. It differs from taking three quarters of two litres or sharing between four people. The available amount and group size both use litres; the quotient counts groups of that size.

Distinguish three questions: exact group count, number of complete groups and remaining litres. An exact quotient includes an incomplete group. Complete groups use an integer count, while the remainder returns to litres. Answer the requested quantity rather than mixing these meanings.

### Example 1 · Three-quarter-litre groups in two litres

1. Available water is **2 L**; a standard group is **3/4 L**.
2. Use **1/4 L** pieces: eight are available and three form one group.
3. Exact count is **8÷3=8/3 groups**, or two and two thirds.
4. Two complete groups use **3/2 L**, leaving **2−3/2=1/2 L**; a third cannot be filled.
5. The remainder contains two quarter-litres compared with three in a target group, so it is **2/3 of a group**, not 2/3 L left.

Check either **(8/3)×(3/4 L)=2 L** or **2×3/4 L+1/2 L=2 L**. Two thirds uses a three-quarter-litre group as reference, while half a litre uses a litre. Both describe the same remaining water through different reference units.

### Example 2 · A group larger than the available amount

1. Only **1 L** is available and a standard group is **3/2 L**.
2. In half-litre pieces, two are available while three are required per group.
3. The total represents **2/3 of a group**, so **1÷3/2=2/3**.
4. No complete group can be filled, but the exact quotient is not zero.
5. Check **2/3×3/2 L=1 L**.

Conversely, 3 L contains six half-litres: **3÷1/2=6 groups**. This creates no water. A smaller standard amount makes more groups from the same total. Division need not reduce the numerical value, and litres and group counts are different measurement objects.

The workshop’s axis stays in litres while each bracket spans one divisor-sized group. The last incomplete group retains an unfilled guide. First identify common small pieces, then compare counts and check exact groups, complete groups and the remainder. Choosing zero as group size gives an explanation rather than an invented infinite answer.

Zero total with a positive group size is valid and gives zero groups. Distinguish this from a zero divisor. **Takeaway:** interpret the relationship and requested quantity before deciding whether a whole-group answer is needed. A parent can ask how the remaining half-litre relates both to a litre and to a standard group. Transfer to 3 L with 2/3 L groups in the exit ticket.
