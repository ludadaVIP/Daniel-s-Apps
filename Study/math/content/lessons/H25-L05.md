# 基本函数的求导规则

从单点变化率走向导数函数：f′(x)给每个允许输入处的瞬时变化率，而f′(a)只是在某个输入处求值。规则让我们不必每次重新展开，但必须知道它们适用于什么运算。

## 本课的四个基本规则

常数函数c的导数恒为0，因为所有非零h的输出变化都是0。对正整数n，有(xⁿ)′=nxⁿ⁻¹。常数倍可以保留，有限和差可以逐项求导。这些规则都可由差商与有限极限的运算得到，而不是把原函数当成一串随意改写的符号。

常数情形单独处理。不要把n=0生硬套成0·x⁻¹，然后在x=0制造原题不存在的除零；常数函数在0也可以有导数0。本课自动练习与工作台使用次数不超过4的有理系数多项式。一般乘积和复合函数法则在H26学习，非多项式求导需另外核规则和定义域，不能从这里盲目类推。

### 例1：规则背后的平方与三次差商

对x²，在任意固定输入x，非零h的差商为2x+h，极限2x。对x³，差商为3x²+3xh+h²，极限3x²。这说明指数如何成为系数、幂如何降低一阶。

推广到正整数n时，展开(x+h)ⁿ−xⁿ，常数项相消；一次h项为nxⁿ⁻¹h，其他项至少含h²。除非零h再令h→0，剩nxⁿ⁻¹。这个推导用前一单元的因子展开与二项式结构，也解释为什么不能把有限h下的高阶项提前说成精确0。

### 例2：函数、导数函数、导数值

f(x)=3x³−2x+5，逐项求导得f′(x)=9x²−2。原常数5的变化量0；−2x的导数为−2，不是−2x。代入x=2后，f′(2)=9·4−2=34。

在x=1，原函数值f(1)=6，导数值f′(1)=7。6、7、9x²−2是三个不同答案，分别回应不同问题。再如g(x)=(1/2)x²−3x，g′(x)=x−3，分数系数同样保留精确运算。

## 别把和差规则当乘积规则

“(x²·x)′=(x²)′·x′=2x”错误。先用已知代数化简x²·x=x³，再求导得到3x²；在x=1，正确斜率3而非2。保留常数倍不等于允许保留另一个随x变化的因子。

工作台紫色表示原函数，绿色表示导数函数；D点位于绿色图上，它的高度是原曲线相应处斜率，不是原曲线高度。先预测关于x的完整表达式，再代a求值。改变a不改导数函数预测；显示视窗不改变任何答案。

# Derivative rules for basic functions

A derivative function f′(x) gives instantaneous rates across allowed inputs; f′(a) evaluates that function at one input. Rules avoid repeated expansion, but their permitted operations and domains still matter.

## Four basic rules in this lesson

A constant c differentiates to zero because every nonzero-increment output change is zero. For a positive integer n, (xⁿ)′=nxⁿ⁻¹. Constant multiples remain, and finite sums or differences can be differentiated term by term. These rules follow from quotients and finite limit operations rather than arbitrary symbolic editing.

Handle constants separately. Do not mechanically substitute n=0 into 0·x⁻¹ and introduce division by zero at x=0; a constant function can have derivative zero there. Automatic exercises and the workbench use rational-coefficient polynomials of degree at most four. General products and compositions are studied in H26; nonpolynomial rules require their own conditions and must not be guessed from these examples.

### Example 1: Square and cubic quotients behind the rules

For x² at a fixed input x, the nonzero-h quotient is 2x+h, tending to 2x. For x³ it is 3x²+3xh+h², tending to 3x². These identities explain the exponent becoming a coefficient and the power decreasing by one.

For a general positive integer n, expand (x+h)ⁿ−xⁿ. The constant term cancels; the linear-h term is nxⁿ⁻¹h and every remaining term contains at least h². Divide by nonzero h, then let h approach zero to obtain nxⁿ⁻¹. The previous unit’s binomial structure explains why higher-order terms vanish in the limit rather than being exactly zero at a finite increment.

### Example 2: Function, derivative function and derivative value

For f(x)=3x³−2x+5, termwise differentiation gives f′(x)=9x²−2. Constant five has zero change, while −2x contributes −2 rather than −2x. At x=2, the derivative value is 9·4−2=34.

At x=1, the original value is six and the derivative value seven. Six, seven and 9x²−2 answer different questions. For g(x)=(1/2)x²−3x, the derivative is x−3; rational coefficients retain exact arithmetic too.

## Do not turn the sum rule into a product rule

The claim (x²·x)′=(x²)′·x′=2x is false. Simplify the original product to x³ and differentiate to 3x². At one the correct slope is three, not two. Preserving a constant multiple does not permit preserving an additional varying factor in the same way.

The workbench draws the original function in purple and its derivative in green. Point D lies on the green graph; its height is the original curve’s slope, not its height. Predict the full expression in x before evaluating at a. Changing a preserves the derivative-function prediction, while display changes preserve every mathematical answer.
