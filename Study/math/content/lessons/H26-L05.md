# 变化率应用题

先说明什么量随什么变量改变，再求导。一个带数字的导数答案只有放回单位、符号与有效范围，才有情境意义。本课所有故事都是明确给定规则的虚构理想模型，不把精确公式当成真实测量事实。

## 量、平均率、瞬时率

若位置s(t)以米计、时间t以秒计，s′(t)是有向速度，单位米/秒；速率是|s′(t)|，非负。位置负说明在参照原点的负方向，速度负说明位置随时间减少，这两件事不同。

平均速度用两个不同时间的位移差除以时间差；瞬时速度先对函数求导，再代时刻。偶然数值相同不能把两个定义混为一谈。范围端点没有双侧的情境观测，本课用给定光滑公式的延拓导数，等价于向域内看的单侧变化率来解释，不声称拥有范围外资料。

### 例1：零速度只是一瞬间

给定位移s(t)=t²−4t+3米，0≤t≤4秒。求导s′(t)=2t−4米/秒。在t=1，位置0米，有向速度−2米/秒，速率2米/秒。

在t=2，位置−1米，速度0；(0,2)内速度负、(2,4)内速度正，表示在2秒转向。s(0)=3与s(2)=−1不同，故绝不能因一个瞬间速度0就说整段静止。速率在转向时为0，而不是变负。

[0,2]平均速度=[s(2)−s(0)]/2=(−1−3)/2=−2米/秒，恰好等于t=1瞬时速度。但t=0的公式端点率为−4，t=2为0，瞬时率并不恒为−2。不要把平均速度的负号去掉后称作一般的平均速率；有转向时总路程还需分段计算。

### 例2：边长率与面积率的单位不同

假设正方形边长ℓ(t)=1+2t米，只授权0≤t≤3秒。面积A(t)=ℓ²=(1+2t)²平方米。用链式法则A′=2(1+2t)·2=4+8t平方米/秒。也可把面积写成ℓ·ℓ，用乘积法则得到ℓ′ℓ+ℓℓ′=2ℓℓ′。

在t=1，边长3米，面积9平方米，面积变化率12平方米/秒。6漏乘内导2；9是面积，不是面积率；4是把两个边长率相乘，单位平方米/秒²，也不对。t=2时边长5米、面积25平方米、面积率20平方米/秒，边长率仍为2米/秒，说明相同的边长增长率不代表面积率恒定。

单位核验：2ℓ以米计，ℓ′以米/秒计，相乘才为平方米/秒。先做单位核验可以识别把量与率混答，却不能单靠单位推导全部系数。

## 范围不是可有可无的装饰

t=−1时边长公式给−1米，不符合正方形边长情境，也超范围。t=10虽给正数21米，仍超出授权区间，不能因为“算得出来”就断言真实边长或面积率。本课工作台在范围外让情境预测填undefined，另外显示代数延拓导数，明确区分“模型没有支持”与“代数表达式根本不存在”。图形只画有效时间段，不画假观察点。

先预测有向变化率，再比较速率或面积；打开核验表时核单位与范围。出口要求写量、率、单位、时间限制和外推理由。家长可让孩子用一句完整话解释“12平方米/秒”，再追问这个数是否适用于范围外。答案必须由家长核论证，工程浏览记录不算孩子掌握证据。

# Applied rates of change

Name the changing quantity and its input variable before differentiating. A numerical derivative becomes a contextual answer only when its units, sign and authorised domain are supplied. These are fictional ideal models with explicitly given rules, not claims about measured reality.

## Quantity, average rate and instantaneous rate

If position s(t) is measured in metres and time t in seconds, its derivative is signed velocity in m/s. Speed is |s′(t)| and is nonnegative. Negative position locates the object relative to an origin; negative velocity means position decreases with time. They are different statements.

Average velocity divides displacement change by a nonzero time increment. Instantaneous velocity differentiates the function and evaluates at one time. Numerical agreement does not merge those definitions. At an endpoint there are no two-sided contextual observations. Here an endpoint rate means the derivative of the supplied smooth extension, equivalently the inward one-sided rate, without inventing observations outside the range.

### Example 1: Zero velocity lasts an instant

Let s(t)=t²−4t+3 metres for 0≤t≤4 seconds. Differentiating gives s′(t)=2t−4 m/s. At t=1 the position is zero metres, signed velocity −2 m/s and speed two m/s.

At two seconds the position is −1 metre and velocity zero. Velocity is negative on (0,2) and positive on (2,4), so the model reverses direction at two. Since s(0)=3 and s(2)=−1 differ, one zero-velocity instant cannot mean rest throughout the interval. Speed is zero at reversal; it does not become negative.

The average velocity over [0,2] is [s(2)−s(0)]/2=(−1−3)/2=−2 m/s, equal by coincidence to the instantaneous velocity at one. The endpoint formula gives −4 at zero and zero at two, so velocity is not constantly −2. Taking the absolute value of average velocity does not in general produce average speed: total distance must account for any reversal.

### Example 2: Side rate differs from area rate

A square has side ℓ(t)=1+2t metres, authorised only for 0≤t≤3 seconds. Its area is A(t)=ℓ²=(1+2t)² m². The chain rule gives A′=2(1+2t)·2=4+8t m²/s. Writing the area as ℓ·ℓ and applying the product rule independently gives ℓ′ℓ+ℓℓ′=2ℓℓ′.

At one second the side is three metres, the area nine square metres and the area rate twelve square metres per second. Six omits the inner rate two; nine is area rather than area rate; four multiplies two side rates and has units m²/s². At two seconds the side is five metres, area twenty-five square metres and area rate twenty m²/s. The side rate remains two m/s, showing that constant side growth need not give constant area growth.

For a dimensional check, 2ℓ has units metres and ℓ′ has units m/s. Their product has the required m²/s units. Units expose confusion between quantities and rates, although they cannot determine every numerical coefficient by themselves.

## A domain constrains the conclusion

At t=−1 the side formula gives −1 metre, incompatible with a square side and outside the stated range. At ten it gives the positive number twenty-one metres, but that time is still unauthorised. Being able to substitute algebraically does not establish actual side length or area rate there. Outside the range, the workbench asks for undefined as the contextual prediction and separately lists the algebraic extension derivative. Lack of model support is distinguished from algebraic nonexistence. The graph draws only valid times and supplies no fake observation point.

Predict a signed rate, compare speed or area, then audit units and domain. The exit requires quantity, rate, units, time restrictions and an explanation about extrapolation. A parent can ask for a complete sentence interpreting twelve square metres per second and whether it applies outside the range. Human review assesses the argument; browser engineering records are not evidence of a child’s mastery.
