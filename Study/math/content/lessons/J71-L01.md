# 正负数与相反数

## 把负数的世界从整数扩展到分数

在 P64 中，我们用整数描述零两侧的位置。现在保留同一个单位长度，把每一单位分成更小的等份，负分数与负小数就有了准确位置。`−3/2` 在零左侧一点五单位；它不是“还没有办法放上去”的特殊符号，也不因为不是整数就失去大小关系。

有理数是可以写成 `p/q` 的数，其中 p、q 都是整数，且 q 非零。整数也在里面，例如 `−2=−2/1`，零可写成 `0/1`。有限小数可化成整数之比，例如 `−1.5=−15/10=−3/2`。循环小数也可表示为分数，例如 `0.333…=1/3`，这里省略号表示三一直重复；有限的 0.333 只是 `333/1000`，并不等于三分之一。我们不把“所有小数都如此”当作定义。

分母可先规范成正数，再用分子的正负确定方向。例如 `3/(−2)=−3/2`，而 `(−3)/(−2)=3/2`。零既不正也不负，且没有另一个不同位置叫“负零”。数轴右侧的数大于左侧的数，不随写成整数、分数还是小数而改变。

### 例 1：不同写法，同一个位置

比较 `−3/2`、`−6/4` 与 `−1.5`。

1. 每单位二等分时，向左三个半单位到 `−3/2`。
2. 改为每单位四等分，同样一段需要六个四分之一，即 `−6/4`。
3. 一点五单位也写作 1.5，因此位置仍是 `−1.5`。

改变等分精细程度没有改变原来的一单位。相反数是在同一数轴上关于零的反射，所以是 `3/2=1.5`。用加法核验，`−3/2+3/2=0`。在工作台先判断正、负或零，再写相反数，最后显示反射点检查；不要只凭点的颜色猜答案。

### 例 2：相反数不是倒数

对 `−3/2`，相反数为 `3/2`，因为两数之和零；倒数为 `−2/3`，因为两数之积一。前者改方向、不改到零距离，后者要求非零并取乘法逆关系，通常连大小也改变。验证条件不同，不能交换名称。

零的相反数仍零，因为 `0+0=0`；零却没有倒数，因为不可能用任何数乘零得到一。再看 `−(−5/3)`：外面的负号表示取相反数，对零左侧的 `−5/3` 再反射，结果是正 `5/3`。需要先知道每个负号的作用，而不是把所有负号不加区别地删掉。

## 正负与大小都保留原单位

`−2.05` 大于 `−2.5`，因为前者更靠右。虽然 2.5 大于 2.05，带上负号后的数并不是只比较无符号数字。零到两点距离不同，与哪一个数“更大”也不是同一个问题；下一课会专门区分距离与位置。

自己解释时，至少给出三个证据：能写成整数之比、在同一单位数轴上定位、与相反数相加为零。家长可问：“写成小数后它为什么没有移动？你现在核验的是和为零还是积为一？”明确关系比背一个符号口诀更可靠。这里先建立有理数的位置与相反数概念，运算规则将在后续课程中逐一说明。

# Positive, negative and opposite numbers

## Extend the scale beyond integers

P64 used integers on either side of zero. Keep the same unit length and divide each unit into equal parts to locate signed fractions and decimals exactly. The number `−3/2` is one and a half units left of zero. Being non-integer does not remove its position or ordering.

A rational number can be written `p/q`, where p and q are integers and q is nonzero. Integers qualify: `−2=−2/1`, and `0=0/1`. A terminating decimal is a ratio too: `−1.5=−15/10=−3/2`. Repeating decimals also have fractional representations; `0.333…=1/3` means that the threes continue forever. The finite decimal 0.333 is only `333/1000`, not exactly one third. Do not replace the definition by a claim about every decimal.

Normalise a denominator to positive before reading the sign: `3/(−2)=−3/2`, while `(−3)/(−2)=3/2`. Zero is neither positive nor negative, with no separate negative-zero position. A point farther right is larger regardless of whether its label is an integer, fraction or decimal.

### Example 1: different notation, one position

Compare `−3/2`, `−6/4` and `−1.5`.

1. With halves, move three half-units left to `−3/2`.
2. With quarters, the same length needs six quarter-units, giving `−6/4`.
3. One and a half units is 1.5, so the position is also `−1.5`.

Finer subdivisions do not change the original unit. Reflecting this position across zero gives its opposite `3/2=1.5`. Check the defining relationship: `−3/2+3/2=0`. In the workshop, classify the original number, enter its opposite and then reveal the reflection. Colour alone is not evidence.

### Example 2: opposite is different from reciprocal

The opposite of `−3/2` is `3/2`, because their sum is zero. Its reciprocal is `−2/3`, because their product is one. Reflection changes direction while preserving distance to zero. A reciprocal is a multiplicative inverse, requires a nonzero input and usually changes magnitude. The checking conditions are different.

Zero is its own opposite since `0+0=0`, but has no reciprocal: no number multiplied by zero produces one. In `−(−5/3)`, the outside minus takes the opposite of the already negative `−5/3`, producing positive `5/3`. Identify the role of each sign before simplifying.

## Explain both sign and order

The number `−2.05` is larger than `−2.5` because it lies farther right, even though unsigned 2.5 is larger than 2.05. Distance to zero and numerical order are different questions; the next lesson explores that distinction.

A checkable explanation gives a ratio of integers, a position on a common scale and a zero sum with the opposite. Ask why a decimal rewrite does not move the point and whether a claimed inverse is checked by sum zero or product one. These relationships will support the operation rules developed in the following lessons.
