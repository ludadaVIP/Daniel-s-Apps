# 等比数列

每次乘同一个数，与每次加同一个数，是不同结构。倍率有符号和绝对值，项值也有自己的正负；“倍率更大”不能直接代替对整个数列的判断。

## 一条核心想法

**本单元等比数列要求每项非零，且每个相邻商aₙ₊₁/aₙ都等于同一个非零q。** 此时aₙ=a₁qⁿ⁻¹。从第一项到第n项只乘n−1次，代n=1必须还原首项。

完整乘法递推可以包含q=0或a₁=0，并能唯一计算后项；但零分母会破坏上述相邻比值定义。这里把“可计算的递推”与“符合本单元等比定义”分开，不用0/0来补公比。

### 例1：负倍率与负首项各有作用

a₁=3、q=−2，项为3、−6、12、−24，所以a₄=3(−2)³=−24。负倍率使符号交替，绝对值3、6、12、24增长，项值却不是严格递增或递减。把a₄写成24只保留了幅度。

另取a₁=−2、q=2，项为−2、−4、−8、−16。正倍率保留负号，绝对值变大而项值更小，因此q>1不单独保证递增。判断顺序要同时看首项符号与倍率，不能只看|q|。

若q=−1、a₁=3，则3与−3交替，绝对值恒3；负公比本身不表示“向下走”。这会在下一课的奇偶有限和中再出现。

### 例2：衰减、常数与零边界

a₁=4、q=1/2，第五项为4(1/2)⁴=1/4，仍为正且非零，不能因屏幕小数很小就画成真正的0。q=1、a₁=5时每项5，相邻比1而公差0，所以既等比又等差。

对a₁=3、每次乘0的递推，得到3、0、0、…。从第一项到第二项的商0/3=0有定义，但再往后0/0无定义，因而整列不符合本单元非零比值等比定义。给定倍率0不等于每一个商都存在。

全零递推也可算，却不能把0/0规定为1以称它等比。它是公差0的等差数列。非零常数列与零常数列在“等差”上相同，在本单元“等比”定义上不同。

## 倍率与相邻商工作台

分别预测aₙ、当前相邻商和完整递推是否符合等比定义。改变目标n只改变所问项/商，整列是否符合定义的预测保持。遇到零分母必须写无定义，不把数值0和无定义混同。

可对照绝对值点，但它是另一组观测，不覆盖原有的符号。图中离散点不连成连续曲线，超窗项不会压在边框上，精确表继续保留真正数值。

## 家长核对与迁移

要求孩子逐次写乘法，说明指数为何n−1，并分开描述项值与幅度。对零递推，先指出具体分母再讨论定义，不能只说“老师规定不行”。后续求和仍能对有限递推项相加，但公式的使用和等比分类的前提必须分别核查。

# Geometric sequences

Multiplying by a fixed number gives a different structure from adding one. The multiplier has both sign and magnitude, and term values have their own signs. A larger multiplier alone cannot classify how all terms behave.

## One central idea

**This unit defines a geometric sequence by nonzero terms and one fixed nonzero adjacent quotient aₙ₊₁/aₙ=q.** Then aₙ=a₁q^(n−1). Term n is n−1 multiplications beyond term one, so n=1 must recover the initial value.

A complete multiplicative recurrence can still use q=0 or a₁=0 and determine later terms. But zero denominators break the stated quotient definition. Separate computable recurrence from geometric classification rather than assigning a value to 0/0.

### Example 1: Negative multiplier and negative first term

For a₁=3,q=−2, terms are 3,−6,12,−24, so a₄=3(−2)³=−24. Negative multiplication alternates signs. Magnitudes 3,6,12,24 grow, but the signed terms are neither strictly increasing nor decreasing. Answering positive twenty-four retains only the magnitude.

For a₁=−2,q=2, terms are−2,−4,−8,−16. Positive multiplication retains the negative sign: magnitudes grow while values decrease. Thus q>1 does not itself imply increasing values. Check the first-term sign as well as the multiplier.

For q=−1,a₁=3, values alternate 3 and−3 while magnitudes stay three. A negative ratio does not simply mean moving downward. This case returns in odd/even finite sums next lesson.

### Example 2: Decay, constants and zero boundaries

For a₁=4,q=1/2, a₅=4(1/2)⁴=1/4. Terms remain positive and nonzero; a small displayed decimal must not be flattened into an exact zero. For q=1,a₁=5, all terms are five, with ratio one and difference zero, hence both geometric and arithmetic.

Starting at three and multiplying by zero produces 3,0,0,…. The first quotient 0/3 is defined as zero, but later 0/0 is undefined. Therefore the whole recurrence fails this unit’s nonzero-ratio geometric definition. Specifying multiplier zero does not make every quotient exist.

An all-zero recurrence is also computable but cannot obtain ratio one by declaring 0/0=1. It is arithmetic with zero difference. Zero and nonzero constant sequences agree on being arithmetic but differ under this unit’s geometric definition.

## Multiplier and quotient workbench

Predict aₙ, the current adjacent quotient and whether the entire recurrence meets the geometric definition separately. Changing only n resets the term/quotient task while preserving classification of the complete recurrence. Use undefined at a zero denominator rather than confusing it with numerical zero.

Magnitude dots are an additional representation, not a replacement for signs. Discrete points are not joined into a continuous curve. Out-of-window terms retain exact values in the audit instead of being flattened to the frame.

## Parent check and transfer

Ask for the successive multiplications and why the exponent is n−1. Describe signed values separately from magnitudes. At zero, identify the actual denominator before discussing the definition rather than relying on a memorised prohibition. Later summation can still add finite recurrence terms, but calculation rules and geometric classification need their premises checked separately.
