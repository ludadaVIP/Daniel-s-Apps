# 轨迹方程

轨迹是满足原几何规则的全部点，不只是一个看起来合适的代数方程。证明“原轨迹点都满足方程”给必要性；还需证明“满足方程并保留限制的点都可由原运动得到”，才有充分性。消元时尤其容易忘记运动范围与被排除的点。

## 中点关系可以反向还原

固定A=(a,0)，动点P=(u,v)，其中点M=(x,y)。中点公式给x=(a+u)/2、y=v/2。反过来u=2x−a、v=2y，所以给定M就能唯一重构P。

若P在u²+v²=r²完整圆上，代入得到(2x−a)²+4y²=r²，或(x−a/2)²+y²=r²/4。圆心为(a/2,0)，半径r/2。这是原点为中心的二分缩放再按固定端点平移的中点轨迹，不应把半径平方r²/4误读成半径r²/4。

### 例1：写方程，并完成逆向验证

取A=(2,0)、源圆u²+v²=16。代P=(2x−2,2y)得到(2x−2)²+4y²=16，化成(x−1)²+y²=4。圆心(1,0)，半径2、半径平方4。

源点P=(0,4)给M=(1,2)，确有(1−1)²+2²=4。要证明不漏不多，不能只验这个样本：任取候选圆上一点M，重构P=(2x−2,2y)。候选方程保证u²+v²=16，中点公式又恢复原M。因此完整源圆运动时，候选整圆的每点都可达。

这个逆构造比“图上点看起来都在小圆上”更强。它既证明所得点不在圆外，也证明候选圆没有多余点。有限画点只能帮助发现错误，不能代替对任意点的论证。

### 例2：方程不变，允许的轨迹却变了

改限源点v≥0，只在上半圆运动。因为v=2y，轨迹还必须y≥0。候选M=(1,−2)满足小圆方程，却重构到P=(0,−4)，违反原运动范围，所以它不在原几何轨迹。

若进一步排除源端点P=(4,0)，其唯一中点是M=(3,0)，也必须排除。完整结论是(x−1)²+y²=4、y≥0、M≠(3,0)。另一端M=(−1,0)仍可保留，因为它对应P=(−4,0)，原运动没有排除这个点。不能含糊地把两个端点都去掉。

## 区分计算候选与合法轨迹点

工作台可改变固定点、源圆半径、运动范围和四个源候选。即使选择不允许的下半圆源候选，代数中点仍能计算；这不表示它成为原轨迹点。表中单独给资格，图只标合法P/M，避免把一个能算出来的坐标误认成可达点。

灰虚线完整源圆仅作参照，紫线才是当前允许的中点轨迹。排除端点用空心圈表示。方程任务单独检查完整圆方程，几何资格任务检查额外条件；完整报告则必须把两者合起来说明。

## 出口与家长提问

用“从源点得到方程”和“从候选点重构源点”两段组织证明。写清固定点、源圆、允许运动范围、方程、圆心、半径和端点。

家长可追问：你刚才证明了必要还是也证明了充分？M=(3,0)在源端点允许与不允许时分别怎样？学生若只报圆方程，就请他重构一个下半圆反例，检查遗漏的是哪条运动限制。

# Locus equations

A locus is the full set of points permitted by the original geometry. Showing that every moving point satisfies an equation establishes necessity. To establish sufficiency, show that every candidate satisfying both the equation and retained restrictions can be produced by the original motion.

## A midpoint relation can be inverted

Fix A=(a,0), let P=(u,v) move, and write its midpoint with A as M=(x,y). Then x=(a+u)/2 and y=v/2. Conversely, u=2x−a and v=2y, so a candidate M uniquely determines a source P.

If P traverses u²+v²=r², substitution gives (2x−a)²+4y²=r², equivalently (x−a/2)²+y²=r²/4. The centre is (a/2,0), radius r/2 and squared radius r²/4. Distinguish a radius from its square.

### Example 1: Derive the equation and prove the reverse direction

Let A=(2,0) and the source circle be u²+v²=16. Substituting P=(2x−2,2y) gives (2x−2)²+4y²=16, or (x−1)²+y²=4. Its centre is (1,0), radius two and squared radius four.

Source P=(0,4) gives M=(1,2), which satisfies the candidate equation. That example alone is not a full proof. Instead take an arbitrary candidate M on the small circle and reconstruct P=(2x−2,2y). The candidate equation ensures P lies on the original circle, while the midpoint formula recovers M. Thus, for the full source-circle motion, every point of the candidate circle is attainable.

This argument does more than plotting several points. Forward substitution rules out points outside the candidate equation; reverse reconstruction rules out extra candidate points. A finite trace is an inspection aid, not a proof about arbitrary points.

### Example 2: The same equation, a different allowed locus

Restrict the source to v≥0. Since v=2y, the midpoint locus additionally requires y≥0. Candidate M=(1,−2) satisfies the small-circle equation but reconstructs to P=(0,−4), outside the allowed source motion. It is not a point of the original geometric locus.

Now also exclude source endpoint P=(4,0). Its unique midpoint is M=(3,0), so exclude that point too. The complete locus is (x−1)²+y²=4 with y≥0 and M≠(3,0). The other endpoint M=(−1,0) remains because its source P=(−4,0) is allowed. Do not remove both endpoints without justification.

## Separate computation from admissibility

Change the fixed point, source radius, motion restriction and source candidate. Even a forbidden lower-half candidate has a computable algebraic midpoint, but computation does not make it attainable under the stated motion. The audit records admission separately, and the graph marks only admissible P/M points.

Grey dashed is the full source-circle reference; purple is the currently allowed midpoint locus. An open circle marks the excluded endpoint. The automatic equation task checks the candidate circle, while the admission task checks geometric restrictions. A complete written answer must combine them.

Organise the proof in two directions: start from an allowed source to derive the equation, then reconstruct an allowed source from an arbitrary admissible candidate. Report the fixed point, source circle, motion range, equation, centre, radius and endpoint conditions. Ask whether a proof established necessity only or sufficiency too. Comparing the status of M=(3,0) under two different endpoint rules reveals why an equation alone is insufficient.
