# 等比数列求和

3、6、12、24的末项是24，前四项和却是45。把整个和乘2会得到6、12、24、48，尾部48不能因为许多项抵消就被忽略。

## 一条核心想法

**写出一份有限和及其q倍，按相同幂次对齐再相减。** Sₙ=a₁+a₁q+…+a₁qⁿ⁻¹，而qSₙ=a₁q+…+a₁qⁿ。中间项抵消后，(1−q)Sₙ=a₁−a₁qⁿ。

仅在q≠1时，才能除1−q，得Sₙ=a₁(1−qⁿ)/(1−q)。q=1时每项相同，直接Sₙ=na₁；不把0/0规定为某个值。项数n仍是有限正整数，不能未经说明改成无限多项。

### 例1：末项与错位后的尾项

取a₁=3、q=2、n=4。S₄=3+6+12+24，2S₄=6+12+24+48，按同幂次对齐相减得−S₄=3−48=−45，故S₄=45。

原末项a₄=3×2³=24；错位后的尾项是3×2⁴=48。两个指数相差1，是因为第二份整体乘了一次2。不能在尾端仍写24而少掉那次乘法。

若a₁=4、q=1/2、n=4，和是4+2+1+1/2=15/2。公式给4(1−1/16)/(1−1/2)=15/2，保留有限尾项。8与它还差1/2，不能直接把有限和替换成一个常见无穷和结果。

### 例2：q=1与负公比的有限和

a₁=5、q=1、n=6时，每项5，所以S₆=30。错位恒等式变成0×S₆=0，对任意S₆都成立；它没有提供能恢复S₆的新约束，更不能推出S₆=0。

a₁=3、q=−1时，六项3−3+3−3+3−3=0；改成五项则为3。公式也给同样结果，因为(−1)ⁿ的奇偶不同。求和保留符号，不以绝对值和18或15代替。

q=0的乘法递推同样可以加有限项，例如3、0、0、0之和3；这只是有限递推求和的计算，不改变上一课零商无定义的等比分类。本课标准等比数列仍要求各项和公比非零。

## 错位相减工作台

先预测Sₙ、乘q后的尾项和(1−q)Sₙ，再展开对齐核验。划去的是两个完整有限表达式中的相同项，不是随意删掉一个原数列项。n=1时没有中间项可消，也仍有同一恒等式。

改变图窗保留预测，不改变n。q=1时恒等式0=0必须与原和一起解释；不自动进行非法除法。有限和与无穷和之间需要额外的定义、条件和极限论证，不能只因图中后项变小就丢掉尾项。

## 家长核对与迁移

解释题对1+1/2+1/4要求给7/4并指出尾项，出口再问0=0为什么不等于未知量0。家长核查的是每一步的对象、指数和可除条件。储蓄课将把每次存款按剩余期数增长的贡献组合成有限等比和，同时说明整个余额不一定本身等比。

# Sums of geometric series

The last term of 3,6,12,24 is twenty-four, but their four-term sum is forty-five. Multiplying the entire sum by two gives 6,12,24,48. The tail forty-eight cannot disappear merely because many middle terms cancel.

## One central idea

**Write one finite sum and its q multiple, align equal powers, then subtract.** Sₙ=a₁+a₁q+…+a₁q^(n−1), while qSₙ=a₁q+…+a₁q^n. Cancellation leaves(1−q)Sₙ=a₁−a₁q^n.

Only when q≠1 may we divide by 1−q to get Sₙ=a₁(1−q^n)/(1−q). At q=1 each term is identical and Sₙ=na₁ directly. Do not define 0/0 to fit a formula. A finite positive term count is not silently replaced by infinitely many terms.

### Example 1: Original last term versus shifted tail

Take a₁=3,q=2,n=4. S₄=3+6+12+24 and 2S₄=6+12+24+48. Subtracting aligned expressions gives−S₄=3−48=−45, so S₄=45.

The original last term is a₄=3×2³=24. The shifted tail is 3×2⁴=48. The exponents differ by one because every term of the second copy was multiplied by two. Leaving twenty-four at that tail loses the final multiplication.

For a₁=4,q=1/2,n=4, the sum is 4+2+1+1/2=15/2. The formula 4(1−1/16)/(1−1/2) agrees, retaining the finite tail. Eight still exceeds the sum by one half; a familiar infinite-sum value cannot replace this finite result.

### Example 2: Unit ratio and negative-ratio sums

For a₁=5,q=1,n=6, six terms of five total thirty. The shifted identity becomes 0×S₆=0, true for any S₆. It supplies no recoverable constraint on the unknown and does not force S₆=0.

For a₁=3,q=−1, six terms 3−3+3−3+3−3 total zero, whereas five total three. The formula agrees because parity changes(−1)^n. Keep signed terms rather than reporting absolute-value sums eighteen or fifteen.

A zero-multiplier recurrence can still be summed finitely:3,0,0,0 totals three. This calculation does not remove the undefined quotients from the preceding definition lesson. Standard geometric sequences here still require nonzero terms and ratio.

## Shifted-subtraction workbench

Predict Sₙ, the shifted tail and(1−q)Sₙ before revealing alignment. Crossed-out entries are equal terms in two complete finite expressions, not arbitrarily removed original terms. At n=1 no middle terms cancel, yet the same identity holds.

A window change preserves work and leaves n unchanged. At q=1 the identity 0=0 must be interpreted alongside the original sum; no division by zero is performed. Relating a finite sum to an infinite one needs separate definitions, conditions and a limit argument, not merely visually shrinking points.
