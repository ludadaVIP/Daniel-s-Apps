## 把括号当整体，按相反次序撤销

**3(x+2)** 描述先把 x 加二，再把整个结果乘三。要求它等于十五，就要从输出倒着回去：最后做的乘三先撤销，再撤销加二。每一步都对等号两边同做，写下中间关系；不能把“逆运算”变成只操作未知数那一边。

本课只处理明确的一步至两步方程。分母是给定非零常数，不含未知数；不需要随意试很多数，也不要求解一定是整数。括号内的固定量可能有负号，代入负数或分数时保留整个数。

### 例 1 · 除整体与正确展开两条路

1. 解 **3(x+2)=15**。先两边同除三，得 **x+2=5**。
2. 两边同减二，得 **x=3**。
3. 代回原式：**3(3+2)=3×5=15**。
4. 另一条路：先展开为 **3x+6=15**，再两边同减六得 3x=9，同除三仍得 x=3。
5. 错误展开 3x+2 漏乘了括号中的二。每一份都有二，三份固定量是六。

两条正确路径都可以接受。选择先除整体常常更短，但关键是说明每一步保持原关系。把三直接“删掉”而右边仍十五，就会得到 x+2=15，这是单边改变，已经不是原方程。

### 例 2 · 不能把分数解悄悄四舍五入

1. 将右边改成五：**3(x+2)=5**。
2. 同除三得 **x+2=5/3**，不能把 5/3 先约成整数。
3. 同减二：**x=5/3−6/3=−1/3**。
4. 代回原式：括号里 −1/3+2=5/3，再乘三恰好五。
5. 若填 −0.33，代回得到 **3×1.67=5.01**，不是五。因此它是近似，不能作为这道精确方程的解。

若式子是 **(x−4)/2=3**，最后做的是除二。先两边同乘二得 x−4=6，再同加四得 **x=10**。代回 (10−4)/2=3。不要沿着原式正向“先减四再除二”处理右边，那是在计算另一个数，并非撤销已有过程。

零也是可能的解：5(x+1)=5 先同除五，再同减一，得到 x=0。方程并未因为出现零而失去未知量或变成“不存在答案”。同时区分“x=0”和“把两边乘零”：前者是某个候选值，后者会丢失原限制。

工作台可切换三种原方程，输入任意合法变形和候选分数，并保留每一步。先说预计撤销哪个动作，再执行；故意只改左边后使用恢复按钮重新开始，避免继续依赖已破坏的条件。最后核验必须回到原式。

家庭挑战：三行纸片每行 n 个再加两个，改为三袋每袋 n+2 个，写出两个不同式子。接着解三倍“某数加二”等于十五或五，记录同做运算并核查精确答案。家长认可正确展开和先除整体两种路径。带走一个发现：**保留括号整体，倒序撤销，分数精确，代回原式。**

## Treat brackets as a whole and undo in reverse order

**3(x+2)** applies two operations: add two to x, then multiply the whole result by three. To recover x from an output of fifteen, reverse the process: undo the final multiplication first, then the addition. Apply every operation equally to both equation sides and record the intermediate relationship.

These lessons use explicit one- and two-step equations with fixed nonzero denominators, rather than denominators containing the unknown. Solutions need not be integers. Retain the complete sign or fraction when substituting into brackets.

### Example 1 · Divide the whole bracket or distribute correctly

1. For **3(x+2)=15**, divide both sides by three: **x+2=5**.
2. Subtract two from both: **x=3**.
3. Check the original: **3(3+2)=15**.
4. Alternatively expand to **3x+6=15**, subtract six to get 3x=9, then divide by three to get x=3.
5. The expansion 3x+2 omits multiplication of the fixed two. Three groups contain six fixed units.

Both valid paths are acceptable. Dividing the whole bracket may be shorter, but each step must preserve the original relationship. Simply deleting the factor three on the left while leaving fifteen on the right creates x+2=15, a different equation.

### Example 2 · Keep a fractional solution exact

1. Change the right side to five: **3(x+2)=5**.
2. Divide by three to get **x+2=5/3**.
3. Subtract two: **x=5/3−6/3=−1/3**.
4. Substitution gives −1/3+2=5/3, multiplied by three to make exactly five.
5. Substituting −0.33 instead gives **3×1.67=5.01**. That finite decimal is an approximation, not the exact solution requested.

In **(x−4)/2=3**, the final operation is division by two. Multiply both sides by two to obtain x−4=6, then add four to obtain **x=10**. Verify (10−4)/2=3. Applying the original forward sequence to the right side—subtracting four and then dividing by two—computes another value instead of undoing the process.

Zero can also solve an equation. In 5(x+1)=5, divide by five and subtract one to obtain x=0. A zero solution does not mean no answer exists. Distinguish assigning x=0 from multiplying both sides by zero: the first tests a candidate while the second destroys the original restriction.

Select original equations in the workshop, predict which action to undo, and enter exact fractional candidates. The record retains each transformation. After deliberately changing only one side, restore the original before constructing a valid path; do not continue relying on a damaged condition. Finish by checking in the original equation. For a family task, contrast three rows of n with two extras overall against three bags each holding n+2. Then solve three times “a number plus two” equal to fifteen or five. Record equal actions and verify exact answers. Accept either correct distribution or division of the bracketed whole. The takeaway combines whole-group meaning, reverse operation order, exact fractions and original substitution.
