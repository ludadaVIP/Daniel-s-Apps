# 坐标描点与读点

## 描点不是只数格子

描点前先看两轴名字、原点、正方向、相邻刻度数值。一个小格可以是一、二或二分之一，两轴还可以采用不同单位长度。横坐标决定一条平行 y 轴的位置线，纵坐标决定一条平行 x 轴的位置线，它们的交点才是目标。

读点是反过来的过程：从点作与轴平行的投影，分别读 x、y，再按有序数对书写。不能沿斜着的连线猜坐标，也不能因为点“看起来在左上”就只写象限而漏具体数值。点在轴上时，另一个坐标仍须明确为零。

### 例 1：从标尺读一个整数点

图中 A 投影到横轴负二、纵轴三，故 `A=(−2,3)`。不是 `(3,−2)`，也不是把左方向的负号漏成正二。负号表示方向，不是说点不存在。

若横轴每格二，A 在原点左三格，就有 `x=−3×2=−6`；这时即使纵轴一格为一，也不能照抄到横轴。把图的显示范围改宽后，A 的数值位置不变，只需按新刻度读回相同数对。

### 例 2：半单位点与轴上点

描 `P=(3/2,−1/2)`，在每格二分之一的网格上，先向右三格，再向下一个半单位格。注意“向下一个格”在这张图表示负二分之一，不是负一。投影回去可读 `1.5` 与 `−0.5`，与精确分数等值。

描 `(0,−3)` 时不用先横移，直接在 y 轴下三单位处标点。描 `(0,4)` 同理在 y 轴上四单位；`(4,0)` 则在 x 轴，位置不同。工作台同时支持整数点、半单位点和轴上点，不把所有坐标都限制成整数。

## 位置变化分别作用于两个坐标

从 `(−2,−1)` 向右五、向上三，终点 `x=−2+5=3`、`y=−1+3=2`，所以为 `(3,2)`。不能只把移动量 `(5,3)` 当终点，也不能把两个方向的变化合成一个数。

关于 x 轴反射时，横位置不变，纵位置取相反数，所以 `(−3,2)` 的像为 `(−3,−2)`。这是按题目明确给出的反射规则练习坐标，不需要靠图上看似对称猜两个符号都变。

描点板可点格点，也可聚焦后按方向键，每一步都依本轴刻度移动。选择新任务会回到原点并清空旧检查，避免上一图的成功状态混入新刻度。先解释横纵两条位置线，再核对点；操作方式只是表达工具。

家长可以只改一个轴的刻度，让孩子说明图上格数为何改变而数对不变；也可给一个带零或负分数的点，要求先说方向再描。若描错，分开检查顺序、符号与单位大小，通常比笼统说“粗心”更能找到原因。

# Plotting and reading coordinates

## Plotting requires scales, not just square counts

Inspect axis names, origin, positive directions and adjacent tick values first. A square may represent one, two or a half, with different intervals on the two axes. The x-coordinate selects a line parallel to y, the y-coordinate a line parallel to x, and their intersection is the point.

Reading reverses this process: use axis-parallel projections, read x and y separately and preserve their ordered slots. A diagonal connection does not directly give a coordinate. A quadrant name is not a complete numerical position, and an axis point still needs its zero entry.

### Example 1: read an integer point from labelled axes

If A projects to negative two horizontally and three vertically, `A=(−2,3)`, rather than `(3,−2)` or `(2,3)`. The negative sign records direction, not an impossible point.

With x intervals of two units, three ticks left gives x=−6. A vertical interval of one does not change the horizontal scale. Widening the viewing window changes the display, but reading the new labels should recover the same data pair.

### Example 2: fractional and axis points

For `P=(3/2,−1/2)` on half-unit axes, move three half-unit ticks right and one tick down. That downward tick represents negative one half rather than negative one. The exact decimals 1.5 and −0.5 name the same pair as the fractions.

For `(0,−3)`, no horizontal move is needed: locate the point three units below zero on y. Similarly `(0,4)` is four units up the y-axis, while `(4,0)` is on x and is a different point. The activity includes integers, halves and zero coordinates.

## Apply changes to the appropriate coordinate

Starting at `(−2,−1)`, five right and three up gives x=3 and y=2, so the endpoint is `(3,2)`. The displacement `(5,3)` is not the endpoint. Keep the two directional calculations separate.

Reflection in x preserves horizontal position and reverses vertical position. Thus `(−3,2)` maps to `(−3,−2)`. Follow the stated transformation rather than assuming both signs reverse from a symmetric-looking sketch.

Click a grid intersection or focus the plotting board and use arrow keys. Each move follows that axis interval. A new task resets the candidate and preceding feedback so success under one scale does not transfer to another without new work.

A parent can change one scale and ask why square counts change while the pair does not. For a zero or negative fraction, state direction before plotting. Diagnose order, sign and unit size separately rather than labelling every mistake as carelessness.
