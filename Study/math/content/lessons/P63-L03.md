## 整体对应两项相加的份数

把 25 L 按 A:B=2:3 分配，不是给 A 两升、B 三升，也不是先把二十五除以三。比的两项是**权重份数**，整个二十五升对应的是 **2+3=5 份**。先找到总份数，才能知道每份实际有多少。

条形图每格等宽，代表一个相同权重份；格上的 A、B 字母标接收对象。实际升数由总量除总份数计算，不能把权重数字直接当单位量。无论比是否最简，相同关系应给出相同分配结果。

### 例 1 · 二十五升分成五份

1. 总量 **25 L**，权重 **A:B=2:3**。
2. 总权重 **2+3=5 份**，每份 **25÷5=5 L**。
3. A 取两份：**2×5=10 L**；B 取三份：**3×5=15 L**。
4. 核对总量：**10+15=25 L**，水没有丢失或增加。
5. 核对原比：**10:15=2:3**；也可看 **10×3=15×2**。若只检查两份额相加正确，还不能保证按了正确的比。

另一种方法是先写部分占整体的分数：A 占 2/5，B 占 3/5，再分别乘二十五。这里分母五来自两项之和，不能误用后项三，算成二十五乘 2/3。

### 例 2 · 连续量保留分数份额

1. 现在总量为 **7 L**，仍按 **A:B=2:3** 分配。
2. 每份 **7÷5=7/5 L**，即 1.4 L。
3. A 得 **14/5 L=2.8 L**；B 得 **21/5 L=4.2 L**。
4. 核对 **14/5+21/5=35/5=7 L**，而两项仍为 2:3。
5. 不能因为图有整格，就把结果截成二升与四升；那只分出六升，既丢量又破坏原比。

实际对象会影响能否按完整件分配。连续水量可以有分数升；若七张卡必须保持完整，就不能按 2:3 精确分完全部七张。应说明条件限制，或另行讨论可以拆分的对象，不能擅自把数学分配结果取整。

**零总量：** 按 2:3 的计划分配零升，两个接收者都得零，没有新增水。权重仍是计划，但实际量 0:0 没有可定义的除法比值，因此不能用零除零来“核查相同比”。零情况核对总量与分配等式即可。

工作台改变总量或任一权重时，旧反馈会清空。先数条形的总格数，再预测哪一方得更多，最后核对。若把权重同时放大，例如从 2:3 到 4:6，虽然每份的计数单位变小，两部分实际升数应保持不变；这连接了上一课的等值比。

**家长陪学：** 让孩子说清总量对应几份、每份多少、两区各得多少，并同时核对和与比。允许先画条形或用部分占整体的分数，不要求固定算法。**带走一个发现：** 按比分配把整体分成两项权重之和的等份，分别取份，最后保留总量与关系。

## The whole corresponds to the sum of both weights

Sharing 25 L in ratio A:B=2:3 does not give A two litres and B three, or begin by dividing twenty-five by three. The terms are **allocation weights**. The whole twenty-five litres corresponds to **2+3=5 parts**. Find that total before calculating an actual amount per part.

Equal-width bar cells represent equal weight parts, lettered A or B for their recipients. Litres per cell come from total divided by all parts; the weights themselves are not measured litre amounts. Equivalent ratios should give the same allocations, whether simplified first or not.

### Example 1 · Twenty-five litres in five parts

1. The total is **25 L** and the weights are **A:B=2:3**.
2. There are **five parts**, each **25÷5=5 L**.
3. A receives **2×5=10 L** and B **3×5=15 L**.
4. Check the total: **10+15=25 L**, with no quantity lost or created.
5. Check the ratio: **10:15=2:3**, or **10×3=15×2**. A correct sum alone does not guarantee the intended ratio.

Alternatively, A receives 2/5 and B 3/5 of the whole. Multiply each fraction by twenty-five. The denominator five is the sum of both weights; using the second term three and multiplying twenty-five by 2/3 would use the wrong reference.

### Example 2 · Preserve fractional shares of continuous quantities

1. Change the total to **7 L**, retaining **A:B=2:3**.
2. Each part is **7÷5=7/5 L=1.4 L**.
3. A gets **14/5 L=2.8 L** and B **21/5 L=4.2 L**.
4. Check **14/5+21/5=35/5=7 L**, with the ratio still 2:3.
5. Whole drawn cells do not justify truncating the amounts to two and four litres. That allocates only six litres and changes the relationship.

The object’s nature affects feasibility. Continuous water allows fractional litres. Seven indivisible cards cannot all be shared exactly in ratio 2:3 while remaining whole. Explain that restriction instead of silently rounding the mathematical allocations.

With a zero total, both recipients receive zero. The intended weights still describe a plan, but actual 0:0 cannot define a division quotient. Check the allocation equations and conserved zero total without calculating zero divided by zero. The empty workshop cells do not invent water.

Changing a total or weight clears checked feedback. Count all bar parts, predict which recipient gets more, then check. Doubling both weights from 2:3 to 4:6 changes the counting unit but should preserve the actual allocations, connecting to equivalent ratios.

**For a parent:** ask how many parts correspond to the total, how much each part holds, and how much each recipient receives. Check both sum and ratio. A bar-first explanation or a part-of-whole fraction method is welcome. **Takeaway:** divide the whole into the sum of both weights, allocate the relevant shares, and preserve both total and relationship.
