# 组合的意义

组合数的是**从不同对象中不重复选出的成员集合**。同一集合换书写顺序仍是同一个结果。重点不是“看到选人就除一个数”，而是解释每个无序结果在有序清单里重复了多少次。

## 为什么除以r!

先数有序、不重复的r个成员，共P(n,r)种。同一r人集合可以排成r!个不同顺序，而且每一集合的表示次数都一样。因此P(n,r)=C(n,r)r!，得到C(n,r)=n!/[r!(n−r)!]，0≤r≤n。

C(n,0)=C(n,n)=1：只选空集或只选全体各有一种。r>n时无合法子集，数为0。C(n,r)=C(n,n−r)可用“选入组成员”与“选未入组成员”的一一对应解释，无需背两个互不相关的公式。

对象必须不同且每个至多选一次。若按相同字母而非实体卡编号区分结果，同字母交换可能不产生新字串；不同组的重复倍数可能不一致，不能盲目除r!。本课不把“忽略顺序”混成“允许重复”。

### 例1：有序清单归组

从A,B,C,D选两个同权成员，有六组AB,AC,AD,BC,BD,CD。写AB只是方便标记集合，BA没有成为第七组。每组在有序选法里恰有两个表示，例如AB与BA，故P(4,2)=12=6×2!。

从五人选三人同权组，先有5×4×3=60种顺序，每组有3!=6个顺序，所以60/6=10组。也可指定不入组的两人，C(5,2)=10。若选队长、记录员、联络员三个不同职位，则顺序对应职责，仍需60种，不能归组。

### 例2：用组合计数求概率

袋内有编号R1,R2,B1,B2,B3。均匀无放回抽两个，忽略抽取先后。每个无序编号球对都有两个等可能有序表示，所以十个球对等可能，总数C(5,2)=10。

恰一红一蓝时，从2红选1、3蓝选1，2×3=6个球对，概率6/10=3/5。结果不是三个“颜色类别”RR,RB,BB等可能；颜色类别包含的编号球对数分别为1、6、3。若抽取机制偏爱某些编号，计数比就不能直接当概率。

## 从分组图回到理由

工作台把所有有序结果按排序后的成员集合归组。每一行对应一个组合，旁边保留它的全部r!个顺序。这些清单是成员与顺序的关系，不是抽样观察频数。r=0时空集有一个空顺序，分组关系仍成立。

出口要求列出四人中全部两人组，证明每组同样有两个顺序。家长可让孩子圈出AB和BA所在的同一行，再询问“如果给两个成员不同职位，为什么又要分开？”理解这一问比熟练代阶乘更能支持后面的二项式系数。

# Combinations

A combination is a **subset of distinct objects selected without reuse**. Reordering the same membership does not create another outcome. The key is not to divide whenever a problem mentions choosing people, but to explain exactly how often each unordered outcome appears in an ordered list.

## Why divide by r!?

First count the ordered nonrepeating selections: P(n,r). Every r-member subset has exactly r! different orders, with the same multiplicity for every subset. Therefore P(n,r)=C(n,r)r! and C(n,r)=n!/[r!(n−r)!] for 0≤r≤n.

There is one empty subset and one whole-set subset, so C(n,0)=C(n,n)=1. Selecting more than n distinct members without reuse is impossible and gives zero. The equality C(n,r)=C(n,n−r) pairs each selected subset with its excluded complement; it is a direct correspondence, not a separate rule to memorise.

Distinct objects and no reuse matter. If outcomes are distinguished by repeated letters rather than physical card identities, swapping equal letters may produce no new string. Multiplicities can differ, invalidating a blind division by r!. Ignoring order is not permission to reuse an object.

### Example 1: Group an ordered list

From A,B,C,D the equal-role pairs are AB,AC,AD,BC,BD,CD. AB conveniently names a set; BA is not a seventh team. Each team has two ordered representations, such as AB and BA, so P(4,2)=12=6×2!.

Choosing three equal-role members from five first gives 5×4×3=60 ordered selections. Every team has 3!=6 orders, leaving ten teams. Alternatively choose the two excluded members, giving C(5,2)=10. If the roles are captain, recorder and liaison, order does represent different responsibilities and all sixty assignments remain distinct.

### Example 2: Probability from combinations

A bag contains numbered balls R1,R2,B1,B2,B3. Select two uniformly without replacement and ignore draw order. Every unordered numbered pair has exactly two equally likely ordered representations. The C(5,2)=10 unordered pairs are therefore equally likely.

Exactly one red and one blue requires one of two reds and one of three blues, giving six pairs and probability 6/10=3/5. The three colour categories RR,RB,BB are not equally likely: they contain respectively one, six and three numbered pairs. If the selection mechanism favours particular identities, a simple favourable-count ratio is no longer automatically the probability.

## Turn the grouped table into an explanation

The workbench groups every ordered selection by its sorted membership. One row is one combination, with all its r! orders retained beside it. This table expresses a structural correspondence, not observed sampling frequencies. When r=0, the empty subset has one empty order, preserving the same relationship.

For the exit, list every pair from four people and show that every team has exactly two orders. A parent can ask the learner to locate AB and BA in the same row, then explain why assigning different roles would separate them again. That explanation supports the coming binomial coefficients more directly than substituting factorials without defining outcomes.
