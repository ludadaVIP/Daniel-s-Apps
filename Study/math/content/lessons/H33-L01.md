# 随机变量与概率分布表

随机实验的结果可以是文字、路径或编号。随机变量把每一个样本结果记成一个实数：不是一个尚未求出的固定未知数，而是实验前不确定、实验后由结果决定的数值。选什么映射，取决于问题要记录什么。

## 结果空间与数值空间

独立公平抛两次硬币，样本结果TT、HT、TH、HH各概率1/4。若X记录正面数，它们分别映到0、1、1、2。X只有三个不同取值，却不是各1/3：有两个不同结果被记作1，必须把这两份概率相加。

离散分布表逐个列出不同数值及对应概率。概率质量非负，总和恰为1，这两个条件同时需要。总和1不能允许负项；全为非负也不能漏掉一部分总质量。不要把观测频数直接写成理论概率，除非已说明如何由实验模型得到这些权重。

### 例1：从四条结果归并成三行

对正面数X，TT给P(X=0)=1/4；HT和TH互斥，所以P(X=1)=1/4+1/4=1/2；HH给P(X=2)=1/4。输入完整表可写0:1/4;1:1/2;2:1/4。行可重排，数值与概率的配对不能变。

P(X≥1)=1/2+1/4=3/4，也可用1−P(X=0)。P(X=0或2)=1/4+1/4=1/2。两个取值互斥，概率可相加；并不是把这两个数值本身相加。

如果改成“正反相同记1，不同记0”，TT和HH映1，HT和TH映0，分布成为0和1各1/2。原四个结果与其概率没有改变，只改变记录问题的数值函数。

### 例2：完整性与合法性都要核

某变量只取0、1、2，概率写成1/4、p、1/4。用总和1得到p=1/2，并确认三项非负。如果写−1/4、5/4，虽然和为1，但负概率不合法；写1/4、1/4则只覆盖一半总概率，不能当完整分布。

再看编号票：四张等可能的票，数值标记−1、1、1、3。数值分布为−1:1/4;1:1/2;3:1/4。不同编号仍是不同结果，同数值只合并质量。负数是合法的随机变量取值；负概率才不合法。

## 用映射解释，不只记一张表

工作台列出每条硬币结果与其数值，可切换正面数、是否同面、正面数两倍减一。先自行构造完整表，再打开质量图核查。分离柱的高度是点概率，柱间空隙不表示连续取值；不要把离散图直接当密度曲线。

自动表格输入每个不同数值只写一行，用分号隔开。支持有理分数与精确小数，拒绝重复值、漏行、负项和总和不为1。含零概率项的表可以保留指定的计数槽，但概率非零的取值才属于这里所说的支持集。

家长可让学生沿着“样本结果→数值→合并质量”讲一遍，追问哪个值合并了多个结果。出口应同时核非负与归一化，并解释为何“不同值有三个”不能替代等可能证明。概率分布来自实验结构或明确给出的模型，不来自对图形对称的随意猜测。

# Random variables and distribution tables

A random variable maps every experimental outcome to a real number. It is not merely a fixed unknown waiting to be solved. Before the experiment its value is uncertain; after the outcome, the chosen numerical map determines it. Different questions can use different maps on the same experiment.

## Outcomes differ from numerical values

Two independent fair tosses have four equally likely outcomes TT, HT, TH and HH. If X counts heads, these map to 0,1,1,2. There are three distinct values, but they are not equally likely. Two disjoint outcomes contribute to the value one.

A discrete table lists each distinct value and its probability mass. Every mass must be nonnegative and the total exactly one. A total of one does not excuse negative entries; nonnegative entries alone do not ensure complete coverage. Observed frequencies and theoretical probabilities also have different origins.

### Example 1: Merge four outcomes into three rows

TT gives P(X=0)=1/4. HT and TH are disjoint, so P(X=1)=1/4+1/4=1/2. HH gives P(X=2)=1/4. The full response can be entered as0:1/4;1:1/2;2:1/4. Row order may change, but each value must remain paired with its mass.

The probability of at least one head is1/2+1/4=3/4, also equal to 1−P(X=0). The probability of zero or two is1/4+1/4=1/2. Add probabilities of disjoint values, not the values themselves.

Change the map to one for matching faces and zero otherwise. TT and HH now map to one, while HT and TH map to zero. Each value has mass 1/2. The original outcomes and their masses have stayed the same; only the recorded numerical question changed.

### Example 2: Check completeness and validity

Suppose the only values are0,1,2 with probabilities 1/4,p,1/4. Total one forces p=1/2, and all entries are nonnegative. The pair−1/4,5/4 totals one but is invalid. The pair1/4,1/4 is nonnegative but does not form a complete distribution.

Four equally likely numbered tickets labelled−1,1,1,3 give masses1/4,1/2,1/4 at values−1,1,3. Distinct ticket IDs remain different outcomes; equal labels merge their masses. Negative values are allowed. Negative probabilities are not.

## Explain the map behind the table

The workbench lists individual outcomes before asking for a full table. Switch numerical maps and trace how groups change. Open the mass plot afterwards: separated bar heights are point probabilities, and the gaps are not a continuous density.

Enter each distinct value once, separated by semicolons. Exact fractions and finite decimals are supported. Duplicate values, missing rows, negative masses and incorrect totals are rejected. A specified table may retain zero-probability count slots; its support here consists of positive-mass values.

A parent should ask which outcomes merge and whether both validity conditions hold. Explain why counting distinct values does not prove equal likelihood. The table must reflect a stated experiment or model, rather than a guess based on the picture.
