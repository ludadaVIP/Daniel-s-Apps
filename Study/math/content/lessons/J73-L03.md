# 含括号的一元一次方程

## 括号是运算结构，不是装饰

方程一边的 `2(x−3)` 表示整个 `x−3` 乘二。展开用分配律：外部二分别乘 x 与负三，得到 `2x−6`。这里用的是对所有 x 有效的表达式恒等；随后才在等式两边同做操作求解。展开和移项的依据不同，不能把常数漏乘后再靠移项补出答案。

负号也有明确的作用范围。`−(x+2)` 是负一乘整组，成为 `−x−2`；`−3(x−2)` 中负三乘负二得到正六。分数因子同样要乘每项。先把括号内每一项与外部因子连线，再写展开式，能让每个贡献有出处。

### 例 1：两边都含未知数

解 `2(x−3)=x+1`。

1. 展开左边：`2x−6=x+1`。
2. 两边同减 x：`x−6=1`。
3. 两边同加六：`x=7`。
4. 代回带括号的原式：`2(7−3)=8`，`7+1=8`。

若错误地写成 `2x−3=x+1`，会求出四，但代回原式左边二、右边五，不相等。这说明原式检验能揭露中间漏乘；不能只检查自己写错后的等式。

### 例 2：负因子与整组减法

解 `−3(x−2)=2x+1`。先展开 `−3x+6=2x+1`，同减 `2x`、同减六得到 `−5x=−5`，再同除非零负五，得 `x=1`。代回原式，左边 `−3(1−2)=3`，右边 `2×1+1=3`。

对 `4(x−1)−(x+2)=0`，第二个括号的外部因子是负一，展开为 `4x−4−x−2=0`，所以 `3x−6=0`，解二。若写 `−x+2`，便只取反了一项。分数版本 `(1/2)(4x−6)=x+1` 展开为 `2x−3=x+1`，解四；原式两边都为五。

## 展开后先读系数，不要强行除

工作台还给出 `2(x+1)=2x+2` 和 `2(x+1)=2x+3`。前者展开后两边相同，消去后得到 `0=0`，所有有理数都满足；后者消去变量后为 `2=3`，因此无解。它们是一次系数抵消的退化比较案例，不是还可以同除某个非零系数的标准情形。

每次展开下一步前，先口头说出理由：这是各边表达式恒等整理，还是两边同减一个式子，或两边同除非零常数？看完步骤再求自己的精确解，不必把中间小数舍入。改变案例会重置步骤与旧答案，因为新的方程需要新的证据。

家长可让孩子把一个错误展开式与原式各代同一值，找到差别；随后再用逐项分配给出普遍理由。一次反例可推翻错误展开，但几个碰巧吻合的值不能取代分配律证明。最终要求读出原式两边，解释系数消失时该如何分类。

# Equations with brackets

## Brackets preserve the intended structure

In `2(x−3)`, the whole difference x−3 is multiplied by two. Distribution gives 2x−6 because two multiplies x and negative three separately. This is an expression identity valid for every x. We then use equal operations on the equation's two sides to solve. Expansion and transposition have different justifications.

A minus also has a scope. `−(x+2)` means negative one times the whole group, giving −x−2. In `−3(x−2)`, negative three times negative two gives positive six. Fractional factors also multiply every term. Connecting each inside term to its outside factor makes every contribution visible.

### Example 1: an unknown on each side

Solve `2(x−3)=x+1`. Expand to `2x−6=x+1`, subtract x from both sides to get `x−6=1`, then add six to both sides: `x=7`. In the original, `2(7−3)=8` and `7+1=8`.

The mistaken expansion `2x−3=x+1` gives four. Four fails the original because its left side is two and its right side five. Checking the original can expose a missed factor; checking only the mistaken intermediate equation cannot.

### Example 2: negative factors and a subtracted group

For `−3(x−2)=2x+1`, distribute to obtain `−3x+6=2x+1`. Subtract 2x and six from both sides: `−5x=−5`. Divide by nonzero negative five: `x=1`. The original sides are `−3(1−2)=3` and `2×1+1=3`.

In `4(x−1)−(x+2)=0`, the second factor is negative one, so expansion is `4x−4−x−2=0`. Thus `3x−6=0` and x=2. Writing −x+2 would negate only one term. A fractional example, `(1/2)(4x−6)=x+1`, expands to `2x−3=x+1`, giving four; both original sides are five.

## Read the remaining equation before dividing

The workshop also compares `2(x+1)=2x+2` and `2(x+1)=2x+3`. The first cancels to `0=0`, an identity for every rational. The second cancels to `2=3`, a contradiction with no solution. These are degenerate cancellation cases, not standard equations with a remaining nonzero x coefficient.

Before revealing a step, name its reason: an identity simplifying each side, subtraction of the same expression from both sides, or division by a known nonzero constant. Then write your own exact solution. Changing the case resets the preceding steps and answer because the new equation needs new evidence.

A parent can ask the learner to substitute the same value into a false expansion and its original to locate the discrepancy, then use distribution for the general explanation. One counterexample refutes a proposed identity; several matching values do not replace the distributive law. Finish by reading both original values and explaining what to do when variable coefficients disappear.
