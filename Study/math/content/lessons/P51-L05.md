## 先问共同整除，还是共同复现

最大公因数找能同时整除两个正整数的最大正整数；最小公倍数在本课指最小正公倍数，找同时是两者正倍数的最小数。一个从两批数量往下分，一个从间隔往上重复，不能看到两数就随便相乘。

一总是两个正整数的共同因数，因此最大公因数至少为一。零可以是共同零倍，但不是正数，也不能作为“下一次”共同出现的时点。先确认目标与正数约定，再比较共同集合里的最大或最小。

### 例 1 · 最多相同袋数

1. **18 红卡、24 蓝卡**平均分进相同数量袋，两批都不剩，每袋各色数量分别相同。
2. 袋数必须同时整除十八与二十四，是共同因数。
3. 十八的因数为 1、2、3、6、9、18；二十四的为 1、2、3、4、6、8、12、24。
4. 共有 **1、2、3、6**，最大为 **6 袋**，每袋三红四蓝。
5. 两袋也能分，却不是最多；核查 **6×3=18、6×4=24**，还要确认不存在更大共同因数。

### 例 2 · 下次共同出现

1. 两种纸上假想节奏从零同时开始，分别每 **8 分钟**、每 **12 分钟**出现。
2. 起点后的共同时点必须是两个间隔的正倍数。
3. 八的正倍数从 8、16、24 开始；十二的从 12、24 开始。
4. 首个正共同点为 **24 分钟**，满足 **24=8×3=12×2**。
5. 最大公因数四用于共同分组，不是共同复现；零是起点，不是下一次。

### 列表窗口不等于完整范围

前八个倍数只是一个查看窗口。比如十一与十二的首个正共同点为 132，需要到十一的第十二个正倍数、十二的第十一个正倍数。前八倍没看见共同点，不表示没有公倍数，也不能把两边最后显示的数当答案。

工作台会明确说明这个限制。展开核对时，分别延长两个正倍数列表直到首个共同点，保留所有更早值，让你检查之前确实没有重合。它不会用省略号隐藏更早的共同点，再把一个计算结果称为“最小”。

相同数的最大公因数与最小正公倍数都是这个数，例如六与六都为六；不是因为有两个六就相乘为三十六。八与九只有共同因数一，最大为一，不是零。二十四与三十六共同因数的最大值是十二，尽管六也是有效共同因数。

这里研究两个正整数，不用本课的列表处理含零的公因数特殊定义。工作台的输入从一开始；先说适用范围，避免把“零不是下一正共同点”误听成“零从不是倍数”。

**家庭挑战：** 二十四红卡与三十六蓝卡比较六袋和十二袋，说明为什么十二袋才最多；再列八分钟与十二分钟假想节奏，用不同关系找二十四分钟。用纸卡和列表完成，不实际购买、不等待计时、不计分。

## Ask whether the goal divides or repeats

A greatest common factor is the greatest positive integer dividing two positive integers exactly. Least common multiple here means the least positive common multiple. One goal divides quantities downward; the other repeats intervals upward. Do not multiply every pair of numbers automatically.

One is always a common factor of positive integers, so their GCF is at least one. Zero can be a shared zero multiple but is not positive or a next occurrence. Identify the goal and positive-number convention before comparing common values.

### Example 1 · Greatest identical bag count

1. Distribute **18 red and 24 blue cards** evenly among the same bags, with equal counts of each colour in every bag and no leftovers.
2. Bag count must divide both batches, making it a common factor.
3. Factors are 1,2,3,6,9,18 and 1,2,3,4,6,8,12,24.
4. Shared values **1,2,3,6** have greatest **6 bags**, holding three red and four blue each.
5. Two bags also work but are not the greatest. Check **6×3=18 and 6×4=24**, then confirm no larger common factor exists.

### Example 2 · Next common occurrence

1. Paper rhythms begin together at zero and repeat every **8 and 12 minutes**.
2. A later shared time is a positive multiple of both intervals.
3. Lists begin 8,16,24 and 12,24.
4. First positive shared time is **24 minutes**, with **24=8×3=12×2**.
5. GCF four answers shared grouping rather than recurrence; zero is the starting instant rather than next.

### A list window is not the whole range

Eight displayed multiples form a viewing window. Eleven and twelve first share 132, reached by twelve intervals of eleven and eleven intervals of twelve. Their first eight lists do not overlap, but common multiples still exist.

The workshop states this limit and expands both positive lists through the first shared value when checking. Earlier entries remain displayed so the absence of an earlier overlap can be verified. It does not hide earlier common points behind an ellipsis and call an unsupported result least.

For identical numbers, GCF and least positive common multiple equal that number: six and six give six, not thirty-six. Eight and nine have only common factor one, giving GCF one rather than zero. Twenty-four and thirty-six have GCF twelve even though six is also common.

These lists concern two positive integers. Inputs begin at one and do not address special zero-inclusive GCF conventions. “Zero is not the next positive common time” must not become the false claim that zero is never a multiple.

**Family challenge:** compare six and twelve bags for twenty-four red and thirty-six blue cards, then use eight-minute and twelve-minute paper rhythms to find twenty-four minutes. Explain why the goals use different relationships, without shopping, timed waiting or scoring.
