# 有理指数与根式

如果一个正数平方后得到16，这个正数是4。有理指数把“反做乘方”纳入同一套符号，但必须先说明底数范围和主值约定。

## 一条核心想法

**本课对正底数a定义a^(m/n)=(ⁿ√a)^m，其中m/n先约分，n为正整数。** 正底数的主根取正值，结果单值；负指数再取倒数。正底数范围保证这些步骤和指数法则互相一致。

分母说取几次根，分子说再取几次幂。例如a^(2/3)先取立方根再平方，不是a乘2再除以3。指数1/2与小数0.5是同一个数，可以表示同一种运算。

### 例1：先根，再幂，再检查倒数

27^(2/3)=(³√27)²=3²=9。将结果立方得729，原底数平方也为729，符合指数关系。16^(3/4)=(⁴√16)³=2³=8，而不是16×3/4=12。

(1/16)^(−3/4)先取四次根1/2，取三次幂得1/8，再按负指数取倒数，结果8。81^(−1/2)=1/√81=1/9。每一步都说明对应哪个指数部分，可减少把负指数误写负值的错误。

### 例2：一个函数值与两个方程根

25^(1/2)=√25=5，是主值。求方程x²=25的全部实根，则x=−5或5。两根代回都成立，但平方根记号本身不附带±。同理√(x²)=|x|，例如x=−3时√9=3，不等于−3。

为什么强调正底数？把(−8)^(2/6)先平方再六次根会得2，先将2/6约成1/3再按实立方根会得−2。不能把这两种流程当作无条件恒等变形。负底数的约分奇分母实根约定可另行研究，本课不把正底数法则任意推广到那里。0的负次幂依然无定义。

## 主根实验

在实验中用16、81和1/16改变有理指数，先预测，再取逆运算核验。额外的底数2只开放平方根对照：输入sqrt(2)表示确切值，1.414只是近似，不能算精确通过。不能写成分数不代表无定义，更不代表等于0。

## 迁移与家长核对

出口请用两句话区分25^(1/2)与解x²=25。家长审核是否同时说清主值、两根和代入检验，不凭出现“正数”一个词就确认理解。可用64^(2/6)=64^(1/3)=4复习指数先约分；后续在log₄8=3/2中再次复现。

# Rational exponents and radicals

A positive number whose square is 16 is four. Rational exponents incorporate this reversal of powers into one notation, provided the base range and principal-value convention are stated first.

## One central idea

**For a positive base a, define a^(m/n)=(ⁿ√a)^m, first reducing m/n and taking n positive.** The principal root of a positive number is positive and single-valued. A negative exponent then takes a reciprocal. This positive-base setting makes the steps consistent with exponent laws.

The denominator specifies the root and the numerator specifies the subsequent power. Thus a^(2/3) means take a cube root and square it, not multiply a by two and divide by three. The exponent one half and decimal 0.5 are the same number and can name the same operation.

### Example 1: Root, power and reciprocal

27^(2/3)=(³√27)²=3²=9. Cubing the result gives 729, the same as squaring the original base, auditing the exponent relationship. Also, 16^(3/4)=(⁴√16)³=2³=8, not 16×3/4=12.

For (1/16)^(−3/4), take the fourth root 1/2, cube it to obtain 1/8, then reciprocate for the negative exponent: eight. Similarly, 81^(−1/2)=1/√81=1/9. Connect each step to the appropriate part of the exponent to avoid turning a negative exponent into a negative value.

### Example 2: One function value versus two equation roots

25^(1/2)=√25=5 is the principal value. Solving x²=25 for every real solution instead gives x=−5 or five. Both roots pass substitution, but the square-root symbol itself does not include ±. Likewise √(x²)=|x|: for x=−3, √9=3, not −3.

Why restrict bases to positive numbers? Squaring −8 and then taking a sixth principal root gives two. Reducing 2/6 to 1/3 first and using the real cube root gives −2. These cannot be treated as unconditionally equivalent workflows for (−8)^(2/6). A separate convention for reduced odd-denominator powers of negative bases can be studied later; this lesson does not extend positive-base identities without checking their hypotheses. Negative powers of zero remain undefined.

## Principal-root experiment

Use bases 16, 81 and 1/16 with different rational exponents. Predict before checking with the inverse operation. The extra base-two case offers a square-root contrast: enter sqrt(2) as an exact value. The decimal 1.414 is approximate and does not pass as exact. Failure to express a value as a fraction does not make it undefined or zero.
