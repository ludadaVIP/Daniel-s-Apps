# 微积分基本定理的直觉

面积累积与瞬时变化率之间有一座桥。设f在含x的邻域连续，A(x)=∫ₐˣf(t)dt，固定下限a，则A′(x)=f(x)。t是积分内部的哑变量，x是会移动的上限，不能混成同一个变化角色。

## 为什么局部平均高度趋向点高

A(x+h)−A(x)=∫ₓ^(x+h)f(t)dt。除以h，小区间平均高度就是差商。当f在x连续时，附近高度都接近f(x)，正负h两侧的平均高度都趋f(x)。这给出导数。有限图展示短区间，却不代替这个用连续性控制全部附近点的论证。

另一部分定理说：若F′=f且f在[a,b]连续，则∫ₐᵇf=F(b)−F(a)。用同一个原函数，两端任意常数会抵消。不能分别在两端随意选不同常数，再说差不确定。反向上下限仍按有向积分规则成立。

### 例1：累积的值与导数不同

令A(x)=∫₁ˣt²dt。原函数t³/3，所以A(x)=(x³−1)/3。A(2)=7/3，而A′(2)=2²=4。前者累计从1到2的贡献，后者是上限在2附近移动时的瞬时变化率。

改为下限0，得到另一函数x³/3，与原A只差1/3常数，导数仍x²。固定下限影响累计值，不影响当地新增薄片的高度。A(1)=0也不意味着A′(1)=0，后者为1。

若B(x)=∫₁^(2x)t²dt，则B=(8x³−1)/3，B′=8x²。在x=1，导数8。按桥接加链式法则也是f(2x)×2=(2x)²×2。漏掉上限变化率2会错得4。

### 例2：跳点处先核定理条件

沿用分段率q：0≤t<1为2，1≤t<3为4，3≤t≤4为1。令A(x)=∫₀ˣq(t)dt。到1时A=2，左侧A(x)=2x，右侧A(x)=2+4(x−1)。两侧都趋2，所以A连续。

但左导数2、右导数4，不相等，A′(1)不存在。虽然右侧约定给q(1)=4，也不能直接说A′(1)=4，因为q在1不连续，前面的充分条件不成立。对x=3，左斜率4、右斜率1，同样如此。

不连续不代表所有地方都不能用基本定理：在每个恒定段内部仍连续，A′等于该段率。若上限为2x，跳点对应x=1/2与3/2，两侧斜率还要乘2。需要明确当前求导位置是否处于被积函数的连续点。

## 精确核验与书面解释

工作台分别预测A(x)和A′(x)，导数不存在可填DNE。变固定下限时，正确的导数预测保留；变上限系数则须重新核链式因子。图只画累积函数A，不把它与f共享同一角色。

再用同一原函数核∫₂¹x²dx=(1−8)/3=−7/3，方向与数值一致。出口需说清连续性、短区间平均高度、链式因子与常数抵消，而非只抄A′=f。家长可追问：“这里f在移动上限处连续吗？”和“改变的是固定下限还是上限移动速度？”

# Intuition of the Fundamental Theorem

Let f be continuous near x and define A(x)=∫ₐˣf(t)dt with fixed lower bound a. Then A′(x)=f(x). The integration variable t is a placeholder inside the integral; x controls the moving upper endpoint.

## From average height to instantaneous change

The increment A(x+h)−A(x) equals ∫ₓ^(x+h)f(t)dt. Dividing by h gives an average height on a short oriented interval. Continuity forces nearby heights, and therefore both one-sided averages, toward f(x). This establishes the derivative. A finite display illustrates the idea but does not replace the continuity argument controlling every sufficiently nearby point.

The evaluation part states that if F′=f and f is continuous on[a,b], then ∫ₐᵇf=F(b)−F(a). Use the same primitive at both endpoints so its additive constant cancels. Choosing unrelated constants at the two endpoints would not be evaluating one function. Reversed bounds retain the usual orientation rule.

### Example 1: Accumulated value and derivative differ

For A(x)=∫₁ˣt²dt, a primitive is t³/3, giving A(x)=(x³−1)/3. At two, A(2)=7/3 while A′(2)=4. The first value records accumulation over an interval; the second describes its instantaneous response to a moving endpoint.

Changing the lower bound to zero gives x³/3, differing by the constant1/3. Its derivative remains x². A fixed starting point changes accumulated value but not the local height of a newly added thin slice. In particular, A(1)=0 while A′(1)=1.

Now let B(x)=∫₁^(2x)t²dt. Explicit evaluation gives B=(8x³−1)/3 and B′=8x². At one the derivative is eight. The theorem combined with the chain rule gives the same result: f(2x)×2=(2x)²×2. Omitting the upper endpoint’s rate of change loses a factor two.

### Example 2: Check continuity at a rate jump

Use q equal to two before minute one, four from one to three, and one after three through four. Define A(x)=∫₀ˣq(t)dt. Near one, its left expression is2x and its right expression2+4(x−1). Both tend to two, so A is continuous.

The left derivative is two and the right derivative four. They differ, so A′(1) does not exist. Defining q(1)=4 by a right-sided convention does not make the two-sided derivative four. The integrand is not continuous there, so the preceding sufficient condition cannot be applied at that point. At three, slopes four and one likewise disagree.

Inside each constant-rate segment the integrand is continuous and the theorem applies normally. With upper endpoint2x, its jumps occur at x=1/2 and 3/2, and both one-sided slopes also acquire a factor two. Identify the actual moving endpoint before deciding whether continuity holds there.

## Audit values, conditions and orientation

The workbench asks separately for A and its derivative; enter DNE when the latter fails to exist. Changing a fixed lower bound preserves a valid derivative prediction. Changing the upper-limit coefficient requires a fresh chain-rule check. The plot represents A rather than silently substituting integrand f.

As an orientation check, ∫₂¹x²dx=(1−8)/3=−7/3. Explain the short-interval average, continuity condition, chain factor and cancellation of one primitive’s constant. Parent prompts should distinguish a changed starting point from a changed endpoint speed, and ask whether f is continuous at the selected upper endpoint.
