## 竖式在记录不同单位

个位计一，十位计十，百位计百。同位对齐，是让相同单位相加或相减，不是为了把数字排得整齐。十个一可以换成一个十，十个十可以换成一个百；换的是表示方式，数量并没有增加。

进位不是“凭空多写一个一”。写到十位的 1 表示一个十，写到百位的 1 表示一个百。退位也不是把原数改小后不再理会：一个大单位换成十个小单位，顶行表示的原量仍要保持。

### 例 1 · 268+157 的两次进位

1. 个位：8+7=15 个一，换成一个十与五个一。个位写 5，把一个十送到十位。
2. 十位：6+5+1=12 个十。这个额外的 1 是刚换来的十，不能忘记。十二个十换成一个百与两个十。
3. 百位：2+1+1=4 个百。
4. 合起来 **268+157=425**。用 425−157=268 检查原有部分。

### 例 2 · 408−179，换组经过零

1. 个位八个不够减九个，但十位原本是零，不能直接从它拿一个十。
2. 从四个百里换一个百，得到三个百与十个十。
3. 再从十个十里换一个十到个位，十位留下九个十，个位成为十八个一。
4. 检查换组表示：**300+90+18=408**，原量没变。
5. 同单位相减：18−9=9 个一，9−7=2 个十，3−1=2 个百，得到 **229**。
6. 用 229+179=408 检查；不能逐列改成“大数字减小数字”。

### 零占位与继续换组

1000−1 需要从千位一路换到个位。一个千变十个百，其中一个百再变十个十，其中一个十再变十个一。留下九个百、九个十与十个一：900+90+10=1000；减一个一得到 999。经过的百位、十位为什么是九而不是十？因为它们把一个单位继续换给了右边。

999+1 则反向连续进位，最后必须写出一个千。若只留下右边三个零，就把一千丢掉了。100−100 得零：没有剩余单位也有一个有效的数值，不能留一个空白当作答案。

工作台每次只展开一列。先在纸上算下一步，再核对顶行换组与本列结果。减法顶行中的 18 不是一个合法的单个数字，而是临时记录“十八个一”；换组结束后，答案各位仍写正常数字。

**小检查：** 502−268 的顶行可换成四个百、九个十、十二个一。先说明这仍为 502，再算结果并加回核验。

## A written column records a unit

Ones count individual units, tens count groups of ten and hundreds groups of a hundred. Aligning places makes matching units meet. It is more than neat formatting. Exchanging ten ones for one ten changes the representation, not the quantity.

A carried 1 is not an extra one appearing from nowhere. In the tens column it represents a ten; in the hundreds column it represents a hundred. Borrowing also preserves the original quantity: exchange one larger unit for ten smaller ones rather than silently reduce the starting amount.

### Example 1 · Two carries in 268+157

1. Ones: 8+7=15 ones. Exchange them for one ten and five ones. Write 5 in ones and carry the ten.
2. Tens: 6+5+1=12 tens. The extra 1 is the newly exchanged ten. Twelve tens become one hundred and two tens.
3. Hundreds: 2+1+1=4 hundreds.
4. Together **268+157=425**. Check 425−157=268 to recover the original part.

### Example 2 · Exchange across zero in 408−179

1. Eight ones cannot supply nine, and the original tens column has zero. You cannot directly take a ten from it.
2. Exchange one of the four hundreds for ten tens, leaving three hundreds.
3. Exchange one of those tens for ten ones. Nine tens remain and the ones now contain eighteen.
4. Check the representation: **300+90+18=408**. The original amount is unchanged.
5. Subtract matching units: 18−9=9 ones, 9−7=2 tens and 3−1=2 hundreds, giving **229**.
6. Check 229+179=408. Do not reverse each column to subtract whichever digit looks smaller.

### Zero placeholders and continuing exchanges

For 1000−1, exchange from thousands through hundreds and tens to ones. One thousand becomes ten hundreds; one of these becomes ten tens; one ten becomes ten ones. Nine hundreds, nine tens and ten ones remain, and 900+90+10=1000. Removing one one gives 999. The intermediate places hold nine rather than ten because each passed one unit onward.

For 999+1, the exchanges continue in the opposite direction and finally produce a thousand. Writing only three zeros drops that thousand. Subtracting 100−100 gives zero: no remaining units still have a valid numerical value, so a blank is not the answer.

The workshop reveals one column at a time. Predict the next step on paper before checking the exchanged top row and result digit. A temporary 18 in the top row records eighteen ones; it is not a single digit. The completed answer still uses ordinary digits in its places.
