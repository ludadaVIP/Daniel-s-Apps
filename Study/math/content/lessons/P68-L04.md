# 图表中的多步推理

## 图是证据入口，不能代替数量关系

先读标题、横轴类别、纵轴单位、零点和刻度。本课给定虚构到访记录：周一有 12 人次，周二有 18 人次，周三缺测，周四有 24 人次。图中明确标出的原数值比肉眼估计柱高更可靠；工作台和题目统一使用 12、18、缺测、24。

改变纵轴上限，从三十改六十，每个人对应的图上高度减半，原记录不会变。柱子是数据的表示，不能把屏幕上的厘米高度直接当人数。图的设计可以改变视觉印象，却不应改变数据本身。多步题开始前，先把需要的数与单位抄成短表，标明哪些信息仍未知。

### 例 1：人数到总费用

周二 18 人，每人票价 NZ$4，另有一次固定组费 NZ$10。

1. 从记录读十八人，而不是读一个无单位的柱高。
2. 票费 `18×4=NZ$72`。
3. 固定费只加一次，全部费用 `72+10=NZ$82`。

`18×(4+10)=252` 把组费错误地收了十八遍。`18×4=72` 只回答了票费，没有回答全部费用。先给每步命名，容易发现遗漏或重复。反查时，总额减一次组费，再除单价，应回到十八人。

### 例 2：整包购买带来的约束

另一个给定组有十八人，票只卖四张一整包，每包 NZ$12。需要至少十八张票。

十八除四为四点五，但不能购买四点五包。四包只有十六张不够，五包二十张才够，费用 `5×12=NZ$60`，多两张。以每张平均三元直接算 `18×3=54` 得到的不是可购买方案的付款金额；整包限制不能在最后忽略。

可以写核验：`4×4<18≤5×4`。它说明五包既足够又没有多买一整包。若人数二十四，六整包刚好，不需要再加一包。向上取整来自包装约束，不是“凡有除法都向上取整”。

## 缺测与零是不同信息

已知三天合计 `12+18+24=54` 人次，但四天完整合计未知。不能把周三设为零，也不能用周二周四平均填进去，除非明确另建估计模型并说明假设。图不能告诉你周三的真实总费；已知收费规则也不能替代缺少的人数。

如果图明确记录某组零人，且题目仍规定登记组必须付一次十元固定费，那么费用是 `0×4+10=10`。这和“没有人数记录”不同，也不意味着真实生活中任何零人组都会被收费；结论只按给定规则成立。

## 区分增加量、增加比例与原因

周二有十八人，周四有二十四人，增加六人，相对周二为 `6/18=1/3`。分母取比较起点十八，不能随意改二十四。精确三分之一不能用近似 0.33 替换。图表记录较多访客，不单独证明天气、宣传或价格造成增加；因果判断还需其他证据。

这里的合计是人次。同一人可能不同日期都来，五十四人次未必等于五十四个不同的人。家长可以连续追问：“这个数从哪里来？哪个信息缺失？这一步算的量是什么？这个结论超出图了吗？”理由应该能回到记录和收费条件，而不是只给一串运算符号。

# Multi-step reasoning with charts

## Treat a chart as evidence

Read the title, category labels, vertical unit, zero and scale before calculating. The supplied fictional records are Monday twelve visits, Tuesday eighteen, Wednesday missing and Thursday twenty-four. The workshop and exercises use `12, 18, missing, 24` consistently. Explicit numeric labels are more reliable than estimating bar heights by eye.

Changing the vertical maximum from thirty to sixty halves each bar’s plotted height but preserves every record. A bar represents a quantity; its height in screen centimetres is not the visitor count. Make a short table of relevant data and units, marking unknown information before selecting operations.

### Example 1: from people to total cost

Tuesday records eighteen people. Tickets cost NZ$4 per person, plus one fixed NZ$10 group fee.

1. Read eighteen people from the record.
2. Ticket cost is `18 × 4 = NZ$72`.
3. Add the group fee once: `72 + 10 = NZ$82`.

The calculation `18 × (4 + 10) = 252` charges the group fee eighteen times. Stopping at seventy-two gives only ticket cost, not the complete cost. Naming every intermediate quantity reveals omissions and duplication. As a check, subtract the group fee from the total and divide by ticket price to recover eighteen people.

### Example 2: whole-pack purchasing

Another supplied group has eighteen people. Tickets are sold only in packs of four for NZ$12 each, and at least eighteen tickets are needed. Eighteen divided by four gives 4.5, but half-packs cannot be purchased. Four packs supply only sixteen tickets. Five packs supply twenty and cost `5 × 12 = NZ$60`, leaving two tickets unused.

Multiplying eighteen by the average three-dollar ticket cost gives fifty-four, which is not an available purchase. Verify the smallest sufficient pack count with `4 × 4 < 18 ≤ 5 × 4`. For twenty-four people, six packs fit exactly; no extra pack is required. Rounding upward follows the packaging condition, not a universal rule about division.

## Preserve missing data and distinguish zero

The three recorded days total `12 + 18 + 24 = 54` visits. The complete four-day total is unknown. Replacing Wednesday with zero or a neighbouring-day average invents a record. An estimated value would require a separately stated model and assumptions, and would still not be the observed count.

Without Wednesday’s count, a per-person total cost cannot be determined, even though the price rule is known. By contrast, an explicitly recorded zero is data. If a question specifies that a registered group owes a mandatory ten-dollar fee even with zero visitors, its bill is `0 × 4 + 10 = 10`. That follows this supplied rule, not a claim about every real provider.

## Separate amount, proportion and cause

From Tuesday’s eighteen to Thursday’s twenty-four, the increase is six, or `6/18 = 1/3` of Tuesday’s count. The comparison base is eighteen, not twenty-four. Exact one third is different from the rounded decimal 0.33.

Higher recorded attendance does not by itself establish weather, advertising or price as its cause. Also, fifty-four visits need not represent fifty-four distinct people; one person may appear on several days. A checkable explanation identifies the source of each value, the missing information, every intermediate quantity and the limits of the conclusion.
