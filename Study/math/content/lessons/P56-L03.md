## 两份三角形，发现一个矩形

一张三角形旗面，底长 6 cm，垂直高 4 cm。先别背公式：在纸上画一份，再画一份完全一样的三角形。预测这两份能否拼成平行四边形，再把它切拼成长方形。旋转、平移不改变一份图的面积；**另加一份副本会使总面积加倍**，这两件事要分清。

底可以选三角形的任何一条边；选好底，高就是对面顶点到底所在直线的垂直距离。图里的直角标记帮助确认高。高不是“朝上画的那条线”，旋转纸张也不改变它与底垂直的关系。

### 例 1 · 从两份回到一份

1. 原图底 **6 cm**、垂直高 **4 cm**。先猜它是否有 24 cm²。
2. 复制一份，旋转半圈，沿相应斜边与原图拼合，得到底 6 cm、高 4 cm 的平行四边形。
3. 沿垂线切下边上的一块，平移到另一侧；没有拉伸、丢掉或重叠任何面积。
4. 现在矩形有 **6×4=24 cm²**，但这是**两份**三角形的总面积。
5. 一份是 **24÷2=12 cm²**。反查：两份 12 cm² 确实合为 24 cm²。

因此三角形面积 **A=b×h÷2**。除二来自两份全等图，不来自“三条边”或“图形斜着”。在网格上看，斜边切到的半格也有面积，不能只数完整格而漏掉边缘。

### 例 2 · 高落在底边外面

1. 底为 **5 cm**，顶点在底左端再往左 1 cm 的上方；顶点到底所在直线的垂直距离是 **3 cm**。
2. 延长底边，画下垂线。垂足在原线段外面，但仍与底所在直线成直角。
3. 两份全等图仍能拼合，再切拼成底 5 cm、高 3 cm 的矩形，总面积 **15 cm²**。
4. 原来一份 **15÷2=7.5 cm²**，也可写成 15/2 cm²，不能舍去半格变成七。
5. 同底同高、顶点移到另一边，面积仍为 7.5 cm²；斜边长度和外形可以改变。

在工作台把顶点移到底的右侧之外时，先将原三角形反射，再配对。反射保留一份面积；配对才增加一份。每次先解释下一步会保留哪个量，再展开。改变底、高或顶点位置会回到原图，旧的核对结果不能当新图答案。

若底 8 cm、高 5 cm，某条斜边长 6 cm，要用 **8×5÷2=20 cm²**，不能用斜边算成 24。面积 18 cm²、底 6 cm 时，先把面积加倍，再除底，得到高 **18×2÷6=6 cm**；代回核查。求高用厘米，求面积用平方厘米。

**自己解释：** 为什么高在外面仍然有效？请指出垂直距离，再描述两份到矩形的过程。家长听关系和理由，不要求背出完全相同的句子。出口题换新底高，先独立计算，再用一句话说明为什么除二。

## Two triangles reveal a rectangle

A triangular flag has a 6 cm base and a 4 cm perpendicular height. Before memorising a rule, draw an identical copy. Predict how the pair could form a parallelogram and then a rectangle. Rotating and translating retain the area of each copy. **Adding another copy doubles the total area.** These are different actions.

Any triangle edge may be chosen as the base. Its corresponding height is the perpendicular distance from the opposite vertex to the line containing that base. Look for a right-angle marker. Height does not mean a line pointing upwards on the screen: turning the paper preserves perpendicularity.

### Example 1 · Return from two copies to one

1. Start with base **6 cm** and perpendicular height **4 cm**. Predict whether its area is 24 cm².
2. Make an identical copy and rotate it half a turn. Join the copies along matching slanted edges to form a parallelogram of base 6 cm and height 4 cm.
3. Cut a piece along a perpendicular and translate it to the other side. No piece stretches, disappears or overlaps another.
4. The rectangle has area **6×4=24 cm²**, representing **both** triangles.
5. One original triangle has **24÷2=12 cm²**. Check that two copies of 12 cm² total 24 cm².

Thus triangle area is **A=b×h÷2**. Halving comes from two congruent copies, rather than the number of edges or a slanted appearance. On a grid, partial cells along a slanted boundary still contribute area; counting only whole cells loses part of the triangle.

### Example 2 · An altitude outside the base

1. Use base **5 cm**. Place the apex above a point 1 cm to the left of the base’s left endpoint, at perpendicular distance **3 cm** from the base line.
2. Extend the base line and draw the perpendicular. Its foot lies outside the original segment, but the right angle remains valid.
3. Two congruent copies still combine and rearrange into a rectangle of base 5 cm and height 3 cm, with area **15 cm²**.
4. One copy has **15÷2=7.5 cm²**, equivalently 15/2 cm². Do not discard the half square.
5. Moving the apex sideways while retaining base and height retains area, even though slanted edges and appearance change.

When the workshop apex moves beyond the right endpoint, a reflection first puts the triangle in a convenient orientation. Reflection preserves one copy’s area; pairing adds a second copy. Predict what each step preserves before revealing it. Changing measurements resets the construction and its previous checked result.

With base 8 cm, perpendicular height 5 cm and a slanted side 6 cm, use **8×5÷2=20 cm²**, rather than the slanted side. To find height from area 18 cm² and base 6 cm, double area and divide by base: **18×2÷6=6 cm**. Substitute back. Height uses centimetres; area uses square centimetres.

**Explain it yourself:** point to the perpendicular distance and describe how two copies become a rectangle. A parent reviews the mathematical relationship rather than requiring identical wording. Transfer the idea to new exit-ticket measurements and explain the factor of one half.
