## 单位不一样，先换成共同小单位

同一个整体的 3/4 与 2/3，分别在数四分之一和三分之一。不同单位的小份，不能只比较份数 3 与 2。先找能共同使用的小单位，把两者改写成等值分数，再比较分子。

**通分改变写法，不改变数值。** 共同分母不只一种；为使图示简洁，可以用最小共同分母。分母的乘积总是一个共同分母，但不一定是最小的。

### 例 1 · 比较 3/4 与 2/3

1. 4 与 3 的最小共同倍数是 12，可用十二分之一计数。
2. 3/4 的分子分母同时乘 3，得到 **9/12**。
3. 2/3 的分子分母同时乘 4，得到 **8/12**。
4. 每一小份一样大，九份比八份多：**3/4>2/3**。
5. 同一整体的差距是一个十二分之一；可以在同长条形上标出这段。

### 例 2 · 比较 4/9 与 5/12

1. 9 的正倍数有 9、18、27、36；12 的有 12、24、36。
2. 最小共同分母为 **36**，不是 9+12=21。
3. 4/9=16/36；5/12=15/36。
4. 因为 16>15，**4/9>5/12**。
5. 108 也可作为共同分母，但比所需的小单位更细，不是最小共同分母。

### 相同分子时，分母大为什么反而小？

固定同一个整体，分成八等份比六等份切得更细，每份更小。因此 1/8<1/6。也可验证：1/8=3/24，1/6=4/24，比较的是同样大小的二十四分之一。

不要把这个观察当成“任何时候分母大都小”。例如 7/10 与 1/2，分子也不同，前者等于七个十分之一，后者只有五个十分之一；7/10 更大。

### 同一个整体的比例与不同整体的实际量

3/4 比 1/2 大是数值比较。中文读“几分之几”时先读分母，再读分子：3/4 读作四分之三。若 3/4 来自一块小蛋糕，1/2 来自一块大蛋糕，没有两块蛋糕的具体大小，不能仅凭比例比较实际蛋糕量。

长度题可把 1 km 作为共同计量整体，即使两段步道不是同一条路，也能比较以千米表示的量。5/8 km=15/24 km，2/3 km=16/24 km，第二段较长。单位若换成米，必须同时换算；不能把相同数字的米与千米当作同量。

工作台先显示原分割，再揭示共同分母的分割与精确份数。先预测，再用共同小单位检查。家庭挑战用同长纸条画假想新西兰步道，保留等值式和理由；再保持标尺不变，把其中一条整体改为 2 km 并改好标签，重新确认实际量结论；只改变画图缩放而仍标作 1 km，不会自动改变实际距离。

## Convert unlike parts to a common small unit

For the same whole, 3/4 counts quarters while 2/3 counts thirds. Comparing counts three and two directly mixes unlike units. Rewrite both as equivalent fractions in one common unit before comparing numerators.

**Common-denominator conversion changes notation, not value.** Several common denominators may work. The least common denominator keeps diagrams simpler. Multiplying denominators always gives a common denominator, but not always the least one.

### Example 1 · Compare 3/4 and 2/3

1. The least common multiple of four and three is twelve, so count twelfths.
2. Multiply both numbers in 3/4 by three to obtain **9/12**.
3. Multiply both numbers in 2/3 by four to obtain **8/12**.
4. Matching parts allow count comparison: nine exceeds eight, so **3/4>2/3**.
5. The difference is one twelfth of the same whole, which can be marked on equal-length strips.

### Example 2 · Compare 4/9 and 5/12

1. Positive multiples of nine include 9,18,27,36; multiples of twelve include 12,24,36.
2. The least common denominator is **thirty-six**, not 9+12=21.
3. 4/9=16/36 and 5/12=15/36.
4. Sixteen exceeds fifteen, so **4/9>5/12**.
5. One hundred and eight is also a common denominator, but its finer units are unnecessary and it is not the least one.

### Why can a larger denominator give a smaller part?

For a fixed whole, eighths are finer than sixths, so 1/8<1/6. Check with 1/8=3/24 and 1/6=4/24: the common unit is one twenty-fourth.

Do not turn this into a rule that every larger denominator means a smaller fraction. When numerators also differ, compare matching units. For example, 7/10 exceeds 1/2 because one half is only five tenths.

### Proportions and actual amounts from different wholes

As numbers, 3/4 exceeds 1/2. Chinese names read the denominator before the numerator: 四分之三 means three quarters. Keep numerator and denominator in their correct roles when reading either language.

If three quarters comes from a small cake and one half from a large cake, their actual amounts need the cakes' sizes. Fraction values alone cannot settle that amount comparison.

Lengths may use one kilometre as a shared measurement whole even for different track sections. 5/8 km=15/24 km and 2/3 km=16/24 km, so the latter section is longer. Converting to metres requires changing the number too; matching digits with different units do not mean matching distances.

The workshop shows original cuts before revealing common-denominator cuts and exact counts. Predict first, then check using the shared unit. In the family challenge, draw an imaginary New Zealand track with equal paper strips and retain expressions and reasons. Keep the drawing scale fixed, change one whole label to two kilometres, and recheck actual amounts. Merely zooming a drawing while retaining its one-kilometre label does not change the real distance.
