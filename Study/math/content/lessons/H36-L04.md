# 向量与解析方法互证

同一个几何问题可以通过向量、坐标方程和几何定理理解。互证的价值不是把同一计算重复写两遍，而是让不同表示暴露各自容易遗漏的条件。数量积等于0要解释成直角，必须先确认两条向量都非零。

## 把角的条件展开为方程

固定A=(−a,0)、B=(a,0)，a>0，动点P=(x,y)。角APB使用向量A−P=(−a−x,−y)与B−P=(a−x,−y)。展开数量积得(−a−x)(a−x)+y²=x²+y²−a²。

当P不等于A/B时，两向量长度都正，数量积公式u·v=|u||v|cosθ表明：点积0等价cosθ=0，因而夹角90°。于是原直角轨迹点满足x²+y²=a²；反之在这圆上且不等于端点的点也确实给直角。

因此完整轨迹是以AB为直径的圆，排除A与B。方程自身包含两个端点，原几何角度却在那里无定义；非零条件要写进结论，不能期待圆方程替你排除。

### 例1：一个真正直角与一个假朋友

取a=3、P=(0,3)。两向量为(−3,−3)、(3,−3)，点积−9+9=0，长度均为3√2且非零，所以角APB确为直角。坐标也有0²+3²=9，与圆方程相合。

若P=A=(−3,0)，第一向量为零，第二为(6,0)，点积仍0。可是夹角公式分母含零，角APB无定义，不能说90°。P=B也同理。这是一个满足代数必要条件却不满足原几何定义的明确反例。

### 例2：圆内外与共线情形要分开

P=(0,3/2)在圆内，点积9/4−9=−27/4，且两向量不共线，因而夹角为钝角。P=(0,6)在圆外，点积36−9=27，非共线夹角为锐角。这说明数量积符号与候选圆内外可以相互检查。

P=(0,0)却是一个边界提醒：两非零向量(−3,0)、(3,0)反向共线，夹角180°，是平角，而不是通常严格小于180°的钝角。若P=(4,0)，两向量同向共线，夹角0°。所以只说正点积必为严格锐角、负点积必为严格钝角，也会漏掉共线情形。

本台同时计算平面叉积2ay；在a>0时它为0恰表明P在AB直线上。先排零向量，再分共线，最后用点积符号分类，逻辑顺序清晰。

## 几何方法怎样核对

由圆周角定理，圆上不等于直径端点的P所张角APB为直角；反向直角三角形的斜边中点到三个顶点等距，所以P在以AB为直径的圆。这个几何论证与数量积展开给同一个轨迹，并且两种方法都不能在端点处假装三角形仍存在。

图上的紫圆是代数候选，空心A/B标原直角轨迹排除点。连接P与A/B的线段用于看向量位置，零长度不能凭箭头外观制造方向。红色候选P不自动属于轨迹，要对照当前角度和资格表。

## 出口与家长提示

报告两向量、展开式、非零前提、必要性与充分性，再说明圆周角方法怎样独立核验。出口一定用P=A反驳无前提的“点积0就是直角”；再用圆心解释为什么负点积可能是平角。

家长可以先遮住图，只给两个分量让学生判断哪些条件可算、哪些量无定义，再把坐标图放回来核对。完整证明由人工审核，自动点积与类型题只核结果，不替代论证。

# Connecting vector and analytic proofs

Vectors, coordinate equations and geometric theorems offer different views of the same problem. Cross-verification is useful because each view can expose assumptions hidden in another. A zero dot product describes a right angle only when both vectors are nonzero.

## Expand the angle condition

Fix A=(−a,0), B=(a,0), with a>0, and let P=(x,y). Angle APB uses A−P=(−a−x,−y) and B−P=(a−x,−y). Their dot product expands to x²+y²−a².

If P differs from A and B, both lengths are positive. The formula u·v=|u||v|cosθ then makes a zero dot product equivalent to a right angle. Thus original right-angle points satisfy x²+y²=a²; conversely, points on that circle other than the endpoints give a right angle.

The complete locus is the circle with diameter AB excluding A and B. The equation includes those endpoints, whereas the original angle is undefined there. Retain the nonzero condition in the final statement rather than expecting the equation to remove extra points automatically.

### Example 1: A genuine right angle and an invalid endpoint

Let a=3 and P=(0,3). The vectors are (−3,−3) and (3,−3), with dot product −9+9=0. Both have nonzero length 3√2, so angle APB is right. The point also satisfies 0²+3²=9.

At P=A=(−3,0), one vector is zero and the other is (6,0). The dot product remains zero, but the angle formula has a zero denominator and no angle is defined. P=B has the same problem. This is a concrete example of an algebraic necessary condition holding while the original geometric definition fails.

### Example 2: Inside, outside and collinear cases

At P=(0,3/2), the dot product is 9/4−9=−27/4. The vectors are non-collinear, so the angle is obtuse. At P=(0,6), the dot product is 36−9=27 and the non-collinear angle is acute. Circle position and dot-product sign provide useful checks on each other.

At P=(0,0), however, the nonzero vectors (−3,0) and (3,0) are opposite and form a straight angle of 180°, not a strictly obtuse angle. At P=(4,0), the two vectors are collinear in the same direction and form angle zero. Positive or negative dot product alone does not exclude these collinear boundary cases.

The planar cross product is 2ay. With a>0, it vanishes precisely when P lies on line AB. Check zero vectors first, collinearity second, then use the dot-product sign for the remaining classifications.

## Cross-check with geometry

The angle-in-a-semicircle theorem gives a right angle at any circle point distinct from the diameter endpoints. Conversely, in a right triangle the midpoint of the hypotenuse is equally distant from all three vertices, placing P on the circle with diameter AB. This geometric route agrees with the coordinate expansion, and both methods must exclude endpoints where the triangle degenerates.

Purple is the candidate circle; open A/B mark points excluded from the original right-angle locus. Segments to A/B help locate the vectors, but a zero-length segment cannot acquire a direction from a drawing. A red candidate is not automatically a locus point; compare its angle classification and admission audit.

Report the vectors, expansion, nonzero prerequisite and both logical directions. Use P=A to refute an unconditional right-angle claim, then use the centre to distinguish a straight angle from a strictly obtuse one. Automated dot products and classifications verify results; a human reviews the full argument and how the two methods support each other.
