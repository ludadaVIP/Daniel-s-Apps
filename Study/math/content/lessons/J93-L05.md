# 最值和实际应用

## 先说在哪些输入中比较

y=a(x−h)²+k在所有实数上有一个顶点最值：a>0时最小k，a<0时最大k。但实际问题可能只允许某个区间，或只允许整数，h未必可用。最值回答“最大的输出是多少”，达到输入回答“哪些x能得到它”，两个问题要分别作答，并附各自单位。

在有限闭区间上，二次函数的最大和最小只需比较两个端点及区间内顶点。依据是平方距离：离h越远平方越大，越近越小，再看a正负决定输出方向。不能任意选几个试点便声称证明最优。若只允许有限整数输入，可以完整列出比较，也可用距顶点最近或最远的整数解释，但所有并列最优都要保留。

### 例 1：同一矩形，不同宽度范围

虚构矩形标牌周长20 cm。设宽x cm，则2(宽+长)=20，长为10−x cm，面积A=x(10−x)，单位cm²；正长度本来要求0<x<10。题目另规定1≤x≤5，这个闭区间的所有尺寸都保持正长度。

配方A=−x²+10x=25−(x−5)²。平方非负，所以A≤25；x=5在允许范围内且使平方为零，最大面积25 cm²，唯一达到宽度5 cm，长也5 cm。这里不是只因为试算5比4好，而是给出覆盖全部允许输入的上界，并找到达到上界的输入。

若另改为1≤x≤4，同一函数的顶点x=5不再允许。区间内距5最近的是4，面积24 cm²；左端点1面积9 cm²，故最大24在宽4 cm，最小9在宽1 cm。不能仍报告全实数顶点的25。定义域变了，最优答案就可能改变，即使解析式没有变。

### 例 2：整数输入可能有并列最优

虚构纸卡游戏允许x∈{0,1,2,3,4,5}张，得分S=−x²+5x+1=29/4−(x−5/2)²。连续延伸的顶点输入5/2、输出29/4，但半张卡不是允许选项。完整列出得分为1、5、7、7、5、1，最高7分，对应卡数2与3。

也可说明2、3到5/2都相距1/2，是所有允许整数中距离最近的两个，因此并列最优。仅把5/2四舍五入到3，会漏掉2；把连续最大29/4直接当整数情境得分也不对。图像只画允许整数点，不连成暗示半张卡可用的曲线；连续顶点只作解释性延伸，不作为合法候选。

## 最小值、时间范围与报告

虚构信号y=−x²+6x+1=10−(x−3)²只在0≤x≤2 min考察。顶点(3,10)不在时段内，区间中最近3的是2，最大读数9单位，在开始后2 min达到；最远是0，最小读数1单位，在开始时达到。输出读数与输入分钟不能混写。

闭区间包含端点，因此端点可直接参加比较；若原题给开区间，是否能达到某个界值需要另作讨论，不能照搬。本工作台的数值最值核验只处理明确给出的闭区间或有限整数集合。家庭挑战用纸上虚构标牌，比较两个范围、一个目标面积水平线及整数卡数例子。家长检查模型数量来源、范围、候选完整性、代回与单位，不以一个漂亮顶点坐标替代最优性的解释。

# Maximum and minimum problems

## State the allowed inputs

On all real inputs, y=a(x−h)²+k has minimum k for a>0 or maximum k for a<0. A context may restrict inputs to an interval or integers, making h unavailable. An extremum is an output; its attaining inputs are separate answers with separate units.

For a quadratic on a finite closed interval, compare both endpoints and any in-range vertex for maximum and minimum. Distance squared from h explains why these candidates suffice, with the sign of a deciding the output direction. Arbitrary trial points do not prove optimality. For finitely many integer inputs, enumerate all or justify the nearest/farthest integers, retaining every tie.

### Example 1: one rectangle, two domains

A fictional rectangular sign has perimeter 20 cm. Define width x cm. From 2(width+length)=20, length is 10−x cm and area A=x(10−x) cm². Positive dimensions require 0<x<10. The additional domain 1≤x≤5 keeps every permitted dimension positive.

Complete A=25−(x−5)². Since a square is nonnegative, A≤25, and allowed x=5 attains equality. Thus maximum area is 25 cm² at the unique width 5 cm, with length 5 cm. This gives an upper bound for every input and an input attaining it, rather than merely testing a few widths.

Restrict instead to 1≤x≤4. The same function now excludes vertex input five. Four is nearest to five, giving maximum area 24 cm²; endpoint one gives minimum 9 cm². Do not retain the unrestricted answer twenty-five. A domain change can alter the optimum without changing the expression.

### Example 2: tied integer optima

A fictional card game permits x∈{0,1,2,3,4,5} and score S=−x²+5x+1=29/4−(x−5/2)². Its continuous extension has vertex input 5/2 and output 29/4, but half a card is forbidden. Exhaustive scores are 1,5,7,7,5,1, so maximum seven occurs at both two and three cards.

Both integers are distance 1/2 from 5/2, the smallest possible integer distance. Rounding the vertex only to three loses a tied optimum at two. The continuous output 29/4 is also inadmissible as this model’s attained score. The graph shows only allowed integer points without a continuous join; an excluded extension vertex is explanatory, not a valid candidate.

## Minima and time intervals

For fictional signal y=−x²+6x+1=10−(x−3)² observed only on 0≤x≤2 min, vertex (3,10) is outside the study period. The nearest allowed input to three is two, giving maximum reading nine units at 2 min. The farthest is zero, giving minimum one unit at the start. Do not mix output units with input minutes.

Closed intervals include their endpoints. Open intervals require a separate attainment discussion; this numerical workbench explicitly handles closed intervals or finite integer sets. In the paper-based fictional-sign challenge, compare domains and a target-area horizontal line, then add an integer example. A parent reviews quantity sources, domain, complete candidates, substitution and units rather than accepting a vertex coordinate as a proof of optimality.
