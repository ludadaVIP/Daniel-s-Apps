# 速度、时间和路程

## 先决定“这段时间”包含什么

速度描述每单位时间走过的路程。`6 km/h` 的意思是在给定匀速模型中，每小时走六千米；它不是六小时，也不是六千米。计算前写三行：所求是什么、已知量各用什么单位、计时从哪里开始到哪里结束。路程与时间对应同一段过程，才能相除。

在恒速条件下，路程=速度×时间，所以时间=路程÷速度，速度=路程÷时间。这里的除法需要非零除数。真实行程可能加速、减速或停留，用总路程除以总时间得到的是那段过程的平均速度，不保证每一刻都等于这个数。也不要把沿路走过的总路程与起终点间的直线距离混为一谈。

## 单位先对齐，再算数

一小时六十分钟，因此四十五分钟是 `45/60=3/4 h`。速度写 km/h 时，时间用小时；若题目要分钟，算出的小时最后乘六十。`12×45=540` 不能直接说成千米，因为十二是每小时的千米数，而四十五给的是分钟。转换单位是在描述同一数量，不是给行程额外加上六十倍的时间。

### 例 1：移动速度与全程平均

假想行程走六千米，移动六十分钟，另停留三十分钟。

1. 只计移动：六十分钟是一小时，移动平均速度 `6÷1=6 km/h`。
2. 计入停留：总时间九十分钟，即 `3/2 h`，全程平均速度 `6÷(3/2)=4 km/h`。
3. 核验：`6×1=6 km`，`4×3/2=6 km`。两个结果各自对应正确的时间范围。

停留不增加路程，却增加全程时间，所以全程平均速度下降。工作台中的绿色线先上升再水平；横轴是统一的分钟标尺。虚线连接整个过程的起点与终点，其斜率表示全程平均，不代表实际一直按虚线的速度走。在本图中把停留改零，两种速度相同；这来自时间范围重合，不是两种定义永远相同。

### 例 2：不能随意平均两段速度

第一段三千米用三十分钟，第二段三千米用六十分钟，没有其他停留。两段速度分别六和三 km/h。

总路程是六千米，总时间九十分钟，全程平均 `6÷1.5=4 km/h`。直接算 `(6+3)÷2=4.5` 不成立，因为在较慢的速度上花了更长时间。用总量核验，`4.5×1.5=6.75 km` 已经不等于六千米。若两段时间真的相同，速度的算术平均才等于全程平均；先检查条件，再决定能否简化。

## 自己检查

在工作台固定六千米和六十分钟，只增加停留。预测全程平均怎样变化，再输入精确结果。想一想：同样十千米，题目说“共两小时，其中休息三十分钟”，若问移动平均，应使用 `2−1/2=3/2 h`，答案 `20/3 km/h`；`6.67` 是近似，不能替代题目要求的精确数。

给家长解释时，请指着分子和分母说明它们来自哪一段过程，再用“速度×相应时间”反查。只背三个公式，却不能说出停留是否计入，说明模型还未建立。这些虚构数据只用于数学推理，不预测真实路线的耗时。

# Speed, time and distance

## Decide what the measured interval includes

Speed describes distance covered per unit time. In a supplied constant-speed model, `6 km/h` means six kilometres in each hour. Before calculating, name the requested quantity, list the units and identify the beginning and end of the timed interval. A distance and a time must describe the same process before dividing one by the other.

For constant speed, distance = speed × time. Reversing that relationship gives time = distance ÷ speed and speed = distance ÷ time, with non-zero divisors. When speed varies, total distance divided by total time is an average over the specified interval; it need not equal the speed at every instant. Distance travelled along a route is also different from the straight-line separation of its endpoints.

## Convert before calculating

One hour contains sixty minutes, so forty-five minutes equals `45/60 = 3/4 h`. With a speed in km/h, use hours. Convert a resulting time back to minutes when that is what the question requests. Multiplying twelve kilometres per hour by forty-five minutes and calling the result 540 kilometres ignores incompatible units. A unit conversion expresses the same quantity in another unit; it does not add time to the journey.

### Example 1: moving average and whole-trip average

A fictional trip covers six kilometres in sixty moving minutes, followed by a thirty-minute stop.

1. Moving time alone is one hour, so its average speed is `6 ÷ 1 = 6 km/h`.
2. Elapsed time including the stop is ninety minutes, or `3/2 h`. The whole-trip average is `6 ÷ (3/2) = 4 km/h`.
3. Check each against its matching time: `6 × 1 = 6 km` and `4 × 3/2 = 6 km`.

The stop adds time without adding distance. In the workshop, the solid distance–time path rises during travel and becomes horizontal during the stop. Every horizontal interval uses the same minute scale. The dashed line joins the overall start and finish; its slope represents the whole-trip average, not the actual speed throughout. Setting the stop to zero makes the two averages coincide because the timed intervals now match.

### Example 2: an unweighted mean can fail

The first three kilometres take thirty minutes and the next three take sixty minutes, with no other stops. The separate speeds are six and three km/h. Total distance is six kilometres and total time is 1.5 hours, so average speed is `6 ÷ 1.5 = 4 km/h`.

The tempting calculation `(6 + 3) ÷ 2 = 4.5` gives equal weight to speeds used for different durations. The traveller spent longer at the slower speed. Multiplying 4.5 by 1.5 predicts 6.75 kilometres, contradicting the supplied total. An arithmetic mean of segment speeds does work for equal segment times, but that condition must be checked first.

## Explain and verify

Keep the workshop distance and moving time fixed, then increase only the stop. Predict the direction of change before calculating. For a ten-kilometre journey taking two elapsed hours with a thirty-minute rest, moving time is `2 − 1/2 = 3/2 h`. Its moving average is exactly `20/3 km/h`; 6.67 is a rounded approximation.

Explain which interval supplies each denominator, then reconstruct distance by multiplying the rate by its matching time. Knowing three formula names without deciding whether a stop belongs in the time is insufficient. These fictional inputs support classroom reasoning; they do not forecast real route conditions.
