## 先找零点，再读方向与小格

假想摄氏刻度的指示线在零下四度，应写 **−4°C**。负号说明读数在零的下方，不是装饰，也不是“没有温度”。同样数字四，**+4°C** 位于零上，与 −4°C 是不同位置。

本课只读假想刻度，不需要测温或查真实天气。零是这里的参照，不是读数的最低限度。图的显示窗只画 −15°C 到 +15°C；窗底不意味着更低的读数不存在。

### 例 1 · 零下四个一度小格

1. 先找到标为 **0°C** 的参照线。
2. 确认指示在它**下方**，再逐格数间隔。
3. 每个小格一度，四格到达指示，所以写 **−4°C**。
4. 不能漏掉负号写四度，那会指向零上四度。
5. 核查：指示比 −5°C 高一格、比零低四格，位置一致。

一条刻度线上可以有正读数、负读数和恰好零。正号通常可以省略，+3°C 与 3°C 相同；负号有明确方向意义，−3°C 不能省成 3°C。零度读作 0°C，既不是正读数，也不是负读数。

### 例 2 · 标签少了，小格没有变大

1. 原来每一度都写数字，现在只标 **−10、−5、0、5、10** 等标签。
2. 邻近两个写出的标签相差五度，但中间仍有五个一度间隔。
3. 指示在 **+5°C 上方一个小格**，读作 **+6°C**。
4. 同理，−5°C 下方两个小格是 −7°C，不是 −15°C。
5. 换标签显示方式不改变原指示线高度、每格大小或真实给定读数。

数的是间隔，而不是把起点那条线也算成已经走过一度。零线到下一条线才是一度，从零往下四次移动才到 −4°C。这个约定与下一课的整数数轴相同。

工作台可以调整假想温度，或一次升高、降低一度。先定位零，再预测带符号读数，最后核对。把温度从 −1°C 升一度会到零，再升一度到 +1°C；零处不用制造一个不同的“负零位置”。改变读数或标签间隔后旧反馈会清空。

**比较的预告：** −2°C 比 −6°C 更高、更暖，虽然去掉负号后的二小于六。以后比较负数要看完整带符号的位置，不能直接照搬正数数码的大小。

**家长陪学：** 请孩子同时指出零、方向、单位间隔和摄氏符号。可以说出来或画刻度，解释不必用固定句子。若孩子写 −4 却说“负号表示没有”，数值虽对仍需回看意义。负读数在数学刻度上有效，不能把显示窗或零当作任意下限。

**带走一个发现：** 负数让我们记录参照零另一侧的位置。读温度时，符号、数字和单位共同决定读数。

## Locate zero before reading direction and intervals

A fictional Celsius indicator four degrees below zero reads **−4°C**. The minus sign locates it below zero; it is neither decoration nor an absence of temperature. **+4°C** has the same unsigned digit but lies above zero, at a different position.

This lesson uses hypothetical readings without requiring measurement or current weather information. Zero is a reference, not a minimum. The drawing shows only −15°C to +15°C; its lower window edge does not rule out lower readings.

### Example 1 · Four one-degree intervals below zero

1. Find the reference line labeled **0°C**.
2. Check that the indicator is **below** it, then count intervals.
3. Each small interval is one degree, so four below gives **−4°C**.
4. Omitting the minus sign would identify four degrees above zero instead.
5. Check its position: one interval above −5°C and four below zero.

A scale can contain positive, negative and zero readings. A positive sign is often optional: +3°C and 3°C agree. A minus sign carries direction and cannot be omitted from −3°C. The zero reading is 0°C, neither positive nor negative.

### Example 2 · Fewer labels do not enlarge the unit

1. Replace labels at every degree with labels such as **−10, −5, 0, 5 and 10**.
2. Consecutive labeled values differ by five degrees, but still contain five one-degree intervals.
3. One small interval above **+5°C** reads **+6°C**.
4. Likewise, two intervals below −5°C read −7°C, not −15°C.
5. Changing label frequency preserves the indicator, unit size and given reading.

Count intervals rather than counting the starting line as a completed degree. Zero to the next tick is one degree; four downward moves reach −4°C. The next lesson uses the same convention on an integer number line.

Adjust the fictional temperature or move one degree warmer or colder. Locate zero, predict the signed reading, then check. One degree warmer than −1°C reaches zero; another reaches +1°C. There is no separate negative-zero position. Changing temperature or label frequency clears earlier feedback.

As a preview, −2°C is higher and warmer than −6°C, although two is less than six when the signs are discarded. Comparing negative values requires complete signed positions rather than unsigned digit order.

**For a parent:** ask for zero, direction, interval size and the Celsius unit together. A spoken or drawn explanation is welcome. A correct −4 accompanied by “the minus sign means nothing exists” still needs discussion. Negative scale readings are valid; neither zero nor the displayed window supplies an arbitrary lower bound.

**Takeaway:** negative numbers record positions on the other side of a reference zero. Sign, numerical value and measurement unit together determine a reading.
