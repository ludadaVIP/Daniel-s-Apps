# 空间中的点线面

一张图必须把三维空间压到二维纸面。图上两条线交叉，不一定是空间相交；图上没有交叉，也不能据此判定平行。先认识对象和条件，才能让图帮助推理。

## 一条核心想法

**完整直线与平面的关系由共同点、方向和共面条件决定，不能由投影外观决定。** 点只有位置；完整直线向两边无限延伸；平面向所有面内方向无限延伸。画出的棱段和有限面片只是这些对象的代表。

两条不同直线在同一平面内，可以相交或平行；空间中还多出异面情形：既不平行，也不相交，因此不共面。重合直线并非两条不同平行线。本课“平行”排除重合，“线面相交”指只有一个共同点，整线在面内另称包含。

### 例1：没有交点，不一定平行

长方体按底面A、B、C、D和对应上面E、F、G、H命名。设A=(0,0,0)、B=(4,0,0)、C=(4,3,0)、G=(4,3,4)，坐标长度单位cm。

完整直线AB上每一点都满足y=z=0；CG上每一点都满足x=4、y=3，所以它们不可能有共同点。AB沿x方向，CG沿z方向，方向不同，不平行。它们是异面直线。如果存在一个同时容纳它们的平面，平面内两条不平行线就应相交，与已核对的没有共同点矛盾。

与此对照，AB和CD都在底面内，方向相同且不是同一直线，所以平行。AB和AE则共享A，是相交线。把四种关系并列，比只记“图上不交就是平行”更可靠。

### 例2：一个共同点与整线包含

底面是z=0。A、B是其中两个不同点，直线AB上从A朝B方向延伸的所有位置仍保持z=0，因此完整直线包含在底面内。

直线AE也经过A，却向z方向离开底面，仅在A与底面相交。因此“一个点在平面内”不足以推出“整条直线在面内”。同一个点重复标成两个字母也不增加证据，必须是两个不同点。

两个不同平面z=0与y=0的共同点满足y=z=0，是整条x轴。相交平面有一条共同直线，而非只有图上看见的一小段交线。

## 关系工作台

先选研究对象，再用相交、平行、异面、重合或包含作预测。改变相机方向只改投影，已写判断保持。展开坐标核验后，用共同点或方向说明理由；不要用颜色和屏幕上的角替代条件。

透明线框保留全部棱。两个不同空间点可能投到同一个屏幕位置，合并的投影标签不意味着空间点重合。有限面片也不表示平面到边框就结束。

## 家长核对与迁移

出口用AB与CG反驳“无交点必平行”，解释中应听见“共面”这个必要条件以及两线方向不同。家长再问AE与底面为何不算整线包含，要求给出A以外的点。下一课使用面与棱的结构分类立体，距离课再用这些关系寻找真正的垂足和公垂线。

# Points, lines and planes in space

A drawing compresses three dimensions onto a two-dimensional surface. Lines crossing on paper need not meet in space, and lines drawn apart need not be parallel. Identify objects and premises before interpreting the picture.

## One central idea

**Relationships between full lines and planes depend on common points, directions and coplanarity, rather than projection alone.** A point specifies a position. A full line extends indefinitely in both directions, and a plane extends indefinitely in every direction within it. Drawn edges and finite patches represent those unbounded objects.

Distinct coplanar lines can intersect or be parallel. Space also permits skew lines: neither parallel nor intersecting, hence noncoplanar. Coincident lines are not distinct parallel lines. This unit separates strict parallelism from coincidence, and a line meeting a plane once from a line wholly contained in it.

### Example 1: Nonintersecting does not imply parallel

Name a cuboid with consecutive bottom vertices A,B,C,D and matching upper vertices E,F,G,H. Let A=(0,0,0), B=(4,0,0), C=(4,3,0), G=(4,3,4), in centimetres.

Every point on full line AB has y=z=0. Every point on CG has x=4 and y=3, so there can be no common point. AB has the x direction while CG has the z direction: they are not parallel. They are skew. If both belonged to one plane, its nonparallel lines would meet, contradicting the coordinate check.

By contrast AB and CD share the base plane, have the same direction and are distinct, so are parallel. AB and AE share A and intersect. Comparing all these cases is more reliable than memorising a rule based on whether strokes cross on a drawing.

### Example 2: One common point versus containment

The base plane is z=0. Distinct A and B both belong to it, and extending their joining line keeps z=0. Full line AB is contained in that plane.

Full line AE also passes through A but leaves the base in the z direction, meeting it only at A. One point in a plane is therefore insufficient to certify line containment. Naming the same point twice does not supply a second distinct point or a unique line direction.

Distinct planes z=0 and y=0 share all points with y=z=0: the complete x-axis. Their intersection is a full line, not merely the short piece visible in a diagram.

## Relationship workbench

Choose the studied objects and predict intersecting, parallel, skew, coincident or contained. A camera change alters only projection, preserving your prediction. Reveal coordinates afterwards and explain using common points and directions rather than colour or a screen angle.

The transparent wireframe retains every edge. Different spatial points may project to one screen position; combined projected labels do not certify spatial coincidence. Likewise a finite plane patch does not make the represented plane stop at its border.

## Parent check and transfer

The exit challenges “nonintersecting means parallel” with AB and CG. Listen for coplanarity and the different line directions. Then ask why AE is not contained in the base, requesting a point other than A as evidence.
