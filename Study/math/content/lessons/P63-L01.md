## 先说量名，再写前后项

A 有四张卡，B 有六张卡。“相差几张”“A 是 B 的几分之几”“A 占全部几分之几”比较的是同样两堆，却不是同一个问题。**比按指定顺序比较两种量**，写成 A:B，前项是 A 的量，后项是 B 的量。

在本课后项为正数时，a:b 的比值是 a÷b。冒号写法保留比较的两项，比值则是一个数。2:3 与 2/3 有关联，但题目要求写比时，应使用冒号明确两项；不能不说对象就把 2/3 当作 A 占整体的份额。

### 例 1 · 部分比部分与部分比整体

1. A 四张、B 六张。按 A:B 的顺序写 **4:6**，等值地写 **2:3**。
2. 这个比值是 **4÷6=2/3**，意思是 A 的张数为 B 的三分之二。
3. 两堆合起来共有 **4+6=10 张**。
4. A:总量应写 **4:10=2:5**，所以 A 占整体 **2/5**，不是 2/3。
5. 若改问 B:A，前后项交换为 **6:4=3:2**；若问差，则为 **6−4=2 张**。对象、顺序与问题都要读完整。

工作台用带 A 字母的圆点与带 B 字母的方块表示真实个数，颜色不是唯一线索。切换 A:B、B:A 与 A:总量，数量不变，问题改变。先口头预测，再按按钮核对；改个数或比较对象会清空旧反馈，不能沿用上一次答案。

### 例 2 · 先统一实际单位

1. A 长 **0.6 m**，B 长 **90 cm**，求 A:B。
2. 同一种长度比较先用同单位：**0.6 m=60 cm**。
3. 因此 **A:B=60:90=2:3**。
4. 若直接比 0.6:90，就把米与厘米当成相同计量单位，差了一百倍。
5. 也可以都用米：**0.6:0.9=2:3**。改变共同单位不改变两条实际长度的关系。

本课同类量比较在统一单位后可以得到无量纲比值。以后用“每升三元”等不同单位的比较时，要保留单位所表达的意义；不能把不同物理量随意混作同一种量。

**零的边界：** A 为零张、B 为五张时，0:5 的比值为零，等值比可写 0:1。后项零不能用前项除后项定义比值；特别是 0:0 不是“一比一”。工作台默认使用正个数，练习中的零边界要用除法定义说明。

**同比的预告：** 4:6 与 8:12 表示相同的比较关系，但实际张数不同，差也从二变成四。与上一单元的百分数一样，同样比例不保证实际个数相同。下一课将解释怎样保持两项关系并化成最简整数比。

**家长陪学：** 请孩子补完“前项是__，后项是__，整体是__”。认可条形、同样小份与口头推理，不能仅凭写出 2:3 就判全部理解。**带走一个发现：** 写比之前先确认对象、顺序与单位；部分比部分不等于部分占整体。

## Name the quantities before writing the terms

A has four cards and B six. Their difference, A as a fraction of B, and A as a fraction of all cards are different comparisons. A **ratio compares quantities in a specified order**: A:B puts A first and B second.

With a positive second term, a:b has quotient a÷b. The colon retains the two compared terms, while their quotient is a number. The ratio 2:3 is related to 2/3, but an exercise asking for a ratio uses a colon to identify both terms. Without naming the reference, do not assume 2/3 describes A’s share of the whole.

### Example 1 · Part-to-part versus part-to-whole

1. A has four cards and B six. In A:B order, write **4:6=2:3**.
2. Its quotient is **4÷6=2/3**: A’s count is two thirds of B’s count.
3. Together the collections contain **4+6=10 cards**.
4. A:total is **4:10=2:5**, so A is **2/5** of all cards, rather than 2/3.
5. Reversing the question gives **B:A=6:4=3:2**. Their difference is **6−4=2 cards**, another comparison again.

The workshop uses lettered circles for A and lettered squares for B, so colour is not the only cue. Switching between A:B, B:A and A:total changes the question without changing the quantities. Predict before checking. Changing a count or target clears the old feedback.

### Example 2 · Match actual measurement units first

1. A is **0.6 m** long and B **90 cm**. Find A:B.
2. Express the lengths in one common unit: **0.6 m=60 cm**.
3. Thus **A:B=60:90=2:3**.
4. Comparing 0.6:90 directly treats metres and centimetres as equal-sized units, introducing a hundredfold error.
5. Both could instead use metres: **0.6:0.9=2:3**. Changing a common measurement unit preserves the relationship between the actual lengths.

These same-kind quantities give dimensionless quotients after units match. Later comparisons such as three dollars per litre retain the meaning of different units; unrelated physical quantities cannot simply be treated as one kind of measurement.

If A has zero cards and B five, 0:5 has quotient zero and is equivalent to 0:1. A zero second term cannot define a quotient through division. In particular, 0:0 does not mean one-to-one. Workshop counts start positive, while the zero-boundary exercise asks you to reason from the division definition.

The ratios 4:6 and 8:12 describe the same comparison but different actual counts. Their differences are two and four. Just as with percentages, an unchanged proportion need not preserve actual counts. The next lesson explains how to preserve the relationship while finding simplest integer terms.

**For a parent:** ask the learner to complete “the first quantity is__, the second is__, and the whole is__.” Accept bars, equal-part models and spoken reasoning, rather than treating a correct 2:3 alone as proof of understanding. **Takeaway:** identify quantities, order and units before writing a ratio; part-to-part differs from part-to-whole.
