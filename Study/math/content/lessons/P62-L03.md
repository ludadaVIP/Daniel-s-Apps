## 精确百分数不一定有有限小数

分数与百分数都能表示比例。把 a/b 写成百分数，就是求一个系数 p，使 **p/100=a/b**。因此 **p=(a/b)×100**。能用整数倍把分母扩成一百时，可以直接用等值分数；不能这样扩分时，百分数仍存在，系数可以是小数或分数。

百格图里的每一格始终占原整体的 1%。某个比例落在一格中间，图会保留相应的部分面积，不把它取整。分数超过一时，沿用原框大小扩展到第二个框，不把更大的图重新当一个整体。

### 例 1 · 八分之三对应三十七格半

1. 原比例为 **3/8**，先想它比一半少。
2. 分子除分母：**3÷8=0.375**。
3. 换成每百份，系数 **0.375×100=37.5**。
4. 所以 **3/8=37.5%**，精确成立；百格图涂三十七格半。
5. 核回：**37.5÷100×8=3**，与原分子恢复一致。结果小于 50%，也符合估计。

另一种方便的例子是 7/20。把分子分母同乘五，得到 35/100=35%。不能只把分母改成一百写 7%，那会把原比例缩小五倍。方法不同，等值是共同要求。

### 例 2 · 三分之一要保留精确关系

1. 原比例 **1/3**，百分数系数为 **(1/3)×100=100/3**。
2. **100/3=33 1/3**，所以精确写作 **1/3=33 1/3%**。
3. 输入时可以写 `33 1/3%`，也可写 `100/3%`；百分号作用于前面完整的分数系数。
4. 取一位小数时，写 **1/3≈33.3%**，必须用约等号。
5. 为什么不能用等号？**33.3%=333/1000**，乘三得到 0.999；而 **(1/3)×3=1**。二者很接近，却不相等。

同样，2/3 的精确百分数是 **66 2/3%**，不是精确的 66.7%。工作台可以展示近似值帮助读数，但计算与判题仍保留精确有理数。这里练习明确要求精确百分数，不能把四舍五入后的答案当作已经完成目标。

**超过百分之一百：** 5/4=1.25=125%。它表示一个又四分之一个参考整体，可以用于比较需求、长度或参考量。若题目说“全部学生中的某一部分”，部分作为子集当然不会超过整体；能否超过 100% 取决于比较对象，不能只背“百分数永远小于一百”的规则。

工作台依次改变分母、分子，范围从零到两个原整体。先比较大小与 50%、100% 的关系，再读图，最后核对。把分母设三、分子设一时，观察第三十四格的部分面积与精确带分数百分数；改变题目后旧解析会消失。

**家长陪学：** 解释题应说明百分号除以一百、原分数的等值性，以及近似符号的作用。可以通过乘回、三份相加或百格图说明 33.3% 为什么不足精确的三分之一，不要求固定术语句式。**带走一个发现：** 所有有效分数都能写百分数；有限小数只是其中一类，精确值与近似值要分别标明。

## An exact percentage need not terminate

A fraction and a percentage can express the same proportion. To write a/b as a percentage, find a coefficient p satisfying **p/100=a/b**, so **p=(a/b)×100**. When an integer scaling makes the denominator one hundred, equivalent fractions are convenient. Otherwise the percentage still exists; its coefficient may be decimal or fractional.

Each hundred-grid cell always represents 1% of the original whole. A proportion that ends inside a cell retains the appropriate area rather than rounding to a whole cell. Beyond one whole, extend the drawing with another identical frame instead of redefining the enlarged picture as one.

### Example 1 · Three eighths becomes thirty-seven and a half cells

1. Start with **3/8**, which should be below one half.
2. Divide: **3÷8=0.375**.
3. Its per-hundred coefficient is **0.375×100=37.5**.
4. Thus **3/8=37.5%** exactly, shading thirty-seven and a half hundred-grid cells.
5. Check **37.5÷100×8=3** to recover the numerator. Being below 50% agrees with the initial estimate.

For 7/20, multiply numerator and denominator by five to get 35/100=35%. Changing only the denominator to one hundred would produce 7%, five times too small. Different methods must meet the same requirement: preserve equivalence.

### Example 2 · Preserve the exact one-third relationship

1. For **1/3**, the percent coefficient is **(1/3)×100=100/3**.
2. Since **100/3=33 1/3**, write **1/3=33 1/3%** exactly.
3. The answer field accepts `33 1/3%` or `100/3%`; the percent sign applies to the complete preceding coefficient.
4. Rounded to one decimal place, write **1/3≈33.3%**, using an approximation sign.
5. Why not equality? **33.3%=333/1000**, which gives 0.999 when multiplied by three. In contrast **(1/3)×3=1**. The values are close but different.

Likewise, two thirds is exactly **66 2/3%**, rather than exactly 66.7%. The workshop may show a rounded value to help reading, but calculations and checking retain exact rational values. These exercises request exact percentages; a rounded answer does not complete that objective.

An improper fraction such as 5/4 equals 1.25=125%, one and one quarter reference wholes. Such a comparison can describe a requirement, length or other quantity relative to a reference amount. A subset of all students cannot exceed its collection, however. Whether a percentage can exceed 100% depends on the quantities being compared.

Change denominator and numerator in the workshop from zero to two original wholes. Predict the relation to 50% and 100%, inspect the grid, then check. With denominator three and numerator one, examine the partial thirty-fourth cell and exact mixed-number percentage. Changing the problem clears the old explanation.

**For a parent:** look for the percent sign’s division by one hundred, equivalence to the original fraction and the meaning of approximation. Multiplication back, three equal shares or a grid can show why 33.3% is slightly below one third. **Takeaway:** valid fractions have percentage representations, but only some terminate as decimals. Label exact and rounded values separately.
