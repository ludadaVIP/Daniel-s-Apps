## 面片与内部空间是不同的量

圆柱有圆形底面和弯曲侧面。把侧面完整展开，得到长方形：长为底圆周长 **2πr**，宽为圆柱高 **h**。它要围完整一圈，所以长不是直径 2r。圆面与侧面使用同一长度比例绘制，展开时不能随意把圆拉成椭圆来凑宽。

侧面积为 **2πrh**；每个圆面面积为 **πr²**。需要 k 个圆面时，面片总面积 **S=2πrh+kπr²**。封闭圆柱 k=2，无盖但有底 k=1，只做侧面 k=0。面积模型不含粘贴舌、接缝、材料厚度和裁剪浪费，不能直接当采购纸张的实际预算。

### 例 1 · 封闭、无盖和侧面分别数面

1. 理想薄壁圆柱 **r=2 cm、h=5 cm**，侧面展开长 4π cm、宽五厘米。
2. 侧面积 **4π×5=20π cm²**。
3. 一个圆底面积为 **π×2²=4π cm²**。
4. 封闭需要两个圆面，**S=20π+8π=28π cm²**。
5. 无盖只需一个圆底，S=24π cm²；只做侧面则为 20π cm²。去掉上盖少一个底面积，而不是少一个周长。

展开图的圆与侧面相切而不重叠，长度比例统一。圆底的直径和侧面长 2πr 是不同的长度；不能把圆的宽度直接当作长方形长。要区分“实际圆面直径二倍半径”和“围一周所需的展开长”。

### 例 2 · 相同系数不代表相同量

1. 同一个理想圆柱内部半径二厘米、高五厘米，底面积 **4π cm²**。
2. 每层底面沿高延伸，**V=πr²h=4π×5=20π cm³**。
3. 侧面积也有数值系数 20π，但单位是 cm²，测的是面；体积 cm³ 测的是空间。
4. 去掉盖片不改变内部 r、h，因此完整内部空间体积仍为 20π cm³。
5. 若按 π≈3.14 算，体积约 **62.8 cm³**；这不是封闭表面积约 87.92 cm²。

实际容器容量要用**内部**半径和内部高度。外部尺寸包含壁厚，不能默默拿来当内部容量尺寸。本课统一使用理想薄壁或明确给定内部尺寸，避免在同一道题里把两种 r 混用。只剩侧面、没有底时，公式还能描述相应完整圆柱空间的体积，却不能声称这张侧面材料可以盛住这些液体。

周长用 cm，面积用 cm²，体积用 cm³；不能把不同维度直接加减。若要换算成毫升，先说明 1 cm³=1 mL，而不是只把平方厘米的单位换成升。

工作台改变盖片数后，侧面长方形和体积不变，只有圆面数量及其面积合计变化。改变 r 会同时影响周长、底面积和体积，改变 h 则保留圆底。家长请孩子先指面、数面、说单位，再计算。家庭任务只需画图比较封闭与无盖，不切割硬罐。带走一个发现：**展开数面求表面积，底面积乘内部高求体积；量名、尺寸参照与单位一起核对。**

## Face area differs from internal space

A cylinder has circular ends and a curved side. Fully unwrapping the side gives a rectangle of length **2πr** and width **h**. Its length wraps a complete boundary turn, so it is not diameter 2r. The net uses one scale for circles and rectangle rather than distorting their proportions.

Side area is **2πrh** and each circular end area **πr²**. With k required ends, total face area is **S=2πrh+kπr²**: k=2 for closed, k=1 for open top with a bottom, and k=0 for the side alone. This excludes tabs, seams, wall thickness and cutting waste. Face area is not automatically a real material-purchase budget.

### Example 1 · Count ends for closed, open and side-only forms

1. Let an ideal thin-walled cylinder have **r=2 cm and h=5 cm**. The side rectangle is 4π cm long and five centimetres high.
2. Its area is **20π cm²**.
3. Each end has area **4π cm²**.
4. A closed cylinder needs two ends: **S=20π+8π=28π cm²**.
5. Open-top area is 24π cm²; side-only area is 20π cm². Removing a lid removes one end area, not one circumference.

The circles touch the side in the net without overlapping it, using the same scale. Base diameter and unwrapped side length are distinct. Do not mistake a circle’s width for the rectangular length needed to wrap around it.

### Example 2 · Equal coefficients do not equate dimensions

1. With internal radius two and height five, base area is **4π cm²**.
2. Extend the base along the height: **V=πr²h=20π cm³**.
3. Side area also has coefficient 20π, but cm² measures a surface and cm³ measures space.
4. Removing the lid leaves internal radius and height unchanged, so full internal volume remains 20π cm³.
5. Using π≈3.14 gives volume about **62.8 cm³**, distinct from closed surface area about 87.92 cm².

Actual capacity requires **internal** radius and height. Outside measurements include wall thickness and cannot silently substitute for inside dimensions. These problems use an ideal thin-walled model or explicitly supplied internal dimensions. A side without a bottom still corresponds to a full cylinder’s geometric volume, but is not a vessel capable of retaining that volume of liquid.

Circumference, area and volume use cm, cm² and cm³ respectively; quantities of different dimensions cannot be added or subtracted. Converting capacity to millilitres requires the separate relationship 1 cm³=1 mL, not replacing a square-centimetre unit with litres.

Selecting different end counts leaves the side rectangle and full internal volume unchanged. Changing radius affects circumference, base area and volume; changing height retains the base. Ask a parent to identify and count faces and name units before calculating. A paper comparison of closed and open-top forms needs no cutting of rigid cans. The takeaway combines unwrapping and face counting for surface area, base area times internal height for volume, and consistent quantity, reference dimensions and units.
