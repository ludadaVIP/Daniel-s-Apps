# 空间距离的向量方法

空间距离仍遵循平面中的“垂线最短”。关键是**先构造属于指定对象的垂足H，再算三维残余P−H的长度**。屏幕投影的长短与最短距离不是同一个量。

## 点到平面：扣去法向分量

平面n·X=d，n≠0，给点P。记s=n·P−d，把P沿法向移动−s/(n·n)倍n，得到H=P−[s/(n·n)]n。代回得n·H=d，H在平面上；P−H与n平行，故垂直平面。

距离为|s|/|n|，或先算(P−H)·(P−H)再开非负根。平面方程整体乘k≠0，法向与右端一同乘k，s乘k、n·n乘k²，投影结果抵消，几何垂足与距离不变。

## 点到直线：留下沿线分量

直线X=A+tu，u≠0，要求(P−H)·u=0。代H=A+tu得t=((P−A)·u)/(u·u)，再算H及|P−H|。直线方向换成ku时参数变为t/k，直线本身与垂足不变。注意平面用法向，直线用沿线方向，不能把两个公式的几何意义反过来。

### 例1：点到平面的垂足和最短线段

P(3,4,5)，平面x+2y+2z=3，n=(1,2,2)。s=3+8+10−3=18，n·n=9，所以H=P−2n=(1,0,1)。它满足1+0+2=3；残余P−H=(2,4,4)=2n，确为法向。

长度√(4+16+16)=6，公式也给|18|/3=6。若点本来是(1,0,1)，s=0、H=P，距离0完全有定义，不能把零距离误说成零法向的无效情况。

### 例2：同一点到不同对象，不同垂足

仍取P(3,4,5)，直线A(1,0,1)+t(1,1,0)。P−A=(2,4,4)，与u点积6，u·u=2，所以t=3，H=(4,3,1)。残余(−1,1,4)与u点积−1+1=0，距离√18=3√2。

这个H不同于例1，因为指定对象不同。把u改成−2u时参数变−3/2，但H仍(4,3,1)；把平面方程整体乘−2，例1的H与距离也不变。不能只把法向乘−2而保留旧右端3，那会移动平面。

## 为什么这个距离最短

对对象上的任意点Q，Q−H是直线方向或平面内方向，和P−H垂直。于是PQ²=PH²+HQ²≥PH²，Q=H时取等号。这同时说明两个公式为何给最短值，而非恰巧算出某段线的长度。

工作台切换点到平面/直线，选择P与非零倍数，分别预测H和精确距离。倍数变化保留实际未变的H、距离；换相机同样保留。核验表列H、残余、平方距离，并核对n·H−d=0或(P−H)·u=0。

家庭出口完整核对两种对象、两种垂足与倍数不变量。家长先查H属于对象，再查残余垂直，最后查非负长度；缺少前两个条件时，漂亮的图或正确的单个数都不足以证明方法有效。

# Vector methods for spatial distance

The perpendicular remains shortest in space. **Construct the foot H on the stated object, then calculate the 3D residual length |P−H|**. A projected screen segment is not that measurement.

## Point to plane: subtract the normal component

For n·X=d with n≠0, let s=n·P−d and set H=P−[s/(n·n)]n. Substitution proves n·H=d. Residual P−H is parallel to n, hence perpendicular to the plane.

Distance is |s|/|n|, equivalently the nonnegative root of the squared residual. Scaling the entire equation by k≠0 scales both normal and right side. The factors in s and n·n cancel in the projection, preserving foot and distance.

## Point to line: retain the along-line component

For X=A+tu with u≠0, require (P−H)·u=0. Substituting H=A+tu gives t=((P−A)·u)/(u·u), followed by H and |P−H|. Replacing u by ku changes the parameter to t/k but preserves the line and foot. A plane uses its normal; a line uses its tangent direction.

### Example 1: A plane foot and shortest segment

For P(3,4,5) and x+2y+2z=3, normal n=(1,2,2), residual s=18 and n·n=9. Hence H=P−2n=(1,0,1), verified by 1+0+2=3. Residual (2,4,4)=2n is normal.

Its length is six, agreeing with |18|/3. For P=(1,0,1), s=0, H=P and distance zero is defined. Zero distance to a valid plane is different from an invalid zero normal.

### Example 2: The same point, a different object and foot

For the same P and line (1,0,1)+t(1,1,0), P−A=(2,4,4), whose dot product with u is six. Since u·u=2, t=3 and H=(4,3,1). Residual (−1,1,4) has dot zero with u and length √18=3√2.

The foot differs because the object differs. Scaling u by −2 changes t to −3/2 but keeps H. Scaling the entire plane equation similarly preserves Example 1. Scaling its normal alone while retaining right side three would change the plane.

## Why the constructed distance is shortest

For any point Q on the object, Q−H is tangent to the line or plane and perpendicular to P−H. Thus PQ²=PH²+HQ²≥PH², with equality at Q=H. This explains why the formulas give a minimum rather than just some segment length.

Choose plane or line, point and nonzero rescaling. Predict foot and exact distance independently. Rescaling preserves unchanged feet and distances; camera changes preserve mathematical answers. The audit lists the foot, residual and squared distance and checks n·H−d=0 or (P−H)·u=0.

The family exit verifies both objects, both feet and scaling invariants. Review incidence first, perpendicular residual second, and nonnegative length last. A plausible picture or isolated correct number does not establish the method without those checks.
