# 双曲线的几何定义

本课把到两焦点的距离和改成**绝对距离差**。绝对值不是装饰：它让左右两支都属于同一个轨迹，也决定了常数的边界。

## 条件先于标准式

固定F1(−c,0)、F2(c,0)，c>0，要求|PF1−PF2|=2a。反三角不等式给绝对差≤2c。非退化双曲线须0<a<c；a>c无点，a=c退化为焦点连线上外侧两条射线，a=0为垂直平分线x=0。

先看右支。由距离平方相减r1²−r2²=4cx，而r1−r2=2a，所以r1+r2=2cx/a。于是r1=cx/a+a、r2=cx/a−a。代入平方距离式，得到x²/a²−y²/b²=1，其中b²=c²−a²>0。左支对应r1−r2=−2a，同一标准式保留它。

反向核对也必要：标准式给|x|≥a，分出x≥a或x≤−a。右支的cx/a±a至少有c−a>0，确为非负距离；左支对称，交换较长和较短距离。因此两支恢复绝对差2a，没有因为平方就允许任意点。沿实轴的平方项为正，焦点轴可以水平也可以竖直。

### 例1：同一绝对差，对应相反有向差

F1(−5,0)、F2(5,0)，绝对距离差6。a=3、c=5，b²=25−9=16，方程x²/9−y²/16=1。右顶点P(3,0)的两距离8、2，有向差+6；左顶点Q(−3,0)为2、8，有向差−6。绝对差均6。

只写PF1−PF2=6就丢了左支；交换焦点名字只是交换被保留的一支。也不能把焦距10当成常数差6，或把减号改成椭圆的加号。对非顶点P(5,16/3)，距离为34/3和16/3，差仍6，可由勾股和标准式分别核对。

### 例2：方向与边界都须检查

y²/9−x²/16=1的实轴竖直，a=3、b=4、c=5，焦点(0,±5)，顶点(0,±3)。沿正y方向的焦点不是(5,0)；正项而非字母顺序决定实轴。

再回到焦距10的水平焦点。要求绝对差12无实点。要求10达到反三角不等式等号，只留下x≤−5或x≥5且y=0的两射线，包含焦点；焦点之间线段上的差并非处处10。要求0则两焦点等距，是整条垂直平分线x=0。这些边界均不能画成普通两支双曲线。

## 工作台如何保留两支

工作台显示两条曲线分支，可选正轴一支或负轴一支的P。F1与F2名字固定，PF1为实线、PF2为虚线。先算PF1和绝对差，再算有向差；换支后有向差反号，绝对差不变，所以未变的常数预测保留。

示范点用有理参数t，|t|<1避免参数分母为0；这不是把双曲线限制成有界图形。曲线在图窗外继续延伸，超窗点明确报告。换轴或中心只改位置；b²仍是c²−a²，与椭圆的关系不同。

家庭出口比较常数12、10、0、6的四种轨迹，须用不等式说明边界，并指出非退化情形的两支。家长还可把圆的固定距离、抛物线的等距、椭圆的距离和、双曲线的绝对差并排解释，但不能以“像哪幅图”代替定义。

# Geometric definition of hyperbolas

Replace the focal distance sum by an **absolute distance difference**. Absolute value includes both branches and determines the boundary conditions.

## Conditions before a standard equation

Fix F1(−c,0),F2(c,0), c>0, and require |PF1−PF2|=2a. The reverse triangle inequality bounds this by 2c. A nondegenerate hyperbola requires 0<a<c. For a>c the locus is empty; a=c gives two outer rays on the focal line; a=0 gives perpendicular bisector x=0.

On the right branch, r1−r2=2a and r1²−r2²=4cx, so r1+r2=2cx/a. Thus r1=cx/a+a,r2=cx/a−a. Substitution into the squared coordinate distance gives x²/a²−y²/b²=1, with b²=c²−a²>0. The left branch has r1−r2=−2a and satisfies the same standard equation.

Conversely, the equation gives |x|≥a. On the right branch the candidate distances cx/a±a are positive, with the smaller at least c−a>0. They are the actual distances. The left branch reverses the longer and shorter distances. Both therefore restore absolute difference 2a. The transverse-axis squared term is positive; its axis can be horizontal or vertical.

### Example 1: One absolute difference, opposite signed differences

Take F1(−5,0),F2(5,0), with absolute difference six. Then a=3,c=5,b²=16 and x²/9−y²/16=1. At right vertex P(3,0), distances eight and two give +6. At left vertex Q(−3,0), distances two and eight give −6. Both absolute differences are six.

Writing only PF1−PF2=6 loses the left branch. Relabelling the foci only swaps which branch is kept. Focal separation ten is not the fixed difference six, and changing the minus to plus changes the conic. At nonvertex P(5,16/3), distances 34/3 and 16/3 again differ by six; check with both Pythagoras and the equation.

### Example 2: Orientation and boundary loci

For y²/9−x²/16=1, the transverse axis is vertical, a=3,b=4,c=5. Foci are (0,±5) and vertices (0,±3). The positive-y focus is not (5,0); the positive term determines orientation.

Return to horizontal foci separated by ten. Absolute difference 12 is impossible. Difference ten reaches equality in the reverse triangle inequality, giving only y=0 with x≤−5 or x≥5, including the foci. The entire segment between the foci does not satisfy it. Difference zero gives the complete perpendicular bisector x=0. These boundaries are not ordinary two-branch hyperbolas.
