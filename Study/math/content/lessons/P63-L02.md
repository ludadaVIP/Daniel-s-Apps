## 改变共同计数单位，保留原关系

6:9 与 2:3 的实际数字不同，却有相同的比值 2/3。把前者化简，不是从 A 拿走四份、从 B 拿走六份，而是把每三个原小份看作一个共同的新计数单位：A 有两组，B 有三组，原数量全部保留。

**最简整数比**要同时满足两件事：与原比等值，并且两项为互质整数。只得到一个等值比不够；两项仍有大于一的共同因数，就还能继续同除。比较顺序也必须保留。

### 例 1 · 两步化简与一步化简

1. 把 **18:24** 两项同除二，得到 **9:12**，比值保持不变。
2. 九与十二仍有公因数三，因此 9:12 **不是最简整数比**。
3. 再同除三，得 **3:4**，三与四互质。
4. 也可直接同除最大公因数 **6**：18÷6:24÷6=3:4。
5. 交叉核查 **18×4=24×3=72**。若两项同减六得 12:18，比值变成 2/3，与原来的 3/4 不同。

工作台先统一整数计数，再选择共同除数。除数一能保留比值，却通常未最简；非公因数不能被截成完整组；选最大公因数后，分组框围住原来的每个小符号，不会删除数量。

### 例 2 · 小数与分数先统一成整数项

1. 两项同单位的 **0.6:0.9**，同乘十，得 **6:9**。
2. 再同除三，得到最简整数比 **2:3**。
3. 对 **1/2:3/4**，两项同乘共同分母四，得 **2:3**。
4. 这可以解释成以四分之一为小单位：一半含两小份，四分之三含三小份。
5. 两项必须同乘或同除同一个非零数；只把 1/2 的分子变二、另一项保持不动，不是在保留原关系。

共同分母不必总是唯一选择，统一单位也可能得到不同的等值整数项。例如 1.2:1.8 可以先写 12:18，也可以按五分之一单位计成 6:9；继续化简都得到 2:3。工作台会说明它所用的小单位，不把不同整数中间式误判成不同数量。

**测量单位要先匹配：** 1.5 m:50 cm 先写 150 cm:50 cm，再化简成 3:1。不能把 1.5 与五十直接化简，否则比较的计量单位不一致。单位换算与最简形式是不同步骤，前一步做错，后一步算得再熟也不能补救。

输入框在要求最简时接受 `2:3`、`2：3` 与前后空格；等值的 `4:6` 会提示数量关系相同但还需完成形式。小数项 `0.2:0.3` 虽等值，也没有满足“整数比”的要求。后项保持正数；若前项为零，0:5 的最简形式是 0:1，不是 0:0。

**家长陪学：** 让孩子说明每次缩放的共同因数，再用交叉乘积或原比值核对，并确认最终互质。可以分多步做，不把“必须一步找最大公因数”当作理解的唯一形式。**带走一个发现：** 最简比保留顺序和比值，只用共同计数单位把两项写得更清楚。

## Change a shared counting unit, preserving the relationship

6:9 and 2:3 have the same quotient, two thirds. Simplifying does not remove four units from A and six from B. Group each three original units as one new common counting unit: A has two groups and B three, with all original quantity retained.

A **simplest integer ratio** must be equivalent to the original and have coprime integer terms. Equivalence alone is insufficient. If a common integer factor greater than one remains, divide both terms again. Preserve the requested order throughout.

### Example 1 · Two stages or one

1. Divide both terms of **18:24** by two to obtain **9:12**, preserving the quotient.
2. Nine and twelve still share factor three, so 9:12 is **not simplest**.
3. Divide both by three for **3:4**, whose terms are coprime.
4. Alternatively, divide eighteen and twenty-four directly by their greatest common factor **six**.
5. Check **18×4=24×3=72**. Subtracting six from both terms would give 12:18, changing the quotient to two thirds instead of three quarters.

The workshop first establishes integer counts, then lets you choose a common divisor. Dividing by one preserves equivalence but usually leaves simplification unfinished. A nonfactor cannot be truncated into complete groups. Group outlines enclose the original symbols rather than removing them.

### Example 2 · Convert decimal and fractional terms to integers

1. For matching units, multiply both terms of **0.6:0.9** by ten to get **6:9**.
2. Divide both by three to obtain **2:3** in simplest integer terms.
3. For **1/2:3/4**, multiply both terms by common denominator four to get **2:3**.
4. Think in quarter-units: one half contains two and three quarters contains three.
5. Both terms need the same nonzero multiplier or divisor. Altering one term alone changes the comparison.

An intermediate common unit need not be unique. For 1.2:1.8, counting tenths gives 12:18, while counting fifths gives 6:9. Both simplify to 2:3. The workshop names its chosen unit instead of implying that different equivalent integer intermediates represent different original quantities.

Match measurement units first: 1.5 m:50 cm becomes 150 cm:50 cm, then 3:1. Simplifying the bare numbers 1.5 and fifty would compare mismatched units. Conversion and simplest form are separate requirements; correct simplification cannot repair incorrect unit conversion.

When simplest terms are required, the answer field accepts `2:3`, a full-width colon, and surrounding spaces. It recognizes `4:6` as equivalent but unfinished. Likewise, `0.2:0.3` is equivalent without being an integer ratio. Keep the second term positive; a zero first term gives 0:5=0:1, not 0:0.

**For a parent:** ask for the common scaling factor, equivalence evidence and a final coprime check. Multiple valid stages are welcome; finding the greatest common factor in one step is not the only evidence of understanding. **Takeaway:** a simplest ratio preserves order and quotient while expressing both terms using a clearer shared counting unit.
