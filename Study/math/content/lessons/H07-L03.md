# 等差数列求和

末项aₙ回答“最后是多少”，部分和Sₙ回答“前n项合计多少”。同一个数列有两套不同的问题，公式之前先说清正在计算哪一个量。

## 一条核心想法

**正序一份加倒序一份，每列都为a₁+aₙ，所以2Sₙ=n(a₁+aₙ)。** 最后把两份总值整体除2，得到Sₙ=n(a₁+aₙ)/2=n[2a₁+(n−1)d]/2。

这个说明不要求n为偶数，也不要求项为正。两行都是完整数列，不是把同一行拆成左右各一半。n是项数，末项仍须用n−1个公差求得。

### 例1：奇数项，中间项在两份中各一次

a₁=2、d=3，前5项是2、5、8、11、14。末项a₅=14；倒序一份为14、11、8、5、2，与原序每列相加都为16。

共5列，因此2S₅=5×16=80，S₅=40。中间列8+8正好来自两份中的各一个8，没有额外重复；整体除2已经恢复一份，不需要再删除8或单独给它除2。

平均数S₅/5=8恰好也是中间项，但既不是末项14，也不是总和40。判断孩子是否区分了这些对象，比只看代数式写对更重要。

### 例2：负项与从部分和恢复一项

对5、3、1、−1、−3、−5，首末和0，每一列同样和0，所以S₆=6×0/2=0。把所有符号去掉会得到绝对值和18，它不是题设带符号的和；不能因为图卡有正面积就把负项改正。

若给S₅=40、S₄=26，则第五项a₅=S₅−S₄=14。前四项在两和相减时全部抵消，因此一般aₙ=Sₙ−Sₙ₋₁。Sₙ/n是平均数，不是一般的aₙ。

规定空和S₀=0后，n=1时也有a₁=S₁−S₀=S₁；求和从0项开始不代表原数列多出a₀。n=1的配对图有两份a₁，整体除2同样正确。

## 两份配对工作台

先预测末项、一列的总值与Sₙ，再切换倒序行。卡片上保留项的符号，颜色只区分两份副本，不表示负面积。展开核验显示两份和与一份和的关系。

切换配对显示、精确表或图窗都保持预测；改变项数或规则才重新计算有关量。扩大图窗只改变观察范围，不增加求和项数，不把aₙ换成aₙ₊₁。

## 家长核对与迁移

对奇数项追问：“中间项属于哪一行？第二行是不是完整一份？”出口应解释8+8为何合法，而不只报40。下一课转为固定倍率，等比求和再用“整份数列变换后相减”，保留本课的完整副本思想。

# Sums of arithmetic series

The last term aₙ asks for the final value. Partial sum Sₙ asks for the total of the first n terms. Identify which quantity is requested before choosing a formula.

## One central idea

**One forward copy plus one reversed copy has column total a₁+aₙ, so 2Sₙ=n(a₁+aₙ).** Halving the combined total gives Sₙ=n(a₁+aₙ)/2=n[2a₁+(n−1)d]/2.

This argument requires neither even n nor positive terms. Both rows are complete copies, not halves of one original row. The last term still lies n−1 differences beyond the first.

### Example 1: An odd middle term belongs to each copy once

For a₁=2,d=3, the first five terms are 2,5,8,11,14 and the last term is fourteen. Reverse a complete copy to 14,11,8,5,2. Every column totals sixteen.

Five columns give 2S₅=80 and S₅=40. The middle 8+8 comes from one eight in each copy. Halving the entire total already restores one copy; removing eight or halving it separately would adjust the middle twice.

The mean S₅/5=8 happens to equal the middle term. It differs from both the last term fourteen and the total forty. Checking those object distinctions matters more than merely reproducing an algebraic expression.

### Example 2: Signed terms and recovering a term from sums

For 5,3,1,−1,−3,−5, the first-plus-last value is zero, giving S₆=6×0/2=0. Dropping signs instead produces absolute-value sum eighteen, a different quantity. Rectangular cards represent signed numerical values, not negative physical areas.

If S₅=40 and S₄=26, then a₅=S₅−S₄=14. The first four terms cancel. More generally aₙ=Sₙ−Sₙ₋₁, while Sₙ/n is a mean rather than the last term in general.

Define empty sum S₀=0. Then the boundary n=1 also gives a₁=S₁−S₀=S₁. Defining a zero-term sum does not add a zeroth term to the original sequence. The two-copy diagram for n=1 simply contains two copies of a₁, correctly halved overall.

## Two-copy pairing workbench

Predict the final term, one column total and Sₙ before toggling the reversed row. Cards retain signs; their colours distinguish copies rather than areas. The audit reveals how the doubled sum gives one sum.

Changing the pairing display, exact table or viewing window preserves work. Changing the count or rule resets relevant tasks. A larger window neither increases the number of summed terms nor substitutes aₙ₊₁ for aₙ.

## Parent check and transfer

For an odd count ask which row supplies each middle term and whether the second row is a full copy. The exit explains why 8+8 is valid instead of only reporting forty. Geometric sequences next use fixed multiplication; their summation later retrieves the idea of transforming an entire copy before subtracting.
