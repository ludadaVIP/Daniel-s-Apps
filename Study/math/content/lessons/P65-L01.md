## 字母代表一个数，代入要前后一致

字母可以为一个数保留位置。例如 **n+n+3** 表示“同一个数的两份，再加三”。字母不是物品名称，也不自动等于一；它的值要看题目给定或情境允许。这里 n 是纯数，可以试零、正数和负数。若 n 表示袋中物品个数，允许值通常改为非负整数；先说明字母代表什么，才知道可以代入哪些数。

**2n** 是 **2×n** 的简写，系数二表示两份 n。它不是 2+n，也不是把二和 n 的数码拼接。把乘号省略是书写约定，不能改掉原本的运算。两数之间仍须写乘号，例如 2×4 不能写成 24。

### 例 1 · 两个 n 必须代入同一个四

1. 计算 n+n+3，题目给 n=4。
2. 在每个 n 的位置都放入四，得到 **4+4+3**。
3. 相同加数可写两份：**2×4+3**。
4. 先乘后加，八加三得 **11**。
5. 核查 2n+3 和 n+n+3 都为十一；n+5 却为九。后者把“第二个 n”误当成二。

同一个式子里的 n 不能第一个取四、第二个取五。不同字母则按各自条件代入：若 a=2、b=5，a+b 为七。不同字母也**可以碰巧取相同值**，不能反过来说字母不同数值一定不同。

### 例 2 · 零和负数不会让常数消失

1. 当 n=0，**2n+3=2×0+3=3**，留下常数三。
2. 当 n=−2，先用括号保留整个负数：**2×(−2)+3**。
3. 两份负二相加为负四，再加三得到 **−1**。
4. 用重复加法核查：**(−2)+(−2)+3=−1**。
5. n+5 在这两种输入时分别为五、三，和原式都不同，反例足以排除这个错误简写。

式子 **2n+3** 本身没有宣称某个等式成立；它在描述怎样由 n 得到一个数。写 n=4 是给定取值；写 2n+3=11 则提出一个相等条件。下一步学方程时要保留这个区别，不能看到字母就一律“求 x”。

工作台改 n 后先读出括号代入，再计算，旧反馈会清空。请至少试四、零和负二，分别说出变化的两份 n 与不变的三。负数出现不代表把式子前面的二也变负。

家庭纸笔挑战：写一个“某数的两倍再加三”的说明，让家长指定两个不同输入。孩子在每个字母位置画小框再填数，家长改变输入后核查是否漏换某处。带走一个发现：**相同字母同值代入，系数表示相同份数，常数独立保留。**

## A letter stands for a number; substitute consistently

In **n+n+3**, n reserves a place for a number: two copies of that same number, then three more. It is neither an object name nor automatically one. Its value depends on an assignment or the allowed inputs in a situation. Here n is a pure number, so zero and negative inputs are meaningful. If n counts objects in a bag, the allowed inputs usually become nonnegative whole numbers. Define the letter before choosing values.

**2n** abbreviates **2×n**. The coefficient two specifies two copies of n. It does not mean 2+n or joining decimal digits. Omitting a multiplication sign is a notation convention, not a change of operation. Keep the sign between two numerical factors: 2×4 cannot be written as 24.

### Example 1 · Replace both n values by the same four

1. Evaluate n+n+3 with n=4.
2. Replace every n to obtain **4+4+3**.
3. The repeated addend gives **2×4+3**.
4. Multiply before adding: eight plus three is **11**.
5. Both n+n+3 and 2n+3 give eleven, whereas n+5 gives nine. The latter incorrectly replaced the second n by two.

One occurrence of n cannot be four and the next five in this expression. Different letters use their own assignments: a=2 and b=5 give a+b=7. Different letters may also happen to have equal values; their different names do not force different numbers.

### Example 2 · Zero and negatives leave the fixed term intact

1. At n=0, **2n+3=2×0+3=3**: the constant three remains.
2. At n=−2, preserve the whole signed input in brackets: **2×(−2)+3**.
3. Two copies of negative two make negative four; adding three gives **−1**.
4. Check by repeated addition: **(−2)+(−2)+3=−1**.
5. The proposed n+5 gives five and three at those inputs, rather than three and negative one. A counterexample suffices to reject a claimed general identity.

The expression **2n+3** describes how to obtain a value from n; by itself it states no equality condition. Writing n=4 assigns an input. Writing 2n+3=11 states that two values must agree, introducing an equation. A letter does not automatically mean that you must solve for an unknown.

Change n in the workshop, read the bracketed substitution and then calculate. Try four, zero and negative two, naming the changing two copies and the unchanged three. A negative input does not make the coefficient two negative as well. For a paper challenge, describe “twice a number, then add three” and ask a parent to assign two inputs. Mark every letter location before filling it in, then check that none retained the old value. The takeaway is consistent substitution, a coefficient as equal copies, and a separate fixed term.
