# 复平面中的几何意义

同一个i出现在加法和乘法里，会产生不同几何操作。本课把**复数、点与相对中心的位移**联系起来，避免省略旋转中心。

## 一条核心想法

复数z=a+bi对应点(a,b)，两轴同单位、正虚轴向上。加u+vi使坐标变(a+u,b+v)，是平移；乘i则给i(a+bi)=−b+ai，把(a,b)变(−b,a)，绕原点逆时针90°。

乘−i为绕原点顺时针90°，乘2从原点把长度放大2倍，取共轭关于实轴反射。乘0把所有点送到0，像仍存在，却不能恢复原来的长度和方向。操作形式必须先说清，不能见到i就把它当平移。

### 例1：同一个原点，不同的操作

对z=2+i，点为(2,1)。加1+i得到3+2i，对应(3,2)，整支位移是(1,1)。乘i得到2i+i²=−1+2i，对应(−1,2)，是绕原点转动，而不是给每点加同一个常数。

原距离|z|=√5，转后|iz|=sqrt((−1)²+2²)=√5，保持到原点的距离。一般(a,b)变(−b,a)，平方距离b²+a²不变。对非零z，原点箭头确有逆时针90°的转角。

z=0时，i·0=0运算仍定义清楚，原点是固定点；但两个零箭头没有方向，不能给它们捏造90°或0°夹角。整张平面的90°旋转映射与它固定中心点并不冲突。

### 例2：把旋转中心从0改成c

要绕c逆时针90°，不能直接写iz。先取相对位移z−c，乘i，再加回中心：w=c+i(z−c)。若z=c，相对位移0，像仍是c。

纸上给z=2+i、c=2−i。直接乘i得到A=−1+2i，坐标(−1,2)，|A|=√5，是绕原点转动。绕c则先算z−c=2i，乘i得−2，加回2−i得B=−i，对应(0,−1)。

原点到B的距离为1，与原来的√5不同；但B−c=−2，所以|B−c|=2=|z−c|，正确保持到中心c的距离。A与B是不同像，不能省掉两个关于中心的步骤。另用c=1+i会得到z−c=1、像1+2i，也与iz不同。

## 点、中心与位移工作台

选择原像z、操作与中心，先预测像的a+bi式、有序坐标和像到参照中心的距离，再展开图。只有“绕c”操作使用中心控件，其余操作保持原点参照；在其余操作下改变未使用的c不清除有效答案。

图中箭头从当前参照中心出发，区别于把z本身当作从任意点出发的自由箭头。扩大视窗不改变操作；同一位置的z、c、w合并显示名字而不人为错开。点在旋转中心时，显示像点和零相对位移，不画虚构方向。

家庭挑战写出A、B、各自坐标、对应中心的距离与所用公式。若只报两个正确坐标，却把中心都说成原点，说明代数与图形还未连起来。

## 家长核对与迁移

要求孩子先指中心，再描述方向与保留的距离。出口必须说明为什么B要先减c再加c，零点问题须区分运算有定义与夹角无定义。下一单元进入直线、圆与圆锥曲线，将继续用坐标、距离与明确的几何条件解释方程。

# Complex numbers on the plane

The same symbol i produces different geometry in addition and multiplication. This lesson connects **complex numbers, points and displacements relative to a centre**, keeping that centre explicit.

## One central idea

The number z=a+bi corresponds to (a,b), with equal axis units and positive Im upward. Adding u+vi changes the point to (a+u,b+v), a translation. Multiplying by i gives −b+ai, mapping (a,b) to (−b,a), a 90° anticlockwise turn about the origin.

Multiplication by −i turns clockwise, multiplication by two scales length from the origin by two, and conjugation reflects in the real axis. Multiplication by zero sends every point to zero. Its image exists but no longer retains the original length and direction. Identify the operation rather than treating every occurrence of i as translation.

### Example 1: Different operations with one origin

The number z=2+i corresponds to (2,1). Adding 1+i gives 3+2i at (3,2), translating by (1,1). Multiplication by i instead gives 2i+i²=−1+2i at (−1,2). This is an origin-based turn, not addition of one constant to every point.

The original modulus is √5, and the image modulus is sqrt((−1)²+2²)=√5. More generally (a,b) becomes (−b,a), preserving squared distance a²+b². For nonzero z, the two origin-based arrows form the stated anticlockwise quarter-turn.

For z=0, multiplication i·0=0 remains defined and the origin is fixed. Zero arrows have no direction, so no 90° or zero-degree angle is assigned between them. A rotation of the whole plane can fix its centre without creating an angle between zero vectors.

### Example 2: Moving the centre from zero to c

A turn about c is not simply iz. First subtract c to obtain relative displacement z−c, multiply it by i and add c back: w=c+i(z−c). A point at z=c remains fixed because its relative displacement is zero.

Take z=2+i and c=2−i. Direct multiplication gives A=−1+2i at (−1,2), with |A|=√5: this is the turn about the origin. For the turn about c, z−c=2i turns to −2. Adding c back gives B=−i at (0,−1).

B is one unit from the origin, differing from the original √5. However B−c=−2, so |B−c|=2=|z−c|: the correct distance from c is preserved. The two image points differ, demonstrating why the centre steps cannot be omitted. The alternative centre c=1+i gives z−c=1 and image 1+2i, also different from iz.

## Points, centres and displacement workbench

Choose an original point, action and centre. Predict a simplified image, its coordinates and distance from the stated reference centre before revealing the graph. Only the about-c action uses the centre selector; other actions retain the origin. Changing an unused centre does not erase valid answers.

Arrows begin at the current reference centre. This differs from freely translating an arrow that represents z from the origin. A wider window changes no operation. Coincident z,c,w keep their identities without being displaced artificially. At the rotation centre the image and zero relative displacement remain visible without an invented direction.

For the family challenge, record A,B, their coordinates, the relevant distances and the formulas used. Correct coordinates paired with the wrong centre still indicate an incomplete connection between algebra and geometry.

## Parent review and transfer

Ask the learner to identify the centre before describing direction or preserved distance. The exit must explain subtracting and restoring c, while the zero-point question distinguishes a defined operation from an undefined angle. The next unit studies lines, circles and conics, continuing to connect equations with coordinates, distances and stated geometric conditions.
