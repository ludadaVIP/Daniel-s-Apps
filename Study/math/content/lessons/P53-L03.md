## 同一个数量，可以按小份数或按整体来数

十一块四分之一，既可以统一计成 **11/4**，也可以先数完整整体，再数余下的小份。这是两种表示，不是把数量改成另一个数。

**带分数表示完整的整数部分，加上不足一个整体的分数部分。** 2 3/4 的意思是 2+3/4，不是 2×3/4。输入时在整数与分数之间留空格，如 `2 3/4`。

### 例 1 · 11/4 写成带分数

1. 每 4 个四分之一合成一个整体。
2. 11=2×4+3：先分出两组完整的四份，再余三份。
3. 两组完整整体是 2，余下三小份是 3/4。
4. 因此 **11/4=2 3/4**。余下小份的单位仍是四分之一。
5. 检查：2×4+3=11，回到原来的十一份。

### 例 2 · 2 2/5 写成假分数

1. 每一个完整整体含 5 个五分之一。
2. 两个整体含 2×5=10 个小份。
3. 再加余下两份，共有 10+2=12 个五分之一。
4. 因此 **2 2/5=12/5**。
5. 反向检查：12=2×5+2，完整整体与余下部分一致。

### 为什么不能只把整数与分子相加？

2 1/3 中的 2 以“整体”为单位，1 以“三分之一”为单位。直接做 2+1，是把不同大小的单位混在一起。先把两个整体换成六个三分之一，再加一个，才得到 7/3。

相反，13/6 写成带分数时，13÷6 的余数 1 是**一个六分之一**。分母不能变成原分子 13；剩余部分应为 1/6，不是 1/13。

若小份数恰好能分成完整组，例如 8/4=2，没有剩余部分，直接写整数更清楚。本课带分数题也接受这种整数结果。若还有余下部分，它必须小于一个整体；`1 7/4` 不是规范的带分数，因为分数部分还能再组成一个整体。

本课没有要求余下分数必须最简，例如 2 6/8 与 2 3/4 同量且分数部分都不足一；最简要求在后面的约分课专门检查。题目明确要求某个分母的假分数时，要保留该小单位；单纯数量相等，还不足以完成那个写法任务。

**小检查：** 九个半米为什么是 4 1/2 米？用一米包含两个半米解释，不只背公式。

## Count one amount by small parts or by complete wholes

Eleven quarters can be counted uniformly as **11/4**, or as complete wholes followed by leftover parts. These are two representations of one amount, not a change in quantity.

**A mixed number adds a whole-number part to a fractional part smaller than a whole.** 2 3/4 means 2+3/4, not 2×3/4. Leave a space when entering it, for example `2 3/4`.

### Example 1 · Convert 11/4 to a mixed number

1. Four quarters make one whole.
2. 11=2×4+3: form two complete groups of four and retain three parts.
3. Two complete groups give two wholes; the remainder gives 3/4.
4. Therefore **11/4=2 3/4**. The remaining unit is still one quarter.
5. Check: 2×4+3=11, recovering eleven original parts.

### Example 2 · Convert 2 2/5 to an improper fraction

1. Each complete whole contains five fifths.
2. Two wholes contain 2×5=10 small parts.
3. Add the two remaining parts: 10+2=12 fifths.
4. Therefore **2 2/5=12/5**.
5. Check backwards: 12=2×5+2, recovering both the whole and fractional parts.

### Why not add the whole number directly to the numerator?

In 2 1/3, two counts wholes and one counts thirds. Directly adding 2+1 mixes unequal units. Convert two wholes into six thirds before adding one more, giving 7/3.

Similarly, the remainder one in 13÷6 means **one sixth**. The denominator does not become the original numerator thirteen; the remainder is 1/6 rather than 1/13.

If the parts make only complete groups, as in 8/4=2, an integer is clearer than an unnecessary fractional remainder. This lesson accepts an integer for such a result. Any remaining fractional part must be below one: `1 7/4` is not a standard mixed number because its fractional part contains another whole.

This lesson does not require the remainder to be simplified: 2 6/8 and 2 3/4 have equal values with proper remainders. Simplest form is checked separately in the later simplification lesson. When a task specifies a denominator for an improper fraction, retain that part unit; equal value alone does not satisfy that representation task.

**Quick check:** why do nine half-metres make 4 1/2 metres? Explain with two half-metres per metre rather than only recite a formula.
