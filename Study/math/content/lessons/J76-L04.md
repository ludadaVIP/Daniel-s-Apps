# 列举概率的样本空间

## 先列清可能发生什么

样本空间是给定随机实验全部可能基本结果组成的集合。先说明做几步、每步能发生什么、是否记录顺序，再按固定结构列举。列表不漏、不重，比凭感觉报“有三种”可靠。一个事件可以包含多条基本结果，不能只列想要的事件便当作整个空间。

条目的书写先后可以改变，但每条结果内部的先后含义不能混淆。两次实验中，第一次 H 第二次 T 与第一次 T 第二次 H，是不同路径；即使事件把它们合称“一正一反”，其概率权重仍需保留。

### 例 1：两次硬币与硬币转盘

两次独立公平硬币，用 H、T 表示两面，空间为 `{HH,HT,TH,TT}`。先按第一步 H 分 HH、HT，再按第一步 T 分 TH、TT，结构保证四条列齐。公平且独立使四条等可能，恰一次 H 有 HT、TH 两条，概率 `2/4=1/2`。

公平 H/T 硬币与公平三等分 1、2、3 转盘独立进行，硬币记前，则空间为 H1、H2、H3、T1、T2、T3，六条等可能。事件 T2 只含一条，概率六分之一。两种首步各配三种次步，是二乘三，不是把两种与三种相加。

### 例 2：骰子的和不是等权基本结果

两枚可区分、独立公平六面骰，先第一枚再第二枚。每个点数对等可能，共三十六个。和二只来自 `(1,1)`，概率 `1/36`；和七来自 `(1,6)、(2,5)、(3,4)、(4,3)、(5,2)、(6,1)`，概率 `6/36=1/6`。

虽然可能和值为二到十二共十一种，它们合并的基本结果数量不同，不等可能，不能把和七写 `1/11`。同样，抛硬币时只有“零次、一次、两次正面”三个数量类别，权重也不是各三分之一；中间类别包含两条路径。

## 计数公式有前提

“符合数除总数”需要所列基本结果等可能。若转盘扇区大小不同，或把多个基本结果合并为颜色名称，就应先核权重；不能因为名称种类少，就一律平均分概率。可以进一步细分成等权单位，也可以直接相加已知结果概率。

工作台先让你主动写完整集合，再展开逐项列表，以勾记标事件成员。两枚骰子的列表用 11 表 `(1,1)`，12 与 21 分开；编号球下一课用 R1-B1 这种代码避免把颜色和对象身份混为一谈。集合输入可换条目顺序，但重复和遗漏都会被指出，不用概率数值代替列举任务。

家长请孩子说清每步假设、列举分组和等可能依据，再检查事件与空间的区别。列齐以后再算概率，既能发现少一条路径，也能发现“列的是合并类别，却当成等权基本结果”的逻辑问题。

# Listing sample spaces

## Specify what can happen before counting

A sample space contains all possible basic outcomes of a stated random experiment. Specify steps, possible results at each step and whether order is recorded. A systematic list prevents omissions and duplicates. An event contains some outcomes; listing only the desired event does not list the full space.

The order of entries in a set may vary, but order inside each outcome code retains its meaning. H then T and T then H are different paths, even when an event groups both as one head and one tail.

### Example 1: two tosses and a coin/spinner experiment

Two independent fair tosses have space `{HH,HT,TH,TT}`. Group by first H to obtain HH/HT and first T to obtain TH/TT. Fair independence gives four equal basics. Exactly one H contains HT and TH, giving probability one half.

An independent fair H/T coin and equal-sector spinner labelled 1,2,3, recorded coin first, give H1,H2,H3,T1,T2,T3. Event T2 has probability one sixth. Each of two first outcomes pairs with three second outcomes, so multiply rather than add their counts.

### Example 2: dice sums are unequal aggregated outcomes

Two distinguishable independent fair dice produce thirty-six equally likely ordered face pairs. Sum two contains only (1,1), giving 1/36. Sum seven contains (1,6),(2,5),(3,4),(4,3),(5,2),(6,1), giving 1/6.

There are eleven possible sum values from two to twelve, but they aggregate different numbers of equal basics. They are not equally likely, so 1/11 is unjustified for sum seven. Similarly, zero, one or two heads in two tosses are three categories with unequal weights; the middle contains two paths.

## The counting formula has a premise

Favourable divided by total requires equally likely listed basics. Unequal spinner sectors or aggregated colour labels need their weights checked. Either refine the space into equal basics or add known probabilities directly; counting category names is insufficient.

The workshop asks for a complete constructed set before revealing its systematic list and event ticks. Dice codes use 11 for (1,1), retaining 12 separately from 21. The next lesson uses token IDs such as R1-B1 to distinguish identity from colour. Entry order may change, but duplicates and missing outcomes fail the listing task.

A parent asks for the assumptions, grouping strategy and equal-likelihood justification before checking event membership. Listing first helps detect both a missing path and the deeper error of treating aggregated categories as equal basics.
