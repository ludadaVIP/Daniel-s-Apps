## 比例尺的两项必须同单位

原地图的数值比例尺 **1:50000** 表示图上长度与实际长度按同一种单位比较：原图一厘米对应实际五万厘米，也就是五百米。后项并不自动是米，更不是千米。先统一单位，才谈换算。

这里使用假想路线，不需要出行。图上厘米标签指给定的**原地图坐标长度**，不是拿尺量手机屏幕。走的路线为 A→B→C 两段；浅色虚线只是端点直线，不是指定道路，不能拿它代替行走距离。

### 例 1 · 两段路线相加再换算

1. 原比例尺 **1:50000**，第一段 **3 cm**、第二段 **2 cm**。
2. 沿路线相加，原图共 **3+2=5 cm**。
3. 实际长度 **5×50000=250000 cm**。
4. 换单位：**250000 cm=2500 m=2.5 km**。
5. 核查原图每厘米五百米，五厘米就是二千五百米。若写成二十五万米，就把厘米当米，扩大了一百倍。

线段比例尺用原图两厘米对应实际一千米，可以帮助读图。路线五厘米是这段标尺的二倍半，同样得到 2.5 km。两种方法都要基于原图单位，不能把屏幕像素当作固定的厘米。

### 例 2 · 反向求原图长度与放大条件

1. 另一张原图比例为 **1:100000**，实际路线 **3 km**。
2. 先统一成厘米：**3 km=300000 cm**。
3. 原图长度 **300000÷100000=3 cm**，用乘回恢复真实长度。
4. 若把这张纸图线性放大两倍印出，同一真实路线在新纸图上为六厘米；新数值比例尺应为 **1:50000**。
5. 对原来的 1:50000 纸图放大两倍，新比例尺则为 **1:25000**。后项减半，而不是加倍，因为一厘米纸图现在代表较少的真实距离。

**显示缩放与纸图尺量：** 工作台放大两倍时，给定的原图坐标三厘米、两厘米仍不变，路径与线段比例尺一起变长，所以真实路线不变。若要量放大纸图上的新厘米，就必须用更新后的数值比例尺。原数值比例尺不能与放大后纸图长度混用。手机截图也不保证物理打印大小，不能直接当原版尺量地图。

**小数图上距离有效：** 原图 1:50000，2.4 cm 对应 120000 cm=1.2 km。不能因为地图刻度按整厘米画，就把 2.4 取成二或三。实际距离换成米还是千米要按题意，`1.2 km` 与 `1200 m` 数量相同，但当前单位输入要与题目指定对象对应，避免数字单位混配。

探索时先改比例尺，观察同样原图长度对应的实际距离变化；再只改显示放大，预测真实路线保持不变。核对按钮会展开原图合计、实际厘米、米与千米。改比例尺、路线或显示倍率都会清空旧反馈，让你重新检查所用条件。

**家长陪学：** 要求孩子画出单位链、说明所走的两段，并解释放大影响哪个量。数值换算正确但把虚线当路线，或把屏幕尺量当原坐标，仍需回看题意。**带走一个发现：** 地图比例是同单位的长度关系；先确定原图坐标与路线，再换算真实距离。

## Both sides of a map scale use the same unit

An original numerical scale of **1:50000** compares map length with ground length in matching units. One original-map centimetre represents fifty thousand ground centimetres, or five hundred metres. The denominator is not automatically metres or kilometres.

This fictional route requires no travel. Centimetre labels are given **original-map coordinates**, not measurements made with a ruler on a phone screen. Follow A→B→C along both legs. The faint endpoint shortcut is not the specified road and cannot replace its travelled distance.

### Example 1 · Add route legs before conversion

1. The original scale is **1:50000**, with legs of **3 cm** and **2 cm**.
2. The original-map route totals **3+2=5 cm**.
3. Ground length is **5×50000=250000 cm**.
4. Convert **250000 cm=2500 m=2.5 km**.
5. Check: each original-map centimetre represents five hundred metres, so five represents 2500 metres. Writing 250000 metres would confuse centimetres with metres and enlarge the result one hundredfold.

The graphic scale bar shows two original-map centimetres corresponding to one kilometre. A five-centimetre route spans two and a half such bars, again giving 2.5 km. Both methods depend on original-map units, not fixed physical centimetres on a screen.

### Example 2 · Recover map length and interpret an enlarged print

1. Another original map has scale **1:100000**, with a **3 km** ground route.
2. Match units first: **3 km=300000 cm**.
3. Original-map length is **300000÷100000=3 cm**. Multiply back to check.
4. Printing this map twice as large linearly makes that same route six paper centimetres long. Its new numerical scale is **1:50000**.
5. Similarly, enlarging a 1:50000 map twice gives **1:25000**. The denominator halves because each new paper centimetre represents less ground distance.

Display zoom and physical print measurement are different conditions. Doubling the workshop display preserves the given three-centimetre and two-centimetre original coordinates, enlarging the route and graphic bar together. Ground distance remains unchanged. Measuring new centimetres on an enlarged print instead needs its updated numerical scale. Do not mix original numerical scale with enlarged paper lengths. A screenshot does not guarantee a particular physical printing size either.

Fractional map distances are valid. At scale 1:50000, 2.4 cm gives 120000 cm=1.2 km. Integer tick labels do not justify rounding that map length to two or three. Respect whether the question asks metres or kilometres, keeping numbers and units together rather than combining a correct number with the wrong unit.

Change the original scale first and compare ground distances for the same map coordinates. Then change only display zoom and predict that the route’s ground distance remains unchanged. Checking reveals the map total, ground centimetres, metres and kilometres. Any input change clears the previous feedback.

**For a parent:** ask for the unit chain, the specified route legs and an explanation of what zoom changes. Correct conversion does not repair choosing the wrong path or treating screen ruler measurements as original coordinates. **Takeaway:** a map scale is a same-unit length relationship; identify original coordinates and the route before converting to ground distance.
