# 标准分数与比较

两个不同参照中的原数值，未必可直接比较相对位置。本课只用**离各自均值几个标准差**描述位置，不把它自动变成百分位、概率或“谁更优秀”。

## 一条核心想法

给定明确参照的均值μ与正标准差σ，z=(x−μ)/σ。分子表示有方向的离均差，分母提供参照波动的单位。z=2是高于参照均值2个标准差；z=−1是低1个；σ>0时z=0才表示x=μ。z无量纲，因为分子与分母的测量单位约去。

一定说明参照是谁，待比较观测是否已经包含在其中。工作台给固定参照，待比较值另列，不把新值加入后悄悄重算均值和标准差。参照若无代表性，标准化也不会自动修复。

### 例1：原数值较低，相对位置较高

A参照为完整总体40、40、60、60，均值50；四个离差均为±10，方差100，标准差10。观测70的zA=(70−50)/10=2。

B参照为75、75、85、85，均值80，标准差5。观测85的zB=(85−80)/5=1。因此A在各自参照的标准差尺度上更高，虽然70小于85。这不矛盾：比较的问题已经从原值变成相对位置。

若B的观测变75，z=(75−80)/5=−1。负号不是负分数或负标准差，而是低于参照均值。若x=80，σ仍5，则z=0。不能因为x不为0就拒绝z=0。

### 例2：换单位与没有可用尺度

对A作正向单位变换x′=10x+100，同时变换整个参照。观测变800，均值600，标准差100，z′=(800−600)/100=2。共同平移抵消，正比例因子约去，因此相对位置不变；只变x而不变μ与σ会造成错误。

若把B参照改为80、80、80、80，则μ=80、σ=0。观测85给5/0，观测80给0/0，两者都无定义，不能给一个伪造的z=0。零波动没有可用的标准差单位。

方向反转x′=−x时，μ′=−μ、σ′=σ，z′=−z。更一般，非零比例k使标准差乘|k|；只有正比例换算保持z的符号。相对高低与“好坏”仍由指标意义决定，较高等待时间可能更差，较高成绩在另一个情境可能更好。

## 标准单位工作台

先对照固定参照，再算A和B的z。切换正向单位换算保留预测；改变B参照只清B的问题，不抹掉A的有效推导。展开核验把两个标准分数放在同一条−3到3标尺上，σ=0时明确显示无定义，不在0处画假点。

没有额外分布假设，z=2不确定百分位。两组具有相同均值和标准差的数据可有不同形状、尾部与累计比例。正态百分位是另有模型条件的结论，不是本公式里藏着的自动转换。也不能把跨科目的z差别直接说成跨能力的排名。

## 家长核对与迁移

出口解释必须同时给zA=2、zB=1，说明比较的是各自参照中的位置，并指出概率和价值判断所缺的条件。追问负号和零标准差，而不只核算减法。下一课将“观测减模型”的带符号差用于残差，但残差并不自动是标准分数。

# Z-scores and comparisons

Raw values from different reference distributions do not directly compare relative positions. This lesson measures **how many reference SDs an observation lies from its mean**, without automatically converting that position into a percentile, probability or judgment of better.

## One central idea

For a stated reference mean μ and positive SD σ, use z=(x−μ)/σ. The numerator is a signed deviation; the denominator supplies a reference unit of spread. A z of two is two reference SDs above the mean, and −1 is one below. When σ>0, z=0 means x=μ. Measurement units cancel, making z unitless.

State who the reference represents and whether the observation is already included. The workbench fixes the reference populations and supplies separate observations; it does not silently add those observations and recompute the summaries. Standardizing an unrepresentative reference does not make it representative.

### Example 1: A lower raw value with a higher relative position

Reference A is the entire finite population 40,40,60,60. Its mean is fifty, every deviation has magnitude ten, variance is one hundred and SD ten. Observation seventy therefore gives zA=(70−50)/10=2.

Reference B is 75,75,85,85, with mean eighty and SD five. Observation eighty-five gives zB=(85−80)/5=1. A is higher relative to its own reference on the SD scale despite seventy being less than eighty-five. The comparisons answer different questions: raw magnitude versus standardized position.

Changing observation B to seventy-five gives z=(75−80)/5=−1. The negative sign indicates below the mean, not negative SD or an inherently bad value. An observation of eighty with SD five gives z=0 although the original value is not zero.

### Example 2: Unit conversion and the absence of a usable scale

Apply the positive transformation x′=10x+100 to A and its entire reference. The observation becomes 800, reference mean 600 and SD 100. Then z′=(800−600)/100=2. The common shift and positive scale cancel. Changing only x while leaving the reference summaries unconverted would be wrong.

Now replace B's reference by 80,80,80,80. Its mean is eighty and SD zero. Observation eighty-five would require 5/0, and eighty would require 0/0. Both are undefined. Zero reference spread supplies no usable SD unit and does not justify an invented z=0.

Reversing direction with x′=−x instead gives μ′=−μ, σ′=σ and z′=−z. More generally, a nonzero scale factor k multiplies SD by |k|; only positive conversion preserves the sign of z. Whether higher means better still depends on the variable: higher waiting time and higher attainment can have opposite implications.

## Standard-unit workbench

Compute A and B using the fixed references before revealing the common z scale. Positive unit conversion preserves predictions. Changing only reference B resets its question without erasing A's valid reasoning. The audit plots scores on a shared −3-to-three scale, while zero spread is explicitly undefined rather than displayed as a false dot at zero.

Without a distribution model, z=2 does not determine a percentile. Distributions with the same mean and SD can have different shapes, tails and cumulative proportions. Normal-model percentiles require separate assumptions; they are not an automatic feature of the z formula. Cross-subject z values likewise do not by themselves define a ranking of different abilities.

## Parent review and transfer

The exit must calculate zA=2 and zB=1, identify the reference-relative comparison, and state what is missing for probability or value claims. Ask about signs and zero spread, not merely the arithmetic. The next lesson uses signed observed-minus-model differences as residuals; a residual is not automatically a z-score.
