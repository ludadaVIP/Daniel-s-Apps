# 终章项目：从问题到模型

把前四课的思路连成一份可检查的项目报告：在目录尺寸、虚构预算和指定天气情景下，找最便宜且每天够用的水箱。目标是构造完整可行集合、比较方案并说明边界，不是硬选一个看起来合理的数字。没有可行方案也可能是最有用的结果。

## 先声明成功标准

目录容量仅0,20,40,…,160L，费用40+2C，单位是虚构筹码。0容量也按这个给定公式计算固定费用，但不能满足正需求。屋顶20m²、收集率3/4、初始0、先收后用、无蒸发与备用水。每个被要求覆盖的情景都必须每天无缺水，且总费用≤预算；在可行者中最小化费用。

名义情景雨量[6,0,0,6,0]mm，每日30L；高用量情景[8,0,0,8,0]mm，每日40L；长干期[6,0,0,0,6]mm，每日30L。它们是事先声明的虚构压力测试，不是未来天气的概率分布。覆盖两个情景不等于覆盖所有可能天气。

### 例1：数学下界与可购方案不同

名义情景连续整数容量至少90L，但90不在目录；第一个可买且供水达标的是100。预算240给40+2C≤240，即C≤100。与供水C≥90、目录每20一档取交集，可行集合只有{100}，费用240。预算等号包含，不能误排除边界。

80L虽只花200，却前三天缺10L，不可行；120L供水够却花280，超预算。说明失败在哪个约束，比只说“错”更有用。费用随C严格增加，因此在完整可行集合中最小容量也最便宜；这个结论依赖当前费用模型，不能自动搬到有折扣的另一目录。

### 例2：压力情景改变可行集合

高用量情景首日收120L，前三天共需120L，因此至少120容量；取120的日末存水为80,40,0,80,40，确实充分。若同时覆盖名义与高用量，预算240只允许C≤100，与C≥120冲突，可行集∅。预算提高到280后只剩{120}，费用正好280；不是改用错误算式来凑答案，而是明确改变约束后重新求交集。

若还要求长干期，首批仅90L进水，前四日却需120L。即使水箱足够大且没有任何溢流，也缺30L；更大空箱不会造水。当前目录与预算怎样增加都不能解决这段输入不足。可以提出备用水、降低需求或另设初始存水作为新方案，但必须声明新增条件并重新计算，不能把它们偷偷塞进原报告。

## 报告要能独立阅读

报告至少六部分：问题与成功标准；数据来源和用途；变量、单位及假设；模型和完整可行集合；最便宜方案、必要与充分理由及账本核验；压力测试、局限和下一步需采集的证据。注明当前数据虚构，收集量观察在入箱前，校准拟合与独立验证不同。

工作台把每种目录容量的费用、各情景缺水、供水条件和预算条件逐行列出。图只画离散点，不连成似乎任意容量都可以买的直线；紫实心表示供水达标，空心不达标，预算线再提供另一条件。先主动写全体可行集合，再打开核验，不只挑出一个喜欢的方案。

家庭挑战可在纸上设计另一个明确雨量序列或需求，先预测哪个约束最可能成为瓶颈，再重新列账本。最终出口由人阅读完整报告，确认结论范围与证据一致。真实家庭试用还要观察学习者是否能解释、修改和核查模型；工程测试通过不能替代这项教学证据。

# Final capstone: from question to model

Connect the previous lessons into an inspectable project: choose the cheapest tank that supplies every day under catalog sizes, a fictional budget and stated scenarios. Construct the complete feasible set, compare options and state limits. A justified finding of no feasible plan can be more useful than forcing a plausible-looking number.

## Declare success criteria first

Catalog capacities are only 0,20,40,…,160 L. Cost is 40+2C in toy credits. Even zero capacity uses the given fixed-cost formula but cannot serve positive demand. Use roof 20 m², capture fraction 3/4, initial zero, collection before use, and no evaporation or backup. Every required scenario must have zero deficit on every day, and cost must not exceed budget. Minimise cost among feasible options.

Nominal rainfall is [6,0,0,6,0] mm with demand 30 L/day. High use has [8,0,0,8,0] mm and demand forty. The longer dry spell has [6,0,0,0,6] mm and demand thirty. These are declared fictional stress tests, not a probability distribution for future weather. Covering two scenarios does not cover every possible weather pattern.

### Example 1: Mathematical threshold versus purchasable size

Nominal supply needs capacity at least ninety, but ninety is absent from the catalog. The first available sufficient size is one hundred. Budget 240 gives 40+2C≤240, or C≤100. Intersect supply C≥90, the budget and twenty-litre catalog steps to obtain feasible set {100}, costing 240. Equality at the budget boundary is allowed.

Eighty costs only 200 but leaves a ten-litre deficit over the first three days. One hundred and twenty supplies enough but costs 280, beyond budget. Identify which constraint fails. Because cost strictly increases with C in this model, the smallest feasible catalog capacity is cheapest. A discounted catalog could require a different comparison; do not transfer the conclusion without checking its assumptions.

### Example 2: Stress tests alter feasibility

High use brings 120 L on day one and needs 120 over the first three days, giving capacity at least 120. At that capacity closing stores are 80,40,0,80,40, establishing sufficiency. Requiring both nominal and high-use supply with budget 240 creates conflicting conditions C≥120 and C≤100, so the feasible set is empty. Raising budget explicitly to 280 leaves {120}, costing exactly 280. Recompute after changing constraints rather than bending arithmetic to produce an answer.

Including the longer dry spell brings only ninety litres before four demands total 120. Even with no overflow and ample capacity, thirty litres are missing. Increasing an initially empty tank cannot create inflow. Backup water, lower demand or initial storage could define a new proposal, but each must be declared and recalculated rather than silently added to the original report.

## Make the report self-contained

Include six parts: question and success criteria; data provenance and roles; variables, units and assumptions; model and complete feasible set; cheapest plan with necessity, sufficiency and ledger checks; stress tests, limitations and further evidence needed. Identify fictional data, before-storage measurements and the distinction between calibration fit and independent validation.

The workbench lists every catalog capacity, cost, scenario deficit, supply condition and budget condition. The plot uses discrete points rather than a line implying all intermediate sizes are purchasable. Filled purple points meet supply, open points fail it, and the budget line supplies a separate constraint. Generate the entire feasible set before revealing the audit instead of choosing one appealing option.

For a paper challenge, declare a new rainfall sequence or demand, predict the likely limiting constraint and rebuild the ledger. A person reviews the final argument and checks that conclusions stay within their evidence. A real family trial should observe whether the learner can explain, revise and verify the model; passing engineering tests cannot substitute for that teaching evidence.
