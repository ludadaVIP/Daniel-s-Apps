# 为什么需要虚数单位i

“方程无解”之前，要先说明允许使用哪些数。实数不足以解z²=−1，不意味着这个式子没有研究价值。本课只用**数域与i²=−1**理解扩展，并检查全部根。

## 一条核心想法

任意实数平方非负：正数乘自己为正，负数乘自己也为正，0的平方为0。因此x²=−1在实数域无解。这由符号规则保证，不是列出几个平方后猜出的规律。

引入一个固定的新数i，规定i²=−1。复数写成a+bi，其中a、b为实数，运算保留加法、乘法和分配律。可以把它落实为实数对(a,b)：乘法定义为(ac−bd,ad+bc)，于是(0,1)²=(−1,0)。新元素有明确运算，不是计算中随意换号的符号。

i是一个选定的数；方程z²=−1的全部根却有i和−i。**一个主平方根与全部方程根是不同问题。** 本课涉及主平方根时明确采用sqrt(−1)=i的约定，不把±i当作i这个数。

### 例1：同一方程，不同数域

求z²=−4。若只允许实数，平方不可能为负，所以解集∅。若允许复数，(2i)²=4i²=−4，(−2i)²=4i²=−4，两个根±2i都满足。

没有遗漏其他根：在复数域z²+4=(z−2i)(z+2i)，零乘积给z=2i或z=−2i。原题求全部根，不能只交2i；也不能把实数±2当答案，因为它们平方为4。

边界z²=0只有一个不同根0。写±0没有得到两个数。对z²=4，两个实根±2在复数域仍然有效：扩大数域不会删除旧有实数，也不会把实数平方改为负。

### 例2：i的四步循环与不能照搬的根式法则

i⁰=1，i¹=i，i²=−1，i³=−i，i⁴=1。随后每四次循环：i⁷=i⁴i³=−i，i¹⁰=(i⁴)²i²=−1。指数0对应非零数的乘法单位1，不是把底数改成0。

再核查sqrt(−1)·sqrt(−1)。采用上述主根约定，左边i·i=−1；若错误地合并根号，会得到sqrt(1)=1。两边不同，说明实数根式法则sqrt(a)sqrt(b)=sqrt(ab)的a,b≥0前提不能随意省掉，不能一股脑延到两个负数的主平方根。

这不是说分配律失效，而是某条根式恒等式被用出了范围。求方程根时，逐个平方代回比只记“负号开根移出来”可靠。

## 数域与循环工作台

先用实数平方表观察，再给出非负的普遍理由。选择方程和数域，预测完整根集，可写{2i,−2i}或±2i，无解写∅；随后另算i的整数幂。展开核验才显示当前数域的解和指数在循环中的位置。

复平面上的空图只表示当前允许的数域没有解，不表示扩展数域也无解。改变指数不清除独立的方程根预测；只改图窗不改变任何数学答案。本单元图中可计算的系数使用有理数，但复数定义中的a、b可以是任意实数。

## 家长核对与迁移

追问“无解在哪个数域？”和“你给的是一个根还是全部根？”出口要明确算出−1与1这个反例，并指出根式法则的条件，不能只说“复数特殊”。下一课把a+bi的两部分分开读，不把虚部系数与含i的一整项混淆。

# Why the imaginary unit i exists

Before calling an equation unsolvable, state the allowed number domain. Failure to solve z²=−1 with real numbers does not make the equation meaningless. This lesson uses **number domains and i²=−1** to understand an extension and check all roots.

## One central idea

Every real square is nonnegative: a positive number times itself is positive, a negative number times itself is positive, and zero squares to zero. Thus x²=−1 has no real solution. This is a general sign argument, not a pattern guessed from a few examples.

Introduce one fixed new number i satisfying i²=−1. Complex numbers have form a+bi for real a,b, retaining distributive arithmetic. A concrete construction uses real pairs (a,b), with product (ac−bd,ad+bc). Then (0,1)²=(−1,0). The new element has specified operations rather than a sign that changes freely during a calculation.

The number i is fixed, while equation z²=−1 has two roots, i and −i. **One principal square root and every equation root are different requests.** Whenever principal roots arise here, the convention sqrt(−1)=i is stated explicitly; ±i is not the single number i.

### Example 1: One equation in two domains

For z²=−4 over the real numbers, the solution set is empty because a real square cannot be negative. Over the complex numbers, (2i)²=4i²=−4 and (−2i)²=4i²=−4. Both opposite roots ±2i work.

There are no further roots: z²+4=(z−2i)(z+2i), so the zero-product property gives z=2i or z=−2i. A request for every root cannot be answered with only 2i. The real candidates ±2 also fail because their squares are positive four.

The boundary equation z²=0 has one distinct root, zero. Writing ±0 does not create two numbers. Equation z²=4 retains its two real roots ±2 in the complex domain. Extending a domain does not remove real numbers or change their existing squares.

### Example 2: A four-step cycle and a root-law boundary

The cycle begins i⁰=1, i¹=i, i²=−1, i³=−i, i⁴=1 and then repeats every four steps. Hence i⁷=i⁴i³=−i and i¹⁰=(i⁴)²i²=−1. Exponent zero produces the multiplicative identity for the nonzero base i; it does not replace the base with zero.

Now check sqrt(−1)·sqrt(−1). With the stated principal-root convention, this is i·i=−1. Incorrectly merging the roots gives sqrt(1)=1. The disagreement shows that the real identity sqrt(a)sqrt(b)=sqrt(ab), valid for nonnegative real a,b, cannot be transferred indiscriminately to principal roots of two negative numbers.

Distribution has not failed. A particular root identity was used outside its conditions. Checking each candidate by squaring it in the original equation is more reliable than an unqualified rule about moving a negative sign outside a root.

## Domain and cycle workbench

Observe the real-square table, then supply the general nonnegativity reason. Choose an equation and domain, predict its complete root set, and separately simplify an integer power of i. Sets such as {2i,−2i} or ±2i are accepted; use ∅ for no solutions. The audit reveals the allowed roots and position within the power cycle.

An empty plane represents no roots in the selected domain, not in every possible extension. Changing the exponent preserves an independent equation-root prediction. Changing only the window changes no answer. The interactive coefficients are rational classroom examples; the definition of a+bi permits arbitrary real components.

## Parent review and transfer

Ask which domain is meant and whether the response gives one root or all roots. The exit must calculate the −1-versus-one counterexample and identify the missing condition on the root identity, rather than saying merely that complex numbers are special. The next lesson reads the two components without confusing a real coefficient with its full imaginary term.
