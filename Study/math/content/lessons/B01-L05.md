## 图里的每一块，都要说清楚代表多少

十进制积木给数位一个看得见的解释。一个小方格表示 **1**；一条有 10 格，表示 **10**；一块正方形有 10 行、每行 10 格，表示 **100**。

比较图形里的数量时，要数**总单位数**，不能把“几块积木”误当成“几个一”。本课的百方格、十条和一小格使用相同大小的小单位格。

### 例 1 · 243 的两种图

1. 标准分组：2 个百、4 个十、3 个一。
2. 它的总值为 2 × 100 + 4 × 10 + 3 = **243**。
3. 把一个百换成十条“十”：变成 1 个百、14 个十、3 个一。
4. 新图总值为 1 × 100 + 14 × 10 + 3 = **243**，没有多拿任何一格。

块的数量增加了，单位总数没有增加。**换组守恒的是数量，不是块数。** 这也是以后理解退位减法的基础。

### 例 2 · 230 与 203

1. 两个数都可以用两个百开始，这部分相同。
2. 230 还剩 3 个十，也就是 30；203 还剩 3 个一，也就是 3。
3. 30 − 3 = **27**，所以 230 比 203 多 27。
4. 不能说“两边都有 5 块，所以一样多”：一块十条与一个单位格并不代表同样的量。

### 图、语言与符号要互相检查

看图 → 说出“几个百、几个十、几个一” → 写出加法式 → 合成数字。若图上画四个百、六个一，数字应该是 **406**，十位保留 0，不是 46。

在模型里选 203、230 或 243，先预测一次交换。点击“一个百换成十个十”，再把十个十换回一个百。每次用另一种算法算总数，检查它没有改变。

**家庭挑战：** 用纸画家里见到的一个三位数，做两种等值分组。请家人不看你的数字、只看图算总数；如果结果不同，先检查图的单位和数量。

## State what every block represents

Base-ten blocks make place value visible. One small square represents **1**. A rod of ten squares represents **10**. A square with ten rows of ten represents **100**.

Compare **total unit value**, not just the number of blocks. In this model, hundred squares, ten rods and single squares all use the same-sized small unit squares.

### Example 1 · Two pictures of 243

1. Standard grouping: two hundreds, four tens and three ones.
2. Total value: 2 × 100 + 4 × 10 + 3 = **243**.
3. Exchange a hundred for ten tens: one hundred, fourteen tens and three ones.
4. New value: 1 × 100 + 14 × 10 + 3 = **243**. No unit square has been added.

There are more separate blocks, but the total units do not increase. **Regrouping preserves value, not block count.** This prepares us for understanding regrouping in subtraction later.

### Example 2 · Compare 230 and 203

1. Both start with two hundreds; these amounts match.
2. The remainder of 230 is three tens, or 30. The remainder of 203 is three ones, or 3.
3. 30 − 3 = **27**, so 230 is greater by 27.
4. “Both have five blocks, so they are equal” is incorrect: a ten rod and a single unit square have different values.

### Check pictures, words and symbols together

Read the picture → describe hundreds, tens and ones → write their sum → combine the digits. Four hundreds and six ones give **406**. Keep zero in tens; 46 would show a different amount.

Select 203, 230 or 243 in the explorer and predict an exchange. Exchange one hundred for ten tens, then exchange ten tens back. Recalculate the total another way each time and check that its value stays the same.

**Family challenge:** draw a three-digit number you find at home in two equivalent groupings. Ask someone to total the picture without seeing your number. If answers differ, check the units and counts first.
