# 分解与展开互相检查

## 关系恒等与任务完成是两次检查

一个候选分解首先要与原整式对所有允许的字母值恒等；其次，要在题目要求的整数范围内分解完整。两件事不能混成一个模糊的“对了”。错误关系需要改系数或符号；等值但未完成则需要继续提因子或分解括号。

先找共同整数单项式因子，再看平方差或完全平方；首项系数一的三项式可用整数积和配对。每出现新括号都回头检查。最后用全部配对展开，逐类比较平方、一次、常数或其他字母部分的系数，不能只看首尾。

### 例 1：等值但没有分解到底

原式 `2x²−18`，候选 `2(x²−9)` 展开确实还原。这说明关系恒等，却没有完成分解，因为 x²−9 仍是平方差。继续得到 `2(x−3)(x+3)`，再展开为 `2(x²+3x−3x−9)`，确认外部二和所有项都保留。

类似 `3x³−12x`，先提三 x，余 x²−4，再到 `3x(x−2)(x+2)`。如果只写 `3x(x²−4)`，应反馈“已等值，继续分解”，而不是说整个关系错误。若写 `3x(x−2)²`，则连展开关系也不符，需纠正平方差与差平方的混淆。

### 例 2：一次巧合与完整系数检查

原式 `x²+5x+6`，错拟 `(x+1)(x+6)`。x=0 时两者都是六，很容易让“常数对了”冒充“整体对了”。但 x=1 时原式十二，候选十四，已经足以否证。

完整展开候选为 `x²+7x+6`，一次项系数七而非五，差异来自两个交叉项合计错误。正确的 `(x+2)(x+3)` 给出中项五。对恒等关系，完整分配并逐类系数核对覆盖所有输入；几次吻合只能增加检查案例，不能代替这种一般推导。

## 回查也要遵守题目要求的方向

若题目要求展开 `(2x−1)(x+3)`，先列 `2x²、6x、−x、−3`，结果 `2x²+5x−3`。保留原乘积虽然等值，却没有完成展开任务。若题目要求分解，反过来保留一个展开和式，也没有完成乘积结构。方向由问题目标决定，不由自己觉得哪个短决定。

本课检查的是表达式，不是方程零点。分解不应返回 x=3、x=−3，也不通过除 x 丢掉 x=0。把正长度图用于虚构花园时另说边长条件，一般代数恒等关系保留其更广取值范围。

工作台可修改候选主张，展开后显示每类系数。输入改变会清除旧反馈和试数记录，避免新式子沿用旧成功。家长请孩子区分三种状态：关系错误、已等值未完成、恒等且完成；最后要求一段不依赖某一次代入的理由。

# Checking factorisation by expansion

## Identity and completion are separate checks

A proposal must first be identical to the source for all allowed letter values. It must then finish the requested factorisation over integers. A wrong relationship needs corrected coefficients or signs; an equivalent unfinished product needs further extraction or splitting.

Start with shared integer monomial content, then examine differences of squares, perfect squares or the monic integer product/sum pattern. Inspect every new bracket. Finally expand all pairs and compare every variable-part coefficient, not just the ends.

### Example 1: equivalent but unfinished

For 2x²−18, the proposal 2(x²−9) expands back correctly. This establishes identity but leaves a difference of squares inside. Continue to 2(x−3)(x+3), then expand with the outside two retained to check every source term.

Likewise, 3x³−12x first gives 3x(x²−4), then 3x(x−2)(x+2). The intermediate product deserves feedback to continue, not a claim that its relationship is false. The proposal 3x(x−2)² is different: it also fails identity by confusing a squared difference with a difference of squares.

### Example 2: numerical coincidence and complete coefficients

The source x²+5x+6 and false proposal (x+1)(x+6) both give six at x=0. A matching constant can disguise an incorrect middle. At x=1 they give twelve and fourteen, disproving the proposal.

Full expansion gives x²+7x+6, with middle coefficient seven rather than five. The correct factors (x+2)(x+3) yield five from both cross products. Distribution and comparison of every coefficient establish the general relationship; several numerical agreements merely check several cases.

## The requested direction matters too

If asked to expand (2x−1)(x+3), list 2x²,6x,−x,−3 and collect to 2x²+5x−3. Retaining the original product is equivalent but not an expansion. Conversely, a sum is not a completed factorisation when a product is requested. Follow the target rather than choosing whichever form looks shorter.

These tasks concern expressions rather than equation roots. Do not return x=3 and x=−3 in place of factors, or cancel x and lose input zero. A positive-length garden drawing needs its own domain; the algebraic identity has a broader one.

The workshop lets you edit a proposed factorisation and compare all expanded coefficients. Editing clears old feedback and numerical observations so a new claim cannot inherit old success. A parent asks the learner to distinguish a false relationship, an equivalent unfinished form and an identical completed product, then explain without relying on one chosen input.
