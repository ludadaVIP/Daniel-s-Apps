# 点到直线的距离

点到直线的距离是所有连接线段中**最短的非负长度**。斜线上与点同横坐标的交点通常不是垂足；看起来方便测量，不等于定义中的距离。

## 垂足让“最短”有理由

设P(x₀,y₀)，直线l为Ax+By+C=0且(A,B)≠(0,0)。法向量n=(A,B)垂直l。令s=Ax₀+By₀+C，沿法向扣去λ=s/(A²+B²)倍n，得到H=(x₀−λA,y₀−λB)。代回直线得s−λ(A²+B²)=0，因此H确在线上；PH沿法向，所以垂直。

对任意线上点Q，直角三角形给PQ²=PH²+HQ²≥PH²。这才说明PH最短；P在线上时P=H，距离为0，仍符合定义。

因此d=|s|/sqrt(A²+B²)。绝对值去掉位于直线哪一侧的符号；分母是法向量长度，负责归一化。不能把未经归一化的代入值当距离。把A、B、C同乘k≠0，分子与分母都乘|k|，距离不变。

### 例1：找垂足，再核距离

P(1,3)，l:3x+4y−5=0。s=3+12−5=10，A²+B²=25，λ=2/5。H=(1,3)−(2/5)(3,4)=(−1/5,7/5)。代回：−3/5+28/5−5=0。

PH的平方=(6/5)²+(8/5)²=4，距离2。公式也给10/5=2。若改写l为−6x−8y+10=0，分子绝对值20、分母10，仍得2。距离不会因为把方程整体放大就变长。

### 例2：竖直差与真正垂线

P(0,0)到y=x+2的竖直交点为(0,2)，该线段长2。垂足却是H(−1,1)：它满足1=−1+2，PH方向(−1,1)与直线方向(1,1)点积0。PH=√2<2。

写成x−y+2=0，公式给2/√2=√2，与图形一致。边界也要检查：P(3,−1)就在3x+4y−5=0上，距离0；(−1,4)到x=2的垂足为(2,4)，距离3，不受纵坐标4影响。

## 工作台中的独立任务

选择直线与点，分别预测垂足坐标和精确距离。核验后显示PH与精确表；H与P重合时合并名字，不画假长度。切换图窗只改变观察范围。改成同一直线的非零倍数时，未改变的距离预测保留；换点或直线后垂足须重新确认。

练习包括点在线上、竖直线和负倍数方程。出口不能只背公式：须说明H在线上、PH垂直、为何最短、绝对值和非零分母为何必要。家长可让孩子先用勾股解释PQ²≥PH²，再选择算式。这个垂直距离马上用于抛物线的准线条件。

# Distance from a point to a line

Point-to-line distance is the **shortest nonnegative segment length**. The intersection directly above a point on a slanted line is usually not its perpendicular foot. An easy measurement is not automatically the defined distance.

## Why the perpendicular foot is shortest

Let P(x₀,y₀) and l:Ax+By+C=0, with (A,B)≠(0,0). Normal vector n=(A,B) is perpendicular to l. Put s=Ax₀+By₀+C and λ=s/(A²+B²). Then H=(x₀−λA,y₀−λB). Substitution gives s−λ(A²+B²)=0, so H is on the line. Segment PH follows the normal and is perpendicular.

For any point Q on l, Pythagoras gives PQ²=PH²+HQ²≥PH². Hence PH is shortest. If P lies on l, H=P and distance zero is valid.

It follows that d=|s|/sqrt(A²+B²). Absolute value removes the side sign; the denominator normalises by the normal vector’s length. A raw substitution value is not distance. Scaling A,B,C by k≠0 multiplies numerator and denominator by |k|, leaving distance unchanged.

### Example 1: Locate the foot and verify distance

For P(1,3) and 3x+4y−5=0, s=10, A²+B²=25 and λ=2/5. Thus H=(1,3)−(2/5)(3,4)=(−1/5,7/5). Substitution verifies −3/5+28/5−5=0.

Squared length PH is (6/5)²+(8/5)²=4, giving distance two. The formula also gives 10/5=2. Rewriting the line as −6x−8y+10=0 gives numerator magnitude 20 and denominator ten, again two. Scaling an equation does not stretch a geometric distance.

### Example 2: A vertical gap is not the perpendicular

From P(0,0) to y=x+2, the vertical intersection (0,2) is two units away. The foot is H(−1,1): it lies on the line, and direction (−1,1) has zero dot product with line direction (1,1). Its distance √2 is shorter than two.

General form x−y+2=0 gives 2/√2=√2, agreeing with the geometry. Check boundaries too: P(3,−1) lies on 3x+4y−5=0 and has distance zero. Point (−1,4) has foot (2,4) on x=2 and distance three, independent of its y-coordinate four.

## Independent workbench predictions

Choose a line and point, then predict foot coordinates and exact distance independently. The audit reveals PH and an exact table. Coincident P and H share names; no nonzero segment is invented. Window changes preserve answers. Scaling to an equivalent line preserves an unchanged distance prediction, while a changed point or line requires rechecking the foot.

Practice includes incidence, vertical lines and negative equation scaling. The exit must explain incidence, perpendicularity, shortest length, absolute value and nonzero denominator. Ask for a Pythagorean argument before a formula. This perpendicular distance will define the directrix condition of a parabola.
