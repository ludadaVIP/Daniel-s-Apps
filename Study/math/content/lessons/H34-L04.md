# 假设检验的基本逻辑

检验先规定一个零假设H0及数据生成条件，再问：若按这个模型重复实验，像当前观察这样或更极端的结果有多常见？p值是这个条件下的尾概率，不是零假设为真的概率，也不是“数据由随机性产生”的概率。

## 在看数据前写好计划

本课H0为每次独立公平抛币、成功率1/2，次数n固定。备择方向可以预设为上侧、下侧，或按|X−n/2|衡量双侧极端程度。还要预设阈值α与停止规则。只有说明了“极端”怎么定义，p值才有明确含义。

本课采用规则p≤α时拒绝H0，否则不拒绝。这个规则不是判断真假的万能开关；结论依赖假设、设计、测量与分析是否成立。不拒绝尤其不能自动改写成“接受并证明H0”。

### 例1：上侧尾必须包含观察结果

固定抛4次，备择为成功率>1/2，观察4次全正。H0下X≥4只有一项，p=1/16。若预设α=1/10，则按规则拒绝；这没有把公平模型变成逻辑不可能，它仍可能产生四次全正。

若观察X=3，至少同样极端是3或4，不是只算更严格的4。p=4/16+1/16=5/16，在α=1/10下不拒绝。把观察值排除在尾部之外会错误缩小p。

若事先规则写p≤α，且p与α恰同为1/16，则等号也导致拒绝。阈值比较必须遵守已声明的约定，不能看到边界结果再改规则。

### 例2：双侧与错误率

若事先选择双侧统计量|X−2|，观察4时同样极端的还有0。p=P(|X−2|≥2)=1/16+1/16=1/8。在α=1/10下这次不拒绝。它与上侧结果不同，因为问题与分析计划不同，不能看完数据后挑较小p来汇报。

对n4上侧、α0.1，拒绝域只含X4。若H0真，实际I类错误率为1/16≤0.1，并不恰为0.1。离散模型只有若干可用概率质量，名义α常是控制上限，而不是保证每100次正好错10次。

I类错误是H0真却拒绝；II类错误是在某个真实备择下不拒绝。大p可能反映样本小、波动大或效应小，不证明效应精确为零。若要主张“足够接近”，还需预先定义可忽略范围并选相应方法，普通不显著不等于等效证明。

## 让模型条件进入报告

工作台以橙柱标至少同样极端的结果，黑点为观察值，红边标给定α下的拒绝域。核验表列精确p及H0真时的实际I类率，帮助检查方向、等号与离散边界。

“看到显著才停止”和“看完数据再换尾”模式不沿用原固定方案的错误率解释，而先指出程序需要重新设计和校准。研究中还应完整报告多次分析，而不是只展示最小的一项p。

p值的条件解释核对[ASA说明](https://www.amstat.org/asa/files/pdfs/p-valuestatement.pdf)。出口报告需说明H0、备择、n、α、极端事件与结论限度；不能把一个尾概率当作关于假设真假的后验概率。

# Logic of hypothesis testing

Specify a null model H0, then ask how often repeated data from that model would be at least as extreme as the observation. The p-value is that conditional tail probability. It is not the probability that H0 is true or that the data were produced by randomness alone.

## Plan before inspecting outcomes

Here H0 specifies independent fair tosses with fixed n. Alternatives may be upper, lower or two-sided using |X−n/2|. Declare α, the extremeness statistic and stopping rule beforehand.

This lesson rejects when p≤α and otherwise does not reject. The rule is conditional on the model and procedure; it does not establish truth automatically. Non-rejection must not be rewritten as proof or unconditional acceptance of H0.

### Example 1: Include the observed count in the tail

Fix four tosses and an upper-tail alternative. Observing four heads gives p=P_H0(X≥4)=1/16. At prespecified α=1/10, reject. Fairness has not become logically impossible; that model can produce four heads.

For observed X=3, include counts three and four: p=4/16+1/16=5/16. Do not count only strictly more extreme four. Omitting the observed mass makes the p-value too small.

Under the declared p≤α convention, equality at 1/16 also leads to rejection. Follow the stated boundary convention rather than changing it after seeing a result.

### Example 2: Sidedness and error control

If |X−2| was the planned two-sided statistic, observing four includes both zero and four as equally extreme. The p-value is 1/8, so α=1/10 gives non-rejection. This differs from the upper-tail test because the planned question differs. Selecting whichever tail gives a smaller p after observation is not the original analysis.

For the four-toss upper-tail test at α=0.1, only count four belongs to the rejection set. Under true H0 its actual type-I rate is 1/16, below the nominal level. Discrete outcomes need not attain α exactly, nor does error control promise a quota in each hundred experiments.

Type-I error rejects a true null; type-II error fails to reject under a specified true alternative. A large p can arise from limited information and does not prove an exactly zero effect. Practical equivalence requires a prespecified negligible range and suitable procedure, rather than ordinary non-significance.

## Report assumptions with the number

The workbench highlights extreme counts, the observed count and the rejection set. Its audit reports the exact p and actual null rejection rate. Examine direction, inclusion boundaries and discreteness.

Peeking until significant or choosing a tail after viewing outcomes changes the procedure. The interface therefore asks for redesign and calibration instead of reusing the original error interpretation. Report multiple analyses transparently rather than only their smallest p.

The conditional interpretation was checked against the [ASA statement](https://www.amstat.org/asa/files/pdfs/p-valuestatement.pdf). A full report names H0, alternative, n, α, extremeness rule and limits. A tail probability is not a posterior probability of a hypothesis.
