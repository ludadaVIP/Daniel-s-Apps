## “落下一位”其实是在换单位

长除法把总量按百、十、一等单位依次分配。每一步先找能分出的完整数量，乘回检查已分走量，再相减保留余量。剩余大单位要换成十倍的小单位，才能和下一位合并。

对非负整数总量与正整数除数，最后必须满足 **总量=除数×整数商+余数**，且余数不小于零、小于除数。如果余数还能组成一整组，就说明还没有分到最多的完整组数。

### 例 1 · 936÷12

1. 九个百不够给十二组各一个百。百位商零，余九个百；这个开头零可以不写入最终商。
2. 九个百换九十个十，加原来的三个十，得到九十三个十。
3. **93=12×7+9**：每组先分七个十，余九个十。
4. 九个十换九十个一，加原来的六个一，得到九十六个一。
5. **96=12×8+0**：每组再分八个一，无余量。
6. 每组七个十与八个一，共 **78**。检查 12×78=936。

### 例 2 · 1005÷5，中间零不能删

1. 一个千不足以给五组各一个千，换成十个百；百位的原数字为零，所以当前是十个百。
2. 十个百分给五组，每组两个百，余零个百。
3. 到十位，当前零个十，每组得到零个十。商必须保留这个十位的零。
4. 到个位，五个一分给五组，每组一个一。
5. 商为 **201**，检查 5×201=1005。若删成 21，乘回只有 105，数位和数量都错了。

### 有余数时，完整组与精确除法值不同

235 张卡，每包六张。二十三个十可给每组三个十，余五个十；五个十换五十个一，再加五个一，得到五十五个一。55=6×9+1，所以整数商 39、余数 1。

写 **235=6×39+1**：三十九个满包与一张余卡恢复全部卡片。不能写普通数值等式“235÷6=39”；精确商是 39+1/6。若问所有卡需要几个包，还得再考虑一个未满包，是另一项问题。

零总量除以六得到零，除数仍非零。除以零没有定义，不能靠长除法步骤造一个答案。工作台只允许正除数，余量始终保留；每个展开步骤都写出当前单位，不把“落下来”当作数字拼接魔法。

**小检查：** 574÷7 中，57 个十给每组八个十后余一个十，为什么下一行是十四个一？算完后用乘法恢复 574。

## Bringing down a digit exchanges units

Long division allocates a total place by place, through hundreds, tens and ones. Find the largest whole amount possible, multiply back to check the assigned quantity, then subtract and retain the remainder. Exchange a leftover larger unit for ten smaller units before combining the next digit.

For a non-negative integer total and positive integer divisor, the final check is **total=divisor×whole quotient+remainder**, where remainder is non-negative and smaller than divisor. If it still forms a complete group, the whole quotient is not yet as large as possible.

### Example 1 · 936÷12

1. Nine hundreds cannot give twelve groups a hundred each. The hundreds quotient is zero and nine hundreds remain; this leading zero may be omitted from the final quotient.
2. Exchange nine hundreds for ninety tens and add the original three tens, making ninety-three tens.
3. **93=12×7+9**: give each group seven tens and retain nine tens.
4. Exchange nine tens for ninety ones and add the six ones, making ninety-six ones.
5. **96=12×8+0**: give each group eight ones with no remainder.
6. Seven tens and eight ones total **78** per group. Check 12×78=936.

### Example 2 · Keep the internal zero in 1005÷5

1. One thousand cannot give five groups a thousand each. Exchange to ten hundreds; the original hundreds digit is zero, so ten hundreds are available.
2. Give each group two hundreds, leaving zero hundreds.
3. At tens, zero tens are available and each group receives zero tens. Preserve this quotient placeholder.
4. At ones, five ones shared among five groups give one each.
5. The quotient is **201**, checked by 5×201=1005. Deleting the zero gives 21, whose product with five is only 105.

### Remainders separate whole groups from exact numerical division

Pack 235 cards six per pack. Twenty-three tens give three tens per group and five tens left. Exchange these into fifty ones and add five, making fifty-five. Since 55=6×9+1, the whole quotient is 39 with remainder 1.

Write **235=6×39+1**: thirty-nine full packs and one leftover card recover every card. Do not write the ordinary numerical equality “235÷6=39”; the exact value is 39+1/6. Counting every pack required for all cards includes a partly filled pack and asks a different question.

A zero total divided by six gives zero, with a non-zero divisor. Division by zero is undefined; a written procedure cannot invent an answer. The workshop permits only positive divisors and retains remainders. Each revealed step names the current unit rather than treating bringing down as magical digit concatenation.

**Quick check:** in 574÷7, dividing 57 tens gives eight tens per group and one ten left. Why is the next available quantity fourteen ones? Finish and check by multiplying back to 574.
