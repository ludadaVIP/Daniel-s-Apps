# 复数加减乘

复数运算继续使用熟悉的分配律，新增的关键是i²=−1。本课把**完整展开、替换i²、合并两部分**连成一个可核查过程。

## 一条核心想法

加减分别处理实系数与虚部系数：(a+bi)±(c+di)=(a±c)+(b±d)i。减法的负号作用于整个第二括号，不能只改实部。

乘法不是把两个坐标逐项相乘。分配四项得ac+adi+bci+bd i²，再用i²=−1，得到(ac−bd)+(ad+bc)i。实部ac−bd包含两虚数项相乘的贡献，虚部ad+bc包含两个交叉项。

本课要求完成a+bi形式。等值的−i+8、8−i以及有理系数写法可以表示同一结果；仅复制原乘积或保留未合并项，还没完成“化简”任务。错误反馈会区分数值不符与数量等值但写法未完成。

### 例1：两个虚数项相乘，实部多了多少

(2+i)(3−2i)分成四项：2×3=6，2×(−2i)=−4i，i×3=3i，i×(−2i)=−2i²=2。因此总结果6−4i+3i+2=8−i。

若最后一项误作−2，会得到4−i；若直接丢掉它，得到6−i。两种错因都能回到四项表定位。把两系数各乘一次得到6−2i，也不是复数乘法，因为它漏掉交叉项和i²的作用。

对一般(a+bi)(c+di)，同样记录ac、adi、bci、bd i²，先不急着收集。替换后实部ac−bd，虚部系数ad+bc。公式来自分配，而不是与二维坐标乘法外观相似就猜出的规则。

### 例2：减括号与完全平方的交叉项

(3−2i)−(1+3i)=3−2i−1−3i=2−5i。若写2+i，就是只减了1却把第二个3i当作加法。对照加法，(3−2i)+(−1+4i)=2+2i，每次保留自己的符号。

(1+i)²=(1+i)(1+i)=1+i+i+i²=2i。两个交叉项不能省；1+i²=0只处理了两项，不是原乘积。也可按公式核对：实部1−1=0，虚部1+1=2。

当某个复数为0，乘积仍是0；不需要给零箭头捏造方向。复数集合中两个非零数的积不会为0，但本课不靠几张图证明这一性质，实际运算仍用完整代数式核查。

## 四项与箭头工作台

选两个复数和加、减或乘，先提交化简结果。乘法另预测两虚数项乘积的实贡献−bd，再展开四项核验。带符号的表项表示数，不把负值画成负面积。

复平面加法把第二箭头接在第一箭头末端；减法u−v由v端指向u端，可平移回原点看相同分量；乘积则按复数乘法计算，不照搬加法箭头。改变运算或操作数清除相关预测，图窗变化保留它们。正式课程作答与这个可反复试验的工作台分别记录。

## 家长核对与迁移

请孩子指出i²带来的实贡献和两个交叉项。出口需写出一般乘法的四项推导，不能只背最终公式。下一课选一对特殊因子a+bi与a−bi，观察交叉项抵消后出现的非负实数a²+b²。

# Complex addition, subtraction and multiplication

Complex arithmetic retains distribution, with the new relation i²=−1. This lesson links **complete expansion, substitution for i² and collection of components** into a checkable calculation.

## One central idea

Addition and subtraction combine the real and imaginary coefficients: (a+bi)±(c+di)=(a±c)+(b±d)i. Subtraction applies to the entire second bracket, not merely its real part.

Multiplication is not componentwise coordinate multiplication. Distribution gives ac+adi+bci+bd i², hence (ac−bd)+(ad+bc)i. The real part includes the product of the imaginary terms, while the imaginary coefficient contains both cross terms.

These tasks require a finished a+bi expression. Reordered terms such as −i+8 and 8−i, and equivalent rational coefficients, can describe the same finished result. Repeating the original product or leaving repeated terms does not finish simplification. Formal feedback distinguishes an incorrect value from an equivalent but unfinished form.

### Example 1: Imaginary terms contribute a real value

Expand (2+i)(3−2i) into four contributions: 2×3=6, 2×(−2i)=−4i, i×3=3i, and i×(−2i)=−2i²=2. Collecting gives 6−4i+3i+2=8−i.

Treating the final contribution as −2 produces 4−i; omitting it produces 6−i. The four-term table identifies both errors. Simply multiplying corresponding coefficients to obtain 6−2i also fails, omitting cross terms and the role of i².

For general (a+bi)(c+di), first retain ac,adi,bci,bd i². After substitution the real part is ac−bd and imaginary coefficient ad+bc. The rule is derived from distribution, not guessed from the appearance of two-dimensional coordinates.

### Example 2: Subtracting a bracket and retaining cross terms

(3−2i)−(1+3i)=3−2i−1−3i=2−5i. The incorrect 2+i subtracts one but adds the second imaginary term. For comparison, (3−2i)+(−1+4i)=2+2i, with each original sign retained.

Squaring 1+i means multiplying two complete copies: (1+i)²=1+i+i+i²=2i. The shortcut 1+i²=0 omits both cross terms. The component formula confirms real part 1−1=0 and imaginary coefficient 1+1=2.

Multiplication by zero still produces zero without giving a zero arrow an invented direction. A product of two nonzero complex numbers cannot be zero, but this property is not proved by inspecting a few diagrams. Check each authored calculation through its complete algebraic structure.

## Four-term and arrow workbench

Choose two numbers and an operation, then submit the simplified result. For multiplication, separately predict the real contribution −bd before revealing the four-term audit. Signed entries are numbers, not negative physical areas.

Addition places the second arrow at the first head. The difference u−v points from v to u and can be translated to the origin with the same displacement. Multiplication follows its own complex rule rather than copying the addition construction. Changing an operand or operation resets the affected prediction; changing the window preserves it. Repeatable workbench predictions are separate from formal lesson records.

## Parent review and transfer

Ask for the real contribution from i² and both cross terms. The exit requires the four-term general derivation instead of a memorized formula alone. The next lesson pairs a+bi with a−bi so that cross terms cancel, revealing the nonnegative real value a²+b².
