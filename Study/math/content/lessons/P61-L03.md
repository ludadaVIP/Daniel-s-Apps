## 分数相同，实际量也可能不同

“取三分之二”还没告诉我们取多少升。先问整体是多少：一升的三分之二与十二升的三分之二不是同样多的水。分数描述与整体的关系，实际量要由分数和指定总量共同确定。先标出总量与单位，再选择运算。

求一个量的 a/b，可以先把总量平均分成 b 份，再取 a 份。也可以写总量乘 a/b，两种方法讲的是同一个关系。分数本身无升单位，乘十二升后结果才是实际升数。不要把分子当升数、分母当人数，或把它们直接加到总量上。

### 例 1 · 十二升的三分之二

1. 整体明确为 **12 L**，要取 **2/3**。
2. 先按分母平均分三份，**12÷3=4 L** 每份。
3. 选两份，**4×2=8 L**。
4. 等价式为 **12×2/3=8 L**。取剩下的三分之一为四升，八加四恢复十二。
5. 工作台这里整条代表十二升，不是像上一课重复条那样每个完整框代表一升。先读图例，不能只凭外形决定整体。

若换成 6 L，平均三份每份二升，取两份只有四升。两个分数依旧同为 2/3，没有谁更大；改变的是它们所依附的实际整体。这也是分数条比较大小时必须先确认同样整体的原因。

### 例 2 · 总量分不成整数升也可继续

1. 整体为 **5 L**，取 **2/3**，先把五升平均分三份。
2. 每份 **5/3 L**，并不是整升，但水允许按分数升测量。
3. 两份为 **5/3×2=10/3 L**，即三又三分之一升。
4. 不能截成三升；剩余一份 5/3 L，两者合计 **15/3=5 L**。
5. 10/3 小于五，符合取一个正真分数份额；3.33 只是近似，若题目要精确量则不等于 10/3。

实际单位可以改变：18 张卡的 2/3 为 **18÷3×2=12 张**；NZ$20 的 3/4 为 **20÷4×3=NZ$15**；15 m 的 4/5 为 **12 m**。都先按分母等分、再选分子份，但不能把十二张、十五元、十二米当成同一类量。

离散物品还要考虑是否可分。如果算出某人分得半张不能剪的卡，需要说明条件或重新安排；这与连续水量的分数升不同。本课卡数题选择能整分的数，水量题允许非整数；遇到新情境先看对象，不机械取整。

工作台允许选择总升数、分母与所取份数，整条的图例会随总量变化。先预测每份实际量再核对；只改变分子会改变所选份数，只改变总量会改变每份升数。两种变化都值得用自己的话解释。

**带走一个发现：** 找整体、等分、取份、带单位、核回总量。家长请孩子对照十二升与六升，解释为什么同样 2/3 得不同升数。出口题改为克，仍要说出“每份量”而不是只报最终数字。

## The same fraction can select different amounts

“Take two thirds” does not yet specify litres. Identify the whole: two thirds of one litre differs from two thirds of twelve litres. A fraction describes a relationship to a whole; the actual amount depends on both. Label total and unit before choosing an operation.

To find a/b of an amount, divide it into b equal shares and take a. Multiplying the amount by a/b expresses the same relationship. The fraction itself has no litre unit; applying it to twelve litres produces litres. Numerator and denominator are not quantities to add to the total.

### Example 1 · Two thirds of twelve litres

1. The whole is **12 L**, with **2/3** selected.
2. Divide into three equal shares: **12÷3=4 L** each.
3. Take two shares: **4×2=8 L**.
4. Equivalently **12×2/3=8 L**. The remaining third is four litres, and eight plus four restores twelve.
5. Here the entire workshop bar means twelve litres. Unlike repeated one-litre frames, it does not automatically mean one litre. Read the reference label.

For a 6 L whole, thirds contain two litres each and two thirds select four litres. Both fractions still equal 2/3; the reference amounts differ. This also explains why fraction comparisons need matching wholes.

### Example 2 · Non-integer litre shares remain valid

1. Take **2/3 of 5 L** by splitting five litres equally into three shares.
2. Each share is **5/3 L**, a valid fractional water measurement.
3. Two shares give **5/3×2=10/3 L**, or three and a third litres.
4. Do not truncate to three. Adding the remaining 5/3 L gives **15/3=5 L**.
5. The selected amount is below five, consistent with a positive proper fraction. Decimal 3.33 is approximate, not exactly 10/3.

The actual unit can vary: 2/3 of 18 cards is **12 cards**; 3/4 of NZ$20 is **NZ$15**; 4/5 of 15 m is **12 m**. All use equal sharing then selection, but cards, dollars and metres remain different quantities.

For indivisible objects, consider whether splitting is permitted. Half of a card that must stay intact needs an explanation or a changed arrangement, unlike fractional litres of water. Card problems here use whole-card outcomes; water problems allow fractions. Read the context before rounding.

The workshop changes total litres, denominator and selected numerator. Its complete-bar label follows the total. Predict each share before checking. Changing the numerator changes selected share count; changing the whole changes litres per share. Explain both actions.

**Takeaway:** identify the whole, share equally, select shares, retain units and check against the total. A parent can compare twelve and six litres to test the same-fraction idea. The exit ticket changes to grams while retaining the same reasoning.
