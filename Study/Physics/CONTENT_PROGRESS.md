## This update

Four new bilingual phase-change courses and four SVG stations complete the introductory melting/boiling coverage in Unit 2.3 and add condensation and heating-curve interpretation. Ice bags, boiling pots, sealed cold drinks and thawing provide the starting questions. The app now has 59 courses, 58 stations and four field projects. All four courses retain prediction, required evidence, three practice questions, a transfer exit and a home activity.

The fusion ledger records both energy directions at 0°C with matched 16.70 kJ and 50/100 g samples; the fraction strip shows mass, not volume. Boiling tracks remaining liquid and escaped vapour together, with pressure conditions and invisible vapour distinguished from mist. Condensation compares surfaces against prescribed air dew points without predicting drop count, humidity or time. Heating curves share fixed axes, draw only elapsed piecewise vertices, and connect state tabs, temperature, transferred energy and an explicit three-part energy ledger. Complete playback is required; seeking alone does not satisfy exploration. All numerical phase models specify pure water, equilibrium and approximations rather than claiming universal 0/100°C transitions.

The preceding thermal batch added five complete bilingual courses and five interactive stations for Stage 2 Unit 2.3: temperature versus internal energy, absorbed energy and water quantity, conduction/convection/radiation, hot/cold insulation and evaporation below boiling. Three complete comparisons per station retain evidence; all courses include three practice questions, a transfer exit and a home observation. Distinct SVG card art and compact responsive controls reuse the existing stack without new libraries. That batch brought the app to 55 courses, 54 stations and four saved field projects.

Temperature comparisons use the same ideal gas and Kelvin ratios, with a repeated velocity distribution when particle quantity doubles. Water uses Q=mcΔT and separates absorbed energy from electric input. Transfer-path diagrams are qualitative. Cup curves use a shared clock/scale, prescribed conductances and constant room temperature; insulation slows warming as well as cooling. Evaporation tracks water loss, latent transfer, signed internal-energy change and room input under stated fixed parameters. Animation speeds and physical limits are visible. Melting/boiling and phase-change graphs are added in the current batch; the learner-entered insulation investigation remains planned.

The previous addition is the Stair Power field investigation from Unit 2.2. It connects the prescribed stair-power lesson to the learner’s own mass, vertical height and time. Two or more vertical riser readings estimate the rise of an approximately equal-riser flight; three or more checked ascent times support the same-task summary. Raw/used means, ranges, reasons, reconfirmation and independent-ascent bars make the evidence inspectable. Drafts save locally, and bilingual reports retain every entered original. There is no race, ranking or automatic health/fitness interpretation. This brings the app to four field projects while retaining 50 lessons and 49 stations.

The gravity batch adds three complete bilingual courses and three SVG stations representing Unit 1.4’s six introductory topics. Mass/weight pairs are treated as different quantities; kg is not universally equated with N. An Earth-calibrated spring-scale example exposes how an inappropriate force conversion can report a wrong mass on the Moon. Matching-mass location comparisons and matching-location different-mass drops control the relevant variables. Free fall distinguishes acceleration from constant speed and current motion from speed just before first contact. Same-height and same-release conditions remain explicit; orbital weightlessness and impact physics remain later work. Each lesson has three practice questions, a transfer exit and a home activity. Force/gravity options, readouts and comparison gates now share a small module, while existing content IDs and storage stay stable.

The density batch adds four complete bilingual courses and four SVG stations representing Unit 1.5’s six introductory topics. Material comparisons retain volume alongside mass; resizing a uniform solid does not change its density. Rule-based geometry, cylinder displacement and static floating balance reuse previously checked models. Both quantities are converted before an SI density calculation. Partial immersion and attached air remain visible in the record rather than silently replaced with expected values. Equal density permits fully immersed neutral suspension; the shown depth is not unique. Floating animations are one-second illustrative transitions, not fluid-motion predictions. Each course includes three practice questions, a transfer exit and a home activity. Mystery Materials now connects those concepts to the child’s own paired readings. Mobile diagrams remove spare margins and enlarge labels, while bilingual numeric options avoid duplicate numbers.

The Materials project adds four-step specimen investigations with geometry or displacement, up to four case files and six trials per specimen. Paired used readings derive mean m / mean V; unresolved entries and exclusion reasons block a complete-record badge. Children compare four prescribed sample densities, then record their own observations, material judgment and uncertainties. No threshold automatically identifies a material. Drafts retain inactive-method fields, checks and original readings; the shared report UI also serves Walking Investigation. Phone entry hints fold away while quantities remain labelled. The learning path now orders Stage 1 units as measurement → motion → forces → gravity → density, matching the curriculum.

The energy batch adds six complete bilingual courses representing Unit 2.1’s ten introductory topics. Battery chemistry, internal-energy change and transfers share the torch investigation; motion, height and linear springs each get a fair comparison. Fixed-scale energy bars make conserved totals visible. Ideal and rough-track ledgers distinguish mechanical energy from the full system ledger, and useful output is tied to a stated task. Probe scans inspect positions rather than predicting motion time. Existing course IDs, local records and shared Chemistry infrastructure are retained.
