AT&T and AST SpaceMobile: Engineering the Connected World, One Handoff at a Time

New milestone brings seamless connectivity between ground-based and space-based cellular broadband networks, becoming one step closer to commercial service.

Key Takeaways

  • AT&T and AST SpaceMobile have achieved the industry’s first successful handoff between a ground-based cellular network and a direct-to-device satellite network.
  • Supporting ground-based radio, network integration and mobility technologies from Ericsson and Nokia enable the handoff and helps maintain service continuity.
  • The achievement advances plans to complement AT&T’s existing wireless network with satellite connectivity in remote and hard-to-reach locations using everyday smartphones.
  • Milestone brings AT&T and AST SpaceMobile one step closer to commercial service keeping customers connected anywhere they work, live, and travel.

AT&T, AST SpaceMobile, with supporting ground-based technology from Ericsson and Nokia, have achieved the industry’s first successful handoff between a ground-based cellular network and a direct-to-device satellite network.

Your phone should just stay connected.

Moving between ground and satellite coverage with other providers can mean a dropped call or interrupted connection. AT&T and AST SpaceMobile are engineering a seamless experience where the network does the work behind the scenes, helping you stay connected in more places.

The milestone occurred Wednesday, October 21, 2026, outside Granite Falls, Washington, at 8:15 a.m. PT. It builds on previous voice calls, text messages and video calls through AST SpaceMobile’s BlueBird satellites using everyday smartphones.

This time, the companies tackled a different challenge: moving a connection between AT&T’s ground-based wireless network and AST SpaceMobile’s satellite network seamlessly. That’s why collaboration matters:

  • AT&T provides America’s strongest nationwide wireless network.
  • AST SpaceMobile provides the satellite network designed to connect directly with everyday smartphones.
  • Ericsson provides AT&T the combined radio and core technology with intelligent network automation to optimize handoffs between ground-based and satellite networks—reducing transition times from seconds to milliseconds, so customers, first responders, and other essential workers stay connected.
  • Nokia provides the radio access technology and mobility software that powers AST SpaceMobile's space-based network, enabling seamless handover, service continuity, and a smooth customer experience as users move between terrestrial networks and satellite coverage.

Building on the strength of AT&T’s terrestrial network, AST SpaceMobile satellite connectivity is designed to complement and extend the network by enhancing service to remote and hard-to-reach areas, helping customers stay connected wherever they live, work and travel. This successful handoff advances the integration needed to deliver that experience, with terrestrial and satellite networks working together behind the scenes. For customers, the experience should be simple: staying connected without having to think about the technology behind it.

Advancing Toward Beta

"The successful handoff is a significant milestone, but it’s not the finish line. As we progress toward real-world beta services and ultimately commercial service, we’re bringing together AT&T’s broad and dense fiber footprint, the nation’s largest terrestrial wireless network, and complementary connectivity enabled by partners such as AST SpaceMobile to create a more seamless, resilient experience. And with AI embedded throughout our network and operations, we’re making that connectivity smarter, helping us anticipate needs, improve performance and deliver the reliable experiences our customers count on,” said Robert Walters, SVP, Network, AT&T.

“Together with AT&T we are making cellular broadband coverage from space a seamless part of the mobile network people use every day,” says Wayne Thorsen, Chief Commercial Officer, AST SpaceMobile. “Our space-based network, powered by our BlueBird satellites, with the largest communication arrays ever deployed in low Earth orbit is designed to give people more places to make calls, use their apps, stream content and stay connected with the smartphones they already own. It also adds a new layer of resilience for first responders who depend on reliable communications when it matters most.”

As AT&T moves toward beta and commercial service, the focus remains on extending connectivity farther and making it simpler for customers. Because when the connection matters, it has to be AT&T.
